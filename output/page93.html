America’s newest aerospace expo and air show – MAX POWER – is fast approaching, and NASA has announced key events beginning Friday, Nov. 6, and running through Sunday, Nov. 8, hosted on and near the agency’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

The expo also is a culmination of the agency’s recent Inspiration Tour, which included numerous flyovers to highlight NASA’s fleet of aircraft.

A current schedule of events is available on the agency’s website.

NASA also will share multiple news updates during the weekend, including hosting a news conference at 5 p.m. on Nov. 7, to provide its latest Moon Base update. Located near the lunar South Pole, the Moon Base is humanity’s first outpost for long-term lunar exploration.

Basically no one is using AI agents for their DoorDash orders

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

As AI tools and agents have continued their march across all aspects of digital life, many companies are trying to use LLMs to improve the experience of purchasing goods and services on the Internet. Thus far, though, a vanishingly small number of people seem to be taking advantage of those capabilities, at least when it comes to using the popular food delivery app DoorDash with an outside AI agent.

In a third-quarter earnings call Thursday, DoorDash co-founder and CEO Tony Xu responded to an analyst question by noting that "AI agents are generating several hundred orders [on DoorDash] across the time that they've been up and running." In a marked understatement, Xu called this a "very low use case" for a company that handled just shy of 1 billion orders total in the third quarter of 2026 alone.

Speaking generally, Xu said he sees the potential for AI agents to be genuinely helpful for "long consideration purchases" and major decisions where agents would be "doing work on your behalf proactively in the background." However, Xu added, "when it comes to a task that's as simple as ordering food, we tend to see that come directly to DoorDash."

Discover Airlines Adding 13 Airbus A350s, Starting With Four Ex-Lufthansa Jets

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Discover Airlines is Lufthansa Group’s long haul leisure carrier based in Germany. The airline has been growing in recent years, as Lufthansa Group (unsurprisingly) sees a lot of potential with its low cost operations. As a matter of fact, Lufthansa Group seems committed to growing everything except its flagship brand, Lufthansa.

In 2024, we first learned that the Discover Airlines fleet will grow in the coming years, and that we’ll eventually see the introduction of a new business class product. Then in 2025, we learned that the airline would start to acquire Airbus A350s. We now have a lot more details about what that will look like, including the timeline, as well as the first routes to be operated by the plane.

Discover Airlines modernizing long haul fleet with A350s

Discover Airlines has plans to acquire a total of 13 Airbus A350-900s. This will be a two-step process:

  • In 2027 and 2028, the airline will take over four former Lufthansa A350-900s, as those will gradually be put into service
  • In 2029 and beyond, the airline will take delivery of nine factory fresh A350-900s, all of which were part of a larger Lufthansa Group order

For context, Discover Airlines’ long haul fleet currently consists of 13 Airbus A330s — that’s now limited to the A330-300 variant, as the A330-200 variant has just been retired.

The current expectation is that the first former Lufthansa A350 will join the fleet in June 2027. It will initially operate short haul routes for crew familiarization, before flying long haul from Frankfurt (FRA) to Calgary (YYC) and Orlando (MCO).

The second A350 will then join the fleet in December 2027, with service on the plane being added to San Jose (SJO) and Belize City (BZE). The third and fourth A350 will then join the fleet in the summer of 2028.

By mid-2028, Discover Airlines expects its long haul fleet to grow to 20 aircraft, which includes the four former Lufthansa A350s, as well as some additional former Lufthansa A330s that will be added to the fleet.

Discover Airlines is growing its fleet in the coming years

It’s worth remembering that Lufthansa is the least profitable airline of Lufthansa Group. Rather than addressing that directly, Lufthansa Group’s solution to this issue is to continue outsourcing flying. For example, Lufthansa is about to drop flights to Seattle (SEA), as those will instead be operated by Discover Airlines. So it really shows you what a core part of Lufthansa’s plan this subsidiary is.

Discover Airlines isn’t the only Lufthansa Group leisure subsidiary getting A350s. SWISS’ leisure subsidiary, Edelweiss Air, is fully refreshing its long haul fleet, replacing A340-300s with A350-900s. In Edelweiss’ case, those are former LATAM jets that were retired early. These planes will also soon get new interiors, and the first aircraft should be retrofitted with all-new cabins in the coming months.

Edelweiss Air is also refreshing its fleet with A350s

What to expect onboard Discover Airlines A350s

In typical Lufthansa Group fashion, Discover Airlines has acquired most of its aircraft simply by being given Lufthansa planes as they’ve been retired. So most of Discover Airlines’ A330s have the typical “old” Lufthansa business class, in a 2-2-2 configuration.

Discover Airlines A330 business class seats

So you might be thinking that these initial four A350s that Discover Airlines is getting also have those interiors, given that most Lufthansa A350s also have business class seats in a 2-2-2 configuration.

Lufthansa A350 business class seats

However, not surprisingly, there’s another angle here. In 2021, Lufthansa opportunistically picked up four ex-Philippine Airlines A350s, as the carrier was going through a reorganization. Lufthansa maintained the cabin interiors, which funny enough, actually elevated Lufthansa’s business class experience considerably, given that these planes have Thompson Aero Vantage XL seats.

Future Discover Airlines A350 business class seats

There was always the question of what Lufthansa would eventually do with these planes, so it’s not surprising that they’re now going to a different subsidiary. Meanwhile Lufthansa’s A350s with the old business class are all eventually going to be reconfigured with Allegris cabins. So when these four special A350s are taken over by Discover Airlines, you can expect that interiors will be maintained.

Now, what about those nine factory fresh A350s that will join the fleet starting in 2029? We don’t yet know what we can expect from those interiors. From a standardization standpoint, I suppose it’s possible that the airline gets the same business class you’ll find with Lufthansa’s A350 Allegris business class and SWISS Senses’ A350 business class.

However, what’s interesting here is that Discover Airlines is also rolling out a new business class product on its existing A330s. This is an off the shelf product, which is probably smart from the company’s perspective, given all the certification issues we’ve seen over time. So I suppose it’s possible that this will also be introduced on the A350s… we’ll see!

New Discover Airlines A330 business class cabin

Bottom line

Discover Airlines will be modernizing its long haul fleet, as the airline will eventually take delivery of 13 Airbus A350-900s. In 2027 and 2028, we’ll see the airline acquire four Lufthansa A350s, and specifically, the planes that are former Philippine Airlines aircraft. Then in 2029 and beyond, we’ll see the airline get nine factory fresh A350s, part of the larger Lufthansa Group order.

This represents a major fleet modernization for the airline, and it’ll be interesting to see how this all plays out, especially when it comes to the brand new planes. Between this and the existing A330s getting new cabins, the Discover Airlines passenger experience is getting a nice boost.

Sarah Pidgeon’s Bouncy Supermodel Blowout Is Volumemaxxing at Its Finest

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Last fall, a tall 8-point Michigan buck showed me just how good—and lasting—the action can be during the first few days of November. The buck hit my mock scrape twice during daylight, which tipped me off that it was time for a strategic hunt where a small woodlot met standing corn. Between my hunt that evening and another the next morning, I had nearly a dozen encounters with the buck as he zig-zagged his way through the broken acres of timber and ag fields, feverishly seeking a receptive doe. The flurry of activity finally ended when he stepped into one of my shooting lanes at 11:10 am.

Bucks are transitioning into full-blown seeking mode right now, and I’m betting that this coming Tuesday will be one of the best days of the year to be on stand. Of course, it is a big election day across the country, so hopefully you voted early or sent in that absentee ballot, because this is a day you’d probably rather be hunting over a scrape line than standing in line waiting to cast a ballot. Here’s what to expect and how to proceed for your best shot at tagging out on Tuesday.

Related: The Best Days of the 2026 Whitetail Rut

Rut Phase: Late Pre-Rut/Seeking

Expect bucks to be checking just about every doe they see right now. (Photo/John Hafner Photography)

Rut hormones are starting to peak in bucks across much of whitetail range, and more and more does are showing signs that they are ready or nearly ready to breed. It should all add up to great hunting. Bucks, young and old alike, are in seeking mode now, often visible in daylight hours and moving more with each passing day.

This is the height of what I’d call the late pre-rut, with bucks largely still hitting scrapes, but possibly your last chance to catch up to a homebody buck before he abandons those scrapes and gets wildly unpredictable in the all-out chasing phase of the rut. Now, more than ever, bucks are susceptible to a wide range of calling and decoying tactics, as their default safety mode is being overridden by their urge to reproduce. This makes setting up “close enough” a viable strategy for bowhunters with a grunt call in pocket and a set of rattling antlers in hand. So, on this Best Day of the Rut, elect to pack a lunch, sit long, and be ready to shoot on a moment’s notice, because bucks are sure to be on the move.

November 3 Morning Hunt Plan: Sit a Junction Near Doe Bedding

Get tight to doe bedding for the morning hunt, and hang in there as long as you can.

Things change fast in the whitetail world, and staying on top of current sign can make or break your pre-rut hunting. Hopefully you’ve been scouting as much as hunting since our last Best Day. A big mistake many hunters make is thinking more time on stand will mean more opportunities, while ignoring the scouting that actually leads to high-quality sits. At this time of year, a scrape that was smoking hot last week may be stone cold when you show up, so use a weather event or midday break in your schedule to speed-scout for the latest sign and use that info to direct your hunting decisions. This could be checking a low-intrusion camera or cruising those scrape lines to confirm recent activity. Just try to minimize your impact and keep it as low key as possible.

For your morning hunt, think like a buck and focus on the does. Look for a transition area near known doe bedding where you can catch them returning in the morning hours. Bucks will be close behind. Focus on zones with numerous attraction factors or lines of travel to increases your chances. These factors could include a scrape line, convergence of well-used trails, a fruit tree, or multiple edges of cover necking down to one location. These things will steer deer traffic right past a carefully placed stand and multiply your chances of encountering a doe-seeking buck.

With my 2025 Michigan 8-point, the only standing corn in the area, just 30 yards from my stand, was prime doe bedding. This kept the tall 8 in the area and constantly moving, attempting to see if a doe was just behind the next stalk. The block of woods created four edges where it met a neighboring chunk of timber, encouraging deer to naturally move past my stand as they followed these edges. The last factor was a mock scrape the buck had taken over, which I set in August with just this time frame in mind.

Find a similar transition zone near does and stick it out. Even if a buck happens to sneak by you, there’s a good chance he’ll be back soon to re-check the area. Be sure to avoid areas where the deer will travel as you enter. For this killer morning ambush to work, you’ll need get settled early—like and hour early—to make sure you beat the deer back to their bedding transition. And don’t be in a hurry to leave either. Remember, I shot that tall 8-point at 11:10 am last year.

November 3 Evening Hunt Plan: Try a Food Plot Stake-Out

Let real does be your decoys at a prime feeding area in the evening now. (Photo/George Schmiesing via Adobe Stock)

You can’t go wrong with an evening sit at a food-plot, ag field, or other prime feeding area that you know does are hitting consistently. They will essentially be your real-life decoys. Nearby bucks will have a hard time resisting coming into the open to check on any mature doe they see, even if they aren’t quite in estrus, giving you an opportunity if you’re ready. And speaking of being ready, it’s a good idea between and just before hunts to practice getting drawn quickly, fast target acquisition, judging distance at a glance, and stopping a buck with a mouth grunt or bleat.

As for the evening hunt itself, this is prime time to employ a 3D decoy and deep-sounding grunt tube. I say 3D because, in my experience, 2D decoys at close range can spook deer that can’t see them from certain angles. A realistic 3D decoy can change this, signaling to a passing buck that something has this strange buck’s interest which requires his investigation. Bottom line, if a buck is seeking does in daylight, he’s probably sufficiently amped up to be vulnerable to these ploys.

What about scent? Using a fresh estrus scent now (where legal) could add the realism you need and make decent setup irresistible. Use a drag rope but add this trick: Don’t drag but dab your scent-filled rag on the ground like deer footsteps, exactly what a buck would be tracking. And speaking of scent, yours should be accounted for; use rubber boots to minimize any scent you leave and do your best to allow your scent on stand to blow just off from where bucks are likely to enter. This gives them the ability to scent-check the plot while also feeling safe enough to enter it.

Bonus Tip: Go to the Ground on Public Land

Be ready to set up an impromptu ground blind near thick cover on public land. (Photo/Realtree Media)

For the diehard public-land hunters out there, this Tuesday could thin out the rut crowd a bit and allow for some natural deer movement patterns, especially in the morning while most people are at work. From my experience, catching up to a buck on public land is still a tough task, especially in pressured areas. Being highly mobile is the fix. If you’re stuck in permanent stands, you may be left out of the action happening just a few yards away in thick cover.

Public-land bucks checking and pushing does tend to stay in denser cover for safety. The solution is to pack that mobile stand or saddle and sticks and adjust to the activity you’re seeing on the fly. If there are no trees big enough for that where the action is, consider a ground sit in exactly the right spot. Use natural cover so you don’t tip deer off to obvious changes to their bedroom or by setting up a blind. Instead, execute a quick, low-impact ambush on a rut-crazed buck on November 3rd.

ISERN SERRA CONCEALS RETRO COCKTAIL BAR BEHIND FOCACCERIA

 

Designed by Isern Serra for hospitality group Grupo La Confitería (led by Lito Baldovinos and Enric Rebordosa), Focacha is a speakeasy cocktail bar situated at Carrer Tuset 17 in Barcelona. The venue pays programmatic and aesthetic homage to the city’s 1960s and 1970s intellectual counterculture, the ‘gauche divine,’ and night-life figure Oriol Regàs, founder of the historic Bocaccio club. Concealed behind a traditional focacceria storefront designed by Pichy Glass, patrons access the venue through an operable refrigerator door, transitioning from a vernacular culinary retail interior into an immersive two-zone lounge modeled after Verner Panton’s 1968 Visiona installation.

CHROMATIC bar interiors PAY HOMAGE TO VERNER PANTON’S VISIONA

 

The interior layout, developed by designer Isern Serra, divides into two distinct spatial volumes. The initial bar area is wrapped in continuous carpeting across the floor, walls, and ceiling, establishing a monomaterial envelope referencing Verner Panton’s 1968 Visiona installation. At the center of the room, a curved stainless-steel bar features rounded edge profiles that catch and redistribute ambient reflections. Surrounding the counter, seven distinct seating modules transition across a calibrated chromatic gradient from yellow to crimson and deep blue. Along the perimeter, curvilinear niches recessed into the upholstered walls accommodate both functional back-bar bottle shelving and secluded, low-slung banquette seating. Multi-Lite pendants by GUBI suspend directly overhead, illuminating the metallic work surfaces while highlighting the contrast between the reflective steel counter and the dense, sound-dampening textile surfaces.

Focacha is a hidden speakeasy cocktail bar designed by Isern Serra on Carrer Tuset in Barcelona

 

 

DOMED DJ LOUNGE INTEGRATES SCENOGRAPHIC LIGHTING design

 

The second spatial volume hosts the DJ room, creating a Retro-Futuristic setting. A circular portal cuts through the carpeted wall plane, transitioning into the adjoining music and DJ lounge. Centered under an organic vaulted dome, the volume explores Space Age aesthetics through fluid geometry and enveloping surfaces. Scenographic lighting systems are concealed within circular ceiling apertures and perimeter wall openings, allowing the light temperature and color spectrum to shift dynamically in response to musical tempo. Period-specific luminaires anchor the perimeter seating arrangements, including Louis Poulsen Panthella floor lamps, &Tradition Flowerpot VP2 pendants, and Wire Stool VP11 units, while a central Dana lamp by Tacchini acts as a sculptural focal point beneath the plaster canopy.
