Rep. Bill Foster is leaning against challenging Rep. Maxine Waters for the top Democratic spot on the House Financial Services Committee, according to two people with knowledge of his thinking who were granted anonymity to discuss private deliberations.

The Illinois Democrat has been considering a bid to topple Waters but has not made a final decision, the people said. The timeline for him doing so is unclear.

Democratic Rep. Sean Casten, also from Illinois, told Waters earlier this month that he plans to run against her, likely teeing up a major fight over the Financial Services role, which the California Democrat has held since 2013.

Most Financial Services Democrats on the committee have publicly backed Waters for the role.

Foster, who was first elected in 2008, previously discussed with Waters ways to reform the committee that would give subcommittee chairs more power to shape the panel's agenda. A physicist with a doctorate, Foster is currently the top Democrat on a House Financial Services Financial Institutions Subcommittee.

A Foster spokesperson declined to comment.

Why are NWSL teams using their goalkeepers in attack?

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Kansas City Current’s 2-1 win over Washington Spirit last Sunday was a meeting of two high-quality teams, about as good as it gets in the National Women’s Soccer League. And yet, the game was defined by goalkeeping giveaways.

In the 12th minute, the Current’s Lorena took a heavy touch off a back-pass, allowing Clara Luvanga in to give Washington the lead. However, having let in an equalizer immediately, Washington then conceded the match-winner in the 17th minute, their goalkeeper Sandy MacIver tackled before Vanessa DiBernardo lashed home.

There were key differences in these two moments. Lorena had more time to clear her lines, whereas MacIver was given a slow, sideways ball under pressure. Still, both moments highlighted one of the major developments in NWSL play in recent seasons, which is using the goalkeeper in attack. And they naturally brought up questions too. Why do teams do this? And is it worth the risk?

Using the goalkeeper in attack is not to be confused with the “sweeper-keeper,” where the ‘keeper runs out of the box to clear up loose balls behind their back line. Ann-Katrin Berger of Gotham is the most obvious example of that role in the NWSL today, but it’s a role that is fundamentally a defensive measure, to back up higher pressing and higher defensive lines.

Oklahoma’s next senator can be seated immediately after election, state AG rules

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The winner of next week's Oklahoma Senate race will be able to take office immediately after the election, effectively ending Republican Sen. Alan Armstrong’s appointment before the current session of the Senate concludes, the state attorney general ruled on Thursday.

Oklahoma Attorney General Gentner Drummond’s ruling could pave the way for Rep. Kevin Hern (R-Okla.), the heavy favorite in the race and a Trump ally, to be seated by early November.

Armstrong, a former energy executive, was appointed in March to temporarily fill the seat vacated by Markwayne Mullin, now Department of Homeland Security secretary.

Both Armstrong and Hern submitted memorandums to the state attorney general’s office in disagreement over when Armstrong’s appointment ends, with Hern arguing he should be able to take office before the beginning of the next Congress.

"Oklahoma law and the U.S. Constitution point to the same answer: the voters fill this seat," Drummond said in a statement. "Oklahomans will choose their next senator at the polls on Nov. 3, and that person will be eligible to take office once the election is certified. This opinion gives the Governor and the Legislature clear guidance on how to proceed."

This November’s election is for a full six-year term in the Senate. Mullin, a former member of the House, initially won a 2022 special election to complete the term of the Sen. Jim Inhofe, who retired early in January 2023.

Armstrong was not a candidate to replace Mullin; Oklahoma law bars Senate appointees from seeking election to a full term.

Drummond’s formal opinion, addressed to Gov. Kevin Stitt and Oklahoma state Senate President pro tempore Lonnie Paxton, effectively sides with Hern and points to a state statute, which directs the governor to appoint a person “to serve in such office until a successor is elected.”

“The Governor has no discretion to decline or delay certifying the election,” Drummond added.

In a statement, the Hern campaign praised the state attorney general's decision. “We appreciate the Attorney General’s review and are pleased that his opinion confirms our understanding of Oklahoma law," it said.

A spokesperson for Armstrong’s office said they are reviewing the opinion, and that “in the meantime, Senator Armstrong’s full focus remains on passing comprehensive permitting reform to lower utility prices, grow the economy, and get America building again.”

Everton targeting Iceland international as Thierno Barry alternative

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Everton could make signing a new centre-forward a priority in January after entering the season short of options in attack.

David Moyes needs additional firepower to strengthen his squad, and a promising Iceland international has reportedly emerged as a potential solution.

Real Sociedad striker Orri Oskarsson is attracting interest from several Premier League clubs, with Everton understood to be paying particularly close attention to his progress.

The 22-year-old has impressed in Spain, and the Toffees could explore a move as they look to address a key weakness in their squad.

Everton Monitoring Orri Oskarsson

According to TEAMtalk, Everton are among the clubs monitoring Oskarsson ahead of the January transfer window. Tottenham Hotspur, Newcastle United, Sunderland and Brentford are also reportedly tracking the Icelandic forward, meaning Moyes could face competition if Everton decide to pursue a deal.

Everton’s need for a striker has become increasingly apparent following their summer business. Beto departed for Fiorentina without a replacement arriving, leaving Thierno Barry as the club’s only recognised centre-forward. Attempts to sign Folarin Balogun from Monaco and Joshua Zirkzee from Manchester United also failed to produce a deal.

That situation has reportedly encouraged Everton to examine their options ahead of January, with Oskarsson among the forwards attracting attention.

The Iceland international joined Real Sociedad from FC Copenhagen in 2024 and made progress during his previous campaign, scoring nine goals in LaLiga. His combination of physical presence, technical ability and finishing has helped establish him as an appealing prospect for clubs looking to strengthen their attacking ranks.

Oskarsson has also continued to impress at international level, with his performances for Iceland adding to his growing reputation. At 22, he could offer Everton both an immediate option up front and a potential long-term investment.

However, Everton would need to establish whether Real Sociedad are prepared to negotiate during the January window. The Spanish club invested significantly to secure his services, and there is no confirmation that they would be willing to let him leave midway through the season.

What This Means for Everton

Oskarsson could be an important addition for Everton as Moyes looks to address the lack of depth at centre-forward. Relying heavily on Barry leaves the manager with limited specialist alternatives, particularly across a demanding second half of the campaign.

The Icelandic striker’s profile could suit Everton’s need for another option capable of leading the line. His technical qualities may also give Moyes greater flexibility in how the team approaches matches, while his age offers scope for further development.

Competition from Tottenham, Newcastle, Sunderland and Brentford could complicate Everton’s pursuit, however. The Toffees would need to move decisively if their interest develops into a formal approach, while the final cost and Sociedad’s willingness to sell would be key factors.

For now, Everton’s reported interest suggests the club are assessing potential solutions ahead of January rather than confirming a deal is imminent. With Moyes needing greater depth in attack, Oskarsson is a name to watch as the winter transfer window approaches.

NFL fines Caleb Williams $23,882 for "unauthorized logo" on shoes in Week 7

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

As to Caleb Williams, the NFL currently stands for the 'Nother Fine League.

Per a source with knowledge of the situation, the NFL has fined the Bears quarterback $23,882 for wearing an "unauthorized logo" on his shoes during the Week 7 game against the Patriots. That represents double the amount of the $11,941 fine imposed on Williams last week for wearing the forbidden footwear in Week 6 against the Falcons.

Williams has said that he plans to continue wearing Caddix shoes, which do not have a deal with the league. He presumably will appeal the second fine.

“I’m huge into trying to stay on the field," Williams said Wednesday. "I’m huge into trying to be healthy. It’s a rough game and things like that. So the cleats, they’ve been great. They’ve been super comfy and they’ve worked. They are a science-type of cleat. It’s something that focuses on when you plant on the hard plants on turf or grass, they call them SmartStuds, and they move when you move in these hard plants. They take tension off of your tendons, your joints and all of that. So over the course of a long career or over the course of a long year, when you can take some percentage -- it could be one, it could be 50, it could be 12, it could be whatever, you know. When you take that percentage off consistently over a period of time, you know, you get to have the chance, a higher chance of being healthy. And so that’s what I focus on.

“I try to be healthy. They’re smart cleats. They’re science cleats, and they’ve been working for me. A bunch of other guys wear them in the league, and they’re forward-focused on making sure players stay on the field, and that’s what I’m always focused on."

The appeal of Williams's fine for Week 6 has not yet been resolved.

Presumably, the league will continue to impose fines in increasing amounts unless and until Williams complies. At some point, the league could (in theory) prevent Williams from playing in the non-conforming shoes. Neither fine letter, however, warns him that he could be kept from playing with the Caddix shoes.
