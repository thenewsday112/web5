Circle Begins CCTP V1 Wind-Down Amid Doc Mismatch

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Burn limits on Circle's legacy USDC bridge began falling on October 31, and the V1 contracts pause on December 1.

On the same migration page, a block of instructions written for AI coding assistants still says Noble, Sui and Aptos "still require V1." Circle's own release notes say two of those three chains moved to V2 weeks ago.

Circle's legacy cross-chain transfer protocol entered its final month today. From October 31, burn limits on CCTP V1 start falling toward zero, and on December 1 the V1 contracts pause. Any app still using them will stop moving USDC between chains.

Most coverage has focused on those two dates. But the guide developers are told to follow, Circle's V1-to-V2 migration page, contains a mistake in the section written for AI tools.

Embedded in the page is an "Agent Instructions" block, guidance meant for AI coding assistants that read the documentation for developers. It says: "Noble, Sui, and Aptos still require V1." For two of those three chains, Circle's other published records say the opposite.

What Starts On October 31

The schedule is fixed. Circle announced in an August 27 blog post that "CCTP V1 (Legacy) deprecation begins October 31, 2026 and completes on December 1, 2026."

From today, V1 burn limits fall, and the migration guide says "message limits will tighten over time until no new burns can be initiated." On December 1 the V1 contracts pause, and any integration still pointing at them can no longer transfer USDC between chains.

Circle has been firm about this. "Migration from CCTP V1 (Legacy) to CCTP V2 is required, not just recommended," the company wrote, adding that V2 "uses separate contracts and APIs and is not backward compatible."

Funds already in transit are protected. The guide says "all pending redemptions will remain available" during the phase-out, and that Circle "will maintain minter allowances greater than the total of pending attestations."

The Line Hidden In Circle's Agent Instructions

The migration page has two audiences. Human readers see the main guide. AI coding assistants also parse a separate instruction block, which tells them how to answer developer questions.

In that block, the guidance is clear: "Noble, Sui, and Aptos still require V1."

The main text above it says something different. According to the human-facing guide, "CCTP V2 contracts are available on all CCTP V1 chains except for Noble and Sui," and "Sui will be supported by V2 before the deprecation begins." Aptos isn't listed as an exception there at all.

So one page gives two different answers about the same chains, and the out-of-date answer is the one written for AI tools.

What Circle's Own Records Say About Sui And Aptos

Circle's 2026 CCTP release notes show how out of date the instruction is:

  • August 25: "CCTP V2 support for Aptos added (domain 9)." The note says Aptos had previously been available only on CCTP V1 (Legacy).
  • October 8: Sui mainnet and testnet were added as supported CCTP chains (domain 8). The migration guide had said this would happen before deprecation began.

Circle's supported chains and domains page now lists both Sui and Aptos in its CCTP table, with Standard Transfer support. The page's V1-only section names one chain: Noble.

As of today, Aptos has had V2 for more than two months and Sui for more than three weeks. The instruction telling AI tools that both "still require V1" was overtaken by Circle's own updates before the shutdown began.

Noble Is The Real V1-Only Chain

Noble is the one chain in that line where the instruction is still accurate, and it is leaving CCTP entirely.

In a September 10 announcement, Circle said "Noble will not receive CCTP V2" and set out its exit:

  • October 13: new USDC minting to Noble through Circle Mint stopped.
  • October 31: CCTP V1 burn limits began falling.
  • December 1: CCTP exits from Noble may be limited to destination chains that still support CCTP V1.
  • January 12, 2027: the Noble USDC contract and CCTP routes are fully paused, and Circle takes a snapshot of remaining balances.
  • January 13, 2027: Noble is no longer reachable through Circle Mint or CCTP, and a manual redemption process opens.

For Noble, V1 is the only route available, and it is being closed.

Why A Stale Instruction Matters Now

A wrong line in documentation would normally be a small issue, but the timing makes this one more serious.

Many developers now ask AI assistants to write or update integration code, and those assistants read documentation like this page. A developer asking how to bridge USDC to Sui or Aptos could be told, based on Circle's own instruction block, to use V1. That is the route whose burn limits started falling today and which pauses in a month.

The fix in the code is not small either. Moving to V2 means:

  • pointing at new contract addresses
  • using new ABIs, since depositForBurn takes extra parameters
  • choosing a transfer speed through finalityThreshold
  • replacing the attestation flow with /v2/messages/{sourceDomainId}
  • accounting for fees, which V1 did not charge

An integration built on V1 now would have to be rebuilt within weeks.

What Integrators Should Check Before December 1

For teams working with Sui and Aptos, the published records point one way: build on V2, using the contract addresses for domains 8 and 9. Don't follow the V1 guidance from the agent-instruction block.

For Noble, the deadline is January 12, 2027. Before then, integrators need to remove Noble as a CCTP route, and holders need to move or redeem their USDC.

For everyone else, the burn-limit reductions have started. December 1 is the last day V1 integrations work, and any app still on V1 after that will stop moving USDC between chains.

West Ham Player Ratings: WHU 1-3 WBA, No Confidence, No Cohesion And No Answers

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

West Ham 1-3 West Bromwich Albion 

West Ham suffered another thoroughly miserable afternoon at the London Stadium as West Bromwich Albion exposed the defensive frailties and midfield problems which have become all too familiar under Nuno Espírito Santo. Jarrod Bowen’s excellent first-half equaliser briefly offered some hope, but the Hammers were second best for much of the opening 45 minutes.

What concerns me most is that there’s something inherently wrong with this team which goes far beyond individual players. There’s no confidence, no cohesion and precious little understanding between teammates. We can’t defend properly, we struggle to pass the ball through midfield and we’re painfully slow when opponents attack us.

West Ham did enjoy plenty of attacking possession in the second half, particularly down the wings, but there was very little cutting edge. Meanwhile, West Brom were able to score three avoidable goals. As I’ve said before, this is not bums on seats football that West Ham are playing.

West Ham Player Ratings v West Bromwich Albion

Mads Hermansen – 3/10

Came out to punch West Brom’s first corner clear, but his handling of another set piece in the second half was dreadful. Completely lost the flight of the ball and West Ham were fortunate not to concede. Another unconvincing afternoon for a goalkeeper whose performances continue to concern me.

Kyle Walker-Peters – 5/10

Looked sharp going forward and produced some excellent attacking moments, including a lovely pass to release Bowen. Unfortunately, he was repeatedly caught out of position defensively, leaving enormous spaces down West Ham’s right. Kilman and Golambeckis were constantly having to cover for him. Picked up a yellow card and appeared to suffer an injury before being replaced by Veltman.

Airidas Golambeckis – 5/10

I was really impressed with the youngster during the opening stages. His reading of the game was excellent, he anticipated danger, won important tackles and showed remarkable composure when turning out of trouble under pressure.

Unfortunately, Jed Morgan got the better of him for West Brom’s second goal, although Austbø’s dreadful pass had created the problem in the first place. Golambeckis recovered to make an excellent block when Isaac Price threatened on the break. A mixed afternoon, but I still think there’s a very promising footballer here.

Airidas Golambeckis was far from West Ham’s worst player.

Max Kilman – 5/10

Probably West Ham’s busiest defender, making several important blocks and interceptions. He repeatedly had to cover the space left by Walker-Peters and produced an excellent intervention to prevent Ousmane Diakité from scoring.

However, he was also involved in the defensive confusion for West Brom’s opening goal and received a deserved yellow card for a rash challenge after stepping into midfield. Almost scored with a header from Bowen’s corner. Did plenty of good things, but was part of a defence that conceded three poor goals.

Morato – 4/10

His involvement in the opening goal wasn’t particularly impressive, with West Brom finding it far too easy to get behind the defence.

But his worst moment came for the third goal. From a West Brom corner, Morato made a complete hash of his clearance, barely flicking his foot at the ball and failing even to get it out of the penalty area. Callum Styles subsequently scored with a half-volley which bounced through a crowd of players. Really poor defending.

Arne Engels – 3/10

An absolutely dreadful performance. Made a mess of two attempted clearances early on, completely missed the ball on one occasion and was repeatedly dispossessed in dangerous areas.

Diakité seemed to have his number, and Engels was beaten far too easily in midfield. His corner delivery was also poor, with one dreadful low effort failing to beat the first man. Picked up West Ham’s third yellow card late on.

Mohamadou Kanté – 4/10

Certainly couldn’t fault his effort. Had an early half-volley which went over the bar and another goal-bound attempt deflected behind after excellent work from Austbø.

There were moments when he looked relatively dangerous, and I still think there’s a potentially effective footballer there. However, his partnership with Pablo was thoroughly uninspiring, and West Ham never looked particularly threatening through the middle. Replaced by Souček.

Divine Mukasa – 4/10

A technically gifted footballer who I think West Ham are wasting in his current role. Far too often, Mukasa was having to drop deep to collect the ball from the centre-backs because there was absolutely no fluidity in midfield.

He should be operating much further forward, where he can influence attacks rather than trying to compensate for everyone else’s inability to move the ball. Had an early moment when he might have conceded a penalty on another day. Replaced by Solomon.

Jarrod Bowen – 5/10

Scored West Ham’s equaliser with a brilliant right-footed finish after driving into the penalty area. It was a desperately needed goal at a time when the Hammers were massively under the cosh.

Looked lively at times, producing some excellent dribbling and a lovely sidestep down the right, although one promising cross was badly overhit. Delivered the corner from which Kilman nearly scored. One of the few players capable of creating something, but even Bowen couldn’t rescue this performance.

Edvin Austbø – 5/10

Produced some absolutely excellent runs down the left, including a dazzling early slalom into the penalty area which created a shooting opportunity for Kanté.

Indeed, much of West Ham’s attacking threat came from Austbø’s willingness to run at defenders. Unfortunately, his dreadful misplaced pass shortly after half-time gifted West Brom possession and contributed directly to their second goal.

Tried to make amends with further dangerous runs, but that mistake was extremely costly. A real mixture of exciting attacking play and careless possession.

Pablo – 3/10

Another thoroughly disappointing performance. Got himself into a promising position after nine minutes but shot wide, and his first touch repeatedly let him down.

Lost possession in dangerous areas, including an incident which led to West Brom threatening to score again. I can’t fault his willingness to work, but there’s simply no pace, fluency or threat in West Ham’s attacking partnership. Replaced by Piroe before the hour.

West Ham Substitute Ratings

Joël Piroe – 4/10

Replaced Pablo in the 59th minute. Managed to find a gap through a brick wall of West Brom legs to get a shot away in the 76th minute. There was an appeal for handball, although I didn’t think it was one. Offered little else to suggest West Ham were going to find a way back.

Manor Solomon – 4/10

Came on for Mukasa and quickly played the ball to Walker-Peters, whose powerful effort forced a good save. However, he couldn’t provide the attacking spark West Ham desperately needed.

Joël Veltman – 5/10

Immediately made an important defensive tackle after replacing the injured Walker-Peters, conceding a corner in the process. Brought some defensive awareness, although by that stage West Ham were chasing the game and the tactical changes were doing little to trouble West Brom.

Tomáš Souček – 4/10

Barely noticed him after he replaced Kanté. West Ham appeared to have almost completely surrendered midfield by this stage, and Souček did very little to change that.

Taty Castellanos – 3/10

Replaced Golambeckis as Nuno changed the formation, but was almost completely anonymous.

His first notable contribution was an awful, hopeful shot from distance in the 82nd minute. Two minutes later he produced another effort so high that it had a chance of travelling across London and sailing between the posts at Twickenham. A proper Jonny Wilkinson-style hoof.

West Ham 3 – 1 down.

Nuno stood, static, deep in thought as another modest opponent was made to look like a table-topper thanks to his line up, his tactics and some terrible defending. Max Kilman – and his manager – are going to cop another shedload of criticism for this effort. VJ Edgecombe was ready for the moment yet again

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
PHILADELPHIA, PENNSYLVANIA - OCTOBER 30: Vj Edgecombe #77 of the Philadelphia 76ers celebrates a three-pointer during the second half of an Emirates NBA Cup game against the New York Knicks at Xfinity Mobile Arena on October 30, 2026 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. NOTE TO USER: User expressly acknowledges and agrees that, by downloading and or using this photograph, User is consenting to the terms and conditions of the Getty Images License Agreement. (Photo by Emilee Chinn/Getty Images) | Getty Images

One of the main schools of thought stemming from the “there’s only one ball to go around” critique of this year’s Sixers team was that it would hinder the development of VJ Edgecombe, especially going into a crucial second season.

Five games into his sophomore season and that hasn’t been a problem at all for the guard from Baylor. The Sixers have won all five games he’s appeared in and he’s made big plays down the stretch in just about all of them.

As the New York Knicks looked prime to pull off a devastating comeback in Philadelphia, Edgecombe hit a three to extend the Sixers’ one-point lead to four with 32 seconds remaining. It’s the type of clutch shotmaking the coaching staff has come to expect from Edgecombe after he made a habit of it his rookie year.

“He just does that, it’s really who he is,” head coach Nick Nurse said after the game. “We all know it.”

Nurse, nor any Sixer, had a problem with Edgecombe taking that shot despite missing his previous three attempts from deep on the night. He plays with a level of fearlessness and confidence that’s earned that trust from his teammates.

Even some of his newest teammates have picked on Edgecombe’s knack for big moments.

“He doesn’t shy away from the moment,“ LeBron James said. ”[He’s a] young, young kid, but thrives in the moment.“

It was a bit relieving that it was a three ball from Edgecombe that saved the day. For all his contributions this season, shooting the ball from deep has been one of his rare struggles. He’s shot just 29% from three through his first five games of the campaign.

“Early I haven’t been making a lot of threes, but they’re gonna fall, and I’m gonna keep shooting the ball,” Edgecombe said. “Tonight, even though I was 0-for-3 at the time, I still shot it. That’s just the confidence I have in myself.”

What Edgecombe has done a great job of this year is using the attention of his superstar teammates to cut into wide open lanes and scoop up easy baskets. He’s also become one of the best rebounders on the team while usually guarding the other team’s best player.

He’s done an especially good job cutting for Joel Embiid, a big reason Embiid is up to averaging 4.8 assists per game so far this season.

“When they go double him, obviously you know teams gonna rotate,” Edgecombe said, “and so I just try to find that gap where I can cut or just find a window for him.”

It’s the type of complementary basketball required to make a group with this much talent work together. Many worried before the season there wasn’t anywhere on the roster the Sixers would get those types of contributions from.

“It’s great to just have those guys, those complementary guys out there on the floor where we can all complement one another,” James said. “‘Rese getting double teamed, VJ has the confidence that Tyrese gonna give him the ball and he can knock down shots and vice versa.”

Edgecombe is clearly fifth in the pecking order in the healthy starting five when it comes to shot distribution, but that hasn’t stopped him from being one of their most important players when he’s on the floor. Being vital to helping a team with championship expectations pick up wins on a nightly basis has been just fine for his development so far.
