Instagram is offering YouTubers up to $500,000 to post with its new horizontal format

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Instagram isn’t just chasing YouTube‘s TV watch time and ad revenue–it’s chasing its talent, too. The Meta-owned platform has offered to pay some YouTubers up to $500,000 to post videos with its newly announced long-form horizontal format “Features.”

According to Business Insider, Instagram has approached “select” YouTubers with a floor price of $50,000; others have gotten six-figure offers, and things top out at a half-million dollars.

“We’ve been public about the fact that we are funding Features content directly with some creators right now to support them,” a Meta spokesperson told BI. “The details of those deals are confidential, but we’re excited to see the content going live.”

It’s not clear how Instagram is paying out these amounts–per video, per month, or as one-time flat compensation. We’d bet the latter, and perhaps the pot’s sweetened with a promise to pay creators performance bonuses or an ad rev share if their Features content does well (especially on TVs, where pricier ads slots can pull in serious cash for platforms).

Meta already said, though, that it’s “too early to share a long-term monetization model [for Features], but we’re exploring ways for creators to earn from brands, fans, and Instagram, including performance-based incentives.”

Introducing Features earlier this week, Instagram hyped its established TV viewership, with VP of Product Tessa Lyons saying “people love to watch Reels together in their living rooms with their friends, so we’ve been working on a TV version of Reels.”

“[W]e know to do that well, we need more than just short-form video,” she added.

Insta chief Adam Mosseri chimed in Oct. 28 after concern from creators, tweeting that “[w]e understand the economics matter a ton, and are exploring multiple options to help long-form creators get paid.”

This YouTuber-bait program is perhaps one of those options.

Instagram, of course, is not the only one gunning for YouTube’s talent–but unlike Netflix (and, increasingly, Disney), it’s not trying to convince creators to give it exclusivity.

People familiar with the deals told BI Instagram’s terms just ask creators to post their long-form videos as Features on the same day they upload to YouTube.

Reza Izad, who reps Kareem Rahma, Noah Beck, and more as co-founder and co-CEO of Underscore Talent, told BI these deals are an “exciting opportunity” for YouTubers. The money is part of that excitement, sure, but Izad said this is also an opportunity for creators’ content to get a boost.

“Every time there’s a new feature in these apps that the company’s promoting, people will have more opportunity to be seen because there’s going to be algorithmic preference to this kind of content,” he said. “And unlike short-form, there’s not a lot of abundance.”

What he’s saying is true: We already know Instagram plans to pump posts made with Clips as Reels (a tool that takes snippets of Features videos and posts them to Reels with a link back to the full long-form upload) on its Explore page, and in other areas around the platform.

Something to think about in all of this is that a major part of YouTube’s living room success is the relative attractiveness and easy UX of its TV app. Google has put stock into making the TV app a banger, and Instagram isn’t there yet.

As Mosseri put it, “We’re going to try to support these different types of formats, both in the mobile app, but also on the TV app, which […] is going to take some time to get it right. But I’m actually very excited about it, and we’re very committed to it long-term.”

Creators looking for equal viewership across YouTube and Features will likely be disappointed–at least for now. But for the folks Instagram wants to pay, this is indeed a rare chance to get get cash for cross-posting.

HYPE Targets $120 as NYSE Owner Moves to Bring Hyperliquid Perpetual Futures to Regulated U.S. Markets

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Michael van de Poppe believes HYPE is ready to pump harder after the NYSE owner moves to bring Hyperliquid perpetual contracts to U.S. regulated markets. In an announcement today, Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange, is preparing to introduce Hyperliquid perpetual contracts to the regulated American market.

ICE to offer on-chain perpetual futures to US clients

Today, HYPE has been the biggest mover in the crypto market. The trigger behind Hyperliquid’s spike is ICE’s plan to roll out a US-regulated on-chain perpetual futures avenue built on Hyperliquid infrastructure. According to the announcement, the NYSE owner has sealed a strategic partnership with Trade.xyz to establish round-the-clock, Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures trading in the US.

Trade.xyz and Hyperliquid already have a structured working relationship, with trading still awaiting regulatory authorization.

Based on the announcement, Hyperliquid will offer underlying infrastructure to bring regulated on-chain perpetual futures to US customers. Trade.xyz, a trading platform built on Hyperliquid, will create a regulated market and clearing structure for perpetual futures contracts in the US, pending regulatory approvals. Meanwhile, ICE will serve as a specialized administrator for Trade.xyz’s regulated markets and deploy strategic investment into the firm.

The collaboration seeks to bring Trade.xyz’s perpetual markets, currently operating on Hyperliquid, into a U.S. regulated framework. Once approved, U.S. customers are expected to access compliant on-chain perpetual futures markets on Hyperliquid’s HIP-3 framework.

Analyst says HYPE ready for a stronger move

Crypto analyst Michael van de Poppe expects HYPE to reach $110-120 soon, citing ongoing preparations to bring Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures to US clients. After the announcement, Hyperliquid’s HYPE token surged over 20% today and is currently trading at $96.30. The asset reached an ATH of $97.96 last month.

The analyst flagged an imminent bullish formation for HYPE, hinging on NYSE owner’s move to bring Hyperliquid perpetual futures to regulated U.S. markets. If it launches successfully, Van de Poppe believes the new offerings will further boost Hyperliquid adoption, pushing its value toward the $110-$120 level.

For courage, says Bulgarian folk wisdom instructs, keep a horse chestnut in your pocket.

Even as a child, I was mistrustful of superstition. But every autumn, I would pick up dozens of them from the sidewalks of Sofia and fill my pockets — not as a spell against fear but as an ode to joy. Conkers, as the English call them, spun me up in delight — shiny as mahogany, smooth as river stones, tiny planets from some other universe, the wonder-smitten eyes of some prehistoric creature.

I didn’t know then that they are emblems of life’s irrepressible insistence on itself — miniature monuments to the stubborn creativity of survival.

Illustration from Die Baume und Straucher des Waldes by by Gustav Hempel and Karl Wilhelm, 1889.

Three million years ago, as the Ice Age was beginning to sink its cool blue fangs into the warm body of Earth, the widespread horse chestnut (Aesculus hippocastanum) began dying off, until only a small population remained, most of it sheltered in the gorges of Bulgaria’s mountains. There, in their sanctum of stone, the trees found themselves unable to attract pollinators during the slender three-week window of their flowering.

This is the story of life — innovate or die: The horse chestnut evolved enormous white flower spikes with pink tips and a chemical hack to block the oxidation of polyphenols in the petal, which turns each flower brown and sheds it after pollination. For three whole weeks, the horse chestnut remains a bride in a gown of amazement, its crown an aria of perfect white blossoms that no bee can resist, all because there was a safe place in which to experiment with the possible.

Illustration from American Medicinal Plants by Charles Frederick Millspaugh, 1887.

That place, I learn from my poet friend Hannah Fries’s absolutely wonderful book A Forest Language, is called refugium. Hannah writes:

When glaciers last crept down from the Arctic, 20,000 years ago, there were places where the icy fingers didn’t reach — mountaintops, hot springs, lowlands beyond the ice. There were pockets of green where things crawled and slithered and ate and mated, protected places where life went on, and from which life set forth again as the ice receded. Refugia.

[…]

The gift of a refugium is time. It holds in its small cup of safety a promise for a new world to come. And it lends out to the living all the years, centuries, millennia it might take to finally find that world.

Even in easier times, it is wise to tend the refuge. What is Earth, after all, but a momentary refugium in the inhospitable blackness of space? From a vantage point far beyond our grasping glows the blue-green dot, a brief and luminous memory in the mind of the universe.

It is the memory of a place where a simple cell had the ambition to harness the energy of a star, and from it, the green wonder of leaves, the trunks rising up, bursting and blooming into the sky — all of life in the sweet, cool shade below.

We too need them, these refugia that shelter us, that save us in the ice ages of life. Whatever your refugium may be — a place, a person, a practice, a poem — it is worth tending to with all the might and all the means you have.

Description

This slice of Teghaza 001, one of the most ancient Martian meteorites ever found on Earth, was used to determine the age of the rock. Reflected light images like these serve as a scientist’s road map, helping them find minerals of interest to analyze by different instruments.

Figure A

Figure A is the same image with annotations showing minerals like iron-titanium oxides (brighter grains) and pyroxene (medium gray), both of which tell a story about the formation of Mars’ crust and mantle. Shocked plagioclase (darker gray) is created by extreme pressures during impacts, including the one that sent Teghaza 001 flying through space to Earth. The alteration represents fine grain carbonate, sulfate and clay minerals formed on Earth.

James Garner, Hackney, and Thierno Barry were all on the scoresheet for the Blues’ 3-1 Carabao Cup fourth-round victory at Hill Dickinson Stadium.  

For Hackney, his strike to help Everton into the quarter-finals was his first-ever goal for the Toffees, and he described the result as an “important moment” for the Club.  

“I’m delighted with that,” he said. “It was a special night. 

“I think us as a team and the fans knew it was a big game. After losing the last two games – I know it was against good opposition – but I feel like that was a big moment tonight. 

“I think it was an important night against Premier League opposition.

“The fans really helped. They were unbelievable.”

On the goal, he explained: “I’m always working on arriving in the box at the right time. It was a great ball from Barry, and I managed to put it in the corner. 

“It was a great feeling to get my first goal for this amazing club and hopefully there’s many more to come.” 

After sporadic starts so far for the Toffees following a summer move from Middlesbrough, Hackney is hopeful an impactful performance against Newcastle, which also included an assist for Garner, can keep him in David Moyes’ starting 11.

“I’ve had a bit of time on the bench,” he continued.

“The lads have been playing really well, so I can’t complain. I kept working hard and hopefully, I took my chance tonight.  

In the next round of the Carabao Cup – the quarter-finals – Everton face an away trip to take on Fleetwood Town.

Stellar Opens Two-Way Bridge to Ethereum as Institutional RWA Gains Momentum on Network

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

To advance interoperability, Stellar has opened a two-way bridge to Ethereum, integrating its protocol with Ethereum’s mainnet. According to market development released today, Stellar revealed the launch of VIA Labs’ cross-chain messaging system on its mainnet.

Stellar rolls out enhanced two-way Ethereum bridge

Stellar’s move to open the VIA Labs’ multichain bridge on its protocol gives its Soroban smart contract platform direct, two-way communication with Ethereum. The connection now lets smart contracts on both Stellar and Ethereum exchange messages and applications through VIA Labs’ multichain messaging system.

The cross-chain bridge integration matters in several ways. As the announcement states, it lets Stellar developers efficiently move supported applications and assets between networks. It also enables developers to orchestrate contract activities across chains, including building applications around stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets. 

Meanwhile, the multichain bridge addresses fragmentation on Stellar by letting users move assets and interact efficiently with different blockchain environments, including the Ethereum ecosystem.

RWA demand on Stellar heating up

The two-way Ethereum bridge integrates at a time when the RWA narrative continues to gain momentum on Stellar. The network is rapidly emerging as a major force in the race to tokenize RWAs, with billions of dollars flocking into the blockchain.

This is evident in Securitize, the largest tokenization platform, which has integrated the Stellar network to issue and manage its tokenized financial assets. Other dominant global asset managers that recently moved tokenized funds to Stellar include Pontoro, ArchaxEX, among others, underscoring the network’s growing popularity.

By unveiling the groundbreaking two-way Ethereum bridge, Stellar addresses the complex engineering challenge in RWA tokenization. To accurately represent physical commodities, bonds, real estate, and more on the blockchain, Stellar needs not only strong, interoperable smart contracts but also a flawless, tamper-proof data bridge. This is where the two-way Ethereum bridge comes in.

Charles M. Schulz Museum With Kids: A Family Guide To Santa Rosa, CA

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Sabina Graves

When the chance came to visit Santa Rosa, California, and experience the Charles M. Schulz Museum, the timing couldn’t have been more perfect. Our little one is ~deep~ in a Snoopy phase. We’ve been rotating Peanuts classics with The Snoopy Show on Apple TV+ because they’re such great low-stimulation wind-down shows before bed (parents included). So Peanuts Gang vibes were definitely what we wanted out of a family trip, and Santa Rosa delivered: walkable cozy dining, cute cartoon sightings around every corner, and plenty for a little kid to get into.

Getting there was definitely the most stressful part because our once-dozing darling of the skies had grown into a ball of energy bouncing around the cabin. Thankfully, the flight was only an hour long from our home base of Los Angeles. Once we hit the road the rest of the way in a rental car, our drive from San Francisco to Santa Rosa washed away the stress as we road-tripped over the water on the Golden Gate Bridge and into the lush greenery of Sonoma County. Visit Santa Rosa hosted Scary Mommy at the Art House Hotel and put together a family-friendly itinerary built around the Charles M. Schulz Museum and the Children’s Museum of Sonoma County, with some of the town’s best food mixed in.

First up was the Poppy Bank Epicenter, where we got to shake loose energy from all the travel sitting. There’s a toddler play area, an arcade, bowling, and Sky Zone, where if your kid is too restless to sit for food, you can pack it up and head over to an activity to tire them out. Ask us how we know. We’re in the restaurant training era, and she was just not having it. Still, Victory House at Epicenter was a great spot; we got massive burgers with fries, plus kids' dino nuggets to go. After a workout on the awesome 4-person Omni Motion VR screen games, we headed back to the hotel to put her to bed and reheat our food.

Sabina Graves

Here's where Art House really shines for families. Once our little one knocked out, we were able to make use of a full kitchen and sit down at a real dining table to eat like actual adults while still keeping an eye on our snoozing kid. Have you ever eaten cold pizza on a hotel bed in the dark because the baby finally fell asleep? Yeah, then you get why this felt like such a luxury.

The next morning, my husband picked up coffee from Land and Water Coffee, a local roaster with flavorful and sense-awakening brews. It gave us the energy we needed to keep up with our kid, who was raring to go. Our breakfast spot was the Americana, a farm-to-table breakfast nook where we filled up on fresh veggie-packed, cheesy omelets and our daughter happily demolished chocolate chip pancakes with chicken sausage and a milk.

Everything was either walkable or just a short drive away, which meant we could really plan around our kid’s schedule instead of the other way around. Our first big outing of the trip was to The Children’s Museum of Sonoma County, just steps from the Schulz Museum (on the deck for the next day). And it just so happened to be her first-ever children’s museum, so we were just as excited for our precocious kid.

Sabina Graves

Established by a group of mothers in Sonoma, including Lucasfilm luminaries, the children’s museum grew from a traveling trailer of imaginative play and education into a permanent nonprofit space focused on making fun accessible to local families regardless of income. There’s an admission fee, but families who qualify, including WIC and SNAP recipients, can have it waived through the museum’s family access programs.

The level of love and diversity in every experience was so deeply moving. We started my kid in Totopia, a soft-play woodland nook, because we were nervous to let her roam right away. The staff there were great — not just with her, but with us too, gently coaching Mom and Dad to a place where our anxiety could subside. And with so many safe spaces built for creative play, it didn’t take long for all of us to relax.

Next up was the Wild Zone, a purposeful recreational area full of sticks, climbing, and wobbly unstructured natural play where kids can basically FAFO under supervision. So if they lose their balance by maybe climbing something too ambitiously, they learn consequences. Our kiddo needed that lesson! An experimental FAFO space? Genius, truly.

Outside, our daughter fell hard for the bubble and water tables, because what toddler doesn’t yearn for splish-splashing? Take it from us: You’ll want to pack a change of clothes, because we definitely had to change her before going inside. Back indoors, she “flew” a rocket to space, “cooked” in a retro diner, and “doctored” in the Science & Medical Lab. Then it was back to the hotel for naps all around before heading out on foot to our next family adventure.

Before dinner, we hit Noble Folk Ice Cream and Pie Bar (who says you can’t have dessert first), a cute and tasty stop. We shared scoops of their heritage flavors — Strawberry Miso, Butterscotch, and Cornflake Maple — until our daughter spotted the freshly baked chocolate chip cookies and it was game over. My pie-loving husband grabbed a to-go slice of the seasonal maple chocolate walnut. Afterward, we walked over to La Rosa Tequileria & Grille for a fresh coastal seafood dinner and margs (for Mom and Dad) before all three of us passed out, full from the day's activities.

Our final day in Santa Rosa brought us to the main event: the Snoopy and Family exhibit at the Charles M. Schulz Museum. Since we had a flight to catch later that day, we swung in for sandwiches at Mac’s Deli (they’re legendary for a reason) and coffee from Espressioso's Coffee, a Latin-inspired roaster specializing in ethically sourced beans.

After getting in a little playtime in the cafe's toddler corner, we made our way to the museum. As animation fans, we geeked out seeing the comic strips up close and learning the story behind Snoopy’s litter of siblings, the focus of the current exhibit. We especially appreciated that it spotlights Bill Melendez, the director and animator behind the Peanuts TV specials — including the one about Snoopy’s origin — as such an integral part of making Peanuts a staple in pop culture.

It was so cool to see how those relatable Snoopy family moments came to life, from being printed on local paper strips by Schulz to the screen by Melendez. In particular, the "Snoopy's Reunion” episode really captures the spirit of Schulz’s work, and how Snoopy’s backstory only made fans love him more. I didn’t know much about Melendez before this visit (including that the Latino animator was the voice of Snoopy himself), and learning about his mark on animation history genuinely moved me. He and Schulz created a vision of childhood still beloved today.

Now I get to share that with my daughter, who is fully in her cartoon dog era. Avi Saxena, left, and Tyler Whitworth. (LinkedIn Photos)

— Two former Amazon executives who helped build Discovery’s direct-to-consumer operation in Bellevue, Wash., are stepping down from their leadership roles following the company’s acquisition by Paramount Skydance. They will remain in advisory roles during the transition.

Avi Saxena joined Discovery in 2019 as chief technology officer of its direct-to-consumer business and led its new Bellevue office. He became CTO of Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) following Discovery’s 2022 merger with AT&T’s WarnerMedia business. Paramount Skydance completed its acquisition of WBD on Oct. 6, with the combined company taking the name Skydance Corporation.

Before joining Discovery, Saxena was with Amazon for 19 years over two stints, most recently as vice president of technology for Amazon Marketplace. He also held leadership roles at Microsoft and internet infrastructure startups.

Tyler Whitworth is stepping down as executive vice president and chief product officer for WBD’s streaming business. He led teams working on digital products for HBO Max, discovery+, Eurosport, TNT Sports, Food Network and others. Before joining Discovery, he spent more than seven years at Amazon, leading teams developing products and services for customers and third-party sellers.

Dane Glasgow, Skydance’s chief product officer, announced the departures of Whitworth, Saxena and WBD Chief Information Officer Dave Duvall in a memo shared with GeekWire.

“Much of the strength of WBD’s product and technology organizations reflects their leadership and dedication. Please join me in thanking them and wishing them the very best,” Glasgow wrote.

The company has not responded to GeekWire’s inquiries about the future of the Bellevue office.

Bahadir Onalan. (LinkedIn Photo)

He left in 2017 as a principal software group engineering manager to join Amazon, where he worked on machine learning initiatives.

“This second tenure feels like a true homecoming,” Onalan wrote. “Our goal goes beyond shipping a platform or a product; we are here to define the global standards for AI safety.”
