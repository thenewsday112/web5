GM cracks the petty cash box for multistate driver tracking settlement

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

General Motors is taking what looks like a light slap on the wrist from a coalition of 35 states and the District of Columbia after agreeing to settle allegations that it collected drivers' data without proper consent and sold it to data brokers that supplied insurance companies.

The Washington state AG's office said on Thursday that GM had reached a $220 million settlement with 36 attorneys general over the automaker's collection and sharing of driving data, including precise location information, speed, seatbelt use, and other metrics. According to investigators, GM sold that data to brokers, which in turn supplied it to insurance companies that could use it to set customers' premiums, without drivers' informed consent.

For reference, the $220 million settlement, which will be distributed among the participating jurisdictions, equates to less than 7 percent of GM's $3.3 billion in net income attributable to stockholders for its third quarter, which ended on September 30, 2026. Don't spend it all in one place, y'all.

The settlement comes more than two years after a 2024 New York Times investigation found that GM had been selling the data collected from vehicle owners through its Smart Driver program. That program, part of GM subsidiary OnStar’s offering, collected the aforementioned data and handed it back to vehicle owners in reports designed to inform better driving habits.

Unbeknownst to Smart Driver subscribers, their data was also being forked over to data brokers despite GM telling customers it wouldn't share that information with insurers without their express consent. The insurers ultimately obtained the data by purchasing it from brokers with which GM had struck deals.

To make matters worse, the Washington AG's office said GM incentivized dealerships to enroll customers in Smart Driver, with employees in some cases accepting terms and conditions on customers' behalf without their knowledge or consent.

GM also failed to notify many drivers that their information was being collected and shared. Its OnStar app incorrectly told more than 370,000 drivers they were not enrolled in Smart Driver, even as GM continued collecting and selling their data, according to investigators.

“The data GM sold was personal, identifiable, and specific,” the AG’s office said. GM canned the Smart Driver program in April 2024, just over a month after the New York Times exposed its data-sharing practices, citing customer feedback.

In addition to the monetary pittance it’s handing over to the states, GM also agreed with the 36 AGs to limit the driving data it collects, better protect customer PII, and establish a "comprehensive data privacy program.” The Washington AG’s office described its settlement as “significantly stronger than any other resolution reached with GM for this conduct.”

Speaking of those other terms, GM finalized its settlement of the same allegations with the Federal Trade Commission in January 2026. The FTC order required GM to obtain drivers' express consent before collecting, using, or sharing covered driving data, subject to certain exceptions. It also required the company to offer a simple way for drivers to request a copy of their data, request its deletion, and disable precise geolocation data collection where their vehicles support it.

The state of California separately reached a $12.75 million settlement with GM in the matter in May; a separate case in Texas is ongoing.

“This agreement … reinforces steps we’ve taken to strengthen our privacy practices,” a GM spokesperson told The Register, though the company noted that it still stands by driver data collection as a best practice.

“Safer, more helpful and personalized experiences are enabled by connected vehicle data, and GM is committed to being clear about what information is used, how it’s protected, and the choices customers have about what they share,” the spokesperson added. ®

As a kid, Bram Vanderbeke wanted to be a bricklayer, so studying woodwork and construction made sense. But after studying design at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Ghent and graduating from Design Academy Eindhoven, he took his hands-on talents in a different direction, pursuing a career as an artist and designer in his hometown.

Vanderbeke explores the relationship between an object, its architectural environment, and its user, with a focus on raw materiality, form, and function. The result is tactile, sculptural work that often blurs the boundaries between art, design, and architecture.

“I just loved how architecture, art, and nature can merge so well with each other,” he says, citing Insel Hombroich, a museum and sculpture park in Neuss, Germany, as a lasting influence. Returning in different seasons, he was struck by how the changing landscape transformed the experience.

Bram Vanderbeke’s Anthology Side Table/Stool, designed in collaboration with Belgian jeweler Lore Van Keer, combines layers of cast concrete and aluminum. Photography by Amber Vanbossel.

Perhaps surprisingly, hardcore punk also informs his creative practice. “I grew up in this subculture, which is built on DIY shows and a very close community,” he says. Its DIY ethos and motivation to make things happen resonate with his own approach.

This fall, Vanderbeke presented Cavea, a group exhibition featuring more than 70 works by more than 40 designers, organized by Brut Collective, the Belgian collectible design group he cofounded in 2018, and DesignUnDisciplined. His recent work also includes designs for the newly reopened museum shop at Design Museum Gent and commissions for Bottega Veneta, Kelly Wearstler, Pieter Mulier and Lore Van Keer.

For today’s Friday Five, we spent five minutes with Bram Vanderbeke discussing the inspiration behind his collaboration with Lore Van Keer on the Anthology Side Table/Stool.

Photography by Amber Vanbossel

Anthology Side Table/Stool from the LVK x BV collection

Vanderbeke and Belgian jeweler Lore Van Keer bring together furniture and jewelry design in a collaboration that explores form, structure, and material: the LVK x BV collection of a silver necklace and sculptural stool, produced in limited editions of 20 and eight pieces, respectively.

The tactile stool features alternating layers of cast concrete and aluminum. Handcrafted in Vanderbeke’s Belgian atelier, each piece uses a new mold, making every one unique. Designed for indoor or outdoor use, the stool/table is made to order.

What Inspired LVK x BV Anthology Side Table/Stool?

Stacked Geometry

Lore Van Keer invited Vanderbeke to reinterpret her Anthology jewelry collection as a furniture piece. “I liked the stacked shapes of the collection,” he explains. “She also plays with circles and hexagon shapes, which I translated into the shapes of the Anthology Side Table.”

Specifically, the Anthology 10 ring inspired Vanderbeke to translate its rhythm of stacked forms and structures into a 3D object. Alternating concrete and aluminum create a layered composition that defines the form.

Architectural Layers

For inspiration, Vanderbeke browsed a tome he keeps in his atelier: Isamu Noguchi’s monograph A Sculptor’s World. The book features the spectacular ceiling and waterfall Noguchi designed for the lobby of 666 Fifth Avenue in New York. Noting the ceiling’s layers of aluminum, Vanderbeke incorporated a similar approach into the Anthology piece.

Ancient Forms

“I’m always inspired by excavations and ruins,” Vanderbeke says, “especially the rough stacked shapes of the Roman columns and their textures.” This influence connects directly to his Cast Bricks collection, which also informs the Anthology side table.

Ancient Roman columns and their stacked forms and textures are among the architectural influences behind Bram Vanderbeke’s sculptural designs. Photography courtesy of Vanderbeke.

Building Blocks

Vanderbeke also drew on his own work, incorporating elements from his Cast Bricks collection into the Anthology piece. For the Brick Chair, he arranged regular bricks in methodical yet intuitive compositions that create negative spaces, which serve as molds for his architectural furniture. The straightforward technique references how construction workers lay the foundations of a building, connecting back to Vanderbeke’s childhood fascination with bricklaying.

EA Just Shelved A Mysterious Sims Project—That Could Be Good News For Players

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It's been another rough year for video games, with countless studio closures, staff layoffs, and projects that barely managed to even get off the ground before they were unceremoniously cut short. But things are feeling especially grim after EA trotted out two beloved franchises--Mass Effect and Dragon Age--for what amounted to a public execution via the announcement that BioWare has been shuttered. 

This was especially painful for Mass Effect players, who were looking forward to the upcoming Mass Effect 5, which we now know was tentatively titled Mass Effect: Universe. But it's not just BioWare fans who are upset at the moment. Fans of EA's other properties are growing increasingly concerned about the future of those franchises, if any future is planned for them at all. My colleague Levi Winslow recently wrote about their concerns for the Skate franchise in the wake of layoffs at its development studio. Meanwhile, I'm quite worried about the fate of The Sims.

In the same breath it announced the end of BioWare, EA also reaffirmed its commitment to Apex Legends, Skate, and The Sims franchises. But everyone knows that when it comes to profits, EA's sports titles outrun its other IPs by a country mile. If EA is willing to pull the plug on legacy franchises like Mass Effect and Dragon Age, where does that leave its other franchises, like The Sims 4?

EA is pivoting its focus from Project Rene to Project Evergreen.

According to EA, Sims fans need not worry, as the company is committed to "focusing on the long-term potential of The Sims." Apparently, focusing on that long-term potential involves shelving a project The Sims team has been working on for years: Project Rene, AKA Project Lotus, AKA the nebulous next entry in The Sims franchise, which appears to be some kind of weird Sims MMO mobile game thing. Instead, EA will be focusing on Project Evergreen, which is rumored to be a sort of remaster of The Sims 4, with all (or most) DLC packs included, and recurring bugs squashed.

"We're supporting this franchise by pausing work on one of the next titles in this franchise" doesn't exactly inspire confidence, especially after the nonchalance with which EA just killed two other beloved game franchises. But I think this actually might be the right move. 

It's been a bumpy couple of years for The Sims, and many players aren't particularly enthused about Project Rene/Lotus, as it seems to be once again trying to recreate The Sims Online, something EA has tried to rebuild over and over. In fact, much of the reason The Sims 4 launched in such awful shape was due to the fact that it was initially meant to be an MMO-style game that could run on mobile devices. Less than a year before launch, EA pivoted to a more traditional Sims game--but by then, so much of the game had been built with online play and mobile devices in mind that it was too late to turn back. Despite releasing over 100 DLC packs and embarking on a concentrated campaign to squash the game's many, many, many bugs, longtime Sims players are still frustrated with the state of The Sims 4, often pointing out the numerous ways it pales in comparison to its predecessor, The Sims 3. 

The Sims 4 is officially "done" in that there are no plans to release any more expansion packs for the game. When it comes to the future of the franchise, most players don't want the next Sims title to be yet another attempt at shoehorning online and/or mobile gameplay into a game that is, at its core, all about building and playing with digital dolls in their digital dollhouses. So EA's decision to hit pause on Project Rene/Lotus's development isn't something I find especially heartbreaking. In fact, I think it's the first truly wise decision EA's made regarding the franchise in a long time, provided EA actually plans to support Project Evergreen long-term (which the name Evergreen would suggest).

Sims 4 players don't want Project Rene, but they do want either The Sims 5 or a complete, playable version of The Sims 4--ideally one that doesn't require the purchase of $1,600 in DLC packs to feel "finished." Hitting pause on its weird, experimental Sims projects in favor of attempting to preserve The Sims 4 in a stable, playable, "evergreen" state is arguably the best decision EA could make, given the current state of both EA and The Sims franchise. Pouring resources into a concept that has failed before (online MMO-style Sims gameplay) rather than a tried-and-true formula (a digital dollhouse) would only harm the franchise at this point.

Whatever Project Rene is supposed to be, it's officially on ice for now.

Of course, there's no telling what kind of shape Project Evergreen will launch in, assuming it does successfully make it to launch. It sounds like the Evergreen devs have a deadline to hit--hence the reshuffling of resources away from Rene/Lotus--but even with the increase in development resources, there's no guarantee Evergreen will be a massive hit with players.

Provided the rest of development does go smoothly and Evergreen is indeed well-received by players, it may still fall short of EA's goals when it comes to profits. EA has spent the last decade doing everything possible to monetize the living hell out of The Sims 4, a game that--unlike EA's lucrative sports catalog--is largely intended to be a solitary gaming experience that doesn't require online play. Back in the olden days of The Sims 1, 2, and 3, players would purchase the base game, a few expansion packs, and maybe a couple of "stuff packs" for extra decor options, but that was all one really needed to enjoy the game. 

The Sims 4, however, introduced an era of seemingly endless DLC: expansion packs, game packs, stuff packs, kits--there are over 100 of them, ranging in cost from $40 to $5, and totaling around $1,600 if purchased individually outside of sales and bundles. In addition to the constant stream of DLC releases, EA has in recent years implemented daily login rewards, various events and challenges, and even a paid mod store nobody asked for--all in the hopes of getting players to spend more time and money on The Sims 4. For a while there, I half-expected EA to implement a Sims 4 battle pass.

EA trimming down its Sims projects is fine with me, as long as the series isn't uprooted entirely.

There's no telling what sort of irritating monetization tactics we'll see in Project Evergreen, but at the very least, Evergreen sounds like an attempt to try and maintain the series' roots, rather than continue chasing the mobile MMO Roblox-like direction Project Lotus seemed to be taking. Even better, Evergreen may turn out to be the game Sims players have been aching for since The Sims 4 launched to middling reviews back in 2014: a game that isn't fundamentally broken and lacking in content.

The real question is if Evergreen will be successful enough to keep The Sims franchise alive. Given that EA's new owners were happy to kill off what was shaping up to be a potentially great Mass Effect game, it's not hard to imagine a world where the Grim Reaper inevitably comes for The Sims, regardless of whether players love or hate Project Evergreen. After all, feelings don't generate dollar signs, and EA's new owners have made one thing abundantly clear: killing off an iconic franchise is "worth it" if it saves EA a few Simoleons.

foster + partners’ generali stadium references the intricate stonework of milan’s duomo

a new home for AC milan and inter is taking shape

 

Foster + Partners and MANICA have unveiled the design for Generali Stadium in Milan, a new 71,500-seat football arena that will become the shared home of AC Milan and FC Internazionale Milano. Planned for the San Siro district, the stadium will feature an exposed concrete X-frame structure and a sweeping double-curved roof. Its design draws from the city’s architectural heritage, with a repeating geometric facade that references the intricate stonework of Milan’s Duomo.

 

The proposal arrives after years of debate over the future of the existing San Siro, officially known as Stadio Giuseppe Meazza, which has hosted Milan’s football clubs for generations. The new stadium will rise beside the historic venue, with plans calling for the partial demolition of the older structure following the move. Foster + Partners and MANICA have approached the project with attention to the atmosphere of San Siro, whose steep seating and proximity to the field have long shaped the experience of attending a match.

visualizations © Foster + Partners, MANICA

 

 

concrete X-frames shape the stadium’s sculptural facade

 

Generali Stadium’s exterior is defined by a series of intersecting concrete supports that extend from the ground-level colonnades to the roof. Foster and Partners and MANICA further use these X-shaped elements to establish a strong geometric rhythm around the oval building. At the entrances, diamond-shaped openings offer glimpses into the stadium, leading visitors toward naturally ventilated circulation spaces and panoramic concourses overlooking the city.

 

Above the seating bowl, a double-curved roof follows the changing height of the stands, rising around the two ends of the stadium. Its spoke-wheel structure covers the spectator seating while leaving the central playing area open to the sky. The roof will incorporate approximately 27,500 square meters of photovoltaic panels, with acoustic design intended to contain and amplify the sound of supporters inside the arena.

Foster + Partners and MANICA unveil their design for the 71,500-seat Generali Stadium in Milan

 

 

steep seating walls bring supporters closer to the pitch

 

Inside, the architects have organized the stadium around a continuous 360-degree seating bowl divided into two tiers. The layout is designed to maintain clear sightlines across the 71,500 seats and draw spectators closer to the field. At opposite ends, two large supporter sections, known in Italian football as the Curvas, rise steeply behind the goals. Each will host about 11,700 fans, with seating angles reaching 37 degrees.

 

These two walls of seating form some of the most distinctive spaces within the design. Their steep inclines concentrate spectators directly behind the goals, maintaining the intensity associated with San Siro’s existing stands. The seating configuration also allows the arena to retain an intimate relationship between supporters and players despite its colossal scale. Acoustic considerations extend into the roof geometry, which has been shaped to help direct crowd noise back toward the field.

a repeating concrete X-frame structure extends across the stadium’s facade

 

 

generali stadium forms part of a wider urban redevelopment

 

Beyond the stadium itself, the proposal forms part of a 281,000-square-meter redevelopment of the San Siro district. New public spaces and landscaped areas will surround the venue, with leisure and retail facilities intended to attract visitors throughout the year. The stadium is also planned to accommodate concerts and other major events to extend its use beyond the football calendar.

 

The development incorporates an all-electric operational strategy, natural ventilation, and systems for rainwater harvesting and greywater recycling. The architects are targeting LEED Gold certification and carbon-neutral operations.

 

Following the design unveiling on October 29th, 2026, the project will advance through further planning and approval stages. Construction is currently expected to begin in 2028, with the first match targeted for the 2031/32 season.

Natural Selection has brought in someone new to lead its ski program, and he knows a thing or two about big mountains.

Cattabriga-Alosa consulted and judged at the event.

CoD: Modern Warfare To Cut Real-Life Korean Tragedy Segment After Backlash

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Publisher Activision has responded to criticism over using a devastating tragedy in Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, saying that the incident will be removed from the military shooter in a November 12 update.

The Seoul Halloween crowd crush occurred in October 2022 in Itaewon. Approximately 100,000 people gathered in a narrow alleyway in Seoul, resulting in 160 casualties as people were suffocated. This year marks the fourth anniversary of the tragedy, with bereaved families and community organizers gathering to remember the lives that were lost.

Modern Warfare 4 hit consoles and PC on October 23, and not long after its release, the game drew criticism from bereaved Korean families. In the opening cutscene to the game's campaign--which focuses on a fictional war between North and South Korea--Modern Warfare 4 depicts a chaotic scene where one person performs CPR on a downed individual, while bodies are either laid out on the streets or carried away on stretchers. Given the game's setting and the events on screen, the 10.29 Itaewon Disaster Bereaved Families Association and the Citizens' Countermeasures Meeting--two organizations formed after the 2022 tragedy in Seoul--were "appalled" at the calamity's inclusion and demanded that Activision remove it from the game.

"Using such an incident as material for a game intended for entertainment purposes is itself highly inappropriate and a senseless act," the groups said, according to the BBC. "The Itaewon tragedy was a tragic disaster that produced hundreds of casualties, and citizens who participated in the rescue efforts or witnessed the event at the time continue to suffer from trauma. If the game developer has even a minimum level of courtesy and empathy toward the victims, they should promptly take corrective measures."

Activision has heard the call and plans to remove the depiction from Modern Warfare 4 when an update arrives on November 12. In responding to the criticism, Activision said it never intended to trivialize the disaster or make light of human suffering.

"In developing Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, we approached the subject matter with care and respect. The game’s opening cinematic utilises some real-world news coverage to establish the game’s fictional narrative," Activision said. "It is not intended to reference or trivialise the Itaewon disaster or any other real-world tragedy in any way, shape or form."

A heavy-racked buck beds down for the day. Wind sweeping from behind, and eyes scanning down below, danger has little chance of finding him before he detects it. After all, that’s why the buck picked this spot in the first place.

If you scan articles on the best rut hunting spots, you won’t find many that highlight buck bedding areas as key locations to target. But often times in deer hunting, going against the grain can produce great results. 

However, you can only hunt buck bedding areas well if you have a firm understanding of how bucks bed, where they bed, and how they learn to survive based on their bedding area choices. 

If you are tired of hunting the same old rut stands with minimal success, consider a change. Hunting a buck bedding area during the rut could be your ticket to hanging an old bruiser on the wall. Here’s what you should know.

Defining Buck Bedding

Let’s start by agreeing on terms. Of course, bedding areas are places where deer rest and sleep. However, it’s important to know there are different types of bedding areas. Deer have daytime and nighttime bedding areas. For this article, we’ll focus on the daytime beds.

On the surface, it can be difficult to determine the difference between buck and doe bedding areas. There are distinctions, though.

Bucks tend to prioritize security over convenience. Does usually take the opposite approach. Bucks rely on themselves to detect danger (because they typically bed alone). However, does usually bed, feed, and travel within family units. When bedding together, does bed down facing different directions. This makes it easier to detect danger from all areas around them. Bucks can’t do this so they are much choosier in their bed locations.

Furthermore, bucks might or might not bed within the same patches of cover as does. When they do, bucks tend to take the best quality bedding. For example, in hill country, that might be the upper third of a ridge line. In swamps, that might be the highest dry ground available. In big woods, it could mean interiors of timber or in spots with the thickest cover and highest stem counts.

Bucks also bed in areas that offer plenty of horizontal cover. They might bed up against a boulder, log, or other object that hides their profile. Oftentimes, from a hunter’s perspective, all you might see is their head, antlers, and maybe the neck. Other times, you’ll see nothing at all (until it’s too late).

Regardless of the habitat type or geographical layout, bucks almost always bed with certain consistencies. For example, they tend to bed with the wind at their back. This allows them to smell anything upwind (behind them) and see anything downwind (in front).

This is one reason leeward ridges are so attractive to deer. The prevailing wind brings scent from over the top of the ridge. Early in the day, thermals bring scent up from below. Oftentimes, this allows deer to smell danger from multiple directions.

Without question, savvy bucks learn how to detect danger within their environment. With time, they learn typical directions that danger comes from. With that knowledge, they bed in areas that offer visibility to see hunters or predators coming from these directions. Therefore, don’t be surprised to see bucks bedding right on the edge of thick cover within some of the areas noted above. This gives them plenty of cover to hide and allows them to see danger approaching from the open areas nearby. 

It’s logical to think that once bucks become confident in certain bedding areas, they’ll go back and use the same beds over and over. In some ways, and in many places, they do. It’s especially common for bucks that live in more desolate landscapes or areas with poorer habitat quality to reuse the same beds with higher frequency.

According to the MSU Deer Lab, in areas with higher timber-to-open ratios and bigger woods settings, bucks are less loyal to specific beds. They will still bed within the same relative geographic locations. However, the absolute locations of their beds tend to vary.

Know That Old Bucks Are Different

You’ll often find buck beds in heavy cover. Photo by Honeycutt Creative

It’s been said many times, but older bucks operate completely differently than younger bucks and does. They take on vastly different habits and tendencies. 

A common thread through most mature deer includes the desire for seclusion. Older bucks don’t want a bunch of other deer bedded close to them. Additionally, it’s important to recognize that higher populations of does, and higher deer densities in general, can cause bucks to bed on the fringes. That’s especially true outside of the rut.

Because of everything noted above, it’s challenging to hunt close to buck bedding. It becomes even harder when the deer is 4.5 years old or older. These deer have learned to survive in part due to their bedding area choices. Therefore, it takes strategic planning and very deliberate approaches to thread the needle and get a big buck on the ground.

So, Should You Hunt Buck Bedding During the Rut?

While it isn’t as popular as other rut-centric stand locations, it’s certainly an option. By continuing to focus on buck bedding areas (similar to early season and pre-rut plans), hunters can find a big ol’ buck.

By digging through deer research and personal experience, it’s clear that bucks express unique habits and tendencies that culminate into perceived personalities. One example is a really old buck that barely participates in the rut. That’s the exception, of course, but it would call for a unique approach. You would have to hunt such a deer as if the rut isn’t even on. You’d focus on travel routes along bed-to-feed patterns.

Some mature bucks that participate in the rut still return to their preferred bedding areas. If they don’t find an estrus doe under cover of darkness, by dawn, they cautiously slink back to one of the advantageous beds they know. When hunting this type of deer, set up between his bed and nearby food sources. Or between his bed and nearby doe bedding.

Hunting this deer is similar to hunting the fringes of buck bedding outside of the rut. Use good entry and exit strategies, and keep wind directions in mind during the approach, hunt, and departure. Don’t be aggressive with calling, rattling, or decoying since reclusive bucks rarely respond well to those aggressive tactics.

Final Thoughts on Hunting Buck Bedding Areas During the Rut

Hunting buck bedding requires a lot of planning and consideration. Hunting buck bedding during the rut requires even more. In many cases, this isn’t the right play. 

Oftentimes, it’s less-dominant, uber-mature deer (5 ½-plus), or specific bucks that tend to be more reclusive across their entire lifespan, that require this hunting tactic.

Once you’ve determined you’re hunting a specific buck like this, you must hunt him carefully. Use the hunting strategies you’d typically use to hunt a whitetail bedding area with the immediacy of the rut in the front of your mind.

Halloween’s Lost Heroes: Michael Pollan on Radical Gardening and the Insurgent Botanical Origin of the Broomstick in Flying-Witch Legends

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

“For most of their history… gardens have been more concerned with the power of plants than with their beauty — with the power, that is, to change us in various ways, for good and for ill.”

“Oh that beloved witch-hazel,” Emily Dickinson wrote to her cousins in 1876 as she tended to her famous garden, “one loved stalk as hearty as if just placed in the mail by the woods… witch and witching too, to my joyful mind” — her garden, across the hedge from which lived the love of her life, joylessly married to Emily’s brother, absent from Emily’s arms for a quarter century. That same year, she wrote in a poem:

Long Years apart — can make no
Breach a second cannot fill —
The absence of the Witch does not
Invalidate the spell —

As a gardener and a poet, ever since she pressed four hundred wildflowers into the teenage herbarium that became her first formal act of composition, Emily Dickinson had an uncommon grasp of how the life of plants and the life of feelings interleave — particularly the forbidden, the subversive, the countercultural.

In many ways, across epochs and cultures, gardens themselves — and not only the plants grown in them — have served as psychoactive agents profoundly transforming the human experience: gardening as resistance, gardening as growing through grief, gardening as finding the roots of happiness, gardening as “an exercise in supreme attentiveness.”

Gardening as witchcraft for the soul — defying the permissible, magnifying the possible.

Red poppy by Elizabeth Blackwell from A Curious Herbal — the world’s first illustrated encyclopedia of medicinal plants. (Available as a print and as a face mask, benefitting The Nature Conservancy.)

This defiant aspect of gardening, so foreign to the dominant modern model of the garden as a place of order walled within a lawn-mowed wilderness, is what Michael Pollan explores in a portion of his 2001 classic The Botany of Desire (public library).

He writes:

I sometimes think we’ve allowed our gardens to be bowdlerized, that the full range of their powers and possibilities has been sacrificed to a cult of plant prettiness that obscures more dubious truths about nature, our own included.

But for most of the history of our species, the relationship between nature and human nature has been one of conviviality rather than conquest and control. Only in the mere blink of evolutionary time called modern civilization did we begin viewing the rest of nature as a “parallel world.”

Pollan envisions a future that might view our present conception of the garden — a place for basic produce, household herbs, and pretty flowers — as “almost Victorian in its repressions and elisions,” which include the modern repression and elision of the garden’s radical past. He traces the subversive botanical roots of the mythic flying witch’s broomstick as one example:

For most of their history, after all, gardens have been more concerned with the power of plants than with their beauty — with the power, that is, to change us in various ways, for good and for ill.

In ancient times, people all over the world grew or gathered sacred plants (and fungi) with the power to inspire visions or conduct them on journeys to other worlds; some of these people, who are sometimes called shamans, returned with the kind of spiritual knowledge that underwrites whole religions. The medieval apothecary garden cared little for aesthetics, focusing instead on species that healed and intoxicated and occasionally poisoned. Witches and sorcerers cultivated plants with the power to “cast spells” — in our vocabulary, “psychoactive” plants. Their potion recipes called for such things as datura, opium poppies, belladonna, hashish, fly-agaric mushrooms (Amanita muscaria), and the skins of toads (which can contain DMT, a powerful hallucinogen). These ingredients would be combined in a hempseed-oil-based “flying ointment” that the witches would then administer vaginally using a special dildo. This was the “broomstick” by which these women were said to travel.

Art by Dorothy Lathrop from Down-Adown-Derry, 1922. (Available as a print.)

It was in one such epoch that Johannes Kepler took six years from decoding the laws of the universe to defend his herbalist-mother in a witchcraft trial, while elsewhere in Europe the world’s first prim and proper botanical gardens were sprouting up.

The Botany of Desire — which also gave us the story of how “broken tulips” shaped the modern world — is thoroughly satisfying in its totality. Complement this fragment with the little-known botanical-cultural story of why NYC is known as “the Big Apple” — a surprise even to most native New Yorkers — then revisit Pollan on how one particular plant magnified the bewitchment of Bach.

‘The Gilded Age’ Is Back! Here’s What to Eat While You Watch the Show

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

I can be persuaded to watch almost anything that promises extravagant set designs and beautiful costumes. If there’s history and a little mischievous social maneuvering involved, even better. My coworkers recently recommended The Gilded Age, and within two weeks, I’d watched all three seasons just in time for season four to drop.

There’s plenty to admire: Bertha Russell’s determination, Peggy Scott’s writing career and rooms furnished with a reassuring disregard for minimalism. I love the history, too, from the railroad fortunes and New York bankers to Lewis Latimer’s work in electric lighting and the woman who helped bring the Brooklyn Bridge to completion. Every episode gave me another reason to keep watching.

My husband is often still at the restaurant in the evenings, so once the kids are asleep, I have a few hours to settle in with a show. While I watched The Gilded Age, I usually poured a glass of wine (some of my favorites are under $25 at Walmart) and enjoyed the fire as Christine Baranski made an art of Agnes’s disapproval. But with the new season arriving on Nov. 1, I’m borrowing a few ideas from the show to make a watch party spread.

Set the Scene

I have a decidedly romantic streak when it comes to my surroundings, so the show’s interiors have my full attention. I’m particularly taken with the glassware and china that can make even a small supper feel indulgent. With a few soft lamps and the fire going, I’m looking forward to borrowing a little of that grandeur for my own evening.

  • A favorite plate and a special glass: Bannister may have reservations about Bertha’s colored glasses, but as a collector of vintage glassware, I find it rather inspiring. Thrift stores are wonderful places to look for floral china, small silver-plated trays and individual glasses to mix and match for a pretty tablescape.
  • A few flowers on the table: A handful of stems from the grocery store in a little pitcher is plenty. They’re equally charming when arranged in tiny bud vases or in one center arrangement. Finding flowers that mirror the colors in your china is a great way to make everything look put together.
  • Something soft beneath the dishes: Because so many watch parties happen in the living room rather than the kitchen, cover your coffee table with a tablecloth. It’ll look pretty, and it’ll catch any crumbs before they end up in the carpet. Add even more Gilded Age elegance with cloth napkins under silverware rather than paper napkins.
  • A little note for company: A handwritten card listing dishes like “Ada’s Clam Chowder” would be a nice addition if you have company for your watch party. Cream card stock and a nice pen are enough to give the food a thoughtful connection to the show.
  • Music before the show: A little opera while crafting vintage cocktails would be perfect for setting the scene, though I’d leave the battle over opera boxes to Mrs. Astor and Bertha.

A Glass for Agnes—or Ada

I’m more than happy to borrow the show’s habit of pouring wine into crystal glasses. Mine usually contains Champagne, but Ada’s season-three temperance campaign gives us a reason to put some thought into an alcohol-free option too.

Agnes’s feelings about wine appear to be settled, but there’s room for both sisters.

  • Champagne or sparkling wine: I’d choose a bottle I enjoy and pour it into a favorite glass. There’s simply no need to improve on a good Champagne.
  • A Champagne cocktail: Brandy and bitters give a Champagne cocktail a little extra ceremony, which is something perfectly befitting of a Gilded Age watch party.
  • An alcohol-free spritz: Sparkling water with cranberry juice and an orange twist would be my contribution to Ada’s cause, but any mocktail would make an equally good toast to the evening. I’d use the same glassware and leave the pledge unsigned.
  • Tea: Some evenings call for a pot of black tea and a little milk. I like bringing the pot into the living room on a tray.

Small Bites for High Society

The Russells may require a meticulously planned succession of courses, but I’d be content with a small selection of savory bites. Oysters, warm crab dip or little sandwiches would make a perfectly elegant dinner by the fire.

  • Oysters: Oysters make frequent appearances at the show’s lavish dinners, and a few with lemon would be enough to make the evening feel special. Our Oysters Rockefeller are baked with butter, spinach and Romano. And with John D. Rockefeller reportedly arriving this season, his namesake oysters certainly deserve a place at the table.
  • Crab: Marian’s visit to the Scotts is difficult to forget, though I’d rather take inspiration from their crab luncheon than her assumptions. A lovely crab meat au gratin would bring some of that indulgence home, served warm with toasted bread or crackers.
  • Asparagus: Few vegetables receive such careful instruction on how to eat them. After Bannister explains the difference between English and American asparagus etiquette, the Russell kitchen decided to avoid the matter altogether. I’d serve our roasted asparagus anyway, with a squeeze of lemon, and trust everyone to choose their own utensils.
  • Tea sandwiches: Mamie Fish’s doll-themed tea party is more commitment than I’m looking for, but I can appreciate the sandwiches. Cucumber and cream cheese, butter with thinly sliced radish or chicken salad would all be good on soft bread.

Dinner Across Fifth Avenue

For something more substantial, you can look to the show’s luncheons as much as its elaborate dinner parties. A bowl of soup or a sandwich can feel quite grand when the ingredients are good and someone else is responsible for the evening’s drama.

  • Clam chowder: A bowl of New England clam chowder is enough to win over Reverend Forte though it does little to impress Agnes. I’m with Ada on this one. Our New England clam chowder has tender potatoes, clams and a creamy broth that sounds perfect for a November season premiere.
  • Lobster rolls: The lobster left behind after Bertha’s disastrous first reception deserves a more appreciative audience. Our lobster rolls put that idea into buttery toasted buns, making them much more convenient for my own seating arrangements.
  • Steak: George’s Arizona business trip includes a steak order in a saloon, a considerable change of scenery from the Russell dining room. A steak seared in a hot skillet and finished with butter and fresh herbs would be a fitting way to bring a little of the show’s indulgence home.
  • Salmon: The exquisite salmon platters at Bertha’s dinners are hard to overlook, even among all the other beautiful dishes. For an easy at-home version, borrow the fish and simplify the presentation: a grilled fillet with lemon and garlic would be perfect for any cozy viewing party.

Dessert in the Drawing Room

A pot of tea and something sweet would be enough for me on an evening when dinner is already behind us. I like having dessert ready before I sit down, whether that means a trifle waiting in the fridge or a few pastries brought home in a paper bag.

  • Trifle: Bannister’s assessment of trifle as a nursery food seems unnecessarily limiting. Layers of cake, cream and fruit have plenty to offer the rest of us. Our strawberry trifle would be a good place to start. A splash of sherry over the cake would move it a little further from the nursery.
  • French pastries: Aurora Fane’s gathering put me in the mood for a plate of French pastries. Buy a few macarons, perhaps something chocolate, and let the bakery determine how ambitious the evening becomes. Arrange the pastries on a pretty plate, and voila!
  • Apple pie: For all the elaborate food in the show, apple pie is an appealing return to something familiar. Armstrong’s visit to her mother casts it in an unhappy light, but I’m prepared to overlook that for a warm slice with a buttery crust.
  • Ice cream: Ice cream has Agnes’s approval, which is no small recommendation. Strawberry would be a fitting choice from the period. A scoop in a small glass bowl with a crisp cookie would be all the dessert I’d need.
