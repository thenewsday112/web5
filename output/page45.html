This Historic Church Condo for Sale Hides a Private 85-Foot Bell Tower Lounge

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It’s easy to swoon over a modern-day home carved into an historic former church. Think stained-glass windows, high ceilings and lots of charm.

A new listing in Washington, D.C.’s Capitol Hill neighborhood has all that—plus another amenity that catapults it to the next level.

“Through my research, I believe this is the only residence in Washington, DC, with a true private bell tower incorporated into the home itself,” says listing agent Shane Byrne of Byrne Real Estate, about the Bell Tower Penthouse within The Sanctuary.

The asking price is $1,667,000. Back in 2016, the former 1897 Gothic Revival church—the former home of Ninth Street Christian Church, designed by Lawerence B. Valk and Son—was converted to condominiums by Bonstra | Haresign Architects.

It includes this 2,019-square-foot unit which has two bedrooms and 2.5 baths. “

My client bought this two years ago as a pied-à-terre,” says Byrne. “She is the second owner.”

Within a former church, this condo in particular boasts a private bell tower—the only such example in Washington, DC. “At the top, the bell tower has been transformed into a private lounge, offering panoramic, 360-degree views above the surrounding rooftops.”

One could easily host parties and social events in that lounge, with the modernized kitchen—stocked with a six-burner Wolf range and Sub-Zero refrigeration, an island and white cabinetry—as another unique gathering space.

Just like the kitchen, each of the baths has been updated, with white-marble finishes and luxury features like a double vanity, soaking tub glass-enclosed shower. Hardwood flooring and exposed brick are throughout the unit, too.

Another stylish perk is a 15-foot stained-glass window in the dining room, “featuring Alpha and Omega flanking a cross, framed by ceilings reaching approximately 33 feet,” explains Byrne, and two stained-glass windows in the primary bedroom.

In addition to the marvel of waking up every day in a former church, where many of its historical architectural elements (such as restored stained-glass windows) remain intact, this condo has the proud distinction of being a former choir loft.

This condo is within a neighborhood that’s long been desirable for homebuyers.

Jeff Bezos once gave Eva Longoria and the admiral behind Osama bin Laden’s capture $100 million—but she says you don’t need wealth to give back

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A lot of people talk about changing the world. But Amazon’s billionaire founder, Jeff Bezos, gave Eva Longoria $50 million to actually do it. And two years on, she says the biggest myth still surrounding philanthropy is that you have to be rich to do it.

“One of the biggest misconceptions about philanthropy is that impact is tied to wealth or scale, when in reality, impact comes in many forms,” the Desperate Housewives star told Fortune. “What I’ve learned is that impact doesn’t come from how much money you have, it comes from how you show up.”

It’s a message that feels pointed at a moment when billionaire philanthropy dominates headlines and ordinary people assume giving back is someone else’s job. 

For Longoria, the most powerful—and most overlooked—form of change happens at street level. “Mentoring someone, supporting small business, sharing what you know—those things matter because not everyone has access to capital or resources, but anyone can be part of someone else’s support system,” she adds. “And I think that’s really what’s missing for a lot of people. They don’t have someone in their corner.”

What Eva Longoria did with Jeff Bezos’s $50 million

The actress is practicing what she preaches. Having built a net worth north of $80 million thanks to a sprawling business portfolio—including a luxury tequila brand Casa Del Sol, the media company Hyphenate Media Group, a stake in women’s soccer team Angel City FC, and an early investment in the multibillion-dollar John Wick franchise. Now she’s channeling paying it forward by mentoring small-business owners through a new partnership with Lenovo.

She’s also been running the Eva Longoria Foundation since 2012 to improve educational and entrepreneurial opportunities for Latinas in the U.S., as well as Eva’s Heroes, a charity that helps young adults with “intellectual special needs,” according to its website. 

Bezos and his wife, Lauren Sánchez Bezos, launched the Courage and Civility Award in 2021 for individuals who make significant contributions to society. They’ve given millions (often $100 million) to help those in need.  Previous recipients include CNN host and civil rights advocate Van Jones, chef and humanitarian José Andrés, and late country legend Dolly Parton—who famously helped fund Moderna’s COVID-19 vaccine research.

It’s why Longoria says receiving the prize, alongside Bill McRaven, a retired Navy admiral, is much bigger than the money. 

“Receiving that support was incredibly meaningful, not just because of the scale of the gift, but because it represented trust and responsibility,” Longoria said, adding that she’s been “more intentional and more strategic” in how she shows up as an advocate.

“This award has allowed me to continue investing those resources into the communities and causes I care deeply about—supporting Latina entrepreneurs, expanding access to education, and backing organizations that are driving real change on the ground,” she added. “The Courage and Civility Award makes it possible for us at the Eva Longoria Foundation to create long-term impact with transformative giving.”

McRaven, who oversaw the 2011 raid that killed al Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, pledged to use his share of the money to develop education programs for future military leaders and the children of deceased veterans, as well as mental health support for those who serve.

You don’t have to be a billionaire to make a difference

Longoria isn’t the only one making the case that you don’t need to be rich and famous to make a difference. 

Just look at the Giving Pledge—the commitment, cofounded by Bill Gates, Melinda French Gates, and Warren Buffett, that asks the ultrawealthy to donate at least 50% of their fortunes—has attracted more than 250 signatories since its launch in 2010.

The number who have actually followed through? A handful—fewer than 10. Most fulfilled the pledge only after their deaths.

It’s why Liz Baker, CEO of Greater Good Charities, says we “can’t afford” to wait on billionaires to solve the world’s problems.:“I think if everybody did something to help in their community, we wouldn’t have the issues that we have.”

The global nonprofit she runs has distributed more than $1 billion in impact across 121 countries since 2006—and she also told Fortune the idea that giving back has to be some grand, expensive gesture is one of the biggest things holding people back.

“Everybody can chip in—and it doesn’t even have to be money. Like, do something,” she said.

“Even if you’re like, I have one hour a week to solve this problem in my community that I care about,” Baker said. “Figure out how to do that. Most local nonprofits need help.”

Other women contacted police about previous incidents with Packers’ Josh Jacobs after his arrest

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

As police investigated whether to file charges against Green Bay Packers running back Josh Jacobs following a May altercation with his then-girlfriend, multiple other women contacted them to detail their own allegations against him.

Those accounts were included in evidence regarding Jacobs’ battery case that the Hobart/Lawrence Police Department released Friday night in response to a public records request. Green Bay TV station NBC 26 first reported the records release.

Jacobs pleaded no contest Sept. 10 to misdemeanor counts of battery and criminal damage to property. Jacobs was arrested May 26, three days after an incident in which a woman said he threw her to the ground.

It’s unclear how the latest allegations will play into the NFL’s investigation into Jacobs. The league placed him on the commissioner’s exempt list in August after charges were filed against him, and that caused Jacobs to miss the Packers’ first six games of the season.

The NFL is still investigating Jacobs over the incident that led to his May arrest, so the league still could hand out more discipline to the running back.

There was no immediate comment from Jacobs or his legal team about the allegations that emerged in the police records late Friday. When he was allowed to return to the playing field earlier this month, Jacobs said he was “remorseful” and his lawyers said he had accepted responsibility for his actions in the Wisconsin case. “Since there are ongoing legal matters, he will not have any further comment,” they said on Oct. 16.

According to the records released Friday, one woman contacted police after Jacobs’ arrest to say she had experienced a similar incident with him around 2023. She later would say the incident actually occurred on Jan. 5, 2022.

The woman said she had communicated with Jacobs for about six months before spending about 11 hours at his home. The woman said that after she told Jacobs she hadn’t come there for sex, he became angry, slapped her across the face and told her not to disrespect him.

She said he took her phone and threw it into a swimming pool when she attempted to leave, and that he grabbed her by the hair, threw her onto a bed, pinned her down, threw her to the floor and said that “I should kill you right now.” She said Jacobs also spit on her.

The woman said that she hadn’t previously reported the incident to law enforcement because she remained friends with Jacobs afterward and was concerned her allegations wouldn’t be believed. The woman said she contacted police this time because she believed it showed a pattern of behavior similar to the allegations in the May case.

Another woman told police she had sex with Jacobs in April 2021. The woman said that his condom broke, so she told Jacobs to get tested for sexually transmitted diseases because they both had been involved with other people.

The woman said Jacobs later removed her from the residence, grabbed her by the arms and bicep area, pushed her against a wall and threw her belongings out. She told police that she sustained bruises to her arms and swelling in her rib area.

Hobart/Lawrence police said they obtained records from that incident indicating officers observed “no visible signs of injury, bruising or damage consistent with a physical altercation.”

Another woman said she met Jacobs through social media and that he flew her to a residence in May 2022. She described an altercation in which Jacobs grabbed her by the hair, placed his hands on her head and neck, dragged her, scratched her chest and took her phone after it fell to the floor.

The woman said she got bruises on her arm and scratches to her chest and neck. She said she didn’t report the incident to police because she wasn’t a U.S. citizen at the time and that Jacobs told her she could be deported.

Jacobs is a three-time Pro Bowl selection who led the NFL in rushing in 2022 for the Las Vegas Raiders, where he played when the three previous incidents were alleged to have occurred. He joined the Packers in 2024.

He returned two weeks ago and scored twice in the team’s Thursday night win over the Carolina Panthers, celebrating one of the touchdowns by briefly placing his hands behind his back. The move prompted immediate backlash from viewers in light of his offseason arrest, but Jacobs said he was modeling his celebration after a gospel song called “Break Every Chain.”

The records released Friday also offered more details of the May 23 incident that led to Jacobs’ arrest. According to the criminal complaint released in August, the woman told them that Jacobs grabbed her and threw her to the ground as she was trying to exit a garage door, causing her to hit her head. Police said Jacobs also took her phone from her while she was on the ground.

The records released Friday noted the woman said that while they were still in the home, Jacobs lunged at her, pinned her on the bed with his body weight on her chest, and that he put his hand on her mouth and nose, so that she felt she couldn’t breathe.

She also indicated that she believed she lost consciousness for a brief time when the confrontation moved to the garage.

A celebration of Shaun Bartlett’s Van Basten tribute goal for Charlton

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

You just can’t teach that technique: the way he lets the ball drop over his shoulder before pulling the trigger is part instinct, part superpower.

You can spend years practising the technique, but the manner in which he receives the ball doesn’t come up all too often in training drills.

The height on that diagonal ball, while making for a delightful aesthetic spectacle, hardly makes things easy for him, but it raises an important question: is it worth trading off efficiency for beauty?

The goal might have been more achievable if his team-mate had carried the ball forward and found a better angle for the delivery, but then it would have been just another goal.

Who knows, the ball might have not reached him at all, never mind it reaching him with the ball dropping onto his foot with perfect precision.

The execution would have been difficult even without the ball passing over the head of a defender. He’d have still needed to watch the ball in flight while perfectly pacing his run, ensuring he arrived in time to catch it first-time with enough power to beat the goalkeeper, but the covering opponent makes it that much tougher.

I suppose that’s why no amount of drills could prepare anyone for this scenario.

The lack of match pressure is one thing, but how are you meant to coach a defender to run directly under the flight of the ball while blocking the forward’s sight of it, like some kind of aerial snooker?

Then again, on the other side of the coin, how is the keeper supposed to prepare for such a clean strike, perhaps the cleanest of his career, catching the ball perfectly with his left foot.

Wait, left foot? Didn’t Marco van Basten connect with his right against the USSR in 1988?

Yes. Yes, he did. But he’s not the only forward born on Halloween to score such a sweetly-struck volley.

Shaun Bartlett is one of those Premier League strikers who, for the most part, was just there.

The South African took a circuitous route to England, via clubs in the United States and Switzerland, and while he only turned out for one Premier League club, he quickly became part of the furniture.

After initially arriving on loan from FC Zurich, he ended his spell at Charlton Athletic with 26 goals in more than 100 league and cup games.

It might not have been the best tally for a striker, but one goal during that initial loan spell will leave a lasting impression.

The 2000-01 season was full of great goals, with Mario Stanic’s worldy against West Ham and a wonderful Thierry Henry effort against Manchester United both the sorts of strike which would have been considered the season’s best on most occasions.

Indeed, many will still argue one of those two should have been the victor, but the technique shown by Bartlett surely makes him a worthy winner.

It’s the sort of goal we don’t see all that often, but when we do we know we’ve witnessed something very special.

I didn’t have the opportunity to see Bartlett’s goal live on TV, but when Robin van Persie scored with a similar effort against Aston Villa more than a decade later I stood up and paced around the room, unsure about the etiquette of celebrating televised goals scored by players you’re lukewarm towards and for clubs you don’t support.

Initially, I wondered how much of a role the TV cameras played in it all.

Is a goal better, for example, when it involves the ball dropping into the viewer’s blind spot on the other side of the covering defender, presenting an added layer of mystery beyond even the scorer?

When Edin Dzeko scored another left foot belter for Roma against Chelsea I realised no, that’s not how it works, stop being an idiot, Tom.

What does matter, however, is the identity of the scorer.

As I have written before for this site, a cracker from an unlikely source is one thing, but it’s elevated when you can see the technique required.

Sometimes a goal is so great it looks like everything is happening in slow motion, but this was the opposite.

It appeared as though Bartlett had been wound up like a Hotwheels car such is the pace at which he approaches the ball. There’s no time to relax, or for him to compose himself while waiting for the ball to drop, it’s a run-and-hit, all in one fluid movement, almost as much NFL touchdown as football goal.

Bartlett scored just once more all season, and just once in total in his first full season following his permanent transfer in the summer of 2001 – bizarrely that, like his Goal of the Season winner, also came in a 2-0 victory over Leicester City.

Still, when you’ve scored a goal better than most will manage in their entire career, you don’t really want to show off in the years afterwards.

So he did what any sensible man would in that scenario: he didn’t repeat those heroics and returned to South Africa after leaving Charlton a few years later, but he scored enough goals in the meantime to plant that seed of doubt.

Sure, he didn’t manage to score as good a goal again, but maybe he could have done if he wanted to.

I Gave My App Away for Free — And It Almost Killed It

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Generosity is a marketing strategy. Unit economics is a survival strategy. Here is how my "Founder's Launch Program" saved my indie app.

The best week of my indie life.

It started with a stranger on Reddit.

He'd tried my native notes app, EpicNotes, on his Xiaomi phone and left a comment: "Feels fast and nice. But there's some minor visual weirdness on my phone." I replied offering him a free 3-month Pro code as a thank-you for the feedback. He said yes. I generated the code in the Play Console, felt like a real company handing out a real perk, and went to sleep happy.

Then came more. A fellow tester who cancelled his subscription on principle but still sent me bug reports with screenshots. A student from India who emailed: "I'm a student, so I can't afford it. If you could make a lifetime promo code available for me, I would be able to fully use it and provide suggestions and bug reports. Please 🙏"

I granted him lifetime Pro in about two minutes. It felt amazing. People were using my app. People were talking to me. After six months of building native Kotlin Multiplatform code at night after my day job as a product manager, this was the part that finally felt real.

I thought I was doing indie development right. I was doing indie development feelingly. Not mathematically.

The math I refused to do.

Here's the embarrassing part: I'm a product manager by day. I build business cases for a living. I would never ship a feature at work without a rough cost-benefit estimate.

And yet at 1 a.m., I was handing out free Pro access to an app with AI features without ever opening a spreadsheet.

So let me open it now, in public.

What a free Pro user costs me:

  • Every AI summary and smart web clip is a call to a third-party AI API. Pennies per call — but power users summarize a lot.
  • Every synced note is compute, storage, and bandwidth on my cloud backend.
  • Every 90-day code, and especially every lifetime grant, is a commitment to pay both of those bills for months or years.

What a free Pro user pays me: $0.

My actual revenue at the time: three paying subscribers. Single-digit monthly recurring revenue.

The scary part wasn't that the number was big. It was that the number was small enough to ignore — and big enough to bleed. A leak doesn't need to be a flood to sink a boat that isn't moving.

Why free users felt like growth (and weren't).

The indie playbook says: give things away early, collect feedback, build goodwill. And the playbook is right — partially.

That Xiaomi tester? His free code paid for itself ten times over. He sent screenshots of two real bugs (a settings sheet clipped by system insets, a formatting popup buried under the soft keyboard), his exact device model, and unprompted pricing research: "I have a rule against using subscription-based services." That's the kind of intelligence money can't buy.

But here's what nobody tells you: most free Pro users don't do that. They redeem the code, run a few AI summaries, and go quiet. No feedback. No review. No referral. Every summary they run is a cost with no return.

The uncomfortable reframe I had to accept: a free user who costs you money every month is not an asset waiting to convert. It's a liability waiting for your motivation to run out.

The Pivot: The Founder's Launch Program

I realized I was trying to force a subscription model on a market that was screaming for a one-time purchase. Multiple users told me, in different words, the same thing: I will not pay monthly.

So instead of defending my subscription, I stopped fighting subscription fatigue and decided to price for it.

I just launched the EpicNotes Founder's Launch Program. It is a one-time Lifetime Deal for the first 100 users.

Here is why I structured it this way:

  1. It solves the subscription fatigue: Users pay once and own the Pro features (including the AI engine) forever. No monthly anxiety.
  2. It solves my unit economics: The upfront cash injection covers my API and server costs for the next year, giving me the runway to keep building without bleeding money every month.
  3. It builds a core community: The 100 "Founders" get direct access to me. Their feedback shapes the roadmap.

If you are tired of bloated Electron wrappers and want a truly native, privacy-first notes app for Mac and Android, this is your chance to support an indie dev and lock in a lifetime license before the doors close. (Link to the Founder's Launch Program is in my bio/below).

What I changed (the boring, unsexy fixes).

1. I hard-paywalled the AI. The free tier now includes the full notes experience — writing, formatting, sync — but zero AI. AI is the only feature with a real marginal cost, so it's the only feature behind the paywall. A free user now costs me roughly the price of storage: pennies.

2. I put my generosity on a budget. Twenty goodwill codes, total. They now go to people who give something back: detailed feedback, honest reviews, real reach. The tester who sends bug reports with screenshots? Instant yes. A drive-by "send code pls"? A polite no with a link to the free tier.

3. I asked. I reached out to my core users and told them the truth: solo dev, real server costs, if this app helps you, here's how to keep it alive. Honesty converts better than guilt — and both convert better than silence.

The Lesson

Generosity is a marketing strategy. Unit economics is a survival strategy. You need both — but in this order: survive first, then be generous on purpose.

I'm still giving EpicNotes away to students, testers, and kind strangers on Reddit. I just open the spreadsheet before I open the billing dashboard now.

President's Circle is the highest published elite tier in Hertz Gold+ Rewards, the rental company's loyalty program, which costs nothing to join. It sits above the entry-level Gold tier and the mid-tier Five Star elite status.

Binance Details $6.3B Hot-Wallet Breach as Exchange Pledges to Cover Losses

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The company reported approximately $3 billion of affected assets frozen on-chain through coordination with issuers, investigators and other industry participants. Frozen tokens are not the same as recovered cash, and assets immobilized on-chain still require legal and technical steps to return.

Binance said unauthorized withdrawals began after its global hot-wallet infrastructure was compromised on October 29 at approximately 20:05 UTC. In its October 30 update, the exchange put the value of affected tokens at about $6.3 billion and said withdrawals were temporarily suspended pending additional security checks.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Binance. It offers a specific basis for assessing Binance’s $6.3 billion hot-wallet breach without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The company reported approximately $3 billion of affected assets frozen on-chain through coordination with issuers, investigators and other industry participants. It said more than $6.34 billion of company resources were earmarked to absorb losses, with roughly $5 billion already held in the relevant tokens while it sourced the balance.

The development arrives as digital-asset markets remain sensitive to funding conditions, infrastructure reliability and the gap between promotional claims and operational results. It warrants attention on its own facts, without assuming a particular cryptocurrency must rise or fall in response.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Binance’s $6.3 billion hot-wallet breach establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

For traders and institutions, the issue is not confined to the headline loss. A platform can promise balance-sheet compensation while users remain exposed to withdrawal interruptions, unsettled orders and uncertainty about the integrity of infrastructure on which they rely.

Users face two different forms of risk during a security incident. Asset losses are the direct financial concern, while withdrawals, service interruptions and unclear communications can make it difficult to assess or manage exposure. Investigations are strongest when confirmed technical evidence is separated from hypotheses about attackers.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

Binance initially cited a potentially compromised laptop, but said subsequent work identified further points of intrusion. It stated that investigators had not found evidence that wallet private keys were leaked. That distinction leaves the precise authorization and infrastructure failure unresolved until a verified technical post-mortem is published.

Frozen tokens are not the same as recovered cash, and assets immobilized on-chain still require legal and technical steps to return. The exchange’s reimbursement commitment is an announcement rather than independently audited proof that every affected account has already been restored.

Security controls have to account for both malicious transactions and legitimate users who need access. Freezing, recovery and reimbursement mechanisms are not interchangeable: a frozen asset may be immobilized, a reimbursed user may be made whole, and a forensic investigation may still be incomplete.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Binance said it was targeting a phased restart of withdrawals after security validation, planned an updated proof-of-reserves report by November 2 and expected to disclose further verified technical findings. Those milestones, rather than speculative attacker attribution, are the tests for the recovery process.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

A Cracked Oil Pan Killed One of Porsche's Most Reliable Flat-Six Engines

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Porsche MA1.04

In the bible of engine reliability, you need a couple of things: A proven design and architecture, proper usage cycles and care, and religious maintenance and service intervals. The Porsche MA1.04 is part of a series of internal combustion engine families from Stuttgart that is regarded as some of the brand’s most reliable powertrains it has ever produced, so for one to come into the shop for a teardown is somewhat of an event.

Even some more modern Porsches aren’t exempt from the occasional engine problem here and there. Porsche’s very quick to revise their design once a flaw is identified, but somehow, the MA1 series just got it mostly right. While not perfect, the flat-six still has a few failure points and maintenance-intensive areas that owners should know about, but none of those failure points appeared to be the culprit when this motor decided to blow.

So that’s why it blew up.

Right off the bat, I Do Cars found the root cause. There’s a crack in the oil pan. So the engine ran without oil once the level dipped to dangerous levels, and it soon became a coffee table.

The damage done was reportedly $10,000 at the time for a new motor without labor. The engine was graciously donated by a fan of I Do Cars after being sat for a while. While the damage to the owner’s wallet is documented, the full extent of the damage to the motor and why it was replaced was only revealed towards the end of the video.

In the middle of the teardown, I Do Cars did notice a few things that were not ideal for a motor, like some shavings and fragments in the combustion chamber. That was just a teaser of things to come, however.

I Do Cars - YouTube

What Blew Up?

So after getting all the nuts and bolts out of the assembly, the engine comes apart. It’s interesting to note that the MA1 splits apart right down the middle. Once the flat six turned into two inline-threes, we get to see what went wrong. Up until this point, everything was peak German precision. However, the deeper I Do Cars dug, the more heartbreaking it ended up being.

All the rods and pistons were intact with acceptable wear, and even the rod bearings were good, but they have seen better days. That all changes, however, when the camera pointed to the main bearings. One bearing welded itself to the crankshaft, while the rest looked like it had been mangled.

That’s not the worst part, though. On one half of the block, we can see a nasty score at the crankcase. Basically, chunks of aluminum are just sitting there in a crevice. This is a bad case of a spun bearing, and it was enough to basically stop the engine dead in its tracks.

The fact that this was all caused by a hit to the oil pan makes this even more heartbreaking. Now, yes, some parts from this motor can still be reused by resourceful builders, but the block itself might be out of commission. An extremely skilled machinist might be able to repair it, but it could cost more to repair than to replace as a whole unit. Maybe the block is destined to be turned into a coffee table after all.
