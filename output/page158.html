The Independent Journalism Atlas looks at “wells” and “springs” in news deserts

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

What do we mean when we talk about news deserts?

The term has become a shorthand for areas devoid of local news coverage. But a new essay from the Independent Journalism Atlas recalls that when Penny Abernathy popularized the term through her research, she used it to describe a more nuanced picture:

To show what information can look like in news deserts, the Independent Journalism Atlas — an initiative that aims to “[document] independent creators who inform, educate, and advance public knowledge outside traditional media structures,” cofounded by Liz Kelly Nelson, Justin Bank, and 2025 Nieman Fellow Ryan Kellett — took a closer look at 46 of the 238 news desert counties identified in this year’s State of Local News report from Medill.

“Look at the ground and news moves like water,” writes Bank. “It doesn’t stop at a county line. It runs off the surface, sinks underground and comes up wherever someone digs.” The team found that “in more places than the desert map shows, someone has picked up a shovel.” (That might help explain why people who live in news deserts don’t feel deprived of local news.) The team identifies a few patterns in how information looks and travels in news deserts, including “surface” information, wells, and springs.

Posts from the local sheriff, weather alerts, and obituaries from the funeral home are all examples of “surface” information that “runs off,” Bank writes, typically telling you what happened without telling you why.

Wells are newsrooms that form when a local paper shutters. The team points to the Anaconda Weekly Review in Deer Lodge County, Montana, as an example of a well appearing in a county Medill identified as a news desert.

Springs, meanwhile, are “harder to spot” than wells because they’re smaller and more ad hoc. The Independent Journalism Atlas applies this label to efforts like the Gallatin County Board Illinois Unofficial Facebook page, which livestreams the board’s public meetings and posts its agendas.

The team also paid attention to coverage of news deserts by outlets in neighboring counties. They found high school sports are often a gateway to coverage, pointing to a channel covering southern Illinois high school sports that interviewed the principal in Harrisburg, the seat of Saline County, another news desert county.

In recent years, efforts to “map” local news through different lenses have proliferated. The Independent Journalism Atlas is one such effort, focused on news creators. A better understanding of information sources in news deserts matters, Bank argues, because “you can’t tend a basin you haven’t mapped.”

Texas Instruments Announces the TI-Rover BT: Bringing Math to Life with a Calculator-Controlled Robotic Vehicle

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

DALLAS, Texas — October 22, 2026 — Texas Instruments (TI) today announced the launch of the TI-Rover BT, a Bluetooth®-enabled robotic vehicle that transforms the classroom floor into a living coordinate plane. By converting algebraic equations into tangible motion, the vehicle empowers students to move beyond the screen and interact with abstract concepts in real time.

“The TI-Rover BT takes the intimidation out of challenging concepts. When a student sees a sine wave drawn across a large piece of chart paper, the scale and physical presence of the oscillation creates a lightbulb moment,” said Nicole Moran, a secondary math teacher in Northside ISD in Texas. “It turns a theoretical lesson into an exciting and interactive adventure.”

From equations to exploration: dynamic discovery in the classroom

The TI-Rover BT is designed to turn traditional math lessons into immersive, hands-on experiences through several key educational capabilities.

  • Mobilize the classroom: Enables wireless control via the TI Bluetooth® Adapter, allowing students to navigate the room and engage with numerical patterns in a large-scale environment.
  • Visualize functions in motion: Plots curves and geometric shapes using built-in Math Drive library templates.
  • Translate equations into art: Uses high-precision stepper motors and an onboard pen holder with up/down capability to physically render formulas, making concepts like continuity and discontinuity visible.
  • Integrate STEM curriculum: Facilitates the transition from algebraic plotting to physical science lessons on rate, distance, and time, with expandable hardware for robotics exploration.
  • Explore input and output: Offers the option to program the onboard RGB LED to change colors when the vehicle gets too close to an object measured by the Ultrasonic Ranger.

“The TI-Rover BT is more than a tool; it’s a way to make abstract mathematical ideas tangible,” said Laura Chambers, president of Texas Instruments Educational Technology. “With this launch, we are bringing math to life, making complex concepts engaging for every learner.”

The TI-Rover BT is currently compatible with the TI-84 Evo, and TI plans to expand support for TI-Nspire CX II and TI-Nspire CX X II CAS graphing calculators in 2027.

The TI-Rover BT is available for purchase at instructional dealers nationwide. For more information, visit https://education.ti.com/roverbt.

The impossible is becoming possible. Last week, Italian climber Pietro Vidi announced that he had made the second ascent of the world’s hardest trad climb: Drifter’s Escape (5.15a) in Squamish, British Columbia.

The 65-foot route was first climbed by Connor Herson in July 2025. Afterward, Herson kept his send a secret for seven months until he was ready to release a documentary about it. The achievement later earned Herson a Golden Piton.

The stats and significance of Drifter’s Escape

Connor Herson on the crux sequence of Drifter’s Escape. (Photo: Christian Adam / Black Diamond)

Drifter’s follows a series of parallel cracks through an otherwise blank headwall of white granite, with two V11 cruxes. The climb does include two bolts at the beginning, which makes it technically a mixed climb. But traditional gear protects the rest of the route, including the cruxes.

Local climber Ethan Salvo, who has projected the route with both Herson and Vidi, said the difficulty of Drifter’s primarily stems from “how punchy the whole upper half is.” Herson famously tackled the first V11 boulder problem by performing a “pogo.” As he bumped his right hand up,  he generated momentum by swinging out with his right leg—a sporty move typically seen in the gym, not on rock.

While the first ascent and first repeat of Drifter’s Escape mark a new era in hard trad climbing, both Herson and Vidi agreed that the discipline will continue to progress in terms of grades. “Trad is not even near the limit,” Vidi told Climbing. 

“Trad climbing has the most immediate potential for progression,” Herson shared with Climbing shortly after announcing his ascent in February. Both climbers also feel aligned that the primary challenge in pushing the limits of trad further is finding more hard trad lines.

IEEE Xplore Digital Library Adds AI Assistant

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →


IEEE recently released the IEEE Xplore AI Research Suite, built on the full text of millions of peer-reviewed publications in the IEEE Xplore Digital Library. The library offers full-text access to IEEE standards, journals, magazines, conference proceedings, and more.

Researchers who use the AI suite to search the digital library receive answers grounded in verified scientific results, ensuring a high level of accuracy and trust.

The suite is available as an optional subscription upgrade to all IEEE Xplore Digital Library institutional subscribers.

Powered by a proprietary AI system optimized for STEM literature, the suite introduces powerful, intelligent features within the digital library. The suite enables users to seamlessly search and discover articles to accelerate their comprehension of complex technical papers.

The system surfaces deep insights, replacing the typical keyword queries with natural, conversational research workflows.

As AI tools become more common in conducting research, IEEE collaborated to build the suite around a simple principle: Answers should be trustworthy, not just fast, says W. Clem Karl, vice president, IEEE Publication Services and Products.

“AI can be a highly effective and beneficial tool that can help researchers accelerate discovery, enhance decision-making, and surface new connections,” Karl says. “However, many AI engines present a great risk of hallucinating answers or introducing bias into otherwise credible research. With this in mind, IEEE has taken a responsible approach in our AI strategy by developing concrete safeguards to ensure our solution is rigorously grounded in scientific evidence that users can trust.

“As stewards of the scholarly record, we held the new AI engine to the same standards as the peer-reviewed research we publish: A researcher shouldn’t have to trust an answer; they should be able to verify the work behind it.”

Collaboration with the scientific community

The IEEE Xplore AI Research Suite was developed in close collaboration with global scientific and library communities.

The development process began with research into user needs and the challenges associated with existing AI research assistants. More than 3,000 testers—including engineers, scientists, and library information professionals—provided feedback to refine the AI engine and support research workflows.

Capabilities include:

  • Semantic AI search. Users can input free-form, natural-language queries or paste long blocks of text to locate similar research and identify novel solutions.
  • Time-saving summaries. The platform generates immediate, accurate AI overviews of search results and provides in-context definitions of technical terms so that users can grasp new concepts faster and accelerate their research.
  • Built on trusted content. The suite is the only AI research assistant that uses both the full text and metadata of IEEE’s peer-reviewed journal articles and conference papers to optimize the accuracy and relevance of the results.
  • Comprehensive coverage. A variety of STEM metadata is available from journal articles and conference papers that have been curated to include scholarly articles without the typical noise found in other tools.
  • Source links and verifiable results. The AI-generated overviews include reference links to source documents, allowing users to verify information and cross-reference findings.

Reducing hallucinations

To mitigate the risk of AI hallucinations, the search-retrieval process is limited to querying the millions of IEEE peer-reviewed full-text documents, alongside an expertly curated dataset of STEM metadata. If the AI tool is unable to find relevant scientific content within the defined dataset, it is designed to inform the user rather than generate fabricated answers.

“IEEE is committed to providing the technical community with a highly precise and reliable research experience,” says Steven Heffner, IEEE’s chief publication officer and managing director of IEEE Publications. “The IEEE Xplore AI Research Suite offers something unique in that it is the only AI research assistant that leverages both the full-text and metadata of IEEE’s peer-reviewed content, rather than abstracts alone.

“The difference is significant: Our internal testing showed a threefold increase in result quality and accuracy across every key metric. These results underscore how critical full-text grounding is for technical research.”

For more information about features and coverage, or to request a demo for your organization, visit the IEEE Xplore AI Research Suite website.

Inside the New D.C. Restaurant That Only Sells Five Curry Steak Frites Every Night

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The hottest new spot in Washington, D.C. focuses on the traditional heat-packed cooking methods of northern Thailand, while still using local ingredients from around the DMV. Alfie’s, from chefs Justin Ahn and Alex McCoy, started as a pop-up in Petworth in 2017 before traveling around the world, serving diners in New York and London. This year, the restaurant put down permanent roots in a Georgetown alleyway. The co-owners led us through a day in the new kitchen, preparing everything from hand-cut green papaya in som tam salad to the limited-quantity Thai steak frites with a massaman curry peppercorn sauce.

In the video, the kitchen team breaks down rib-eye steaks for their take on a steak au poivre, which they only make five of each night. The steaks are carefully seared for each order, with the au poivre massaman curry sauce is finished in the pan with the leftover beef fat. Classic “bar fries” are served up with the massive 26-ounce rib-eye.

Elsewhere in the kitchen, another chef carves green papayas. The irregular slices of papaya get tossed in a funky, spicy dressing for each order. Ko calls it the “most labor intensive dish on the line;” each order is carefully mixed with fresh tomatoes, peanuts, and a palm syrup and tamarind-heavy dressing. A rich pork belly curry is simmering in the background. Gaeng hung lay (a traditional northern Thai curry) cooks down large pieces of pork belly to get that fatty layer, with pickled garlic and pineapple balancing out the rich flavors of curry paste, tamarind, and fish sauce.

Right before service, the chefs begin prepping herbs and sticky rice that has been soaking overnight and is quickly steamed for that malleable, glutinous texture. McCoy talks about how being a chef is more of a lifestyle than a job, but there’s immense payoff when you get to see diners enjoy your hard work every night. He hopes that Alfie’s will become a place where “you can just forget where you are, have a good time, and just lose yourself in the experience and the food.”

Everyone loves a vacation. But the cleanup and tidying as the trip is finishing up? Not so much.

It’s not always clear what guests are responsible for cleaning. Dishes, sheets, towels, and taking out the trash are sometimes part of checkout instructions at Airbnb rentals. With checkout typically at 11 a.m., the last morning of a trip can turn into a mad dash through an unreasonably long chore list, even when a cleaning fee has been tacked onto the bill.

That frustration, along with a growing number of travelers returning to hotels for more consistent quality and fees, is behind Airbnb’s new checkout rules. Learn which tasks guests may still face, and how hosts are responding.

What Airbnb’s New Checkout Rules Mean for Guests and Cleaning Requirements

Starting with reservations booked on or after November 20, 2026, Airbnb will require hosts to clearly explain their house rules and checkout requests before guests book. Hosts won’t be allowed to add new requirements through messages or signs inside the rental after a reservation is confirmed.

“House Rules are now displayed more prominently on your listing, where guests can easily see them before they book,” according to Airbnb’s announcement. Guests can also find these instructions in their Trips tab and reservation reminders.

Under the new policy, hosts generally can’t require guests to strip beds, start laundry, wash and put away dishes, or take trash to an off-site disposal facility. However, hosts can still request simpler tasks, such as placing dirty dishes in the sink, gathering used towels, or putting trash and recycling in designated bins. Guests may also be asked to turn off lights and return keys before leaving.

Airbnb’s policy distinguishes between house rules and requests. “Guests must follow hosts’ house rules, and are encouraged to comply with hosts’ requests,” according to the company. Some exceptions may apply. For example, rentals in areas with bears may require guests to use animal-proof trash containers, while drought restrictions could affect water use.

Guests are still responsible for leaving the property reasonably tidy. Airbnb’s ground rules for guests prohibit excessive messes and require guests to take responsibility for damage beyond normal wear and tear. Hosts can still charge standard cleaning fees and request reimbursement for unexpected cleaning costs or damage.

What Happens When Airbnb Hosts Violate the New Checkout Rules?

Hosts who don’t follow Airbnb’s new checkout rules could receive warnings or requests to update their listings. Repeated violations may lead to notices on their listings or lower placement in search results. Hosts can also appeal flagged requests.

The policy also prevents hosts from charging their own fines for missed chores or restricting access to amenities already included in a reservation.

However, not every host supports the changes. Some believe washing dishes after cooking is a reasonable responsibility for guests, while others worry the new rules could increase cleaning costs. A Change.org petition has called on Airbnb to reverse its restrictions on requiring guests to wash dishes.

Guests who encounter unexpected chores or requests that violate the new policy can contact Airbnb for help.

Turn subsurface uncertainty into decision-ready insight

Drilling campaigns are among the most expensive and operationally complex activities in the energy industry. Rigs, crews, equipment, and logistics are scheduled months in advance, leaving little room for delay. Every hour of downtime can carry significant costs. Geoscientists and engineers rely on reservoir models to understand the subsurface and guide drilling decisions, often under intense time pressure. Yet the current modeling infrastructure that many companies rely on for these critical decisions was designed for smaller datasets, simpler workflows, and siloed operations. As subsurface data grows in scale and complexity, teams need a faster, more connected way to transform uncertainty into actionable decisions.

Now generally available, agile reservoir modeling (ARM) from SLB, integrated with Azure Data Manager for Energy (ADME), is co-engineered for cloud scale to close that gap. The combined solution connects ensemble-centric reservoir modeling and simulation with a governed data foundation, elastic Azure high-performance computing, and scalable storage designed for the throughput and concurrency of large ensemble studies.

Scale ensemble modeling with ARM on Azure

A single reservoir model represents one plausible interpretation of the subsurface. Ensemble modeling evaluates hundreds of model realizations together, giving asset teams a broader view of potential outcomes and a stronger basis for understanding uncertainty and risk.

The industry has long recognized the value of ensemble modeling for risk-aware decision-making, but adoption has been constrained by infrastructure and software designed for smaller, sequential studies. Moving from a limited model set to hundreds of realizations multiplies the compute, storage, data movement, and results that must be managed. ARM addresses this barrier by distributing realization generation and simulation across elastic Azure high-performance computing, while scalable storage provides the capacity, throughput, and parallel access required by ensembles of different sizes.

Connect reservoir workflows to a governed data foundation

Building and updating reservoir models draws on many systems: seismic, wellbore, interpretation, and simulation data, plus the applications and compute that process them. When these pieces are fragmented, customers must connect and orchestrate them on their own, and large-scale simulation becomes a disconnected, one-off exercise.

SLB and Microsoft combined their respective technologies, ARM engineered by SLB and ADME engineered by Microsoft, to remove that burden. Rather than a set of loosely connected tools, customers get one integrated workflow in which each component has a clear role:

  • Azure Data Manager for Energy is the governed data foundation. OSDU® Technical Standard-aligned services and OSDU Domain Data Management Services (DDMS) manage seismic, wellbore, interpretation, and simulation data.
  • Petrel™ subsurface software is where engineers define interpretation and uncertainty workflows, connected directly to data in ADME.
  • Agile reservoir modeling is the ensemble workflow engine. It orchestrates the generation of reservoir realizations and simulation runs and supports discovery and analysis at enterprise scale.

As a result, teams build ensembles from trusted corporate data and publish results back for discovery and reuse, which reduces data movement and improves collaboration across asset teams.

Put ensemble modeling into practice with a connected workflow

Beyond integration and scale, ARM distinguishes itself by orchestrating all stages of an ensemble study across modeling, simulation, data management, and analysis. The workflow tightly connects the tools reservoir teams already use with Azure-scale infrastructure and the governed data services provided by Azure Data Manager for Energy.

Agile Reservoir Modeling workflow using cloud HPC to generate and simulate multiple reservoir-model realizations in parallel delivered to web-based interface for analysis.

A walkthrough of how this works today:

Reservoir engineers define uncertainty workflows in Petrel software and prepare simulation models for supported SLB simulators, including the Intersect™ reservoir simulator and Eclipse™ reservoir simulator. ARM generates the realizations, prepares the simulation cases, and coordinates their execution across the Azure environment.

Azure high-performance computing executes multiple realizations in parallel. A shared, high-performance Azure Files volume holds model inputs, grids, simulation decks, temporary files, and results so the simulation fleet can read and write concurrently. Because compute capacity, storage capacity, throughput, and IOPS can be scaled independently, the environment can be sized for the concurrency of the ensemble rather than the constraints of fixed infrastructure.

As simulations complete, SLB reservoir engineering services manage the resulting ensembles and use OSDU search capabilities on ADME to support discovery, filtering, and sorting across large studies. Teams can locate relevant realizations and supported, published results without manually navigating disconnected project stores.

Engineers can analyze ensembles in the reservoir engineering workspace or load them back into Petrel software for further interpretation, history matching, and reservoir engineering studies. This allows teams to work in the environment and application best suited to each task while maintaining continuity, without having to step outside their workflow to manage orchestration.

ADME provides entitlement-based access control integrated with Microsoft Entra ID and supports compliance controls such as OSDU legal tags. Connecting ARM workflows to this governed data foundation helps maintain consistent access and compliance as corporate subsurface data is used across modeling and simulation. Because ARM workflows are integrated through Petrel software in ADME, reservoir engineers can perform ensemble-scale studies leveraging their corporate-wide data stores and publish results for discovery, eliminating friction and driving collaboration. Microsoft and SLB engineering collaborated closely to build the integration between Petrel software and ARM for enterprise-scale performance.

For reservoir engineers and asset teams, ARM turns ensemble modeling into a repeatable workflow rather than a bespoke infrastructure project. Teams can stress-test development concepts against calibrated ensembles that honor observed production history, not simply run a larger number of models. As new wells are drilled and new data becomes available, they can update their reservoir understanding without waiting for a major model rebuild. For energy IT and data leaders, the workflow connects simulation to an OSDU standard-aligned architecture with consistent identity, governance, search, and interoperability, reducing isolated data copies and disconnected applications.

The future of the reservoir lifecycle

Building on this partnership, Microsoft and SLB are working within the OSDU community to advance a more cloud-native standard for reservoir data. This work will help models, simulation results, and engineering workflows operate more consistently across applications, improving interoperability while reducing dependence on proprietary formats and silos. A shared, governed data foundation also paves the way for AI and agentic experiences that can discover data, coordinate workflows, and surface insights across the reservoir lifecycle. Combined with Azure-scale compute and storage, this creates a path toward more automated and continuous reservoir modeling.
