A ghostly cat is lurking in space, and NASA captured it

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Just in time for Halloween, NASA has revealed a ghostly figure lurking in a distant cloud of gas and dust. It looks remarkably like a cat, complete with a glowing eye and a long, shadowy body.

The eerie formation, nicknamed the Cat’s Head Nebula, lies about 7,500 light-years from Earth within the enormous Carina Nebula.

New images from the Hubble and James Webb space telescopes reveal this cosmic cat in extraordinary detail. 

But behind its haunting appearance is something even more fascinating: a turbulent region where new stars are taking shape.

A cat in the cosmos

The Cat’s Head Nebula gets its nickname from a towering column of dust that resembles a cat sitting with its back toward the viewer.

Its head appears turned to one side, revealing a single glowing eye. A narrow trail of dark material extends downward like a tail.

The resemblance is a coincidence, created by the forces that shape clouds of gas and dust in space.

Powerful ultraviolet radiation and stellar winds from massive nearby stars have gradually carved away the surrounding material.

Denser portions of the cloud have resisted this erosion, leaving behind the distinctive feline silhouette.

The structure is not permanent. Over time, radiation and winds will continue breaking apart the cloud, changing its appearance as new stars emerge.

Hubble reveals the cat

Hubble’s visible-light image shows the cosmic cat as a dark brown pillar surrounded by brilliant shades of blue, yellow, orange, and red.

Its glowing red eye may mark a young star that remains partly hidden inside the dusty structure.

Along the right edge of the pillar, a bright yellowish border reveals where radiation from nearby massive stars is affecting the gas.

This radiation can strip material away through a process called photoevaporation, gradually dispersing the cloud.

The surrounding colors also carry scientific information.

Hubble’s observations capture light associated with oxygen, hydrogen, and sulfur, along with starlight scattered by dust.

Together, these signals help astronomers understand the physical conditions inside this active stellar nursery.

Webb sees through dust

While Hubble reveals the nebula’s dramatic outline, the James Webb Space Telescope provides a different perspective.

Webb observes infrared light, allowing it to detect structures that are difficult or impossible to see in visible light.

In its near-infrared image, the cat’s dark body becomes more transparent. Intricate patterns emerge within the dusty pillar, while numerous stars appear throughout the surrounding region.

Some of these stars remain partly obscured, but others shine clearly through the material.

Webb’s sensitive instruments even reveal faint background galaxies far beyond the nebula.

The cat’s glowing eye remains visible, appearing yellow rather than red in this infrared view.

The observations suggest that the bright source may be a young star still surrounded by the material from which it formed.

Hidden cosmic details

Webb’s mid-infrared observations reveal another layer of the nebula’s structure.

Warm dust and carbon-based molecules, energized by starlight, produce emissions that outline delicate strands and clumps of material.

In this image, the cat’s body appears bluish-gray against a predominantly red background.

Wispy formations to the left of the main pillar become especially noticeable, revealing details that are much harder to distinguish in Hubble’s visible-light image.

Another bright source appears to the right of the pillar, potentially marking an infant star embedded in dust.

A narrow filament seems to cross the source, although the image alone cannot establish whether the two structures are physically connected.

These different observations allow scientists to investigate the same region using several kinds of light.

Each wavelength reveals different features, providing a more complete picture of the gas, dust, and developing stars.

Stars are taking shape

The Cat’s Head Nebula is part of the much larger Carina Nebula, one of the Milky Way’s most remarkable regions of star formation.

Within such stellar nurseries, gravity draws clouds of gas and dust together. As dense pockets collapse, they can eventually form new stars.

At the same time, existing massive stars release powerful radiation and winds that reshape the surrounding environment.

These competing processes make star formation complicated. The same radiation that disperses a cloud can also influence how its remaining material evolves.

By combining Hubble’s visible-light observations with Webb’s infrared views, astronomers can examine these processes in greater detail.

The cosmic cat will not keep its familiar shape forever. Its dusty silhouette will gradually change and eventually disappear.

Warriors’ comeback vs Lakers unprecedented: ‘25, it’s the new 12′

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

SAN FRANCISCO — It was deader than dead inside Chase Center after the first quarter Friday night, a fitting graveyard-like atmosphere on the eve of Halloween.

A motivated Luka Doncic exploded for 21 points and the Lakers built a 23-point lead by the time Steve Kerr gathered his team on the Warriors’ bench between periods.

They should have been spooked.

It was about the worst 12 minutes of basketball Golden State had played this season. It appeared Kerr’s squad was headed for a second rout in 10 days at the hands of the Lakers.

Then, everything flipped.

Golden State Warriors guard Stephen Curry (30) smiles during an NBA Cup basketball game against the Los Angeles Lakers on Saturday, Oct. 31, 2026, in San Francisco. AP Photo/Justine Willard

Austin Reaves got to the foul line on the Lakers’ first possession of the second quarter, extending their advantage to 25 points, 40-15, and that’s as wide as it would get.

De’Anthony Melton scored the next seven points, and Golden State went on to come all the way back — and then some — for a convincing 112-95 win over Doncic and the Lakers.

From the end of the first-quarter timeout on, the Warriors outscored Los Angeles 97-57.

“I don’t think I got too upset. I was trying to stay poised,” Kerr said. “We couldn’t have played any worse, and they knew it. I didn’t need to tell them. I just said it’s a long game.

“I don’t think I said anything about the modern NBA — I didn’t say anything to our players — but what I would [say] is 25, it’s the new 12. These games happen nightly in the league.”

Golden State Warriors head coach Steve Kerr shouts to get an official’s attention during a game against the Los Angeles Lakers in San Francisco on Friday, Oct. 30, 2026. Getty Images

They don’t usually happen quite like this, though. In fact, the Warriors’ Jekyll-and-Hyde act produced a kind of game that had never been seen in the NBA, at least not in recent history.

As poor as the opening period went for the Warriors, the second was just as bad for JJ Redick’s squad. By halftime, Golden State had erased the entire deficit to pull even at 53.

It was the first time since the NBA started tracking play-by-play (in 1996-97) that a team had trailed by 25 or more in the first half and come back to even the score at halftime.

The final 17-point margin of victory was the largest by any team that has trailed by 25 or more.

“These games are wild,” Kerr said. “With the pace and the 3-point shooting, there are just these wild swings. You just have to stay with it.”

While Kerr kept his cool after the disastrous first quarter, the assistants in the second row of the Warriors’ bench let the players have it. They committed 10 turnovers, double their number of made baskets, and seemed to be rattled by Doncic’s furor in his rematch with Yaxel Lendeborg.

Anthony Vereen was the “lead voice” in calling out the Warriors players, Steph Curry said.

Golden State Warriors guard Stephen Curry (30) shoots a 3-point basket against the Los Angeles Lakers in an NBA Cup basketball game, Saturday, Oct. 31, 2026. AP Photo/Justine Willard

“You need that kind of fire to see how we’re gonna respond,” Curry said.

And respond, they did.

After coughing up the ball 10 times in 12 minutes, they turned it over only six times over the remaining 36. Only one team ended up with a positive assist-to-turnover ratio, and it wasn’t the one that jumped to the early 25-point lead.

The Lakers ended up turning the ball over five more times than Golden State (20-15) and assisting on 13 fewer buckets (31-18).

Brandin Podziemski of the Golden State Warriors points toward the Los Angeles Lakers bench at Chase Center in San Francisco, Friday, Oct. 30, 2026. Getty Images

“Everything went wrong,” Kerr said of the first quarter. “It was just a weird quarter. It was not anything strategy related. We didn’t change anything after the first quarter.

“Sometimes when you come back off a weeklong road trip and you have a day off, no shootaround, that first few minutes can be a little rough. That would be my excuse for tonight.”

In addition to his 21 first-quarter points, Doncic looked unstoppable as a playmaker, dishing out five assists. He finished with 37 points to best Curry’s 31, but he didn’t set up another Lakers bucket for the rest of the game.

“Luka had an unbelievable first quarter,” Curry said. “He looked very determined coming into tonight. Our offense got stagnant. Turnovers the first four or five minutes were deflating. 

“We had a lot of chatter at the end of the first quarter, and it was kind of cool to see the second unit — just the overall execution on both ends in the second quarter.”

The big-racked, muscled-up brute crashes through cover. Operating like a pedigreed cutting horse, he carves out two rival bucks and sends them packing for distant hills. Quickly, he returns to the small patch of cover where the wide-eyed estrus doe waits. The pair will likely stay in that obscure piece of real estate for the next 24 to 48 hours. That’s well away from most other deer — and hunters, who are focusing on better-looking ground with better traditional habitat hundreds of yards away. But it doesn’t have to be that way.

While traditional rut stands work just fine during the peak rut, other unorthodox spots can, too. Hunters should be aware of both sides of that coin. The rut-hunting playbook isn’t as small as some might believe. That’s why I’ve ranked the best 15 spots for peak-rut deer hunting.

1. Funnels and Pinch Points

Funnels and pinch-points are among the most orthodox stand locations during the rut. These are simply natural terrain features that encourage deer to travel through confined spaces. Of course, these are crucial for bowhunters, but they serve as excellent stand locations for gun hunters, too.

Because they’re so effective, however, funnels and pinch-points draw attention from a lot of hunters. On public land and shared private properties, such spots can become pressured and overhunted. In these scenarios, study a parcel’s pressure levels to determine if these spots are worth your time.

2. Doe Bedding Area Fringes

Does usually use the same, or the same few, bedding areas throughout the year. Over time, bucks learn where these locations are. During the rut, they’ll visit these in search of estrus does. Hunters should know where these are located, too.

Hunting doe bedding can be tricky, though. As with hunting any bedding area, you must get in early — long before daylight. Settle in on the downwind side of bedding areas and intercept bucks cruising these edges as they scent check for does. If multiple bedding areas are connected by cover, set up along these routes as bucks travel from one to the next.

3. Upper-Third Ridge Benches

Topography plays a major role in deer movement. Specific features can influence how, when, where, and why deer traverse certain routes. One such feature is an upper-third bench. This is a bench that, as it sounds like, spans the higher part of a ridge — but not the top of the ridge itself. Because morning thermals rise, this is a common area to catch bucks cruising the first few hours of the day. They can smell potential does down below. Furthermore, bucks often prefer the leeward side of the ridge because they can smell from both directions.

4. Lower-Third Ridge Benches

Similarly, lower-third benches are important, too. These are more effective hunting locations later in the day. In the evening, and sometimes even earlier in the afternoon, thermals fall. As the evening air pulls downward, scent collects and pools in the lowest points of the landscape. That’s why bucks travel along lower-level benches to detect potential does that are traveling, or have recently traveled, along the ridge above.

Don’t be afraid to still-hunt along the downwind sides of peak-rut hotspots. Photo by Honeycutt Creative

5. Leeward Ridge Lines

Now, back to those leeward ridges I mentioned above. These are simply the downwind sides of ridges, and they’re excellent locations to intercept deer travel patterns. These serve as great bedding areas as well as travel routes for deer, which mean they’re especially great stand locations during peak rut. Whether deer are bedding there, or merely using these as travel routes, hunters can intercept them.

6. Saddles in the Hills

Another hill-country feature to hunt is the famous saddle, or a low spot between two ridges. It might be where the same continuous ridge dips down and rises back up. Or it might be where two ridges running in different directions taper and meet. Either way, a saddle allows deer to pass through from one side or the other without having to climb up and over. It’s a smart spot to lie in wait for when they do.

7. Overgrown Fields

Throughout the whitetail range, early successional habitat is vital to deer. Overgrown fields can provide that necessary edge cover. Oftentimes, these fill up with briars, brambles, grasses, and even forbs. Such growth might not look like much to us, but it provides good bedding cover and food sources. During the rut, bucks might even push estrus does into such areas to get them away from rival bucks.

8. Water Sources

Deer drink water year-round, but bucks get especially thirsty during the rut. All the cruising and chasing develops a need to rehydrate, and they frequent watering holes. Isolated water holes are especially attractive. Deer have limited options for finding water, particularly in dry conditions, and that increases the odds of shooting deer over one. You can hunt natural water sources or create your own, although creating these in huntable locations can require some serious strategy.

9. Quality Field-Based Food Sources

Deer spend most of their lives within cover, and that’s the best place to find a big deer. But during the rut, don’t overlook the power of a quality field-based food source. Standing soybeans, freshly cut corn, or any other crop with waist grain on the ground can be great. So can food plots, especially those planted with timely species. Hunting deer over food sources that are directly adjacent to quality bedding cover can produce sightings of a lot of deer, including mature bucks that are checking does or replenishing their energy.

10. Buck Bedding Areas

Buck bedding areas are incredibly productive spots throughout the early season, pre-rut, and late season. Don’t overlook these areas during the rut. Often really old bucks will continue bedding in the same areas they have been using all fall, and only search for does at night. If they don’t find a hot doe, they retreat to the bedding areas they feel safest in.

So, continue to monitor the edges of these areas with trail cameras, and move in to hunt as necessary if you think a buck is still bedding there. Keep in mind that bucks and does often bed within the same block, but bucks tend to bed at higher elevations or in areas that are more advantageous.

The author shot this Missouri buck along the edge of a food source with adjacent bedding. Photo by John DePalma

11. Scrape Lines

I know, scrapes, go cold during the peak, rut, right? Sure, scraping activity drops off. But they don’t completely shut down. Bucks continue to check scrapes, and so do does. Does, and bucks that are between does, continue checking scrapes. So it doesn’t hurt to consider their prcense as a secondary or tertiary element when weighing strand locations, especially if scrapes are nearby an area you already plan to hunt.

12. Random Brushy Cover

Bucks don’t like competition. Because of this, older deer that know what they’re doing tend to push does into random areas the remainder of the deer herd rarely uses. Often this includes random brushy areas. Maybe it’s a fencerow, thick sinkhole, patch of grass, or other obscure — but maybe visually obvious — location.

13. Small Pockets of Cover

Similarly, other small pockets of cover can be good, too. Even a half-acre, or even quarter-acre of cover can hold a breeding pair of deer. If it looks like a spot at least two deer could hide from other deer, and hunters, they might just be there. It might feel weird or unproductive to hunt these spots, but if you have good reason to, it just might pay off. Perhaps you saw a buck run into such a spot, maybe you didn’t. But during gun seasons, you should at least glass these areas or consider doing a deer drive through  these small pockets.

14. Drainage Ditches

Drainage ditches are always overlooked. After all, who wants to hunt a hole in the ground? But drainage ditches with the right characteristics can hold deer. At the very least, deer might travel across or through it. Ditch crossings can be good all season long, but during the rut, it’s not uncommon for bucks to push does into brushy areas within ditches.

15. Brush Piles

This is another area you wouldn’t expect a deer, but one worth considering. Brush piles with enough cover can hold a couple deer, and breeding pairs often shack up in such spots. Finding ways to hunt these can be difficult, but don’t overlook potential activity in such areas.
