Skilled trade jobs still have a stigma. That’s a problem for everyone

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Would you be as proud to tell someone your child is an electrician as you would be to say they are a business major? 

We rely on these workers to build our infrastructure, power our industries, and keep essential systems running. Yet we still talk about their careers as if they’re somehow less ambitious. 

The result? A persistent shortage of skilled-trade workers at a time when too few young people see technical careers as first-choice options. 

Many keep talking about this as a skill shortage. But there’s actually a mismatch between where demand is concentrated and the specific skills employers need most. From where I sit, we’re failing to connect people, skills, and opportunity.

I’ve spent more than three decades in the staffing industry, and it’s clear to me now more than ever that the skilled-trades stigma and the economic reality for these careers are increasingly at odds. And it couldn’t come at a worse time: Many young workers are looking to AI-proof their careers, and skilled trades could be a viable answer.

The problem isn’t that we encouraged people to go to college, but that we treated it as the default definition of long-term opportunity and earning potential.

The Skilled-Worker Shortage Isn’t as Simple as It Sounds

From August 2025 to August 2026, the construction sector added approximately 120,000 jobs, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Nonresidential specialty-trade contractors accounted for over 71% of those jobs. This growth is highly uneven geographically, with only 48% of U.S. metro areas adding construction jobs within that approximate time frame. Demand is increasingly tied to what is being built in a market, like data centers, power infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, or other major projects.

Here’s where it gets interesting. According to the 2026 Workforce Survey conducted by Associated General Contractors of America and the National Center for Construction Education and Research, 87% of construction firms reported openings for hourly skilled trade positions. Of those firms, 88% said those positions were as hard or harder to fill than a year earlier, with 42% reporting that worker or subcontractor shortages had delayed projects.

This tells us that even at a time when U.S. construction activity has softened overall, the availability of qualified skilled labor remains a constraint on contractors’ ability to get work done. This mismatch is even harder to solve when fewer young people aspire to enter these careers, leaving employers to compete for a smaller pool of workers who have the skills they need.

Employers Can’t Just Wait for Skilled Workers to Appear

What if employers stopped defining “qualified” as “already trained”? It’s time to consider alternative approaches, instead of the “find someone who already has all the skills we need” model. I challenge employers to look more seriously at hiring entry-level helpers and apprentices who can be developed on the job. Helpers are entry-level workers who assist experienced tradespeople with basic job site tasks while gaining hands-on experience, while apprentices follow a more structured training path that combines on-the-job experience with technical instruction.

We are beginning to see this shift happen, but it requires extra investment in training that not everyone can provide. While it requires more front-end work, developing the next generation is just as important as retaining experienced workers. If employers can’t find the workers they need, they have to become part of the process of creating them. 

A Helper Job Can Be the First Step, Not the Final Destination

In markets like Florida, electricians can progress from earning roughly $38,000 to $52,000 as apprentices to $75,000 to $95,000 or more as experienced journeypeople or leads, with opportunities to exceed six figures as they move into leadership or business ownership. 

The opportunity is not limited to workers who enter the industry fully trained. In the placements we see at TradeCorp, HireQuest’s skilled-trades staffing brand, an entry-level helper who might have started at $16 to $18 an hour a few years ago can now often start around $20 to $22, depending on the assignment and experience level. In Tampa, one electrician’s helper with 18 months of experience now earns $24 an hour, thanks to the development of critical pipe-bending skills.

It’s time for society’s definition of career success to keep pace with the economy, and for more employers to invest in recruiting, training, and retaining young talent.

My own father was a mason, and I saw firsthand that a skilled trade could provide a full, successful life. That experience shaped how I think about these careers.

AI may change how work gets done, but it can’t replace the people who build our world. From data centers to infrastructure, the demand for skilled tradespeople will remain critical, even as the broader construction market cools. For young workers weighing their future, the trades offer a path to build tangible skills and do work that can’t be automated.

If we want our country to keep growing, we need to make sure the next generation sees skilled trades for what they are: essential, ambitious careers worth building.

Harry Styles Revives His 2010 ‘X Factor’ Audition Look for ‘Isn’t She Lovely’ at Harryween

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Harry Styles went back to where it all started for Harryween.

At Madison Square Garden on Friday (Oct. 30), the 29th night of his 30-show Together, Together residency, Styles changed into the outfit he wore for his 2010 X Factor audition and performed Stevie Wonder’s “Isn’t She Lovely,” the song that got him through to the next round at 16. He even repeated a line from that day, telling the crowd, “I work in a bakery,” a nod to the part-time job he held in his Cheshire hometown of Holmes Chapel.

The audition outfit was a midshow swap. Styles opened the night dressed as Elvis Presley, while his band and dancers came as different versions of Styles from across his career. The Elvis nod tracks: the first song Styles ever recorded was a cover of Presley’s “The Girl of My Best Friend.”

Styles wasn’t the only one in costume. Aside from fans in the packed arena wearing their Halloween finery, Zoe Kravitz – to whom Styles is reportedly engaged – was dressed as a devil, wearing a red jacket and horns while dancing in the front row between Styles and the up-close-and-personal pit surrounding his in-the-round stage. Kravitz caught the show with Riley Keough, who sang along to “Watermelon Sugar” while Styles performed the Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 dressed up as her famous grandfather. Throughout the show, Styles periodically threw cheeky glances toward Kravitz, winking at her during “American Girls” and briefly cheesing in her direction during “Are You Listening Yet?”.

As for the X Factor audition back in the day, “Isn’t She Lovely” wasn’t Styles’ first pick. Footage released by the show in 2022 revealed he opened with Train’s “Hey, Soul Sister” and only sang the Wonder song a cappella after Simon Cowell asked to hear him without a backing track. He was later placed in One Direction alongside Niall Horan, Louis Tomlinson, Zayn Malik and the late Liam Payne. Earlier this month, Styles debuted “What Is Enough,” a song written for Payne, at the same venue on the second anniversary of Payne’s death.

Costumed covers have become a Harryween tradition. At the first edition, at MSG in 2021, Styles dressed as Dorothy to sing “Over the Rainbow,” then came out as a clown the next night to cover Britney Spears’ “Toxic.” In 2022, at Los Angeles’ Kia Forum, he dressed as Grease’s Danny Zuko for “Hopelessly Devoted to You.”

The residency wraps Saturday (Oct. 31) with a final Harryween show. The MSG run drew a record 11.5 million presale registrations on Ticketmaster. Styles has sent all four of his solo albums to No. 1 on the Billboard 200, most recently Kiss All the Time. Tame Impala Take On Seal’s ‘Kiss From a Rose’ for Like A Version

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Tame Impala got into the Halloween spirit a day early, covering Seal’s “Kiss From a Rose” for Australian radio station triple j’s Like A Version series on Friday (Oct. 30).

At triple j’s Sydney studios, the band reworked the 1994 ballad into a stripped-back, synth-driven take, then followed it with a pared-down version of their own hit “Dracula.”

For Kevin Parker, the cover was a trip back to the start. He explained that “Kiss From a Rose” was on the first CD he ever bought, the Batman Forever soundtrack, which also introduced him to acts including U2, the Flaming Lips and Nick Cave.

It’s Tame Impala’s fourth Like A Version cover, following OutKast’s “Prototype” in 2013, Kylie Minogue’s “Confide in Me” in 2015 and Edwyn Collins’ “A Girl Like You” in 2021.

The session caps the biggest year of the project’s career. “Dracula,” first released as a solo single in October 2025, took off after a February remix featuring Jennie, giving Tame Impala their first top 10 on the Billboard Hot 100 with a No. 5 peak. The song also reached No. 1 on Pop Airplay and Hot Rock & Alternative Songs, and spent more than six months atop Hot Dance/Electronic Songs. Tame Impala have just come off their biggest Australian tour to date, and their album Deadbeat leads next month’s ARIA Awards with eight nominations, tied with Genesis Owusu.

Tame Impala recently wrapped the Australian leg of their Deadbeat Tour, which featured multiple arena shows in Brisbane, Melbourne and Sydney before finishing with two hometown performances in Perth on Oct. 24 and 25. Parker and his band are set to return to the road in May 2027 for a string of UK and European arena dates, followed by several summer festival appearances.

Seal’s original topped the Billboard Hot 100 for a week in August 1995 after its Batman Forever placement and won three Grammys the following year, including record and song of the year.

France’s AI Strategy: Turning Models, Compute, and Public Investment Into National Capability

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

France's potential role in AI rests on its ability to organise a chain of capabilities. Model developers can produce useful technologies, computing institutions can provide infrastructure, and public administrations can establish sustained demand. National capacity grows when these activities become connected through arrangements that work under practical constraints. The decisive question is whether French institutions can secure the resources they need, adapt them to their purposes and remain responsible for the resulting decisions.

That question matters because the visible scale of an AI strategy can obscure its operational content. In February 2025, the Élysée reported €109 billion in announced investments associated with AI infrastructure and deployment projects. [1] This signalled commitments by investors. It was neither a measure of installed computing capacity nor a statement that the French state had already spent that amount. Assessing France requires following projects and access arrangements beyond the announcement.

Models that users can adapt

In the Invention layer, an important distinction concerns what users can actually obtain and modify. Mistral's documentation lists Ministral 3 8B, released in December 2025, as generally available under Apache 2.0. [2] An available model with permissive licensing gives developers a different set of options from a service accessible only through a proprietary interface. It can support adaptation and deployment decisions closer to the institution using it.

That possibility is not equivalent to a complete operating capability. An administration still needs suitable infrastructure, technical staff, evaluation procedures and authority to use the relevant data. A model that performs well on a general benchmark may require considerable work before it can support an administrative task reliably. The French opportunity is to develop those supporting capacities around models that institutions can understand, test and replace.

Mistral's August 2026 announcement provides a useful illustration of access becoming more specific. The company said its regional endpoints were generally available, allowing customers to select inference in Europe or the United States. It also disclosed that limited, safeguarded transfers to subprocessors outside the selected region could occur. [3] This is evidence of a defined service offering, with stated limits. A European processing location alone cannot establish complete control over the surrounding technical and contractual relationships.

Computing infrastructure under shared authority

Industrialisation requires infrastructure that can support demanding work at useful scale. Alice Recoque illustrates both the ambition and the institutional complexity. EuroHPC signed its procurement contract in November 2025. The planned system will be owned by EuroHPC and hosted in France, with GENCI managing the project and CEA operating the machine. The Jules Verne consortium includes the Netherlands and Greece, and computing resources are to be allocated jointly in proportion to investment. [4]

France therefore occupies a significant organisational position without acquiring exclusive national disposal of the system. The planned architecture also combines European components with AMD processors and accelerators. [4] European coordination can enlarge access and influence while retaining external technological dependencies. The relevant policy issue is whether those dependencies remain manageable through procurement, support and continuity arrangements.

Project timing is equally consequential. GENCI's current project page identifies the end of 2027 as the expected operational date. [5] This profile therefore treats Alice Recoque as contracted future capacity. It does not add its promised performance to a present French inventory. The distinction affects decisions by laboratories and public organisations that need resources before the new system becomes available.

The state as an organised user

Operationalisation becomes more tangible in Albert API. DINUM, France's interministerial digital directorate, operates this shared gateway to generative models. Its official description covers access to open-weight and proprietary models, alongside functions such as document classification, retrieval and optical character recognition. It identifies Albert API as an instance of the state-developed OpenGateLLM software. [6]

A shared interface can help administrations avoid rebuilding the same connections and can make changing providers less disruptive. Its strategic value depends on the surrounding institutional arrangements: which data may be processed, who evaluates performance, how errors are corrected and who can suspend a failing service. DINUM's page states that an approved version for internal state data is still being finalised. [6] Claims about universal readiness for sensitive public workloads would therefore exceed the evidence.

This is where governance connects Invention, Industrialisation and Operationalisation. The state can shape demand through procurement specifications and common infrastructure, while retaining responsibility for each public use. An architecture that permits model substitution, independent evaluation and continuity of service would make national capability less dependent on the fortunes of a single supplier. Achieving this requires administrative expertise as well as technical investment.

The evidence to watch

Four developments would make France's trajectory easier to judge. First, documented acceptance and access milestones for Alice Recoque would establish when contracted infrastructure becomes usable. Second, reports on completed and energised facilities would distinguish delivered investment from announcements. Third, Albert API's authorisation status and service records would clarify which workloads it can responsibly support. Fourth, evaluations of public deployments would reveal whether AI improves accuracy, processing times or service quality at an acceptable cost.

France could play a substantial European role by connecting model development, shared computing and public demand into dependable arrangements. The strongest evidence would be institutions that can continue to operate, evaluate and adapt their systems when suppliers, prices or political conditions change. That would demonstrate a capacity to mobilise AI for public purposes, extending the significance of the companies and infrastructure located within French territory.

This is original country analysis applying the GOAI capability conversion framework. It is not a peer-reviewed country assessment or a scored ranking. Proposed applications and indicators are analytical judgments; company and government claims are attributed to their sources.
