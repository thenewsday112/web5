Volkswagen Literally Can't Afford to Develop the Electric Golf Right Now

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Crisis Mode

Volkswagen went through very choppy waters in 2025, but 2026 has been anything but kind to Germany’s biggest automaker. In the third quarter of this year, it posted massive losses amounting to $6.6 billion. It posted profits of nearly $4 billion in the second quarter and $2.8 billion in the first quarter, but the most recent financial report reveals everything it had gained had practically been wiped out.

It’s been one problem after another for VW, as it needs to cut costs amidst plunging sales, the beating it’s getting in China, and resistance from workers’ unions due to mass layoffs. With the way things are going, projects are bound to be suspended, or worse, canceled altogether.

Getty

Electric Golf at Risk

With all of that going on, one project that has been put on ice is the electric Golf. Reuters noted VW Passenger Cars CEO Thomas Schäfer during the earnings call, saying, “I know what the Golf means for Volkswagen and for Wolfsburg. That’s why this decision was really not an easy one for us.”

Simply put, there simply isn’t enough money to continue the car’s development. Beyond the losses, another reason it’s been put on ice is that VW couldn’t hit its cost-cutting target, falling short by $336 million. VW could’ve carried on, or at least slowed, the electric Golf’s development if it had achieved that number, but that’s not the case right now.

This doesn’t mean the project is canceled outright. However, the electric Golf is at greater risk of becoming a stillborn model if VW can’t pull itself out of the rut it’s stuck in right now. On the flip side, if things do look up, perhaps a little work goes into it, but it likely won’t be of the highest priority.

Volkswagen

Just When Things Were Looking Up (Sort Of)

It’s a major blow to the automaker’s EV push, especially given that its subcompact EVs are actually making headway. The ID. Polo has already raked in a whole lot of orders, and the reviews so far are actually positive. That’s something that couldn’t be said about VW’s initial attempts at mass-market EVs. With the way things are going, the ID. Cross is poised to be a hit for the brand in Europe.

The electric Golf, likely to be called the ID. Golf, could have carried the momentum that the ID. Polo and ID. Cross have been gathering, but alas, it won’t be the case right away. That’s a bit of a shame given that it was being hyped up just a few months ago. Based on the previous report, it will follow the form of the MK. 4 Golf, and it will have a button-rich cabin like the subcompact EVs.

The ID. Golf does, indeed, sound promising, but we won’t know what it would be like for the next couple of years, and that’s if development for it pushes through. Right now, though, there are more urgent matters that VW has to attend to before moving forward.

Arsenal will have a few key injury updates ahead of their clash with Liverpool on Sunday, but Mikel Arteta still has some tough choices to make in his front line.

Declan Rice, Riccardo Calafiori and Christos Tzolis have returned to training so there is hope they can play in the Premier League clash against Anfield.

Bukayo Saka is still out with a quad injury, but Noni Madueke looks to be fit to go into the final game. Max Dowman is also suspended after Arsenal were unable to overturn his red card suspension after the defeat to Fleetwood in the Carabao Cup.

However, Arteta has some good options to choose from. He can ill afford any more injuries in attack.

Arsenal could welcome three players back against Liverpool

Rice’s return will be key after missing the midweek cup clash.

The midfielder was subbed in the win against Everton with a knock. His absence was a blow against Fleetwood.

He trained on Friday. As long as he does another session on Saturday, he will be available. Rice is a big boost for Arsenal in the middle of the park as they face a Liverpool side who have been in good form recently.

Calafiori is also edging closer to a return. The Italian has played in the last seven Premier League games for Arsenal but missed against Everton.

However, he was only on for a short period when he came on as a sub. But he seemed to have an issue. Arteta said that it didn’t seem like a big problem, but they’re taking some caution.

Like Rice, Calafiori trained on Friday and will be able to go to Merseyside if he finishes his session on Saturday.

Tzolis is another possible addition. The winger has missed Arsenal’s last five matches after suffering a hamstring injury while playing for Greece in October.

His latest scan showed encouraging progress, and he has now returned to training. A further successful session would put him in contention for Sunday’s match.

Madueke and Dowman leave Arteta short out wide

The main issue for Arteta will be his lack of attacking width. 

Madueke did not join the squad for the trip to Fleetwood due to an ankle injury sustained in the Everton game. He did not train on Monday, but Arteta has not ruled him out of the game against Liverpool. He will have to finish his session on Saturday. 

Dowman is also missing due to suspension. He was sent off in the 1-1 draw against Fleetwood after a challenge from Harrison Neal. His appeal was rejected by the FA, so he will miss the game against Liverpool. 

Saka makes it even worse for the Gunners. The England international has a quad injury from the Bayern Munich game and is likely to be out for several weeks. 

Arteta confirmed that he will need another scan before there is a clearer timeline for his return but is likely to be out for a long period of time.

Saliba recovery offers longer-term encouragement

William Saliba is still missing from the squad, having aggravated a back injury during his time playing for France at the World Cup this summer.

But the defender has returned to doing one-on-one training on the training ground.

Arsenal will perform an MRI scan to monitor his progress. Arteta has suggested there have been positive signs, and he is expected back in December.

The main talking point will be Rice, Calafiori and Tzolis returning to training ahead of Sunday.

It would give Arsenal a nice boost with all three players fit. Madueke is still a doubt and they have fewer options going forward with Saka and Dowman out.

'RHONY' Alum Leah McSweeney Drops Lawsuit Against Andy Cohen and Bravo

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Real Housewives of New York City alum Leah McSweeney has dropped her lawsuit against Bravo and Housewives executive producer Andy Cohen.

McSweeney, 44, voluntarily withdrew her petition in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, according to court documents obtained by People. The Bravo alum dismissed her claims with prejudice, meaning she cannot file another lawsuit with the same allegations.

The terms of the dismissal were not publicly disclosed. Us has reached out to McSweeney and Bravo for comment.

“We are extremely satisfied with the outcome [and] we are proud of Leah and her perseverance,” McSweeney’s lawyer, Gary Adelman, told Page Six in a Friday, October 30, statement.

What to Know About 'RHONY' Alum Leah McSweeney's Lawsuit Against Andy Cohen

McSweeney, who appeared on RHONY during seasons 12 and 13 and Real Housewives: Ultimate Girls Trip season 3, named Bravo and Cohen, 58, in a 2024 lawsuit. She claimed that production allegedly encouraged her to relapse after years of sobriety.

“Your favorite Bravo shows are run by people who create a dangerous work environment, encourage substance abuse to artificially create drama and cynically prey on the vulnerabilities of their employees,” McSweeney alleged in a February 2024 statement shared via Instagram. “It’s a workplace culture where toxicity, alcoholism and pain are not only expected but encouraged and facilitated.”

In her legal filing, McSweeney further claimed that Cohen “engaged in cocaine use” with multiple Bravolebrities and allegedly rewarded participants with “more favorable treatment and edits.”

Both Cohen and Bravo, meanwhile, vehemently denied the accusations — and tried to dismiss McSweeney’s lawsuit on several occasions.

Andy Cohen Responds to Leah McSweeney Lawsuit Claims

“What I’ll say about the alcohol is that we have so many sober people and people who have gotten sober on the show, like Countess Luann [de Lesseps],” Cohen told The Hollywood Reporter in May 2024. “We have people who’ve never had a drink during the entire run of the show: Jill Zarin, Kandi Burruss [and] Heather Dubrow will just have an occasional glass of champagne.”

The TV host continued at the time, “So sure, there are people who drink. There are many people who never drink. We don’t force anyone to do anything. But no one is secretly hiding liquor bottles on set. That’s ridiculous. We’ve been very supportive of people’s sobriety.”

Bravo conducted an investigation into McSweeney’s allegations that same year, ultimately clearing Cohen of misconduct.

“The outside investigation into the recent allegations … has now been completed, and the claims were found to be unsubstantiated,” a Bravo rep stated at the time.

Who are Ilhan Omar’s lawyers? Meet the high-powered Dem attorneys the embattled rep hired

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., has tapped two high-powered Washington, D.C.-based attorneys who have previously represented elected officials at the highest echelon of American politics.

Amid dueling federal investigations — one for immigration fraud and another for financial disclosure discrepancies — the embattled Somali-born lawmaker hired Amy Jeffress and Joshua Matz, according to a CBS report, both of whom are partners at Hecker Fink LLP.

The attorneys have in the past represented Democrat clients and taken on left-leaning cases — especially those taking aim at President Donald Trump, his administration and his supporters.

Jeffress is a former federal prosecutor whose biography says she tried more than 40 cases during a decade in the U.S. Attorney’s Office in the District of Columbia, where she was Chief of the National Security Section. She has also been a Department of Justice (DOJ) Attaché.

She served as former President Joe Biden's attorney in his lawsuit against the DOJ earlier this year. In that case, Biden sought to halt the release of audio recordings between himself and his "Promise Me, Dad" book ghostwriter, Mark Zwonitzer. The audio was recorded in 2016 and 2017, and were subject to a FOIA request from the Heritage Foundation in 2024.

They were cited during special counsel Robert Hur's investigation into whether Biden illegally retained classified information from his term as vice president, and piqued the interest of conservatives demanding answers about Biden's mental acuity.

The court declined to block the release of the recordings where Biden ad frequent memory lapses, and they were made public in July.

Before her time at Hecker Fink, Jeffress, a Yale Law graduate from 1992, served as counsel for then-House Speaker Nancy Pelosi and the Democrat-led House January 6 committee, which spent two years investigating the riot at the U.S. Capitol on January 6, 2021. She represented the committee when it was sued by a Trump campaign spokesperson, Taylor Budowich, after the committee subpoenaed his bank records.

Jeffress also represented Trump foe and former FBI attorney Lisa Page — a key figure in the left's Russiagate conspiracy theory — during congressional scrutiny into the FBI's handling of the matter. Page later sued the DOJ for releasing her text messages with her then-FBI colleague and lover Peter Strzok.

In the messages, Strzok assuaged Page's concerns that Trump would become president by vowing that the FBI would stop it, and referred to an "insurance policy" against his presidency. That case was settled in 2024.

Kennedy Performing Arts Center to add Trump's name. Trump publicly criticized Cooper as "highly conflicted," due to his relationship with Jeffress.

Matz is also a partner at Hecker Fink. He graduated from Harvard Law, and clerked for former Supreme Court Justice Anthony Kennedy.

"Joshua’s practice has long included a focus on democracy and elections," his biography page says.

He represented the state of Pennsylvania in defending the certification of its election results, including arguing before the Supreme Court.

In 2022, Carroll accused Trump of defaming her in his denial of her account of an incident she claims happened in 1996. A New York jury found Trump liable for defamation and for sexual assault.

Prior to that case, in 2019, a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million for defamation stemming from other comments made by Trump.

He also successfully represented Democratic Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear when he was sued for COVID-19 lockdowns, including school closures, and victims of the 2017 "Unite the Right" rally in Charlottesville.
