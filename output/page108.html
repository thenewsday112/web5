Review: Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai (DXB – Concourse A)

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

I returned to the Emirates First Class Lounge in Dubai’s Concourse A before my flight to Brussels. The lounge is virtually unchanged since my earlier visits, so rather than repeat the entire review, I’ll share my latest experience and updated photos below. My original detailed review follows.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Review (DXB – Concourse A)

I arrived early to visit both the Concourse A and Concourse B first class lounges, but this one is significantly larger and the “flagship” first class lounge.

Hours + Access + Location

This lounge is open 24/7 and located in Concourse A of DXB beyond security and passport control. Access is available to:

  • Emirates First Class passengers
  • Emirates Skywards Platinum members
    • Members may bring one adult guest and two children under 17 traveling on the same flight.
  • Qantas Platinum and Platinum One members
    • Complimentary access when traveling on an eligible Qantas or Emirates flight
  • Passengers with Emirates Business Class Lounge access
    • Upgrade to the First Class Lounge for $150 per person for Skywards members or $175 for nonmembers, plus 5% UAE VAT. That brings the totals to $157.50 and $183.75, respectively
  • Other eligible paying passengers
    • First Class Lounge access costs $250 for Skywards members or $300 for nonmembers, plus 5% VAT, totaling $262.50 or $315

Paid access is subject to availability and covers one visit for up to four hours before scheduled departure. Check Emirates’ current eligibility and pricing before your trip.

Seating

You really feel like you are in a separate terminal…especially when you have your own duty free shop, wine shop, and Costa Coffee. It’s massive and whether you head left or right from the entrance, it seems to go on and on. I’ve been in this lounge several times over the years and it never gets crowded.

(Yes, all these pictures are from inside the lounge!)

Smoking

A large designated smoking area offers an oasis for smokers. Cigars are available too. I appreciate that unlike the other first class lounges, smoking here is actually in a separate area with walls/doors.

Showers + Restrooms

Shower suites include toilets and I had no wait to use the shower, which featured Voya shower products (from Ireland).

Dining + Drinks

You’ll find plenty of food options in this lounge, including a full buffet, bakery, the aforementioned coffee shops, and a restaurant with sit-down a la carte dining.

My Delhi Belly had finally passed and I was hungry and ready for a good meal.

Although it was only 5:00 am, filet mignon is always available and I had a declines bowl of split pea soup and a filet mignon with French fries. It was perfect…well, almost. Medium rare was not allowed, but I got medium.

Charging

Universal A/C outlets, wireless charging, and even a dedicated charging station are available throughout the lounge.

Quiet Area

While this lounge does not have private bedrooms like the Qatar Airways Al Safwa First Class Lounge, there is a quiet area with comfortable chairs and blankets.

Newspapers + Magazines

I loved that there was a section of “real” newspapers and magazines from around the world, including my own LA Times flown in from Los Angeles.

Children’s Play Room

For families with children, there’s a large playroom with many activities to keep the little ones occupied.

Spa

There’s a spa attached to the lounge and first class passengers enjoy complimentary 15 minute treatments (I had a “de-stress” back massage). A full menu of spa options is also available.

Service

Despite the cavernous size of this lounge, I was frequently asked by lounge attendants if I needed anything. The service in the restaurant was excellent and I grateful the chef agreed to make me some pea soup even though it wasn’t on the breakfast menu.

Original Emirates First Class Lounge Concourse A Review — 2017

The review below documents my earlier visits. Photos, menus, and observations in this section are from 2017 and as you’ll see, not much has changed…

The Emirates First Class Lounge in the A Concourse of Dubai (DXB) is a sight to behold: it feels more like being in a private terminal.

I’m merging two visits into one for this review. I’m in the midst of a oneworld first class trip report and while that may seem an odd place to insert an Emirates First Class Lounge review, the close partnership of Qantas and Emirates meant I had access to the lounge during my transit in Dubai. Qantas Flight 1, the flagship Sydney to London service, stops in Dubai to change crews.

My second visit was just a week later, an overnight layover as I flew from Los Angeles to Frankfurt via Dubai in Emirates First Class.

Signage to the lounge is clearly marked. If transiting, you’ll need to re-clear security and proceed upstairs to the lounge, one level above the business class lounge.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai is Massive

After checking in, you walk past the desks and into what appears to be another large terminal. To your right is seating, to your left are duty free shops. But this is all part of the lounge.

Have a walk around. It will take you a good five minutes to walk from one side of the lounge to the other.

You’ll also find convenient charging stations around the lounge:

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Dining

Dining is such a treat in the lounge. Anywhere you have a seat an attendant will come up to you and offer you a choice of beverage. My go-to drink in the lounge is a berry smoothie.

Throughout the lounge there are buffets with light snacks, beverages, as well as a full bar. If you really want to indulge in something special, there is a wine shop that will sell pricey bottles of wine. My advice: the free stuff is more than adequate.

Cool coffee machines, though attendants are happy to make you coffee from scratch–

The true dining highlight, however, is the restaurant. The buffet is nice enough, though hardly unique. What I loved, however, was the comprehensive selection and high quality of the a la carte menu. I sat down to eat at 4am (5pm LA time). The kitchen had already transitioned to breakfast but was happy to accommodate my supper request.

I do try to limit my carbs, but it was hard to resist the delicious bread in the lounge paired with my favorite French butter in the world (both salted and unsalted).

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Spa

First class passengers receive a complimentary 15-minute spa treatment. During my overnight stay I was offered an appointment at the time of my choosing and took it just before eating the meal above.

I chose a back and shoulder massage. It was fine, but not memorable. Tips are not expected, but appreciated.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Restrooms and Showers

The lounge has several bathrooms and a central shower area. I did take a shower before my flight to Frankfurt and was surprised that the shower did not appear all that clean (check out the soap scum *hopefully* on the tile floor of the shower).

Amenities were available as well–

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Smoking Room

If you’re a smoker (I’m not), the lounge features a beautiful smoking room (itself larger than many airline lounges) with a private bar (and bartenders).

Emirates First Class Lounge Sleeping Room

Jetlagged and wide awake, I never used the sleeping area despite the overnight stay in the lounge. If you do need to rest, it’s not like the private rooms at the Qatar’s Al Safwa lounge, but does offer a better sleep experience than the Etihad First Class Lounge in Abu Dhabi:

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Service

Service sparkled in the lounge. Every employee interaction was professional and friendly. On my way out I had my shoes shined and was amazed at what a tremendous job the man did. It is easy to get lost in this huge lounge, yet the staff unobtrusively yet effectively keeps watch over you.

Don’t forget, you can directly board your flight from the lounge if leaving from the A Gates:

CONCLUSION

What else can I say other than this is an amazing lounge? I actually do like both Qatar’s Al-Safwa and Etihad’s First Class lounge better, but this is quite a bronze lounge. I enjoyed both visits and hope to return again soon.

The challenging housing environment remained front and center as Green Brick Partners, Meritage Homes, and Dream Finders Homes reported third-quarter results.

Rising mortgage rates and affordability constraints continued to pressure consumer sentiment and margins. Green Brick Partners grew orders and deliveries despite the difficult backdrop, while Meritage Homes prioritized margins over a higher sales pace. Dream Finders Homes achieved record third-quarter closings while absorbing costs associated with its acquisition of Beazer Homes.

Quarter by the Numbers

  • Green Brick Partners: Net new-home orders increased 3% year over year to 926, while deliveries also increased 3% to 978 homes. Home closing revenue declined 9.7% to $437 million as the average sales price of delivered homes fell 12.1% to $446,700. Home building gross margin was 28%, compared with 32.1% a year earlier. The builder reported net income attributable to Green Brick Partners of $59.2 million, or $1.35 per diluted share, compared with $77.9 million, or $1.77 per diluted share, in the prior-year period.
  • Meritage Homes: Orders declined 15% year over year to 3,083, while closings fell 14% to 3,187. Home closing revenue declined 17% to $1.16 billion as the average closing price decreased 4% to $365,000. Home closing gross margin was 17.4%, down 170 basis points from 19.1% a year earlier. Meritage Homes reported net earnings of $67.8 million, or $1.04 per diluted share, compared with $99.3 million, or $1.39 per diluted share, a year ago. The company ended the quarter with 348 communities, up 4% year over year.
  • Dream Finders Homes: Home closings increased 3% to a third-quarter record of 1,967, while home building revenue increased 3% to $942 million. Net sales totaled 1,712, down from 2,021 a year earlier, though net sales excluding built-for-rent activity increased 6%. Home building gross margin declined to 14.9% from 17.5%. Net income attributable to Dream Finders Homes was $7 million, compared with $47 million a year earlier. Excluding $23 million of Beazer Homes acquisition costs, net income attributable to the company was $27 million, or $0.22 per basic share.

What They’re Saying

“Despite continued affordability pressures, broader economic uncertainty, and an increasing rate environment, demand across our markets remained resilient as buyers responded to our incentive strategy and attractive product offerings, particularly within Trophy Signature Homes. We continue to respond to deteriorating market conditions to maintain sales pace.”
— Jim Brickman, co-CEO and co-founder, Green Brick Partners

“In the third quarter of 2026, we navigated an increasingly challenging home buying environment marked by rising mortgage interest rates and persistent geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainty. Constrained affordability weighed on consumer sentiment, which was reflected in our 15% year-over-year decline in third quarter orders despite achieving an average absorption pace of 3.0 net sales per month per store.”
— Steven J. Hilton, executive chairman, Meritage Homes  

“Buyers faced a challenging summer, as rising mortgage rates further strained affordability and weighed on consumer confidence. I’m proud of how our team navigated these market conditions. We closed 1,967 homes in the quarter, a third-quarter record, and 6,127 homes year-to-date, also a record, while net sales increased 6% compared with the prior year.”
— Patrick Zalupski, founder, co-chairman and CEO, Dream Finders Homes

Since quarter-end, Dream Finders Homes completed its approximately $2.2 billion acquisition of Beazer Homes. Zalupski said the company expects additional nonrecurring transaction and integration costs during the fourth quarter as it works toward $100 million in targeted cost savings from the combination. The company reiterated its full-year 2026 guidance of approximately 9,250 home closings.

Eddie Aikau 2026/27 Invite List Drops as John John Florence, Kai Lenny and Landon McNamara Target Historic Waimea Bay Swell

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Surfing’s most exclusive guest list just dropped. And getting an invitation is only half the battle.

On Thursday, October 29, title sponsor Rip Curl unveiled the 32 surfers invited to the 2026/2027 Eddie Aikau Big Wave Invitational, along with 25 alternates. The lineup reads like a who’s who of big-wave surfing, all hoping for one thing: a historically massive day at Waimea Bay.

See the full list of invitees, linked here, and below.

View this post on Instagram

Among the invitees are John John Florence, Nathan Florence, Kai Lenny, Jamie O’Brien, Mason Ho, Bruce Irons, Billy Kemper, and former champions Luke Shepardson and Ross Clarke-Jones. Ha’a Aikau, a member of Eddie’s family, also earned a spot. On the women’s side, Paige Alms, Annie Reickert, Emily Erickson, and Felicity Palmateer received invitations.

And then there’s defending champion Landon McNamara.

The North Shore local won the last Eddie on December 22, 2024, thanks in part to a perfect 50-point wave. Following his historic victory, McNamara told SURFER, “To have my name amongst all those legends, that’s the best part of it all.”

But receiving an invite doesn’t guarantee the surfers will ever paddle out.

Related: It’s Official: The Eddie Is OFF for 2025/26 Season

The Eddie has one of the toughest green-light requirements in sports: wave faces must reach 40 feet, with high-quality conditions holding for a full day of competition. No exceptions. That’s why, since its inception in 1985, the contest has run just 11 times.

More than a competition, The Eddie honors legendary Hawaiian waterman and Waimea Bay lifeguard Eddie Aikau, who was lost at sea in 1978 while trying to save fellow crew members aboard the voyaging canoe Hōkūleʻa.

This season’s waiting period runs December 6, 2026, through March 4, 2027, following an opening ceremony on December 3. Former Eddie champion Keone Downing will serve as contest director.

As Downing put it, echoing his father’s famous words, “The Bay calls the day.”

Now, everyone waits for Waimea to make the call.

Appeals court reverses rejection of damages award against NFL in Sunday Ticket class action

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

This is the kind of Friday afternoon bad news dump that counts as horrible news for the NFL.

Via Mike Scarcella of Reuters, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit has reinstated the $4.7 billion verdict in the Sunday Ticket class action.

The Reuters article, however, apparently has misread the decision. Mark Maske of Front Office Sports characterizes the outcome as ordering a new trial on damages.

"The jury clearly awarded damages based on its improper consideration of the evidence rather than according to the district court’s instructions," the Ninth Circuit wrote, per Maske. "This warrants a new trial on damages. We remand this case for the district court to reinstate the jury’s liability verdict and hold a new damages trial.”

A new trial on damages could result in a lower (or similar or, in theory, higher) final verdict.

The original award, given by a jury in July 2024, was scrapped by the presiding judge over concerns regarding the evidence the plaintiffs presented of economic harm. The judge did not throw out the finding that the NFL had violated federal antitrust laws in the pricing of the Sunday Ticket package.

If/when any verdict becomes an official judgment, the damages will be tripled.

The original verdict sent shock waves through the league, since it ultimately would require each team to come up with $440 million in cash. Statutory interest will push that number even higher.

The NFL undoubtedly will appeal the final decision to the U.S. Supreme Court, which would first have to decide whether to accept the case before issuing a decision. The case possibly would not be ripe for an appeal to the Supreme Court until a new trial on damages happens — if that's what the Ninth Circuit has indeed ordered.

Whatever happens next, the situation instantly becomes the most consequential piece of litigation in the 100-plus-year history of the league.
