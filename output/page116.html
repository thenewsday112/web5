Bayern Munich are still interested in signing Rio Ngumoha.

Gianluca Di Marzio now reports that the Bavarian giants will try to make a push for the teenage gem in the upcoming January transfer window.

Bayern Munich could do with Rio Ngumoha after Luis Diaz injury

(Photo by Carl Recine/Getty Images)

The simple fact of the matter for FC Bayern is that their season – or, to put it more accurately, the fate of their Champions League campaign – may hinge on how they solve their left wing concern.

Luis Diaz, without question, is a sensational talent, but his hamstring injury (picked up in the 2-2 draw against Arsenal) raises questions around the security of the position long term.

Hypothetically, were the reigning Bundesliga champions to find themselves deep in European competition, they’re unlikely to want to be forced to use either Tim Binder or Alphonso Davies. Serge Gnabry can also cover the left flank, if need be, but this is a far from perfect solution.

So, if anyone could do with an extremely talented, 18-year-old left winger whose long-term pathway wouldn’t be obstructed by a senior starter (in this case, 29-year-old Diaz) – it’s most definitely Bayern.

“It’s true that Bayern Munich want Rio Ngumoha, and it’s true that Liverpool want to extend his contract – so it’s a difficult situation because anything is possible,” Di Marzio told Wettfruende.

“Liverpool are pushing to extend his contract and keep him in the team in the future, but it’s also true that Bayern Munich will try to sign him as early as January.”

The journalist went on to add: “I think it’s not a very high probability, but a good one that he will extend his contract, as he enjoys his time at Liverpool and has a good relationship with the manager; therefore, it’s hard to imagine Liverpool letting him go.

“FC Bayern Munich is pushing for it, and Arsenal is also interested.”

Arsenal shouldn’t be of concern to Liverpool

(Photo by Leonhard Simon/Getty Images)

We’re pretty confident that Rio Ngumoha will commit his immediate and long-term future to Liverpool with a contract extension.

The Englishman made clear that he chose life at Anfield over staying at Chelsea due to the pathway that was offered to him at L4, telling ITV Sport earlier in October: “Liverpool was always the right option – and the only option really.

“[I thought]: ‘If I go here I’ll get the opportunity and the pathway to showcase what I can do’. It looks like so far it’s paid off!”

After the 2-1 win over Brentford, the winger suggested that talks between his agent and the club were going well.

Yet, here we are, with rumours swirling around Arsenal and Bayern Munich. If we’re talking about a pathway to more regular first team minutes – we’re not particularly worried about Arsenal. We just can’t see the Gunners offering Ngumoha a better deal in that regard.

The concern for us is whether the teenager looks at the current state of play in English football – and the joy talents like Diaz and Michael Olise have had since departing – and opts for life in a less demanding league. Or, to put it differently, one in which he can operate without being kicked around quite as much.

In that sense, it would be naïve of us to just assume that Liverpool is definitely the “better” project compared to Vincent Kompany’s Bayern Munich.

If anything, the current climate, coupled with Harry Kane’s Ballon d’Or win in London, indicates that life in Germany isn’t quite so bad.

How did the biggest crypto heist in history just happen?

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Binance is the biggest cryptocurrency exchange ZUMA Press Wire/Shutterstock

The world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange Binance has been hit with the biggest ever cryptocurrency heist, in which billions of dollars of reserves were drained in just 20 minutes.

From 8:05pm on 29 October, $6.36 billion in cryptocurrencies were shifted from wallets in a coordinated attack believed to have originated from a North Korean-linked hacking unit called TraderTraitor. This group was also behind a $1.5 billion hack of the Bybit crypto exchange in February 2025.

The US Treasury has previously said that such stolen funds are used to finance North Korea’s nuclear missile programme.

Advertisement

Despite cryptocurrency’s founding idea of decentralisation, meaning no one has singular control over the money flowing through the network, there are some large single points of failure. It is thought one of these was the cause of the Binance hack, which gave access to a system the exchange uses to authorise transfers out of customers’ accounts.

Quantum computer quickly mines cryptocurrency while using less energy

In a statement, Binance said its early investigations indicate the security incident may stem from a compromised laptop belonging to an engineer. TRM Labs, a cybersecurity firm, suggests that preparations for the attack may have begun several days ago.

Binance claims it has found no evidence that the private cryptographic keys used to authorise transactions were stolen. Instead, the attackers appear to have gained access to the software that requests withdrawals, potentially allowing them to instruct Binance’s own systems to approve the transfers. However, the investigation is continuing and the firm has said in a subsequent statement there may have been “additional points of breach”.

“It shouldn’t be possible for a single [developer] PC to be compromised and somehow for that to allow the entire exchange to be compromised,” says Alan Woodward at the University of Surrey, UK. “I don’t understand how that can happen.”

If that is the case, it highlights how precarious major financial hubs in the crypto world can be to single points of failure.

“You can build a vault with mathematical firewalls, but if the individual holding the keys can be duped, the vault is opened,” says Clive Summerfield at FARx, a cybersecurity company. “Most crypto platforms are as centralised as any bank, with a small number of privileged individuals controlling billions. The difference is that once crypto is transferred, it’s gone.”

That centralisation can be a blessing as well as a curse, though: cryptocurrencies Tether and Circle have been able to freeze billions of dollars of stolen coins, stymying the hackers in attempts to transfer about half the stolen funds any further.

Woodward says the Binance incident potentially strikes a distinction between crypto security and more traditional finance. “You would hope there’d be things like two-man rules [where two separate people have to approve a change] or things that banks have for changing critical systems, but it doesn’t look like those were in place.”

When paid social performance begins to decline, your instinct might be to create more ads.

That usually means a new variation of the existing creative, often with a different headline, a static image, or even a user-generated content (UGC) video that looks similar to the previous one.

You launch the new ads, wait for the algorithm to decide a winner, and then wonder why performance barely moves.

The problem is that you need more creative variables. There’s a meaningful difference between creative volume and creative diversity. You can have 20 ads running and still be testing the same idea in 20 different ways.

Creative diversity isn’t about developing an arbitrary number of assets. Instead, it’s about giving the platform and your team genuinely different inputs to learn from.

Creative diversity feeds advertising algorithms

It’s important for advertisers to feed the algorithm more signals. Meta increasingly relies on automation to determine which users should see your ads in an effort to achieve your campaign goals and respond to your optimization settings.

Broad targeting and automated, streamlined campaign structures have gotten more powerful because of the seemingly unlimited pool of behavioral data that ad platforms can reference when making delivery decisions.

Yet the algorithm can only optimize against the creative inputs you give it. If all your ads communicate the same messages and use minimal variations, you limit the number of hypotheses the system can explore. Testing a red background versus a yellow one won’t move the needle much.

Ultimately, different people can respond to the same product for completely different reasons, so personas also play a large role. One person may care about the price, while another may care more about convenience or a specific outcome. Give the system enough strong creative variations to discover which messages resonate with which people so the algorithm has more opportunities to make that match.

Aim for balanced creative testing

Making more diverse creatives doesn’t have to be a chaotic process. You don’t have to launch every idea that comes up or change everything at once.

Strategy is crucial because you need a clear understanding of your customers, your offering, and your business objective. You also need enough data to make your tests meaningful.

Aim for a balanced approach. Consider the number of meaningful creative variations you need relative to your ad spend and objectives.

A brand spending $2,000 per month doesn’t need the same creative testing structure as another brand spending $100,000 per month. There’s no universal number of ads that every account should run. The right number depends on budget, conversion volume, audience size, product capacity, and how quickly your account can generate useful data.

Build a creative portfolio, not a content treadmill

The strongest ad accounts treat creative as a portfolio of hypotheses. You may have some proven concepts that you continue developing, emerging concepts that you’re actively testing, and brand-new ideas that challenge your assumptions and break some best practices.

With this approach, you create a healthier creative system than you would with ads replaced one at a time. When media and creative teams successfully work together, the strategic conversation should be around what you’re seeing in the data and what creative variables you should introduce next.

Use creative testing to experiment with more ideas

Multiple ads can still represent a single idea. Take a brand with 10 user-generated content (UGC) videos, for example. Each one features a slightly different edit and shows a different creator wearing a different outfit.

But every video opens with the same type of hook, talks about the same product benefits, demonstrates the product in the same way, mentions the same offer, or targets the same customer motivation outlined in the brief.

Strategically, these videos represent 10 creative assets. But they aren’t 10 unique ideas.

Creative testing should introduce meaningful variations across elements to influence how someone responds to an ad. Here are some elements to test:

  • Hooks: What earns attention in the first three seconds?
  • Messages: What are you saying?
  • Angles: What reason are you giving your audience to care?
  • Creators: Who is delivering the message?
  • Formats: How are you delivering the message?
  • Visuals: What does your audience see?
  • Offers: What incentive are you presenting to the viewer?
  • Proof: Why should the viewer believe you?
  • Tone: Is the creative entertaining, emotional, educational, aspirational, or direct?
  • Context: Are you showing the product in a home, studio, real-life situation, office, or somewhere unexpected?

Don’t change all of these variables in every creative test. Change one at a time so you can understand what’s working (or not).

Think of creative in terms of concepts

A creative asset is a finished piece of content, and a concept is the strategic idea behind it. If your brand makes 12 videos, you might think you’re creating 12 new assets. In reality, they might represent two concepts, two creators, three hooks, and one core message.

A strong concept may deserve several executions. But you need to be clear about exactly what you’re testing.

If your goal is to understand why performance is changing, counting assets doesn’t tell you much. Instead, map your creative by the variables you’re testing. Here’s how:

While it’s helpful to have options, you don’t need to create every possible combination. The point is to build a portfolio where your creative isn’t saying the same thing over and over. This gives the algorithm more variety, which in turn can yield more and better results for your brand.

Which ad should you make next?

To decide on your next creative experiment, ask yourself which variables you haven’t tested yet. Suppose all your creatives lead with a product benefit. Test starting with the problem first, delaying the product reveal by a few seconds.

Or if your ads are product-focused, test lifestyle creatives. If every ad features polished brand content, seek out some creators and test creator content. If every ad has a serious tone, try humor if your brand can find an appropriate angle.

Instead of targeting a number of ads, think in terms of needing to test three new angles, two new formats, and several new hooks against the concepts that you see are already working.

Your winning ads should influence more tests

A winning ad should serve as the beginning of the next round of testing. For example, say you see that one hook significantly outperforms the others. Take time to assess why it worked.

Was it the visual? Or the creator, the hook, the customer problem it addresses, or the message?

Once you have an idea, build new hypotheses around those insights. Take a winning hook and test different angles.

If it was a winning angle, test different creators. If the message was the winner, introduce additional evidence, such as testimonials or demonstrations.

Avoid starting from scratch each time you need a new ad for your testing mix. Take what you learned and use it to generate your next, more informed hypothesis.

Treat creative variables as more ways to learn

The next time you think you need more creative, don’t immediately focus on how many new assets you should produce. Instead, assess what you’re trying to learn. Will you need new hooks, creators, customer motivations, offers, or ways to demonstrate your product?

Maybe you do need more ads. But if those ads aren’t introducing meaningful new variables, the volume may not give you particularly valuable insights.

The best creative strategies are built around developing enough variations to uncover what works, what doesn’t, and why. From there, you can build on those learnings. That’s the difference between making more creative and having a true creative strategy.

Mexico City has broken a Guinness World Record during its preparations for this year’s Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos) celebrations. At nearly 110 feet tall, the colorful sculpture of a female skeleton currently standing in the capital city’s town square (the Zócalo) is officially the world’s tallest La Catrina monument. The roughly 11-story project made from steel and fiberglass required about 140 people to assemble, and overshadows the town of Puerto Vallarta’s 92.3-foot Catrina built back in 2023. The height isn’t the only impressive aspect, either. Her purple dress measures over 88 feet long, while the memorial altar (called an ofrenda) is surrounded by an estimated 70,00 marigold plants grown in the nearby Xochimilco borough.

Observed each year on November 1 and 2, Mexico’s Day of the Dead is famously dedicated to celebrating and honoring deceased family members and loved ones. Historians are divided on its exact origins—while some researchers contend it possesses genuine Indigenous pre-Hispanic roots, others argue the holiday is a more recent rebrand of Spanish traditions intended to promote cultural pride. Regardless of the answer, Day of the Dead has evolved into one of the country’s most popular and iconic observances. Every year, celebrants often wear skeleton-themed costumes and construct ofrendas, while families and friends gather to fondly recall the departed.

The Catrina will remain erected until November 2. Credit: Daniel Cardenas / Anadolu via Getty Images Anadolu

Apart from its size, Mexico City’s record-breaking sculpture is a relatively traditional example of La Catrina, one of Day of the Dead’s most recognizable figures. It’s also much easier to trace La Catrina’s history compared to the holiday’s origins. The figure is based on early 20th century Mexican illustrator José Guadalupe Posada’s most famous work, “La Calavera Catrina” ( or “The Elegant Skull”). Posada’s illustration was released posthumously, and depicts a woman’s skeleton dressed in stylish clothing. Art historians widely view “La Calavera Catrina” as a critique of upper-class Mexican women who attempted to mirror European fashions of the era. Posada’s piece was later expanded upon by the muralist Diego Rivera in 1946-1947. 

Similarly inspired iconography has since become a mainstay of Day of the Dead celebrations. La Catrina sculptures like the new record breaker in Mexico City are frequently surrounded by marigolds. Also known as cempasúchil, the flowers are traditionally believed to help guide spirits home with their scent and colorful petals.

Like life itself, Mexico City’s building-sized La Catrina is only temporary. Admirers will be able to visit the installation and its altar only until November 2.

It’s not just dry messes that shop vacuums can handle. When unexpected wet messes happen, like a water main break, leak, or flood, a wet/dry shop vac is an indispensable tool that can quickly suck up the liquid into a canister for easy disposal. And, the variety of attachments that come with most shop vacs make them ready for flat surfaces, carpet, or even hard-to-reach crevices. The trick is knowing how to use a shop vac for water removal the right way. Here’s what you need to know.

Before You Begin

Water and electricity don’t mix. Although modern shop vacs are typically double-insulated, it’s never a bad idea to plug yours into a GFCI outlet for extra assurance and if you use an extension cord, make sure it’s wet-rated. 

Also, when using a shop vac for water, keep in mind that you will need to lift the shop vac’s canister to dump out the water when you are finished cleaning up a mess. It’s usually easier to dump out the water before it’s full.

How to Use a Shop Vac to Clean Up Water

A shop vacuum can handle a ton of jobs like sawdust collection, carpet cleaning, and gutter cleaning. Here’s how to use a wet vac and how to suck up water with a shop vac. 

Step 1: Remove the bag and filter from the vacuum.

If your model has a bag, remove it before using your shop vac for water. If you don’t, it will fill up with liquid and rip, making a nasty mess in the canister. These bags are typically very easy to remove and reinstall, so don’t throw yours out if it isn’t full.

Then remove the air filter. (If your shop vac has a wet filter, insert the model’s foam filter that’s designed for liquids.) If the air filter is installed when sucking up water, it will clog quickly and reduce airflow. The motor will then work harder than normally necessary, which could shorten the lifespan of your shop vac.

Step 2: Attach the best shop vac attachment for the situation.

Photo: Vacmaster

It’s easier to remove water from a surface if the end of the vacuum is compatible with the surface. For flat floors, look for the wide, T-shaped attachment. This attachment does the best job of removing water from hard floors of all types. Some of these floor attachments even have rubber squeegees that you can insert to increase their effectiveness.

Consider using the crevice tool for vehicle floors, tight spaces, depressions, or other spaces where the floor attachment won’t fit. You can also shop for a handheld wet vac meant specifically for small spaces. 

Step 3: Set the shop vac on a level surface and plug it into a GFCI outlet.

Place the shop vac near the spill, and then run the cord to the closest GFCI outlet to extra protect yourself from any possible shock during use. If the cord doesn’t reach a GFCI outlet, then run an extension cord that’s wet-rated. You’ll see a W on the cord’s jacket if it’s wet-rated. 

Step 4: Turn on the shop vac and start vacuuming the water.

Pushing the vacuum attachment back and forth across the floor rapidly will spread puddles and make them harder to clean up. Here’s how to start vacuuming water. 

  • Turn on the shop vac. 
  • Place the nozzle in the water. 
  • Hold the nozzle in the water until it sucks up most of the water in the area. 
  • Then, move to a new area and repeat.

For carpets and areas that require the crevice tool, adopt the same approach but on a smaller scale. Work in small areas, and give the vacuum a few seconds at each location before moving it to a new space.

Step 5: Empty the canister.

Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com

Your shop vac has a built-in safety device that stops the vacuum when it’s full. There is a ball or puck in the filter attachment that floats, and when the water reaches an unsafe height, this device blocks the suction. To keep vacuuming, you’ll have to empty the canister. 

  • First, remove the lid and motor assembly. 
  • Look at the canister full of water. If there are any large chunks of items or debris, remove them before draining. 
  • Next, carry the canister to a utility sink or somewhere you can safely drain the water. 
  • If you have a large shop vac that has a plug or cap at the bottom of the shop vac, open it to begin draining. Replace the cap once the canister is empty and head back to suck up more water. 
  • If your shop vac does not have a plug at the bottom, lift the canister and pour out the water. 

Step 6: Clean the shop vac when you’re done.

After you finish cleaning up the water, you will still need to clean and dry the shop vac to prevent mold and mildew. 

  • Remove the vac hose and rinse it with a garden hose. 
  • Then, use the garden hose to clean the dirt and grime out of the shop vac. 
  • Then clean the inside of the canister and the vacuum assembly with an all-purpose cleaner. 
  • Rinse everything once more before allowing it all to dry. For best results, turn the canister upside down while it dries. 
  • After everything is clean and dry, reattach the lid, filter, and vacuum bag.

When to Use a Wet Vac

Using a shop vac for something wet usually means it’s a surprise and an urgent situation. From plumbing emergencies to severe storms and accidental spills, a wet-rated vacuum is a handy tool that can help contain wet messes and speed-up the return to normal. Here are some situations when a wet vac is a smart tool that can help. 

  • Pipe burst or leaky pipe
  • Toilet clog overflow
  • Sewer drain or sink backup
  • Dishwasher or washing machine overflow
  • Minor basement flood
  • Broken aquarium
  • Sump pump failure
  • Soggy carpet from cleaning or spills
  • Water pooled in a crawl space
  • Rainwater in post holes for a deck or fence

How to Choose the Right Shop Vac

Photo: DeWalt

There are a variety of shop vacs that are designed to handle different types of messes. Since everyone’s situation is different, consider your needs so you can choose the best shop vac for you. Here are some popular types of shop vacs.

  • Large-capacity shop vacuums are great for big jobs and for those who frequently clean up after projects like professional remodelers and DIYers who are renovating entire homes. 
  • Battery-powered shop vacuums make it easy to transport and set up wherever a vacuum is needed. Since they run on batteries, they can run out of power during a big job unless you have an extra battery ready to go. 
  • Compact shop vacuums are designed to help clean up small messes in small spaces. If you don’t have frequent spills or messes, and if you don’t have a lot of storage space for another tool, a compact shop vac might be a good fit.

FAQ

Q. Should I rent or buy a shop vac?

If you have a surprise basement cleanup to tackle, it may make sense to rent a shop vac, since big box stores also rent shop vacs by the hour or day for less than the cost of a new one. However, it might make sense to purchase one if you expect that you’ll use it at least several times a year. 

Q. How long can you run a shop vac?

Experts suggest running a shop vac for 2 hours or less before giving the motor a break to cool down, which can help extend the life of the tool. It’s also important to keep in mind that tools have an expected lifetime. For example, Ridgid says their vacuums expect a life of about 500 to 700 hours. 

Q. Can you use a wet vac on other liquids than water?

Yes, a wet vac can suck up liquids beyond just water as long as the liquid is not toxic or hazardous.

Q. When should I call in a pro?

A shop vac is a great first response tool, and it’s useful for smaller messes. However, for substantial flooding or when water’s been present for long enough to cause structural damage or mold and mildew issues, you might need to call in the experts.

Ilona Maher Now Looking to Take Rugby—and Girls’ Fitness—to the Next Level

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

During the 2024 Paris Olympics, USA women’s team rugby player Ilona Maher suddenly became a household name.

“I think for some people when they get famous, maybe it changes who they are and who they believe themselves to be,” Maher tells Muscle & Fitness. “I just never found that to be me.I think I’m kind of a similar person I was before the Paris Olympics.”

But that was merely just the beginning.

Ilana Maher

The bronze-medal winner posed on the cover of Sports Illustrated shortly after the games wrapped. From there, Maher went on to compete on Dancing with the Stars, launch a successful podcast, and secure major partnerships with brands like Maybelline New York, Adidas and Burn Boot Camp.

Although it seems like all eyes are on Maher, very few of her fans are watching her while she’s on the field that made her a celebrity.

“Honestly, it doesn’t frustrate me because we don’t have enough places for them to watch rugby,” Maher says. “So I would tell you to watch rugby. I could tell you there’s games on this weekend, but it’s not like you tune into one game, then I’ll keep watching that team.”

Despite the lack of eyeballs on the game, much like Caitlin Clark in the WNBA, Maher is credited for helping put the game back on the map and even boosting ticket sales.

“That’s where it’s hard for me is that there’s really not a lot of opportunities to watch the game,” Maher explains. “And when they want to watch me play—a lot of people would love to watch me play, but I play in places like South Africa and then I go to Perth. So if you want to wake up at 4 in the morning to watch me play the first two minutes of a game…”

Ilana Maher

Maher Finds Ways to Balance Fame with Commitment to Team USA

Although Maher admits it’s a hard pill to swallow, she doesn’t let it discourage her and remains hopeful for the future of the sport.

“It’s a very tough thing in that I want to get more people in. I think more people should be in, but there we need more opportunities or need more things for [fans], games and in terms for them to be able to watch that are on home soil, too.”

The San Diego resident currently has her sights set on the 2028 L.A. summer games so all of her other projects get planned around her training for Team USA.

“We have meetings throughout the day and rehab and stretching to ramp the body up and whatnot,” explains Maher. “I have to do whatever timing my team says. I’m not an individual athlete that can build my life around my podcast. I have to build the podcast and everything else around my training life, I have to be in the gym at 8 a.m. with all the girls. It’s not worth it to miss a day of training when maybe something else will come up, because I do think I’m a team athlete through and through.”

Despite knowing her obligations to her teammates come first, Maher understands there’s a balance, especially to keep people intrigued in the sport for the upcoming olympic games.

“If my girls are grinding, I want to be out there grinding with them and not just taking the easy road,” Maher says. “But it is important for me to also focus on things like the podcasts or speaking, because that’s how I financially live the life I do.”

Maher admits she’s become so popular that she’s had to pass on some things to prioritize her love of the game.

“I have to say no to a lot of opportunities,” Maher says. “I will get invited to this cool event and I just have to pick my battles.”

 

Ilana Maher

Maher is Hoping Rugby Finds More Opportunities for Fans to Watch 

Maher admits her newfound fame has created a “pretty hefty schedule,” her physical training remains her top priority. Her training schedule includes two daily on-field sessions along with a gym workout that can include speed and skills work, and up to about 20,000 steps of running. She also adds four days fo weightroom work, focusing explosive movements. The days she’s out of the gym, Maher is recovering and prepping to repeat the same schedule the following week.

The athlete says she thrives on pushing her limits in the weight room, especially with squats and bench presses.

“I like the ones where you really get to like to test yourself and see how much more you can do,” Maher says.

Despite being a professional athlete, Maher admits she still has her own personal wins in the gym.

“I do love dumb bell incline bench press,” explains Maher. “Where you do a weight and then you’re like, ‘I think I can do a little bit more weight!’ and you put a little more weight and you did and you’re like, ‘OK, I can do a little more weight. Those are the moments where I really love being in the gym”.

Off the field, Maher knows girls everywhere are looking up to her for guidance and empowerment, which is why she partnered with Burn Boot Camp to spread the message about their 45-minute high-intensity fitness classes.

“I just watched it and the energy and the way that these women came to this class, so excited to be there,” says Maher. They were high-fiving each other and they were getting stronger and it was the sort of community that I’m trying to foster and I think create, and that’s why I decided to work with this brand. Because I have so many girls who message me saying, ‘I wanna get strong. I wanna be strong like you. I just don’t know where to start and I’m intimidated by the gym.’

Carne Guisada

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless beef chuck roast (3 pounds), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt, divided
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 tablespoons canola oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 jalapeno pepper, seeded and chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 bottle (12 ounces) beer
  • 2 cups beef broth
  • 4 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 to 3 teaspoons crushed red pepper flakes
  • 2 teaspoons chili powder
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 large unpeeled red potatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon red wine vinegar
  • Tortillas, hot cooked rice, lime wedges and chopped fresh cilantro, optional
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, toss beef and 1/4 teaspoon salt. In a separate large bowl, combine flour and paprika. Add beef, a few pieces at a time, and toss to coat.
  2. In a Dutch oven, heat oil over medium heat. Brown beef in batches, adding additional oil as necessary. Remove with a slotted spoon. In same pan, add onion; cook and stir until tender, 3-5 minutes. Stir in tomato paste, jalapeno and garlic; cook until fragrant and color starts to darken slightly. Add browned beef back to the pan. Add beer; bring to a boil, scraping the bottom to release browned bits. Simmer until reduced by half, about 5 minutes. Stir in broth, Worcestershire sauce, crushed red pepper flakes, chili powder, cumin, bay leaf and remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt; bring to a boil. Reduce heat; cover and simmer until meat is almost tender, about 1-1/2 hours.
  3. Add potatoes and red wine vinegar. Cover; simmer until meat and vegetables are tender, about 30 minutes longer.
  4. Discard bay leaf. Using the back of a spoon or ladle, slightly mash potatoes. If desired, shred pork slightly with 2 forks. Serve tortillas, rice, lime and cilantro.

Nutrition Facts

1 cup: 347 calories, 16g fat (5g saturated fat), 88mg cholesterol, 445mg sodium, 19g carbohydrate (2g sugars, 2g fiber), 29g protein.

About our hiring process

Our recruitment process is tailored to do our best to avoid that you or us realize that we’re not a perfect fit. Every step serves a purpose and will allow us to get to know you better as much as it’ll help you understand more where you’ll be stepping in.

We also want you to have time to ask all of your questions regarding the mission, the long-term vision of your team and the company, our values, with the relevant interlocutor in front of you.

If you believe design and AI can awaken what’s best in us then this isn’t a career move.
