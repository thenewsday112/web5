This month, I had the amazing opportunity to join all of you in the WoW: Forever beta and see all the new and exciting features Blizzard introduced into the Vanilla experience to make it fresh and intriguing for everyone, not just the old heads who were around in 2004. I absolutely loved what I saw, even though some archaic Vanilla mechanics remain in place.

The Skyborne race was my faction of choice, aligned with the Alliance of course. I was a Rogue, like most of my characters in WoW, except in post-WOTLK expansions where I would pick anything, level it to 55, and then go and make a blood Death Knight.

I made a Rogue Skyborne as my character of choice. Image via Blizzard

This new race’s starting zone was refreshing. It had a plethora of different areas clearly drawing inspiration from other locations in Vanilla WoW, and moving from one place to another gave you a sense of progression through its environmental storytelling alone. It boasted a lot of cool ancient temples, alongside dark forests shrouded in mist and mystery and strange cities attuned to the very world and the powers of the wind.

The leveling in general was fine. The quests mostly followed the usual MMORPG formula of go there, kill that many creatures, and by chance loot them to bring me their unique items. That’s the overall gist, but some of the quests did have narrative grounding, and the writing in the quests themselves was actually better than I had expected of modern WoW. However, the structure of the zone meant a lot of players skipped most of its content and never saw the ultimate showdown, i.e., the zone’s narrative conclusion.

This is both good and bad. The game does not force you to stay in this zone for much longer than you want to, and as soon as you’re fed up and done, you can go to one of the ships and reach Dalaran, Stormwind, or any other faction capital you want to visit. Once there, you get quests that take you across the world and allow you to pick up leveling from areas in regular Vanilla WoW, but staying in the starting Skyborne zone is an option.

The starting zone is cool. I reckon more Blood Elf focus would’ve been cooler, but that’s just me. Image via Blizzard

Read what’s behind the latest news in gaming (with a bit of snark) by subscribing to the Destructoid Checkpoint newsletter!

It starts to drag after level 10 or so, though, so leaving for Stormwind was actually what I did around level 13 and started my journey across the Eastern Kingdoms using boats and trains and walking on foot.

It was an adventure of sorts—I hadn’t played World of Warcraft for a long time now, and had not set foot in Vanilla for literally 15 years. This solemn odyssey across the world, to Teldrassil and the Ironforge and into Westfall and through Stormwind, was a trip down memory lane into a time that longer exists, a childhood I was born into, of which remain unseen fragments that only the mind’s eye can glimpse.

The overall experience, significantly boosted by the unprecedented amount of other players achieved both through nostalgia and the new no-realm system, was one that slingshot me back to the good old days, and I’m glad I got to play it.

I recommend checking WoW: Forever out if you liked old WoW but wanted something new added to the mix. The Vanilla is intact if you want to ignore all the novelties, but you always keep the option to try them out.

This $18.25 Million Florida Beach Mansion Sits on a Rare Double Lot With 125 Feet of Private Gulf Coastline

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

A Florida home has more than double the beachfront access as its neighbors.

“It’s incredible. There's nothing like it at all on Bonita Beach and one of the things that really makes this so special is this is two and a half lots of frontage. That's 125 feet of beach access,” says listing agent Colleen Pensa with Premier Sotheby's International Realty, Vanderbilt office.

Most of the other nearby lots have 50 feet of frontage.

“Once you get into the house, the whole back of the house is glass and all you see is the Gulf,” she says. “You don't have to go out to the beach to where the water breaks and lean over. It's incredible.”

The front yard makes the home stand out. (Premier Media Group) There are walls of glass to capitalize on the views. (Premier Media Group)

Completed in 2019, the home is 6,543 square feet of “coastal elegance” and is listed for $18.25 million.

“Once you go in and you shut the door, it's like you're in a different world. It's soothing and it's relaxing and it's really well done,” Pensa explains, adding it was custom-built for the sellers who used it as a secondary home. “I have been doing this a long time and this house literally took my breath away. I was just absolutely stunned and the quality of everything that they've used is just incredible.”

There are five bedrooms and seven full bathrooms, all capitalizing on the views.

“I wish I could buy it. I wish I could live there. I'm a beach girl, so this house to me is absolutely everything I would want,” she says with a laugh. “Plenty of room for my family, for my kids, nobody's on top of each other.”

The garage is on the lowest level. (Premier Media Group) The house has plenty of space. (Premier Media Group) The home was custom-built to take advantage of the views. (Premier Media Group)

The lowest level of the house is the six-car garage, with the large common living spaces above it.

“You can sit 10 people on the kitchen island without sitting in the table that sits in the window overlooking the beach,” Pensa says. “There's plenty of room for everybody and it doesn't come along that often. They seem to be building much more modern now. This is really like old style Florida coastal elegance.”

The color palate is primarily white and blue with raised ceilings and custom millwork. The furnishings are negotiable and the home could either be a vacation property or full-time residence.

“I think you'd be very lucky if you could live every day of your life at the beach. I mean, that would work for me,” she says laughing. “My favorite thing about the house is that I could get up every single day and be in a place that to me is magical. When I'm on the beach, any worries, any issues, anything just kind of melts away. So, to me, this is really like heaven on earth.”

Each bathroom has spa-like amenities. (Premier Media Group) There are five bedrooms, all with views. (Premier Media Group)

The outdoor area is just off the main living space with huge windows opening to it.

“If you don't want to go to the beach, there's a saltwater pool. There's a covered entertainment area that has a gas fireplace and has ceiling fans and an outdoor kitchen,” she says.

“The pool has an infinity edge, so if you're sitting in a chaise lounge and you're looking across the pool. It looks like the water just drops right off the edge. It's incredible. It is literally a different world.”

The pool is on the main level and has an infinity edge. (Premier Media Group) The home has more than twice the beach frontage as nearby homes. (Premier Media Group)

The front of the house also stands about from its neighbors and not just because it’s more than three quarters of an acre.

“This [house] actually has a front yard and the the driveway is stone with grass in between. When you look at it, it just draws your eye right to the house,” Pensa explains. “Some [other driveways] are just paved. Some of them are just gravel, but this is completely done. There's nothing left to do in the house and it's very, very well maintained.”

Everything is ready for the next owners.

“I think this really is just a place that you're going to create memories. That's really what it is,” she says. “It's really an escape to get away and just kind of unplug and recharge. And if you're lucky enough to live there every day, that's great too.”
