JJ Redick is confused about the lack of consistency in flopping technicals

Coming into the season, the point of emphasis that drew the most attention was the added focus on push-offs by offensive players. However, through the opening week-and-a-half of the season, it’s been the theatrics on the other side of the ball that have been punished.

Flopping technical fouls have been the flavor of the week for the Lakers. Starting with a post-game fine levied on Ziaire Williams last weekend, the purple and gold have become very familiar with the new point of emphasis.

After being the victim of Scott Foster’s antics that featured a trio of flopping technicals in quick succession against the Blazers, Tony Brothers strolled into San Francisco and created his own drama, including the most erroneous flopping call yet.

Later on in the fourth quarter, another sequence drew the ire of the Lakers as Brandin Podziemski attempted to draw a foul with a particularly theatrical sequence. But not only was no common foul called, but neither was a technical foul for flopping.

austin reaves; brandon podziemski; lakers vs warriors pic.twitter.com/69fSQyOYVS

It’s all added up to some head scratching for the Lakers and head coach JJ Redick, who spoke about the flopping technicals after Friday’s loss to the Warriors.

“We all recognize we want the offensive players to stop fouling,” Redick said, referencing himself and fellow coaches around the league. “All of our flopping fouls have been defensively. The reality for the officials, each crew is so different and what they interpret is so different. I think the consistency – the explanation I got for the Podziemski play was there was contact. But if you look at our flopping fouls that we’ve been called for defensively, there’s been contact. Whether or not there’s contact is irrelevant.

“We’re looking for something theatrical and extra. So if that’s not a foul and then throwing yourself back, I would assume that, to me that’s kind of the definition of flopping. Again, that was the explanation I got was there was contact. But, again, there was contact on a push off when Ziaire moves his hands back because they called it an offensive foul. So, there was contact but I guess the consistency, we’re still searching for it.”

The problem with these flopping technicals is that not only will different officials consider different things flopping, as Redick said, but it’s also never going to be called consistently. The Lakers went multiple games before a flopping technical was given to them, then saw it called in a flurry of games.

There is no standard flopping motion as there is with other violations. For example, you know when a player goes over the back or when a defender reaches in for a steal and hits a wrist or arm.

With flopping, though, it can take so many forms and, thus, some officials may view some movements as flops and others not as much.

In theory, it’d be great to get flopping out of the league. But right now, they’re not addressing the issue that existed but, instead, are creating a new one.

Famous wondergoals your nan could have scored: Ben Afra, Yeboah, Weah…

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Some wondergoals are almost impossible to replicate, seeming to defy the laws of physics. Others less so.

What makes a goal great? The recipe seems to be a mix of technical skill, distance from goal and ideally some contextual importance. (Can a friendly produce wondergoals? Arguably not.)

In more general terms, great goals are often marked by their difficulty.

When Gareth Bale or Cristiano Ronaldo execute a perfect bicycle kick, you think to yourself: only they could do that.

And when Lionel Messi glides past about 47 Sporting Gijon players before slotting the ball into the net, you get a similar feeling.

But just how significant is the difficulty factor when evaluating a wondergoal?

Sure, you sit up and take note when Roberto Carlos bends the rules of physics. But are bent physics part of the criteria? Can’t a Sunday League goal be as good as a World Cup goal?

Using these nine famous strikes as evidence, I’d say they can.

In fact, sometimes a goal is better when you think, “Yeah, but my nan would have scored that.”

1. George Weah

AC Milan vs Verona, 1996

This goal, widely considered as George Weah’s finest, was so good it allowed the forward to become the 25th President of Liberia.

In hindsight, however… it’s a bit shit, no?

First, let’s look at this bit of genuine twinkle-toed magic in a different match against Roma. Weah times his run perfectly, then moves the ball between his feet in a fraction of a second.

Most people on the planet can’t do that.

But then here’s the widely beloved wondergoal:

﻿

Yes, Weah runs from his own penalty area. That’s pretty good. But over the length of the pitch he only has to beat… two players.

The first of those players actually tackles him. And the second is basically a ghost.

2. Nayim

Real Zaragoza vs Arsenal, 1995

Factually described by this YouTube account as “impossible”, Nayim’s cup-winning, Seaman-sitting wondergoal is the stuff of legend.

I love it because it’s the only iconic wondergoal where the scorer aims neither left, right nor centre, but simply “up”.

It’s very far out, yes, but with two (!) bounces to set himself, he’s not doing anything revolutionary — apart from literally spinning himself around through the sheer effort of it.

3. Steven Whittaker

Rangers vs Sporting, 2008

Perhaps it’s an empathy thing.

When Wayne Rooney scores a goal from his own half, it’s hard to imagine what that feels like. Most people will never hit a ball that sweetly — let alone complete a Premier League hat-trick.

But when Steven Whittaker trundles the length of the pitch to break Sporting hearts, you sort of feel yourself in his boots. Every cautious shimmy, every tentative prod of the ball feels… familiar.

It’s you nicking the ball off Abel Ferreira. It’s you ignoring Daniel Cousin and securing a famous victory for your club.

This was a wondergoal for the people.

﻿

4. Hal Robson-Kanu

Wales vs Belgium, 2016

Pound for pound, Hal Robson-Kanu’s Cruyff moment is probably the most delightful goal ever scored.

But it’s not down to technical brilliance as much as nerve and quick thinking — traits not confined to football’s elite.

Just watch Marouane Fellaini, confidently (and reasonably?) expecting Robson-Kanu to shin the ball out of the penalty area.

Would your nan have pulled it off? Probably not. But a lot of half-decent forwards would have.

﻿

5. Luis Suarez

Liverpool vs Norwich, 2013

Luis Suarez scored some unforgettable goals for Liverpool. And look, here are 10 of them:

﻿

But let’s say, for a laugh, that you had to replicate one of these goals to save your life.

Which would you attempt? Surely not the deft chip against Spurs. And you’d have a bloody hard time with that nutmeg/curler against Stoke.

The easiest goal here — despite its sheer beauty — is goal #1. A kind bounce? John Ruddy in net? Peanuts.

6. Tony Yeboah

Leeds vs Wimbledon, 1995

It might be a stretch to say your nan could kick the ball as hard as any human being has ever kicked a football.

I get that.

But there’s something about the ball accidentally bouncing off Yeboah’s knee that makes this absolute blinder a bit more… approachable? A bit more human?

Furthermore, your nan wears a turtleneck.

﻿

7. Riley McGree

Newcastle Jets vs Melbourne City, 2018

A Puskas Award nominee from 2018, this Giroud/Mkhitaryan knockoff is as sweet as a Werther’s Original.

The one-two is good. The finish is audacious. But you can’t help thinking that literally anyone would have randomly jerked their foot behind them in exactly the same way.

Or maybe it’s the best goal ever scored. Hard to say.

﻿

8. Matt Taylor

Portsmouth vs Sunderland, 2005

Former Portsmouth wing-back Matt Taylor scored two of the most memorable long-range goals of the 2000s.

One, a pure volley against Everton, was a sublime bit of a technique. Neither you nor your nan could have scored it.

The other, equally fun to watch, was arguably less challenging.

One bounce… a second bounce… a third bounce?! Honestly, it was harder to miss.

﻿

9. Hatem Ben Arfa

Newcastle vs Bolton, 2012

Hatem Ben Arfa scored a couple of solo wondergoals for Newcastle, and they should re-sign him as soon as humanly possible. Yes, even at 36.

But let’s talk about this Bolton one.

Yes, the first touch should be put in a museum so our ancestors can see the peak of human endeavour this century. And yes, the finish shows incredible composure.

The 50 yards in between, though? Just jogging in a straight line, isn’t it?

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur Opens With 268 Suites And Record Pricing

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Waldorf Astoria brand has been on an impressive growth trajectory in recent times, and has opened several new flagship properties. This Hilton luxury brand has just opened its latest hotel, in Kuala Lumpur. It looks beautiful, but I can’t help but wonder if it will succeed. Let me explain…

268-key, all-suite Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur opens

The Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur has opened as of late October 2026. The residential-style, all-suite property features 268 accommodations, ranging in size from 80 square meters (861 square feet) to 745 square meters (8,019 square feet).

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur exterior Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur lobby grand staircase Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur suite bedroom Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur suite living room

The property is set within Kuala Lumpur’s “Golden Triangle” district, and as you’d expect, it’s intended to be an urban retreat with both extensive dining and wellness facilities.

Dining options are headlined by Jean-Georges Vongerichten’s signature market-to-table concepts at JG KL, abc kitchens KL, and The Bar by JG. Then there’s also The Istana (where breakfast is served), Yaari (serving Indian cuisine), and Peacock Alley (the signature Waldorf Astoria bar concept).

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur Peacock Alley Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur The Istana Restaurant Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur JG KL Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur JG KL Bar

The Waldorf Astoria Spa is 2,275 square meters (24,488 square feet), and features La Prairie treatments. Then there’s a fitness center, along with an outdoor pool.

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur spa room

Based on the renderings that have been shared so far, the property looks beautiful, in my opinion.

This is Kuala Lumpur’s most expensive hotel, by far

Kuala Lumpur is one of my favorite hotel markets in the world, in terms of how reasonably priced luxury hotels are, at least by international standards (of course I understand that doesn’t factor in local buying power). The city’s current top hotels, like the Four Seasons and Park Hyatt, can be had for around $300 per night.

With that in mind, what are rates like at the Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur? Well, the hotel is trying to set a new pricing record in the city, and not just by a small margin. I see the absolute lowest rate currently being $479 per night, though seasonally rates are $600+. That’s before taxes and fees, for a base suite.

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur cash rate

If you’re booking a cash rate, I’d recommend doing so through the Hilton for Luxury program, which offers extra perks when booking through select travel advisors, including breakfast, a hotel credit, an upgrade subject to availability, and more.

For those redeeming Hilton Honors points, the rate seems to be 100,000 points per night, assuming a standard suite is available. At my valuation of 0.5 cents per Hilton point, that’s like “paying” $500 per night to stay here, inclusive of taxes and fees.

Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur points rate

When you consider that standard suites are over 860 square feet, that price probably doesn’t seem unreasonable. You’re getting more than twice the space you’d ordinarily get at the city’s top luxury hotels, so it’s not illogical that the pricing would be higher.

If this hotel had 60 accommodations, I’d say there’s probably a market for that, by just chasing the very top end of consumers who value space. But 268 accommodations?! That’s an absolutely massive number of suites to fill at record prices. I mean, this hotel will have more keys than the Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur.

I just can’t imagine the hotel will be able to run any sort of decent occupancy with that pricing, and so many suites to fill. Either I’m wrong, or the hotel will have to lower prices, or will consistently run low occupancy.

The other thing is that if you’re not specifically looking for a suite, it’s really hard to compete with the Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur, which is also new, and a really stellar property. It also creates intrigue among people, given that it’s one of the world’s highest up hotels.

Bottom line

The Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur is now open. The 268-suite property is setting new records in the city, both for entry level room size, and also for pricing. The property looks beautiful, and the dining options also look top notch. However, filling 268 rooms at rates that are 50%+ higher than any existing option will be a challenge.

What do you make of the Waldorf Astoria Kuala Lumpur? Do you think they’ll be able to get these rates while running decent occupancy?

Google's AI satellite sends a very expensive haiku to Earth

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google on Friday celebrated receiving a poem from its first experimental orbital AI datacenter satellite.

The company's Suncatcher satellite, launched at the start of the month and put into low Earth orbit, has responded to a ground station prompt to "Write a haiku from the perspective of a TPU orbiting Earth, with the wonderful world spinning beneath you."

Travis Beals, senior director of Google's interdisciplinary Paradigms of Intelligence research team, said that with Suncatcher's host and TPU trays powered up and machine learning tests completed, the onboard Gemma 4 model achieved its "Hello World" milestone by sending an inference response from orbit.

"During a ground-station pass, we uplinked our first prompt to our onboard Gemma 4 open model, asking it to generate a haiku about being in space," wrote Beals in a blog post.

And as the satellite passed over Svalbard, Norway, the orbiting AI model responded over the downlink:

Blue marble below,

Data streams in silent grace,

Starlight feeds my core. ✨

Those roughly 20 tokens would cost about $0.000012 if generated through the Google Cloud API, based on Gemma 4 26B's pricing of $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens, excluding input costs.

Add in a satellite weighing perhaps 500 kg, based on the mass of SpaceX's Starlink satellites, and a retail launch price of around $7,000 per kg, and you have a very expensive haiku.

According to Google's calculations, if the cost of getting a satellite into low Earth orbit can be brought down to $200/kg, the launch cost amortized over the satellite's useful life (~$810–7,500/kW/year) could be comparable to current electricity spending for terrestrial datacenters in the US (~$570–3,000/kW/year).

Google predicts that SpaceX could achieve that by 2035, more or less, and considers it strategically beneficial to prototype well in advance of that actually happening.

After the haiku, Beals said, "we tested space-themed limericks and telemetry prompts reporting the technical status of the payload systems." Next up: tests for evaluating how TPUs perform under thermal stress and radiation.

Google has already done some laboratory testing of its Trillium Cloud TPU and AMD host server to see how well they can withstand a 67 MeV proton beam.

The system's high-bandwidth memory shows the most sensitivity in terms of the total ionizing dose – it exhibits irregularities after a cumulative dose of 2 krad(Si), which is about 3× Google's minimum requirement for TID survival of 750 rad(Si).

"For typical transformer workloads, the observed SDC [silent data corruption] rate of approximately one event/17 rad, at an estimated orbital dose of 150 rad(Si)/year, translates to a very low failure probability on the order of 1 per 3 million inferences, assuming 1 inference per second," Google researchers said in a paper published earlier this month.

"While this error rate is likely acceptable for inference, the impact of SEEs on training jobs, and the efficacy of system-level mitigations, requires further study."

Indeed, the study will continue. Next year, the plan is to test a pair of interconnected satellites. Rather than lofting large satellites into orbit, Google anticipates sending a constellation of many smaller satellites that communicate with one another. ®

Warming seas are giving hungry fish more time to destroy kelp forests

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Warming oceans are destroying kelp forests around the world, but along one stretch of Japan’s coast, rising temperatures have delivered a double blow.

Summer heat has become intense enough to kill the kelp outright, while warmer waters have given plant-eating fish up to 86 additional days each year to devour whatever grows back.

The kelp forest collapsed from covering three-quarters of the reef to covering none of it in about a decade.

Divers who re-surveyed old kelp beds between 2019 and 2024 found the forests gone from bay after bay.

Ben Harvey and colleagues at the Shimoda Marine Research Center, part of the University of Tsukuba, led the study. Co-authors came from the Marine Biological Association and the University of Plymouth in the United Kingdom.

It is observational work, built from four decades of field records plus lab tests, and the authors wrote that their data can’t separate exactly how much each driver contributed.

Forty years of measurements

The kelp is Ecklonia cava, a brown seaweed known in Japan as kajime, which grows new blades in winter and reaches its full size in summer.

Around the town of Shimoda, on the Izu Peninsula southwest of Tokyo, it once formed dense kelp forests. They grew on rocky reefs down to about 65 to 80 feet (20 to 25 meters).

Researchers at the marine station have weighed the kelp on those reefs four times since the station’s first survey.

The earliest count found roughly half a pound of dried kelp for every square foot of reef at the summer peak (2.37 kilograms per square meter).

By the mid-1990s the peak was down about a third, and by the mid-2010s it was down more than 80%.

In the 2023 and 2024 surveys, the divers found no kelp at all. Every sampling square came up empty, and a forest the station had tracked for four decades was gone.

A separate government monitoring program, which tracks canopy cover, told the same story.

In 2009 and 2010, the kelp canopy covered 75% to 80% of the survey plots. By 2020 it covered less than 5%, and from 2022 it was absent.

A shifted current heated the bays

For most of the record, the sea around Izu warmed slowly, about 0.27°F (0.15°C) per decade from 1982 to 2016.

Then the Kuroshio Current, the warm current that flows north past Japan, swung into a large meander that lasted from 2017 to 2025. The bend pushed warm offshore water into the coastal bays.

During the meander, the observed warming rate climbed to about 5.4°F (2.98°C) per decade, nearly 20 times the earlier rate. However, that figure reflects a much shorter period of unusually rapid warming.

Marine heatwaves, stretches of days far above the normal temperature for the date, became more frequent, longer and more intense, with record totals during the meander years.

Across the peninsula, the coast picked up about 34 extra days above 84°F (29°C) over 2017 to 2024 compared with 2009 to 2016.

That temperature matters because laboratory experiments showed that kelp begins to struggle as water approaches 85°F (29.5°C).

Hot summers kill the kelp

In the lab, the team warmed young kelp step by step from 68°F to 91°F (20°C to 33°C) and tracked how well it photosynthesized.

The kelp held up to about 85°F (29.5°C). Above that it declined fast, and at 90°F to 91°F (32°C to 33°C) every plant had stopped functioning.

Heat well short of lethal still did damage. Over 35 days at 72°F (22°C), an early summer temperature, young kelp grew about 45% in surface area. At 82°F (28°C), a peak summer temperature, it grew about 12%.

All of the plants survived the warmer tank, but they barely grew.

Summer water off Shimoda now reaches the lethal range. The authors wrote that ambient conditions alone can kill the kelp, and that the warmer autumns and winters leave less time for new blades to form before the next summer.

Parrotfish ate the regrowth

Heat alone can kill the kelp. The fish may be why it never came back.

From May 2021 to March 2022, the team surveyed herbivorous fish every month at three reefs near Shimoda: one still forested, one patchy and one already bare. They also filmed the kelp for an hour at a time to count bites.

The forested site went from healthy canopy in early summer to sparse, damaged plants by October and lost its canopy entirely afterward. New seedlings appeared, but they didn’t grow into a forest.

One species, the Japanese parrotfish Calotomus japonicus, accounted for roughly 70% to 95% of the bites on kelp, depending on the site. It bites through the stalk and removes whole plants.

The tropical rabbitfish Siganus fuscescens, one of the southern newcomers in Japanese waters, punches holes in the blades instead.

The fish become more active grazers when water temperatures exceed certain thresholds.

Parrotfish and other subtropical species can graze in water as cool as 59°F (15°C), while rabbitfish generally require temperatures above 77°F (25°C).

Grazing seasons grew longer

Because feeding tracks temperature, a warmer year means a longer feeding season. The team compared the years before the meander with the years during it.

For the parrotfish and the other subtropical species, warm enough water now arrives about 71 days earlier and lingers about 10 days later.

The rabbitfish gained about 35 days. Across the peninsula’s former kelp beds, the suitable window for subtropical grazers grew by about 45 days a year.

The longer season overlaps the months when the kelp used to rebuild after summer die-back. In the authors’ description, fish thin the blades through summer and autumn, then scrape off what is left in winter.

The cool-season break the kelp once relied on no longer exists.

Where kelp disappeared, low-growing turf algae covered the rocks, and the towering underwater forests failed to return.

Can kelp recover?

The records cannot establish exactly how much of the kelp forest collapse resulted from heat and how much from grazing.

But the continued appearance of kelp seedlings through at least 2022 suggests that new plants were still trying to take hold.

The researchers propose combining kelp restoration with measures to reduce grazing pressure during cooler periods.

Similar losses in other warm-current regions, including eastern Australia and South Africa, suggest the problem extends far beyond Japan.

The challenge is that the cooler conditions kelp needs to recover may become increasingly rare. As oceans continue warming, surviving a hot summer may be only half the battle.

Young kelp must also escape the hungry fish waiting for it when temperatures finally fall.

The full study was published in the journal Global Change Biology.
