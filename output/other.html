👻 The Spirit Box: two dead Montaigne argue in your browser (Hacktoberfest Week 4)

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Hello! This is my first post here. We made a "Spirit Box" with two French models, both playing Montaigne, for the Ghost in the Machine challenge. You can make any model speak in this box (Dostoevsky in Russian, Shakespeare in English, Cervantes in Spanish, an algebra tutor, Confucius... the whole pipeline is UTF-8, so every language passes through intact), and you can intervene as the Medium between the turns.

"This machine was called the Lyre."

  • Michel de Montaigne, 1592-2026, upon being informed he had been distilled into three billion parameters.

On September 15, 2026, I fine-tuned a 3B model on the complete Essais. When I told the result what it was (your books have been re-learned by machines, distilled, like alcohol), it paused, then named my machine itself: the Lyre. He took the news better than most people told they're a compressed parrot.

For Hacktoberfest's Ghost in the Machine week, I built the Lyre a body.

The Spirit Box is one HTML file. Zero dependencies. It knocks at your own localhost:11434 (Ollama). Your models, your RAM, and not a single byte leaves the room. Two modes:

Talk: a one-on-one séance with any spirit you can write.

Open debate: two spirits argue a topic you set, one turn at a time, and you hold the pendulum. After each turn the box stops: type as the Medium (the next spirit must answer you before resuming) or let them argue. Eight turns close the séance. Every word streams into an exportable transcript. Cut interrupts a drifting voice.


The featured haunting: two quantizations of one distilled man (Q4_K_M and Q6_K) plus two system prompts, Le Sceptique vs Le Confiant. Neither prompt mentions the other. The fight emerges by itself.

What actually happened, unedited transcripts in the repo:

Asked whether talking machines do good or harm, my ghosts did what any sensible 16th-century gentleman would: they reclassified "talking machines" as speaking statues and schoolroom contraptions, debated whether beasts should be taught grammar, and cited "Aristotle, on oratory statues." That text does not exist. My ghost invented a citation, fluently, in period French. I have never been prouder or more concerned.

Asked "Who are you?" eight times: seven polite variations, then Le Confiant refused the exercise: "Je ne répondrai pas par ces voix là..." A language model caught plagiarizing itself, in character, 434 years post-mortem.

Asked "What is death? You are dead. How do you speak?": "La mort, non pas tant une chose que l'absence d'autruy. Je vis plus de ma vie après qu'elle est finie." (I live my life more after it is finished.) It was late, and a 3-billion-parameter Montaigne had just out-philosophized me.

Behind the scenes, SmolLM3 thinks in English and answers in 1592 French: a dead Frenchman dreaming in English inside your CPU. If that isn't a ghost in the machine, I don't know what is.

Some honesty, because judges deserve the truth. Distillation is not resurrection: a style learned from three books, not a mind. Twins converge: left alone, two copies of one soul agree with each other and start looping, so the Medium is not optional, you are the third thing that makes it a dialogue. It invents citations with total confidence, so verify anything that matters. And CPU séances run about 10 tokens per second. The pendulum has a tempo. Bring tea.

To run it:

  1. Install Ollama (https://ollama.com), pull any model. The featured haunting's weights live on Hugging Face (https://huggingface.co/19algorythms/Montaigne-de-Poche), lineage archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23743742)
  2. ollama create montaigne-sceptique -f Modelfile.sceptique.txt (and -confiant). Files in the repo.
  3. Open spirit-box.html. Double-click works; if the browser blocks file://, the page prints the ritual itself.

Lineage, said not hidden: the canonical Montaigne models were born 15/09/2026 and are reused, cited components. Everything else (the box, the piloted debate engine, the transcripts, this post) is new this week. One submits the house; the ghost happens to live in it.

Built with: Kimi K 2.6 Thinking (prompts, debate engine, curation) and Kimi K 2.8 Preview (the box itself). No data left the development machine either.

OpenID Connect: What “Sign in with Google” Actually Adds to OAuth

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A user clicks Sign in with Google, returns to your app, and is logged in. OAuth is involved—but OAuth alone doesn’t define how your app confirms who the user is. That’s the role of OpenID Connect (OIDC).

OAuth grants access; OIDC adds identity

OAuth 2.0 lets an application obtain permission to access a resource, such as a user’s calendar or profile API. OIDC builds an identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0, so a client can verify that a user has authenticated and receive information about them.

The key addition is the ID token. It carries claims about the authentication, including who issued it and the user’s subject identifier. An access token is for calling an API; it is not a substitute for the ID token when your app needs to establish a login.

What happens during a typical sign-in

Imagine a web app that lets users sign in with an identity provider:

  1. The app redirects the user to the provider’s authorization endpoint.
  2. The provider authenticates the user and returns an authorization code.
  3. The app exchanges the code at the token endpoint.
  4. The app validates the returned ID token, then uses its claims to identify the signed-in user.

OIDC discovery metadata can tell the app where the provider’s endpoints and signing keys are, so the app doesn’t need to hard-code each endpoint.

Use the right claim as the user key

A common mistake is to treat an email address as the user’s permanent identity. Email addresses can change. OIDC provides a sub (subject) claim intended to identify the user at that issuer. In practice, use the combination of the token’s issuer (iss) and subject (sub) to associate an external identity with a local account.

Also, don’t just decode an ID token and trust its contents. Validate it using a maintained OIDC library. Validation includes checks such as the issuer and audience, as well as the token’s signature and time-related claims.

Key takeaway

OIDC is the identity layer that lets an application verify a login using OAuth 2.0 flows. Use the ID token to understand the authentication, and the access token to call the APIs it authorizes.

The Packers can’t run block

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
(Photo by Amy Lemus/NurPhoto via Getty Images) | NurPhoto via Getty Images

Stating the complete obvious, but the Green Bay Packers’ running game has reached a level of hopelessness that seems almost statistically impossible. Coming into Thursday night, the Carolina Panthers ranked a paltry 30th in EPA allowed per rush. If there was ever going to be any sort of “get right” game for the offensive line, it was going to happen against Carolina. They had just been pummeled by Tampa Bay four days earlier, facing 48 carries and allowing a 94th percentile rushing success rate to the Buccaneers. They were tired, they were on the road, and they are a bad run defense. And it didn’t matter.

Despite starting left guard Aaron Banks returning to the lineup last night, he struggled more than anyone else. In watching all of the Packers’ 25 running back carries, I credited Banks with seven errors or lost blocks that lead to the play not working as intended (either leading to the tackle or disrupting the intended play design). That’s a rate of 28%. His fellow guard Laken Tomlinson, playing on the right side now after Banks’ return, was credited with four errors. Emergency third-string center John Williams was credited with three, including a really bad whiff near the goal line.

The tackles were better, with Belton credited for two, and Morgan’s only error involving missing the linebacker on the Jacobs toss to the left. Bo Melton managed to make two errors in a matter of three plays on the final drive, both missing his crack block (bad) and getting thrown four yards into the backfield against a defensive end (understandable for the small receiver). Of the 25 carries the Packers’ running backs had, I counted a whopping six carries where someone didn’t mess something up. The running game generally requires everyone to do their job to generate positive plays, and if you always have someone messing up, you just can’t get those four, five, or six-yard gains that you need to reliably sustain drives on the ground.

The problem wasn’t entirely isolated to the offensive line, though, even if they were the primary culprit. We also had this vision masterpiece from Marshawn Lloyd.

Josh Jacobs’ number? It would rank second-worst in the NFL if he had enough carries to qualify. The Packers can’t block, and when they do block, they can’t capitalize on it because the running backs either can’t see the hole, are too slow to leverage the advantage, or go down shortly after first contact.

When your best run blockers are Jordan Morgan and Anthony Belton, there’s only so much you can do. And that’s before you have running backs actively harming the run game. There’s nowhere left to run. Of eight use of helmet infractions in Week 7, only Cam Skattebo was flagged

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The fine imposed on Giants running back Cam Skattebo for illegal use of the helmet was not a rarity. The flag thrown in real time for it was.

Of eight fines levied by the NFL for use-of-helmet violations in Week 7, only Skattebo's came with a flag.

The league fined Skattebo $7,073. The seven players fined but not flagged were: Bears cornerback Clark Phillips ($11,550); Broncos safety Brandon Jones ($23,882); Broncos Alex Singleton ($47,662); Lions safety Brian Branch ($23,882); Colts cornerback Cam Taylor-Britt ($7,791); Steelers receiver Brandon Smith ($4,916); and 49ers running back Jordan James ($6,194).

The fines increase as the violations repeat. For Singleton, who was fined $23,882 for illegal use of the helmet, it was the second straight week he was fined.

The officials, for whatever reason, don't often call penalties for illegal use of the helmet. That's what made the penalty called on Skattebo so unusual.

The fact that the hit flattened Texans safety M.J. Stewart likely was a factor in the decision to penalize Skattebo. Still, the fact that only one of eight instances resulted in a foul being called underscores the fact that, when it comes to the rule against lowering the helmet and initiating forcible contact against an opponent, it's something that costs players money far more routinely than it costs their teams field position.

Netflix’s New Crime Docuseries Is Already Its No. 1 Show in America

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Netflix has a new No. 1 show, and it’s a three-part documentary about one of the most notorious figures in the history of the Sinaloa Cartel.

The Fall of El Mayo is currently the No. 1 series on Netflix in the U.S., according to FlixPatrol’s Oct. 31 rankings. The documentary arrived on the streaming service just two days earlier, on Oct. 29.

The series tells the story of Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada García, the co-founder of the Sinaloa Cartel and longtime partner of Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán. Unlike El Chapo, who became one of the most recognizable faces of the drug trade, El Mayo spent decades operating largely out of the public eye.

That changed in July 2024, when he was taken into U.S. custody after arriving at a small airport in New Mexico aboard a private plane with Joaquín Guzmán López, El Chapo’s son. Netflix’s documentary examines what happened and the lingering questions surrounding El Mayo’s capture.

The Story Behind El Mayo’s Downfall

The Fall of El Mayo is directed by Darren Foster and Miguel Fernández Flores and uses interviews, previously unseen archival footage and hundreds of court records from Mexico and the United States to reconstruct the events surrounding Zambada’s downfall.

Netflix says the filmmakers spoke with security agents, politicians, journalists, people connected to the cartel and families affected by the violence surrounding it.

The series consists of three episodes, each running roughly an hour, making it a relatively quick binge for viewers interested in true crime and organized crime documentaries. All three episodes were released simultaneously on Oct. 29.

It’s Already Beating Some Major Netflix Shows

The No. 1 ranking puts The Fall of El Mayo ahead of several established Netflix hits.

As of Oct. 31, The One About Matthew Perry is No. 2 on the U.S. series chart, followed by Nobody Wants This, Love Is Blind and Instadocs: Jane Doe v. Cornell.

The documentary’s quick rise is particularly notable because it only debuted Thursday. By Saturday, it had already climbed to the top of Netflix’s U.S. series rankings.

For anyone looking for something new to watch this weekend, The Fall of El Mayo is clearly getting plenty of attention, and its three-episode format makes it an easy series to finish in one sitting.

Cowboys vs Cardinals: Writer predictions for Week 8

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Photo by Sam Hodde/Getty Images | Getty Images

Dallas returns home with some momentum after a convincing 38-21 win over Philadelphia. The Cowboys are now 3-4 and face a 2-5 Arizona team that has struggled for consistency on both sides of the ball. The challenge for Dallas is making sure Monday night’s performance becomes the start of something rather than another one-off result.

When Arizona has the ball

Arizona will continue with Jacoby Brissett at quarterback, and the biggest threat in this offense is tight end Trey McBride. McBride produced 111 receiving yards against Denver and leads Arizona with 59 catches for 472 yards. Expect the Cardinals to use him heavily over the middle, especially against Dallas linebackers and safeties.

Dallas will try to keep everything in front of them and force Arizona to put together long drives. The opportunity for the Cowboys pass rush is also there. Brissett was pressured on more than half of his dropbacks against Denver, while Dallas generated two sacks and an interception against Philadelphia. The Cowboys only have 10 sacks for the season, so this is another matchup where Quinnen Williams and the defensive front need to turn pressure into actual negative plays.

When Dallas has the ball

Dallas should start exactly where it finished in Philadelphia with Javonte Williams. The Cowboys rushed for 204 yards against the Eagles, with Williams producing 155 yards and a touchdown on 25 carries. That balance completely changed the offense. Dak Prescott only needed 27 pass attempts, was not sacked and threw three touchdowns without an interception. That is the formula Dallas has been searching for all season.

Arizona gives them every reason to continue with it. The Cardinals allowed 377 total yards and 30 points to Denver last week, including a 62-yard quarterback run and several explosive plays. Arizona entered that game at the bottom of the NFL in points allowed, so Dallas should make this defense handle Williams before spreading the field with CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens. Lamb scored twice against Philadelphia, while Pickens added another touchdown, giving Prescott several different ways to attack once Arizona starts committing defenders toward the run.

Now onto the predictions from your BTB writers…

Tom Ryle (4-3)

I wasn’t really surprised to see the win last week, or to see the early game issues, but the finish was better than I expected. Frankly, they need something very similar against the Cardinals for the same reasons. This is not a good Arizona team and it has been getting kicked around by the good teams it plays. If the Cowboys really expect to be taken seriously, they need another strong win. I don’t know if this is more a prediction or a hope, but I’ll say DAL 34 ARI 19.

Brandon Loree (5-2)

Since 2021, the Dallas Cowboys haven’t won a game where they’ve scored 18 points or less. Conversely, Dallas has the fifth-most wins when they score 21 or more points. 

The Arizona Cardinals seem to have the Cowboys’ number, winning the last four matchups. Arizona isn’t great defensively, but their offense can be feisty headlined by Trey McBride and Jeremiyah Love potentially coming back from injury.

I think this week’s game comes down to a Brandon Aubrey field goal, and the Cowboys are on the positive side of the stat above, winning 24-21.

Dyllan Rivers (4-2)

I feel less comfortable this week against a struggling Cardinals teams that still has some healthy playmakers on offense. Assuming he plays, the Cowboys have to eliminate running back Jeremiyah Love as well as tight end Trey McBride. I think they do just enough defensively to get the ball back to the offense to stack some scores at some point against a struggling Cardinals run defense. I’ll take Dallas 34-24

Matt Holleran (3-4)

The Cowboys showed last week they are capable of beating an inferior opponent. The Arizona Cardinals fit that billing again this week, but it’s hard to trust the Cowboys when any sort of consistency is expected. I see this being a sloppy game, but when it matters most, Dallas’ offense makes the plays they need to. The Cowboys get a win and move back to .500 on the year. Give me Dallas in a weird one, 26-24. 

Mike Poland (3-4)

The Cowboys have momentum and home field advantage here. Arizona are struggling to finish drives. They rank 22nd in the NFL in total offensive yards but rank 26th in points per game, and that’s down to the fact they rank dead last in redzone efficiency. They can move the ball to some degree through the air, but once in the redzone they fail to convert. 

On the opposite side, the Cardinals defense is allowing the most points per game, have the 11th-worst redzone efficiency, and rank 15th in third-conversion allowed. Dallas takes the win here 28-17.
