When mountain bikes first went electric almost a decade ago, the impact and emotions mirrored Bob Dylan going electric in the 60s: Initial shock quickly gave way to curiosity and a dawning realization that a genius innovation had changed our culture forever—be it rock n’ roll or bike riding.

Every revolution has leaders, and in the case of e-bikes you can count Aventon among the key pioneers. The company has been there since the beginning, expanding the possibilities of our singletrack adventures with e-bikes that bring singular vision, quality, and performance.

Why is Aventon’s Current line of e-bikes so good? Advancements in battery life, motors, and design have enabled a technological leap forward. These bikes open entire swaths of terrain to a broader set of people, keeping everyone active, outside, and engaged with both a rich adventure sport and nature itself—all through the freedom and thrill you can only get on two wheels.

Current EXP (Photo: Aventon)

Easier, Faster, Farther

All Aventon Current models feature an Ultro X motor and 800Wh integrated battery with up to a 105-mile range on a single charge. Translation: Climbs are easier and faster, enabling you to gobble up more trails and thrilling downhill runs in a fraction of the time it would take on an analog bike. This expanded range and added power acts like a time machine in the best way. Now far-flung trailheads and life-list rides lie within your grasp even when you’ve only got an afternoon to spare.

On big rides, you also get a leg up on the bugbear even the most badass cyclists can’t beat: fatigue. The intelligent assist helps keep your legs fresh while reducing strain on joints, hips, and back. You can fine-tune assist levels and pedal sensitivity via wireless, top tube-mounted or integrated top tube display.

Current LTD (Photo: Aventon)

Power Through Performance

Regardless of your riding level, you’ll see your on-trail performance spike with the mid-drive Ultro X motor at your back. A boost mode offers up to 850 watts and 120Nm of torque in boost mode, delivering precise, punchy acceleration from a standstill and smooth momentum as you whip through changing grades on long grinds. Internal torque sensors respond to your pedaling, making the bike feel like an extension of your own body no matter which assist setting you choose (Auto, Eco, Trail, Turbo, and Boost). Boost is especially fun, offering a 60-second buff to help you blast through cruxy bits, Mario-style.

A longer wheelbase and smart, do-it-all body geometry (140mm travel, 150mm fork, 29-inch wheels, 64.6-degree head, 76.6-degree seat) plants you to the dirt, offering an exceptional balance between agility and stability to tackle rooty tracks, tricky rock gardens, and steep hairpins.

The ACU Smart Bike ecosystem lets you get into the weeds for customizing everything from pedal sensitivity to power output and overrun, either onscreen or via the app. Get granular to dial in and edit your preferences—or coast on the ride tuneable Auto, which offers a balance of assist and performance for most people. A big bonus for stat freaks: The Current tracks basically any metric you could desire, from GPS data to jump tracking to ride recording. It’s also 4G enabled, meaning you can track your bike, install geofencing, or even update it in the field.

‘Is That An Aventon?’

With the Current line, Aventon doesn’t just improve performance with a seriously powerful motor, premium build quality, and flexible-yet-intuitive features. The brand also does it at a price point nearly half that of any reasonably comparable models. Within that range ($3,999-$7,999) you’ll find three trims to suit any rider. NASA Explores Potential Commercial Servicing for Hubble Telescope

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →
The Hubble Space Telescope returns to orbit as an improved telescope after its second servicing mission in February 1997. NASA

NASA issued a Request for Information Oct. 29 to learn more about industry capabilities for in-space servicing to support the agency’s Hubble Space Telescope.

“Hubble still has important science ahead of it, and we should be looking at every reasonable opportunity to keep it delivering for as long as possible,” said NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman. “If there are innovative and cost-effective ways to extend the life of an asset already on orbit, we owe it to the American taxpayer and the scientific community to explore them. Extending Hubble’s life would mean more discoveries, more science, and more opportunities to expand our understanding of the universe.”

The Request for Information seeks commercial interest in: potentially conducting a mission to boost Hubble to a higher altitude to extend its science lifetime; servicing Hubble to enhance its capabilities; or performing a safe, controlled re-entry into Earth’s atmosphere or orbital disposal at the end of Hubble’s mission.

“Hubble is a national icon that has fundamentally changed how humanity views our universe for more than 35 years,” said Nicky Fox, associate administrator, Science Mission Directorate, NASA Headquarters in Washington. “Commercial industry has rapidly evolved in recent years to offer new capabilities for in-space satellite capture and servicing. Given the time we have until Hubble’s anticipated re-entry, we are eager to explore the breadth of possibilities to boost Hubble to a higher orbit while we develop the flagship mission to follow.”

The Request for Information is available online and will remain open for responses until 3 p.m. EST, Monday, Nov. 30.

Like all spacecraft in low Earth orbit, Hubble’s altitude is slowly decreasing over time because of atmospheric drag. Hubble does not have a propulsion system to maintain its own orbit, and analysis predicts Hubble is likely to re-enter the atmosphere in the 2033 to 2034 timeframe. As such, NASA anticipates that a mission would need to take place by 2032.

The Hubble Space Telescope has been operating for more than three decades and continues to make ground-breaking discoveries that shape our fundamental understanding of the universe. Hubble is a project of international cooperation between NASA and ESA (European Space Agency). NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland, manages the telescope and mission operations. Lockheed Martin Space, based in Denver, also supports mission operations at Goddard. The Space Telescope Science Institute in Baltimore, which is operated by the Association of Universities for Research in Astronomy, conducts Hubble science operations for NASA.

Welcome to the 361st installment of A View From the Easel, a series in which artists reflect on their workspace. This week, Anna Pajak paints symmetrical will-o'-the-wisps in the woods of southern Sweden.

Want to take part? Check out our submission guidelines and share a bit about your studio with us through this form! All mediums and workspaces are welcome, including your home studio.

Anna Pajak, Tingsryd, Sweden

How long have you been working in this space?

Four years.

Describe an average day in your studio.

I like to start early, with coffee. I lay out the paint and go straight to the canvas. I often imagine that I’ll sit down and thoughtfully plan what I’m going to do, but I’m unfortunately too restless for that. The moment I enter the studio, I just want to squeeze out the paint and get going. The coffee gets cold and forgotten.

I usually listen to music while I paint, unless the painting requires too much thinking or measuring, then I need complete silence. I often work on two canvases at once, like sisters standing side by side.

I watch the sky outside my window. When the gradient is at its best from orange into light green, it’s usually time to stop for the day, before I get overtired and stop seeing clearly, or start overthinking and invite the critical voice into the room. I wash all the brushes so I can start faster tomorrow.

As you plan the tablescape upon which your Thanksgiving dinner will be presented, look to autumn’s stunning natural bounty and colors for inspiration. Earthy green, orange, and red tones can help set the scene, but natural elements like leaves, flowers, pinecones, and foliage, are what will really deliver the “natural abundance” vibe. The best part? Decor that you bring in from outdoors is compostable and inexpensive, if not free.

1. Cork Leaves

Photo: DIY Candy

If you don’t have a ready supply of real leaves, consider creating some of your own, like DIY Candy does. These were made with stencils and thin sheets of cork (both of which you can purchase at craft supply stores). In addition to adding style, these leaves also serve as handy, decorative trivets. You can also purchase premade cork leaf coasters if you don’t want to make them.

2. Good Enough to Eat

Photo: A Beautiful Mess

Short on time? According to A Beautiful Mess, a beautiful centerpiece can be assembled quickly from items found in the produce department at the grocery store: Group colorful fruits, like oranges, tangerines and pomegranates. Sprinkle green pistachios and sprigs of fresh rosemary along the middle of the table to create the illusion of a decorative runner. When dinner is over, invite your guests to partake of the fruit and nuts as a post-feast treat, or save the tastiest morsels and place them in your kitchen fruit bowl. 

3. Eucalyptus

Photo: A Night Owl Blog

The curves of a pumpkin’s stem makes it a natural choice for tabletop decor. Give your simple squash an unexpected twist, as A Night Owl blog does, by attaching a piece of eucalyptus and a nametag to the stem and fashioning a place card. You can purchase faux eucalyptus stems to use for your DIY decor year after year.

4. Harvest Array

Photo: A Piece of Rainbow

Butternut squash is probably in season now at your local farmers market. Bring this culinary hero to the table by featuring it in a Thanksgiving centerpiece, as blog A Piece of Rainbow does here. When placed in a nest of moss and pinecones, it makes a rustic statement.

5. Floating Fruit

Photo: The Crafty Blog Stalker

This simple yet stunning idea from Cherished Bliss isn’t the typical way of serving Thanksgiving cranberries, but it’s certainly stylish. Float a tea light candle atop a handful of fresh cranberries in a water-filled vase. For extra pizzazz, add a sprig of eucalyptus to the water and dip the base of the vase in metallic paint. Or, skip the extras and let the bright berries shine on their own!

6. Fall Fruit

Photo: The Crafty Blog Stalker

The autumn harvest days spent at the local apple orchard might be behind us, but America’s favorite fruit still has a place in your Thanksgiving festivities. Place apples (always in groups of three) of any variety at different places along your table runner, and fill in with pillar candles and faux or real greenery for a rich display.

7. Pumpkin-Free Decorating

Photo: Cupcakes and Cashmere

If you’re burned out on pumpkin-themed decor and pumpkin spice-flavored everything, take a cue from Cupcakes and Cashmere blogger: Swap out the ubiquitous squash for an unexpected edible. Persimmons, for example, are of a similar size, shape, and color but bring a beauty to the table that is distinctly their own.

8. Put Pears to Work

Photo: RemodelaCasa

Cristina Garay at RemodelaCasa uses pears as a beautiful addition to a natural Thanksgiving tabletop display. Add it to an edible centerpiece or place it atop your guest’s place, on its own or with a name card, as an extra touch of hospitality. If, by lucky coincidence, someone has sent you some Harry & David pears for the holiday, why not put them to work before eating them?

9. Paint Your Pumpkins

Photo: It All Started with Paint

Russet, crimson, mustard, and brown: These colors typify the Thanksgiving tablescape, but that doesn’t mean you can’t think outside the box when it comes to your holiday centerpiece. Follow the lead of the blog It All Started with Paint and paint seasonal squash in the color palette of your choice to marry tradition with contemporary style for a look that captures your personality. Or, purchase a set of painted faux pumpkins to use for your holiday table settings every year.

10. Autumn Leaves

Photo: StoneGable

Nature offers an abundance of color this time of year, so use it to your advantage. At the StoneGable blog, napkins are topped with golden-hued leaves, which you can buy online or pluck from your lawn. The centerpiece of tall and short vases and white pumpkins carry through on the theme.

11. Succulents and Seasonal Plants

Photo: Design Dazzle

Design Dazzle’s basket of seasonal bounty may look like a headache to assemble, but many of the makings of this Thanksgiving display can be found in your own garden beds. If you do choose to forage in your backyard for your tabletop display, be sure to shake, wash or brush off the greenery, then let it sit for a couple of days before placing it on the table.

12. Amazing Maize

Photo: The Crafty Blog Stalker

Normally hung on a door this time of year, Indian corn can be a beautiful addition to the Thanksgiving Day table. Here, the corn is placed atop plates, along with a seasonal card, to bring color and life to neutral stoneware.

13. Adorn Yours With Acorns

Photo: Hello Little Home

The squirrels in your backyard already know the secret: Acorns are everywhere at this time of year, so there’s no reason not to bring them to the harvest table. Use them as fillers in glass containers or, if you are ready to recreate this DIY craft from Hello Little Home, make acorn napkin ties like those shown here.

14. Waves of Grain

Photo: Carolyn’s Homework

Golden-hued wheat is certainly a symbol of the season and the Thanksgiving table. Create miniature sheaves bound with ribbon, as did the blogger at Carolyn’s Homework, or simply use the wheat to create a casual centerpiece of candles, gourds and fruit.

17. Pumpkin Place Names

Photo: Edalin via Adobe Stock

A pie pumpkin, placed atop a dinner plate, makes a wonderful place marker for the Thanksgiving table. Using a paint marker, write your guests’ names on the front of a pumpkin, and set it carefully at each person’s place. It’s a simple gesture but makes for a natural gift they can take with them when they leave and enjoy again, when added to their own homemade centerpieces. 

18. Birds of a Feather

Photo: Carolyn’s Homework

The traditional holiday bird won’t be the only scene stealer this season, when you add feather and cork place cards to your Thanksgiving table. Feathers, corks, and ribbon can be purchased at your local craft store; bind them together to create the look, then watch the expression on everyone’s faces when they marvel at your crafty tablescape.

15. Bold Blooms

Photo: Homemade by Carmona

Bright and cheery, let a sunflower bloom greet each guest when they sit down at the table. Place one inside a folded napkin atop each guest’s plate. For less formal buffet dinners, float a couple flowers in a bowl of water, then place the bowl on a tray filled with rolls of silverware. When your guests go through the line, they’ll see the sunny arrangement and smile.

16. Fallen Leaves

Photo: Leonid Iastremskyi via Adobe Stock

For a touch of whimsy, sprinkle a handful of fallen leaves over your tablescape. Not for neat freaks, this fanciful display evokes a walk in the woods. However, if you’re playing host to grandma (who never did quite approve of your slapdash cleaning routine), nix this one from your Thanksgiving idea file.

17. Fall Branches

Photo: Leonid Iastremskyi via Adobe Stock

Branches create a natural centerpiece, and a dramatic statement in this country chic home. If you still have trees and shrubs with autumn-colored leaves, consider pruning a selection to decorate your Thanksgiving Day table. Just remember to keep them a reasonable height for dining, or remove them when the meal is served.

18. Print Your Decor

Photo: Kiki & Company

Evoke the beauty of nature, without giving the kids ammo with which to torture their cousins: This Thanksgiving printable from Kiki & Company makes an adorable addition to the kids’ table and lends a little fanciness to the little ones’ big meal.

19. Flowers Abound

Photo: A Beautiful Mess

This modern take on a table runner proves that order and symmetry might be overrated. Ruch and wrinkle your runner just so, and nestle vases of bright flowers and little pumpkins among the folds of the fabric for a perfectly imperfect arrangement.

20. Light Your Gourds

Photo: The 36th Avenue

Even after Halloween, pumpkin-carving season persists. Make these clever little votive holders by hollowing out a mini pumpkin and placing a tea light inside. Leave the outside of the pumpkin natural, or gild it with gold-colored paint. The 36th Avenue has the tutorial.

21. Pinecones on Plates

Photo: Pixel-shot via Shutterstock

Sometimes simple is best. This understated table setting pairs a single pinecone with dinner plate and charger for an autumnal display that’s perfect for Thanksgiving.

22. Bright and White

Photo: The 36th Avenue

Pumpkins and gourds make great table decorations at a big fall meal. That includes white pumpkins, too!  Here, a grouping of faux white pumpkins and other natural-hued elements set a distinctive Thanksgiving Day table, but it would look just as nice with naturally white (or painted) pumpkins.

23. Walnut Place Cards

Photo: Cupcakes and Cashmere

Walnuts and pecans are back on the menu at this time of year, so add them to your Thanksgiving table settings for a festive touch. A handful of walnuts gives a name card a boost on this rustic display.  

24. Leaves and Light

Photo: Lemon Thistle

Illumine the simple beauty of natural (or faux) foliage with an arrangement of white pillar candles of varying heights. When the candles start to drip on the kraft paper tablecloth, you’ll know it’s time for pie.

25. Wood Slice Chargers

Photo: Stonegable

A wooden charger brings a rustic sensibility to a Thanksgiving table of crystal and silver. You can make your own from wood rounds available at most craft stores. Apply wood stain to the rounds or leave them plain for a touch of woodland whimsy. 

26. Edible Arrangement

Photo: Stonegable

Another edible arrangement, this one brings together winter seasonal vegetables and fruit (like cabbage and pomegranates) with other colorful picks like bell peppers, globe grapes, and red radishes. Place your produce in a wooden trough or long basket and fill in with parsley and greenery for a unique centerpiece that you can turn into next week’s salad.

27. Evergreen

Photo: In Honor of Design

Who says evergreens are only for Christmas? Break out the boughs early and fashion them into a delightful place marker. This one from In Honor of Design is made from a couple sprigs of rosemary tied with string.

28. Couple Copper with Copper

Photo: Homey Oh My

Nature and artifice combine in this stunning Thanksgiving tablescape. Clear and copper candle holders are complemented with white pumpkins adorned with stripes copper-colored tape, while a patterned table runner continues the theme. 

29. Board Runner

Photo: Blooming Homestead

A study in sturdiness, this table runner from Blooming Homestead is nothing more than a pine board stained an elegant dark brown. When laid atop the Thanksgiving table and topped with pinecones, adornments, and a vase full of wheat, it evokes and warmth and cheer.

30. Mighty Mums

Photo: A Beautiful Mess

Mums are the official flower of fall, so give them a spot at the Thanksgiving table. Place small vases of the blooms all along the length of the table for a short centerpiece that’s easy to peer over but still makes a big—and beautiful—statement.

31. A Flower at Every Seat

Photo: Courtesy Native Poppy

Flowers don’t always have to be arranged in a vase. San Diego-area florist Native Poppy recommends laying a mini-bouquet of dried flowers at every place setting, which guests could then take home as favors. Fresh-flower bouquets are just as pretty, as are single stems in a seasonal color. Placing flowers at each place setting also eliminates the problem of trying to hold a conversation over a tall centerpiece—and probably costs a lot less than that tall centerpiece, too.

In the MFA Fine Arts program at Otis College of Art and Design, we believe artists thrive when they can explore a wide range of approaches in artmaking and all its related disciplines. 

Our program provides an artistic learning environment where artists of different backgrounds and creative practices can flourish together. Our courses, studios, labs, and galleries are welcoming places where everyone in our community can develop as artists and as engaged participants in society.

MFA students have 24/7 access to individual working studios. They are also guaranteed at least one semester of a Graduate Teaching Internship or Graduate Assistantship. Students receive access to painting, sculpture, and lighting studios; state-of-the-art wood and metal shops; photography, video, film, printmaking, and ceramics labs. A mid-residency show, annual open studios, and a final thesis show offer ample opportunities for exhibition over the course of the two-year program. 

Distinguished alumni include Carlos Almaraz, Alonzo Davis, Roberto Gil de Montes, Judithe Hernandez, Vita Kari, Alison Saar, Masami Teraoka, and Kent Twitchell.

The Einstein Visiting Artist Lecture Series at Otis College brings distinguished artists, designers, and creative thought leaders to campus to engage with students and the public. Past guests have included Doug Aitken, Kelly Akashi, Dawoud Bey, Njideka Akunyili Crosby, Theaster Gates, Trenton Doyle Hancock, Edgar Heap of Birds, Shirin Neshat, Hank Willis Thomas, Jeffrey Vallance, Kara Walker, Zoe Whitley, and Anicka Yi, among others.

Priority deadline for fall entry is January 15 and October 1 for spring. After these deadlines, applications will be accepted on a rolling basis as space remains available. 

To apply, visit otis.edu

Strawberry Shortcake is back in Berryville, this time looking more like her '80s dolls, with a bakery and a familiar cat. The first two episodes of Strawberry Shortcake’s Bake Shop arrived on YouTube on Oct. 16. The new animated series is designed for children ages 4 to 8. In the show, Strawberry runs a neighbourhood bake shop where her friends drop by to lend a hand, grab a snack or spend time together. The episodes are three minutes long, making it perfect for short breaks when mom needs a quiet moment.

What parents need to know

Episodes are available on the official Strawberry Shortcake channel on WildBrain’s YouTube network, according to WildBrain. The company says the series will include 26 episodes in total, rolling out over the next year. The season will also introduce eight new songs. The show brings Strawberry back to her strawberry-shaped home in Berryville, alongside her cat, Custard. Its stories centre on baking, friendship and the neighbourhood community, with the characters moving between the bakery, Strawberry’s upstairs apartment and her garden. WildBrain describes the series as a new CG-animated take on the character, who has been part of the brand for more than 40 years.
