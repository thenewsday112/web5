We asked Dave Marshall to give us his match ratings and comments on individual Newcastle United players after Everton 3 Newcastle 1 in the Carabao Cup.

It was there for the taking.

The first of a home-and-away doubleheader four days apart with Everton, this one for a spot in the Carabao Cup quarter-finals, a date at a League Two squad who are just two points above the drop zone to non-league.

With the return a Premier League match at St. James’ Park on Monday, 2 November.

Yes, Fleetwood Town knocked out Premier League champs Arsenal on Tuesday, but Newcastle supporters thinking the chances of lightning striking twice wouldn’t be great.

Now, it’ll be Everton supporters thinking that.

Newcastle started brightly, with plenty of chances in the first half as Everton’s defence was all over the place. But not hitting the back of the net on any of them, something that would come back to haunt them.

Newcastle dominated possession and pressured double-Mackem Pickford much of the night but to no avail.

Despite Everton taking over a quarter of the match to find their footing, their commitment to defending their goal was a stark contrast to Newcastle’s.

Listening to Matthew Raisbeck and John Anderson on the radio commentary while watching on TV, it seemed like every decent United shot on goal was followed by, “It’s blocked by the defender.”

As shaky as Everton were at times in the defensive structure or organisation, the commitment to lay their bodies on the line to defend their goal was something to watch.

And it was missing from the United defenders for most of the night.

Yes, Newcastle had chances. Even a decent penalty shout that went unheeded. But the commitment to mark Everton on their counter, or to close down shots, or to throw themselves in front of shots, was mostly missing.

As a Newcastle United fan, seeing the opponent with more commitment to the cause is more than a concern. It’s unforgivable. That’s what United has always been famous for: commitment and effort. It’s not that the players gave no effort. But there was little commitment.

It got to a point that anytime United failed to put away a chance or even create a good one, you’d be forgiven for expecting the resulting counter to result in an Everton goal. Because the commitment to get back and shut down a Toffee attack wasn’t there.

United players and supporters may be correct in assessing referee Farai Hallam’s performance as subpar, especially on Barnes’s shout for a penalty. Yes, he was all over the place. Soft fouls given, other more obvious ones not given, no consistency on playing advantage, etc. In fact, radio analyst and former toon player Anderson kept asking if the ref had ever played football before, with Raisbeck answering that he swore he’d played for Stevenage

But that’s not an excuse for a lack of defensive commitment. Or for not playing to the whistle when the ref has given advantage for the first time all game to give up the second goal. Or for flubbing all the chances in front of the Everton goal.

All resulting in another multi-goal defeat on the road. United’s away form hasn’t improved under Matthias Jaissle.

And that’s worrisome.

Remote code execution on Linux systems with multipath TCP

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

On October 27, LLM provider Anthropic reported on a remotely exploitable use-after-free bug affecting systems with multipath TCP listeners open. The bug works by triggering a response to a retransmitted SYN packet at the same moment that the TCP connection is established in another thread, causing the first thread to access the connection's options structure after the second thread has freed it. The first thread can then disclose kernel memory or cause a buffer overflow, writing an attacker-controlled payload. The fix was to only free the structure after an RCU grace period. On October 29, the Go security team advised that all programs written in Go use multipath TCP by default, so many systems may be unknowingly vulnerable. And, of course, there is always the possibility of related bugs in other sections of the TCP stack. Users may be wise to update to a patched kernel as soon as one becomes available for their distribution.

A powerful psychedelic compound best associated with the Sonoran desert toad (Incilius alvarius) has been recreated in a lab and transformed into a fast-acting nasal spray. A recent clinical trial outlined this week in the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) suggests this spray could help people with treatment resistant depression, without the often less pleasant element of psychedelics. In the study, a single squeeze of the spray eased patients’ depressive symptoms with benefits lasting throughout the eight week trial. 

Crucially, those long-term benefits (which have also been observed in other psychedelic research) didn’t require enduring an all-day mystical trip. Most patients were cleared for release less than two hours after taking the spray. Thankfully, the approach also eliminates the need to fondle a toxic amphibian. That’s potentially a plus both for patients and overharvested toad populations. The finds come amid a resurgence in new research and investment involving psychedelics, supported in part by evolving political attitudes towards the compounds on federal and state levels. 

From toad glands to synthetic nasal spray: a psychedelic journey 

The nasal spray was developed by biotech firm AtaiBeckley. Its  active ingredient is called mebufotenin, which is also known as 5-MeO-DMT. The compound occurs naturally in several places, but it is most famously associated with the secretions of the Sonoran desert toad. For decades, amateur psychonauts have sought out these meaty North American toads for the prominent glands located on the amphibian’s head and hind legs. Squeezing those glands releases a milky secretion (sometimes inaccuracy referred to as venom), which when dried and then vaporized, produces an intense psychedelic experience. Many people who have smoked it describe a profound sense of oneness or ego dissolution, and some even report the sensation of dying. 

Though based on the same compound, the drug in the nasal spray is synthetic and made in a lab. The researchers wanted to see if a controlled, clinical dose of 5-MeO-DMT could deliver some of the compound’s reported therapeutic benefits while keeping the overall treatment brief, especially compared with longer-acting psychedelics like psilocybin. They  limited their study to 193 adults between the ages of 18 to 75 with treatment-resistant depression. The participants, who came from the United States, Europe, and Australia, had each already tried at least two antidepressant treatments without success. By some estimates, around a third of all patients experiencing major depressive disorder also have treatment resistant depression. 

Each participant received nasal spray in either a low (.3mg), medium (8mg), or high (12mg) dose. Neither the patient, nor the clinical staff or researchers involved knew which dose was given. The most intense psychedelic effects wore off relatively quickly, regardless of the dose. Clinicians checked on the patients at regular intervals to determine if they were deemed safe enough to leave the clinic. Among participants who received the 8-milligram dose, 85 percent were ready for discharge within two hours, compared with 78 percent of those who received the 12-milligram dose.

However, the therapeutic effects of the spray appeared to persist far longer.  Patients who received the medium or high dose showed significantly greater improvement than the comparison group as early as two days after treatment, and that advantage held through the end of the eight-week study. The team measured depression with a clinical scale measuring from 0 to 60, with  higher scores indicating more severe symptoms. After four weeks, the 12 mg group had dropped an average of 11.1 points on that score. The 8mg group dropped an even higher 12.5 points while the .3mg group dropped 5.5 points. These findings suggest the nasal spray application could provide some of the same therapeutic effects of other scheduled therapies less less need to have healthcare workers monitoring patients for many hours at a time. 

“In adults with treatment-resistant depression, single doses of intranasal mebufotenin benzoate were well tolerated and produced rapid, robust, and durable reductions in depressive symptoms,” the study authors write. “Intranasal mebufotenin benzoate may improve treatment access and convenience, requiring a shorter monitoring period and fewer clinic visits compared with psilocybin and esketamine, respectively.” 

Psychedelic research gains momentum…and money 

This experimental nasal spray arrives amid a transitionary period for psychedelic research. State governments have shown more interest in the field in recent years, with Oregon and Colorado each launching their own psilocybin programs. Even Texas, a state traditionally associated with a tough on drugs approach, just committed $50 million to research the powerful psychedelic ibogaine. Signs of movement are occurring at the federal level as well. In April, President Donald Trump signed an executive order aimed at accelerating research and FDA approval of psychedelic-based therapies. 

Though it attracted significant attention, the executive order is really more signaling than action. It notably does not lower the bar a therapeutic needs to reach before archived FDA approval. 

Still, pharmaceutical companies are clearly taking note. Earlier this year, Eli Lilly, the largest pharmaceutical company in the world, acquired AtariBeckley for $2.8 billion. AtaiBeckley developed the nasal spray used in the recent JAMA study.

The Met Museum and Vacheron Constantin Unveil Artworks Produced Through New Residency

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Earlier this month at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the three artists chosen for the Artisan Residency Programme, a collaboration between the museum and Vacheron Constantin, unveiled the works that each has created through the program.

The Swiss luxury watch company launched the 18-month residency program with the Met in May 2025, making the third such partnership for the company. (The company also has partnerships with the Louvre, dating to 2019, and the Palace Museum in Beijing, established in 2024.)

The program is meant to pass on, preserve, and reinterpret traditional craft techniques. As part of the program, artists spent considerable time at both the Met and the Vacheron Constantin Manufacture in Geneva, and then used those experiences as launching points for their own works. The three artists selected were Aspen Golann, Ibrahim Said, and Joy Harvey.

For the residency, Golann produced a monumental wooden vessel adorned with nature motifs carved in bas relief. For the piece she drew inspiration from a series of wooden chests from the 17th to the 19th century in Connecticut and Pennsylvania. Those works and other similar ones in the Met’s collection exemplified a neoclassical New England style that informed her work, titled Vessel. During her time at the Manufacture, Golann worked with artists on enameling and engraving techniques.

Said’s work, Shaping Time, is a three-tiered vertical sculpture also adorned by relief carvings, as well as piercings and glazed surfaces that draw upon ancient Egyptian and Islamic objects in the Met’s collection. At the Manufacture, Said also worked with the artisans on enameling and engraving, as well as lacquer painting.

Harvey’s Ruined splendour is a bronze diadem composed of forged rhomboid bronze interlaced with panels inspired by the fine filigree seen on ancient Greek necklaces. After creation the work was pierced with rods in order to create its “ruination.” For the work, Harvey drew on ancient jewelry in the Met collection from Greece, India, and China, and then spent time in the Manufacture learning guillochage, a technique for engraving intricate patterns like leaves and flowers onto metal.

In a statement, Met CEO Max Hollein called the program “a collaborative initiative that embodies our mutual commitment to supporting artistic innovation and cultural dialogue.”

With less than a month left until the 61st Venice Biennale closes on November 22, the United States pavilion commissioner Jenni Parido has pleaded for additional donations to ship Alma Allen's sculptures out of Italy.

This week, the New York Times reviewed a letter by Parido, who founded the American Arts Conservancy (AAC) and commissioned Allen's exhibition in Venice. She told potential donors that the Trump administration-aligned arts nonprofit was “facing an urgent funding need associated with the final deinstallation and safe return of the artworks to the United States.”

The time-sensitive fundraising effort falls in line with Parido's financial model for this year's Biennale presentation. After months of opacity surrounding the selection process and funding ventures for the pavilion, Parido told Hyperallergic in April that Allen's exhibition, Call Me the Breeze, received no “corporate or foundation funding,” and that it was facilitated through the “generous support of private citizens who believe in the importance of American artists and cultural exchange.” The AAC also had an online donations portal to help fund the pavilion ahead of the Biennale's opening in May.

New appeals court ruling could end Sunday Ticket

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Yes, the NFL could "deconstruct" Sunday Ticket in the next wave of broadcast deals. The smart move could be to kill it immediately.

One of the strangest aspects of the NFL's reaction to the $4.7 billion verdict in the class action attacking the pricing of Sunday Ticket was the league's decision to continue to proceed with the same approach to the out-of-market package. The trial judge's decision to throw out the 12-figure verdict (which would have become a 13-figure judgment) seemed to embolden the NFL in this regard, but the decision to wipe out the damages award did not erase the jury's finding that the NFL had violated the antitrust laws.

The argument against Sunday Ticket was fairly simple. The league historically required its Sunday Ticket partner to impose a charge that was high enough to prompt many consumers who would have wanted to watch out-of-market games to settle for the in-market options provided by CBS and Fox.

The lengthy trial transcript (which we obtained and read not long after the verdict was entered) is replete with evidence of the NFL walking a tightrope between maximizing the value of the CBS/Fox packages and the Sunday Ticket offering. Multiple witnesses confirmed that CBS and Fox had open contempt for the Sunday Ticket package, and that the pricing was specifically designed to mollify the networks that were paying enormous fees for the ability to broadcast games in any given market.

But the Sunday Ticket package endures, with ongoing exposure to fresh claims covering the period of time not encompassed by the pending class action. The violation, frankly, continues for each and every Sunday of the football season.

It's somewhat surprising that the NFL has yet to be enjoined from continuing with the lingering antitrust violation. The only recourse is for additional plaintiffs in additional class actions to seek reimbursement for the exorbitant prices they have been charged in order to protect the NFL's relationship with CBS and Fox.

It seems inevitable that the NFL will pull the plug on the Sunday Ticket package, either as a result of a court order or to stop the financial bleeding. The current deal with YouTube runs through 2029. Come 2030 (if not sooner), Sunday Ticket could be replaced with another method of making out-of-market games available to consumers.

The new approach will definitely be cheaper. It possibly could result in the out-of-market Sunday afternoon games being available on a variety of cable channels and/or streamers, with the in-market games still carried by CBS and Fox.

Thus, while today's development in federal appeals court represents very bad news for the league, the end result could be very good news for consumers.

Use this hidden search feature to delete duplicate contacts

Credit: Pranay Parab

If you have the same number or email address saved under multiple contacts, you can easily clear these from your iPhone. iOS shows you a prompt at the top of the list of contacts that lets you remove duplicate contacts easily. The same feature is hidden in the Mac version of Contacts. To try it, open Contacts on the Mac, and select the Card menu at the top of the screen. Then, click Look for Duplicates. This will identify duplicate contacts for you, and you can quickly merge or delete them.

Try the two-finger swipe gesture to select multiple contacts

It's pretty easy to select multiple entries when you use the Contacts app on the Mac. You can hold the Shift or Ctrl buttons and use arrow keys or click multiple contacts to select them all. On your iPhone or iPad, this feature is available via a hidden gesture. You need to use two fingers and swipe upwards or downwards on the list of contacts. Once you get the gesture right, you'll be able to select multiple contacts quickly. After you select one batch of contacts, you can scroll up or down and use the same gesture to add more contacts to your selection. This is the fastest way to execute bulk actions in your iPhone's Contacts app.

Copy a contact and paste it in Messages to share phone numbers quickly

When you share a contact, iOS shows you a prompt to select the fields you wish to send. This is great for privacy because you don't want to send your partner's home address, birthdate, your anniversary, nicknames, or other personal details by default. However, this extra step adds a little bit of friction while sharing contacts. If you want a faster way to send a contact on your iPhone, you can copy and paste it in the Messages app. To try it, open the Contacts app and scroll till you see the contact's name. Hold the contact's name and press Copy. Open Messages, paste it in any chat, and send it. Unfortunately, this doesn't work with third-party messaging apps like WhatsApp, but it's great for texts and iMessage.

Hold the message button in a contact card to switch to a different messaging app

On your iPhone, you can use Contacts to message people on WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, or other apps. Just open a contact and hold the messaging button. Hold the call button to change the app used to call a person

Just as you can use Contacts to message people on third-party apps, you can also use it to call them on those apps. Go to any contact in the Contacts app on your iPhone, and hold the call button. This will let you call them on WhatsApp, Signal, or any other apps where their phone number is linked.

Use the Mac app to export your contacts and back them up offline

Use smart lists to filter contacts on your Mac

Credit: Pranay Parab

If you added a number recently, such as a salesperson at a car dealership, but forgot their name, the Contacts app doesn't make it easy to find the contact. Instead of scrolling through the entire list of contacts and trying to guess which one could be the person you spoke to, you can use the Contacts app on your Mac to locate them quickly. It lets you create smart lists to sort contacts by recently added. Open Contacts on your Mac, and go to File > New Smart List. Now select Card in the first drop-down menu, followed by has changed in for the second one. You can then add the number of days, such as 10 days, in the final field, and click OK. Click the newly created smart list in the left pane, and you'll see all contacts that were either created or updated in the last 10 days, which will include the hypothetical car salesperson.
