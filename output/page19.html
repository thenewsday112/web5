Tonight’s mission is close the gap in the standings. How do you approach the match?

Cardinale issues bold comments ahead of the derby: “My goal is to bring Milan back to the top”

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

By: Oliver Fisher

AC Milan owner Gerry Cardinale has reiterated his backing of head coach Ruben Amorim, and spoke about a desire to invest more in the squad.

Milan are looking for a bounce-back after the midweek defeat against Bologna, a game that they led 2-1 at half-time but eventually lost 4-2 after a second-half collapse that stunned the San Siro crowd, who eventually jeered the team off.

The result is that Ruben Amorim chose to make six changes from the team that lost to the Rossoblu three nights ago, which signified a desire to change things up after a performance that was also conditions by an injury crisis.

Cardinale spoke to Sky not long before kick-off at San Siro to give his thoughts ahead of a crucial game in the scheme of the season, with his comments relayed by MilanNews.

What are your feelings?

“It’s incredible to see all these people, I couldn’t ask for anything better: it’s truly the best for football, it’s going to be a great night.”

Yesterday you had a dinner with the club’s legends: what was the significance of that?

“My goal is to bring Milan back to the top, and obviously, to do that, we need content. When I arrived here, I immediately understood the importance of this, and obviously the legends who built Milan are the foundation of this.

“We want to do more and more in this regard, and we also want to create partnerships with the past to take Milan back to where we want to be in the future.”

Amorim was your choice…

“Yes, he’s my coach… We have a great relationship, he’s doing a great job with the team, with the players. He’s truly a coach, he’s a manager: he’s helping the players grow, and that’s what I want. I’m happy with the choice I made.”

Photos by RedBird and Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

How will you move on the market?

We want to be active for a long time: I want and will always want to be active in every transfer window, trying to take advantage of every opportunity that arises.

“As I said and always repeat publicly when I took ownership of this club in June 2022, I want to bring it back to the top of Italian and European football. We are doing our best, and an announcement will be made soon.”

What did you think the first time you were told that the stadium would be shared with Inter?

“The new stadium is truly a partnership between Milan and Inter, and I have the utmost respect for Inter. That said, we want to win tonight.

“There’s a partnership for the stadium, and I, for my part, want to make the most of my experience gained in the United States, and therefore of the financial aspect we’ll put on the table as Milan, to ensure this stadium is the best.

“Bringing the best of our experience in the United States not only to Milan, but also to Serie A, compared to what it has always been.”

Destiny 2 sure had a once-in-a-lifetime announcement this year. Three or four of them, in fact. 2026 has been a decade of a year for the game, including death, swan song, glorious rebirth, and an afterlife.

This week, Bungie detailed all it aims to bring back to the game after being cut in the past years. It’s certainly ambitious, and the studio doesn’t seem too daunted by that prospect—though having the backing of millions of players can’t hurt.

The return of the missing content by itself isn’t the key takeaway, though. For the first time in years, Bungie’s new leadership seems finally at liberty to communicate openly and sincerely with the Destiny 2 community. No half-concealed words or conveniently ignoring difficult topics, instead embracing the tough questions and finally building momentum.

Adopting this stance earlier could have meant Destiny 2 wouldn’t need awe-inspiring support from hundreds of thousands of fans to be saved. Maybe it wouldn’t need saving at all.

It’s refreshing to see the new decision-makers at Bungie being so candid, in particular about hard topics. Of course, saving the game from death through community support earns heaps of goodwill to your fans. But for the first time in a couple of years, the relationship doesn’t feel transactional, dismissive, or outright antagonistic.

Bungie’s new leadership certainly deserves credit for steering the ship during this comeback. They’re bound to run into some colossal challenges, especially once you look at the extent of everything they plan on reinstating.

But let’s face it: it wasn’t that hard to do the right thing in the first place.

I’m not saying this to hate on the new leadership (and I’m using this term broadly to define the people with decision-making power). Head of product Josh Deane, new studio head Poria Torkan, and others have absolutely been doing a great job fostering goodwill, being transparent about its big-picture plans, and sharing timelines, in no small part with the help of community manager Dylan Gafner.

Because of this new direction, I finally have faith in Bungie for the first time since The Final Shape in mid-2024. Not just because they’re doing the right things, but because, unlike the previous leaders, they’re not doing the wrong things—and for Bungie, that’s often just as important.

When you look at it, a lot of the studio’s shortcomings were self-inflicted. Siphoning resources from Destiny 2 for an uncertain bet on the nascent Marathon caused a schism that might be irreparable to the community, and the eldest of the two titles undoubtedly suffered for it. The vision for The Edge of Fate seemed borderline sadistic, cranking the power grind back to 11 and branding it as content.

You might have done too much of this. Image via Bungie

Complaints about obvious pain points would go unanswered, particularly after The Edge of Fate. Players were begging for any change, but Bungie’s inaction seemed to show it was unable—or worse, unwilling—to put out fires, instead letting the house burn down. The studio appeared to either not understand or not care about the overwhelming feedback, which feels like a special skill all on its own. It’d clean house at the “worst superpower” contest, that’s for sure.

Compare it to now. One end-of-service announcement, a killer update, and millions of displays of support later, Deane told Destiny 2 fans not to buy Marathon as a donation to keep the developer afloat because “It’s our job to show up for you.” If I saw a certain CEO writing that a couple of years ago, I would’ve assumed they were either hacked, under duress, or aiming for gold at the World Series of Saying Things They Don’t Mean.

Fans sent the message loud and clear that the previous approach was unsustainable, which is why Destiny 2 is back from the dead and equipped with new people at the helm. We’ve spent years telling Bungie what not to do, and the new leadership is finally listening and taking it to heart. That’s another skill on its own, of course, especially in a community as varied as Destiny 2‘s.

Comms are cautious yet candid. The old Bungie might have blurred dates with vague “in a future update,” but the latest announcement is clear: don’t expect the unvaulting until at least early 2028. It’s imperfect and far off and difficult and not what some may have hoped to hear. But it’s real.

I’m unabashedly excited for new content for Destiny 2 again, which I haven’t been since Heresy. I don’t mind that I’ll have to wait a year and change for it. I know this time, at least it will be done right. There’s also the possible Destiny remaster and whatever’s in the future of the franchise.

It’s hard not to wonder what would have happened if maybe leadership had changed sooner, before apathy became the norm. It would have been a lot easier to wait two years if the process had started two years ago, so we’d be reaping the benefits of it now—and, as a bonus, we wouldn’t have had to watch Destiny 2 agonize.

I wish the good news hadn’t come at the cost of the game dying, but I’m glad we’re getting anything, even if it’s a bit far off. Until then, I’m confident Bungie will surprise me along the way, and I don’t even remember the last time I felt that.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Alan Shearer has revealed he once turned down the chance to manage Cardiff City.

Shearer was interviewed by the Championship club after being relegated from the top-flight in 2009 as interim boss of Newcastle – his hometown club where he spent most of his playing career.

Shearer took interim charge of Newcastle for eight games 17 years ago, but was unable to save his beloved Magpies from relegationGetty

But the all-time leading Premier League goalscorer decided that he was done with management at that early stage, instead turning has hand to punditry with the BBC – most notably as a regular on Match of the Day.

Why did Shearer say no to Cardiff?

Explaining his decision to snub Cardiff, Shearer told the BBC: “Newcastle were in desperate trouble and I could never say no [to taking that job] – we were bottom of the league, fighting for our lives.

“I should have known that when I signed the contract on April Fool’s Day that it wasn’t going to work out!”

Following his brief spell in the St. James’ Park dugout, Shearer was initially open to staying in management – but he ultimately changed his mind.

“I always felt that I was going to go into it again and I wanted to,” he continued.

“I had a couple of interviews and got offered the job at Cardiff. And then things changed… and I thought, ‘What am I doing here?'”

Shearer stays loyal to Match of the Day

Shearer has now been part of the Match of the Day team for the best part of two decades, also working for the BBC on live coverage of the World Cup, FA Cup and more.

The 56-year-old also co-hosts The Rest Is Football alongside Match of the Day colleagues Gary Lineker and Micah Richards.

Lineker fronted Match of the Day when Shearer came onboard, but left the BBC last year.

Since Lineker’s departure, the iconic Premier League highlights show has employed a rotating presenting line-up of Kelly Cates, Mark Chapman and Gabby Logan.

Among Shearer’s fellow Match of the Day pundits is another legendary former Premier League striker, Wayne RooneyBBC

Shearer, who scored a record 260 Premier League goals for Blackburn and Newcastle, remains part of a refreshed punditry pool.

Former Tottenham boss Thomas Frank and ex-Manchester United full-back Ashley Young are among the new names involved for 2026/27.

However, the BBC have been warned to learn from the demise of Football Focus in order to ensure Match of the Day survives.

“I think, like every show in the world, if you don’t have good talent on there, then you are not going to have a good product,” said talkSPORT’s Simon Jordan in August.

“Shearer’s good. I think Wayne [Rooney] is getting much better. The digital landscape is, of course, undermining linear broadcasting.

“It is decimating linear broadcasting. And the figures that you get now of that once upon a time you’d turn on EastEnders and 20 million people would watch it, those days have gone.

“But digital is still underpinned to some extent by the credibility of mainstream media.

“And so the good pundits that land in mainstream media and do digital as well will still want mainstream media to seed some of their credibility, viability, and validity so that they can do the digital stuff.”
