Sixty of the world’s best bass anglers, plus two average Joes, have been competing on Old Hickory Lake near Nashville, Tennessee, for the last two days at the inaugural World Bass Enterprises The Champions Tournament. What’s up for grabs? The biggest purse in bass fishing history: $3.25 million. The winner will take home $1.25 million, and the other top four finishers will all take home cash prizes ranging from $100k to $500k.

The first leg of this tournament also featured a team competition: Team Stripes (MLF anglers) vs. Team Stars (BASS anglers). After two days of fishing, Team Stars took home The Champions Cup and bragging rights. Now the top 15 anglers prepare for the final day and massive cash prizes.

Kentucky’s Matt Robertson took the unofficial lead early on Day 1 and eventually dropped 20 pounds on the scales to establish a commanding lead over second-place Fisher Anya, who weighed 15 pounds, 7 ounces. To start Day 2, Robertson returned to the bottom end of Old Hickory Lake to fish the tailraces on the Cumberland River, where he boated his day-one mega-bag of smallmouth bass. 

Robertson quickly put three quality fish in the boat and was apologizing to spectators for ending his day early. Since he already punched his ticket to the final round, where weights would be zeroed, pulling another bass out of his honey hole would do more harm than good. So Robertson stopped fishing and went into full-blown guardian mode, spending the rest of the day guarding his spot. While he will start dead even with the rest of the field on Day 3, he has been on a bigger fish throughout the event. The average weight of bass he’s caught across two days comes in around 3-¾ pounds. If Robertson can boat five more of those tomorrow, he will almost certainly pull off the win and, in an instant, triple his career earnings with the $1.25 million first-place prize. 

But Ron Nelson might rain on his parade. Nelson and Robertson are two of only three anglers to fish below the Old Hickory Dam so far. The duo has shared a small area in the most congenial way, something we haven’t seen in quite a while. And while a couple of casts have separated the two, Nelson hasn’t fished where the Kentucky native caught his bass, in a great display of sportsmanship. What’s been Robertson’s response? With $1.25 million on the line, the leader encouraged Nelson to keep fishing the juice until he could secure a limit and a spot in the final day. 

Although the two have shared water all week, they have caught their bass on slightly different baits. Nelson has fished a Ned rig almost exclusively to boat his smallmouth bass, which must measure at least 18 inches to be kept here in Tennessee. And Robertson has done his damage with a small swimbait on a jighead. Each angler lets their bait drift in the current on semi-slack line to create a natural action, allowing the lures to wash around rocks and shelves into eddies where bass wait for an easy meal.

Team Stars celebrates winning the Champions Cup during day two of the 2026 World Bass Enterprises The Champions tournament. (Photo/WBE)

On the main lake, Fisher Anaya put his big Day 1 bag together by pairing a jerkbait with his forward-facing sonar. Chris Johnston tied with Justin Lucas for the biggest bass on Day 1, with both fish weighing 5 pounds and 3 ounces. Johnston’s big bass came on a buzzbait, a lure we’ve seen several anglers lock in their hand to try to catch a kicker. Chatterbaits and other vibrating jigs have gotten a lot of play, too, with anglers like Takahiro Omori and Zach Birge relying on these baits to make their push to make the cut. Lipless cranks, shallow-running crankbaits, spinnerbaits, urchins, and drop shots have also gotten a lot of airtime on the PPV broadcast. Ultimately, big bites have been hard to come by, which is no surprise to the anglers fishing and the fans watching. Fall fishing on this reservoir is notoriously tough.

This tournament is far from over. Smallmouth in tailraces below the dam can shut down at any time, and this area has been pressured the last two days. It should replenish, but even then, an angler could catch a big bag on the Cumberland River and still get locked out, forcing him to miss the weigh-in. We’ve seen this happen before—Gary Klein got locked out on the Ohio River, and it cost him the 1987 Bassmaster Classic. With barge traffic regularly working its way through the lock, this is a real concern for the anglers locking down. 

We’ve seen that Old Hickory is capable of producing a 15-pound-plus bag this week. And that could be enough to get the job done on the final day, even if one of the river rats can make it down and back. Keep in mind, while Robertson has caught fish throughout the event, he never caught a keeper below the dam in practice, impressively turning the clues he got from a couple of 16- and 17-inch non-keepers into a two-day whackfest. So fishing pressure could catch up to anglers on the Cumberland River. Either way, one thing’s for certain: someone will be a bass fishing millionaire in a matter of hours.

The Binance hack has shaken the crypto market, triggering a widespread selloff after the exchange reported roughly $6.3 billion in losses. Yet three major altcoins remain surprisingly close to their all-time highs. 

Hyperliquid (HYPE), LEO Token (LEO), and WhiteBIT Coin (WBT) are all within 17% of their previous records.

Technical analysis suggests HYPE has the strongest chance of reaching a new high. LEO is also showing bullish momentum, while WBT faces a crucial test that could determine its next move.

1. Hyperliquid (HYPE): Wall Street Deal Could Push Price Above $100

HYPE has emerged as one of the strongest performers during the selloff.

The token rallied following a deal between Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) and trade[XYZ], which operates perpetual futures markets on Hyperliquid. 

The agreement could bring regulated US trading activity onto the network, subject to approval.

HYPE daily chart. Source: TradingView

At $90.66, HYPE is just 7.4% below its September record of $97.99.

Its technical outlook is encouraging. HYPE reclaimed the important $85.19 level on October 29, accompanied by its highest trading volume since August. Its Relative Strength Index (RSI), which measures price momentum, has climbed to 65.

However, sellers have repeatedly emerged near $97.

What the price analysis suggests:

  • Bullish target: A daily close above 93–95 could push HYPE toward $97.99, followed by $104.
  • Bearish risk: Losing $85.19 could trigger a retreat toward $75.14.

2. LEO Token (LEO): Strong Rally Faces an Overheating Warning

LEO has also resisted the broader market weakness, trading at $9.46, just 9.3% below its $10.43 record.

The token broke out of a month-long trading range on October 29 after repeatedly finding support near $8.91.

But there is a warning sign.

LEO daily chart. Source: TradingView

LEO’s RSI has surged to 80, its highest since April. This indicates powerful buying momentum, although it also suggests the rally may be overheating.

The immediate obstacle is resistance around 9.49–9.60, where previous rallies have struggled.

What happens next?

  • Bullish target: A breakout above $9.60 could send LEO toward $9.80 and eventually its $10.43 ATH.
  • Bearish risk: Rejection near $9.49 could bring another decline toward $8.91.

3. WhiteBIT Coin (WBT): Can Buyers Stop the Decline?

WBT presents the riskiest outlook among the three.

Trading at $73.76, the token is 16.3% below its September record of $88. Unlike HYPE and LEO, its recent price action has been bearish.

Selling volume has surged, while the RSI has dropped toward 30, approaching oversold territory. The token has also formed progressively lower highs.

WBT daily chart. Source: TradingView

The decline comes amid heightened exchange security concerns following the Binance attack, although no direct connection to WBT’s selling pressure has been established.

Still, a potential recovery remains possible if its current support holds.

Two price scenarios stand out:

  • Bullish target: Holding the 73–75 support zone could allow WBT to recover toward $86, with $88 remaining the record to beat.
  • Bearish risk: A break below $70.48 could accelerate losses toward $65.08.

For now, HYPE appears best positioned to challenge its record. LEO needs to overcome resistance, while WBT must stabilize before a meaningful recovery becomes likely.

Seven Wall Street firms cut their Coinbase (COIN) price targets on Friday after a weak start to the fourth quarter. Trading fees reached only about $165 million through October 25, and the stock fell 6% to $150.75 before the open.

Wall Street cut its Coinbase forecasts because people are trading less crypto, and Coinbase makes most of its money from fees on each trade.

Coinbase Stock (COIN) Pre-Market Performance. Source: Yahoo Finance

Why Does $165 Million in 25 Days Worry the Banks?

A price target is an analyst’s estimate of where a stock will trade in 12 months. Trading fees made up nearly half of Coinbase’s $1.22 billion in third-quarter revenue, so a slower pace cuts the earnings estimates behind those targets.

“Transaction revenue through October 25th is approximately $165 million,” Chief Financial Officer Alesia Haas said on Thursday’s call.

Barclays went lowest, cutting to $120 from $149, or 25% below Thursday’s $160.59 close.

Mizuho, which cut to $145 from $155, projects about $607 million in Q4 transaction revenue. That is slightly above Q3’s $590 million but 11% below the $684 million consensus before the results.

Wells Fargo moved to $174 from $200, Compass Point to $165 from $187, Needham to $197 from $205, and Canaccord to $275 from $300. Jefferies, at $174 from $183, cited the expense outlook, while Mizuho flagged retail traders drifting to Robinhood.

Banks Cut Coinbase Stock Targets

Q3’s figure was already down 44% year on year, per Coinbase’s quarterly filing with US regulators.

COIN shareholders, already down 51% over the past year, now face lower targets one day after a $718.7 million headline profit built mostly on paper gains.

Why October’s Slow Start May Not Decide Coinbase’s Quarter

Early-quarter figures have undershot before. In July, Coinbase reported about $130 million in transaction revenue through July 26, according to its Q2 deck. The third quarter closed at $590 million.

Haas also said trading conditions improved in September. Coinbase lowered its 2026 adjusted expense guidance to $4.15 billion to $4.3 billion, a move Mizuho called a positive.

Profit, however, still leans on token prices. Operating income fell to $61.7 million from $480.5 million, while a $715 million unrealized crypto gain drove most of net income.

Prediction markets, where users trade contracts on event outcomes, passed $200 million in annualized revenue. Average holdings of USDC, a dollar-pegged digital token, hit a record $22 billion on Coinbase.

What Comes Next for COIN Shareholders?

The cuts landed as crypto stocks slid after the $6.3 billion Binance hack, a figure Binance itself reported. The exchange said withdrawals should resume within 48 hours.

Pre-set share sales by CEO Brian Armstrong, co-founder Fred Ehrsam, and President Emilie Choi can begin November 16, covering up to 13.6 million shares.

Washington governor rips Microsoft over TV ad in leaked audio

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson, speaking at an event in Olympia in June, called out Microsoft last week over a TV ad from a PAC the company has helped fund. (Office of the Governor Photo)

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson called out Microsoft for bankrolling a political action committee responsible for a TV ad describing state government as broken, according to newly leaked audio from a Seattle Chamber leadership conference last week.

Remarks at the Seattle Metropolitan Chamber of Commerce’s Regional Leadership Conference, held at the Suncadia resort near Cle Elum, Wash., are supposed to be off the record. But The Washington Observer, an independent newsletter on state politics, published the audio Friday, saying it was obtained from an unnamed source.

In the audio, Ferguson zeroed in on a line near the start of the ad — that in Washington, “it used to be all you needed was an idea, a dream and the guts to chase it.”

The governor repeated the words “it used to be” for emphasis, in a tone of disbelief.

“What’s up with that?” Ferguson asked the crowd of business and government leaders. “First, does anyone else in this room believe that? I sure as hell don’t.”

“I talk to people every day who have that idea, that dream. They’re chasing it and they’re getting it,” he said. He cited the record $3.5 billion that Seattle-area startups raised in the third quarter, more than triple the same period a year ago, according to PitchBook data.

The Microsoft relationship: Ferguson, who noted that he had worked as a lawyer for Microsoft, said he loves the company and respects what it does. But he seemed especially peeved that the ad ran during the Seattle Seahawks game, where it reached people across the state and those from elsewhere who might want to come here and start a company.

“If we’re partners, partners should treat partners like partners, right?” he said.

He asked the room to imagine him running a multimillion-dollar ad criticizing Microsoft. “You guys would think I lost my mind if I did that,” he said.

Ferguson called Microsoft a key partner and a member of his economic roundtable, and said he works well with the company. “But it works both ways,” he said.

Ferguson acknowledged that the state has problems. “Are there challenges? Sure,” he said. But he said he didn’t think the ad was “helpful for where we all want to go.”

He told the crowd he welcomes constructive feedback, but asked business leaders to think about how they communicate, and to talk up the state’s workforce, economy and startup funding along with its challenges.

“What we have here is so bloody special,” he said.

Ferguson, who took office in January 2025, also reminded the room that he isn’t going anywhere. “I plan on being governor for a long time,” he said, adding that he works with all of them, “Microsoft included.”

The Observer noted that $10 million, roughly what the group has left to spend, would be a good start on a campaign to run someone else for governor in 2028.

The newsletter said it released just the audio from a video of Ferguson’s remarks, to protect its source. Among those in the audience was Michael Mattmiller, who represents Microsoft in Olympia. He and Ferguson “kinda-sorta hugged it out afterward,” the Observer reported.

Mattmiller declined to comment to the Observer, and Ferguson’s office didn’t respond to the publication. Microsoft referred GeekWire’s inquiry to Washington Needs to Compete, which the company described as a growing coalition of more than 60 funders that want to keep the state competitive. GeekWire has contacted the group and the governor’s office for comment.

Who’s behind the ad: The campaign comes from Washington Needs to Compete, a PAC funded in large part by Microsoft and its current and former executives. The group has raised about $13.8 million, according to state campaign finance records, including more than $5 million directly from Microsoft.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella and former CEO Steve Ballmer each gave $250,000, and Vice Chair and President Brad Smith gave more than $500,000. Other backers include T-Mobile, F5, Alaska Airlines and Weyerhaeuser.

Washington Needs to Compete has spent about $3 million so far, according to state records. Most of it has gone to state legislative races, the Observer reported, including more than $700,000 backing Rep. Amy Walen, D-Kirkland, in the 48th District, home to Microsoft’s Redmond headquarters.

The group spent $97,000 to air the ad during the Seahawks’ Oct. 15 Thursday night loss to the Denver Broncos on Prime Video and $130,000 during their Oct. 25 Sunday night win over the Kansas City Chiefs on NBC, according to the Observer.

Washington Needs to Compete’s TV ad, which aired during two Seattle Seahawks games this month and drew Gov. Bob Ferguson’s criticism. (WA Needs 2 Compete via YouTube)

What the ad says: The spot opens with a nod to “inventors in garages,” “mom-and-pop shops down Main Street” and farmers “feeding the world,” before declaring, “In Washington, it used to be all you needed was an idea, a dream and the guts to chase it.”

Then it turns: “But today, Olympia is broken, spending more while you get less.” Jobs are leaving, the ad says, and “it’s harder than ever to afford a home and raise a family.”

It doesn’t name Ferguson or any legislation. It ends by saying Olympia needs to “quit wasting money, fix what’s broken and put Washington families first.”

Taxes and the business climate: Ferguson this year signed a 9.9% tax on household income above $1 million, known as the millionaires tax, which takes effect in 2028 and is projected to raise about $3 billion a year. Initiative 645, which would repeal it, is on the Nov. 3 ballot.

In September, Smith joined former Gov. Chris Gregoire and dozens of regional officials to launch a partnership aimed at reversing the Seattle area’s economic slide. “The warning signs are flashing on our economy,” Smith said at the time.

The region lost nearly 7,000 jobs in 2025, according to the group, and Washington has fallen from 32nd to 47th in CNBC’s ranking of states by cost of doing business since 2017.

In the same CNBC study, Washington ranks 11th overall, up from 14th last year, and fifth for its economy. WalletHub ranks Washington’s economy second in the nation, behind Massachusetts.

Washington’s gross domestic product grew faster than any other state’s in the first quarter of 2026, driven largely by its tech sector, according to federal data released in June.

Long-term plans: On its website, Washington Needs to Compete describes itself as businesses and residents building “a modern, permanent political organization” to support leaders and ideas that “strengthen our economy, improve affordability and expand opportunity,” through elections and public education.

The site lists its top five contributors as Microsoft, T-Mobile, the Washington Coalition for Responsible Taxes and Spending, F5 and Smith.

The group is saving most of its money for future campaigns, the Observer reported.
