📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It’s time to dust off one of the age-old debates of the internet era: Can a platform known for pornographic videos become a viable home for safe-for-work creator content?

Pornhub is trying to do exactly that. The popular site owned by Aylo (formerly known as MindGeek) has launched a campaign that invites creators to share videos of any type with Pornhub viewers.

In the campaign video, a broken-down car looks like the end of the road for a mother and her son. Luckily, mom explains that she learned how to change a flat by watching how-to content uploaded by pornographic actress Natassia Dreams. The car starts moving again, and viewers are left to consider whether Pornhub can really be an alternative to the likes of YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram.

We’ve asked that question before. Nearly a decade ago, friction between creators and platforms led some musicians and firearms enthusiasts to set up shop on Pornhub. As part of its current campaign, Pornhub is once again positioning itself as a solution for creators who are banned or restricted on other platforms.

There are plenty of reasons why Pornhub’s offer is worth considering. Creators typically go to the platforms that can offer access to new audiences, and porn sites have never been lacking in the scale department. Pornhub claims to reach more than 130 million users each day. Some advertisers have taken advantage of the relaxed guidelines on racy platforms like OnlyFans, and Pornhub has a built-in premium subscription creators can link to their safe-for-work uploads. The recent arrival of Shorties — TikTok-style videos on Pornhub — provide yet another distribution option.

“Pornhub is a cultural access point where creators can build an audience around the full range of who they are,” said Pornhub VP of Brand and Community Alex Kekesi in a statement. “Creators invest time, creativity, and resources into their content. Safe-for-work uploads give them another way to earn from the broader range of what they create, including content they may have published elsewhere, giving content a second life and earn more.”

Clearly, some creators have already taken Pornhub up on its offer. On the platform, the popularity of searches like “gaming,” “fitness,” “cooking,” and “travel” have trended up over the past year.

Can those searches form the foundation of a safe-for-work hub that can rival the one OnlyFans has built? Pornhub clearly thinks so, and it is hoping that its new campaign will convince creators to try a, shall we say, unusual distribution strategy.

The NFL, for reasons still neither known nor apparent, opted not to flex the game between the 2-5 Eagles and 1-4-1 Commanders out of prime time, despite multiple other compelling Sunday afternoon games.

The league did, however, flex the officiating crew out of the Sunday night game.

Via Football Zebras, Alex Moore's crew has been moved from Eagles-Commanders to Patriots-Dolphins. Shawn Hochuli's crew will work the Sunday night game.

The report, citing unnamed officiating sources, says that the change is "more about optics rather than a disciplinary action."

Whatever the precise reason, it seems to be a reaction to the various bad calls Moore's crew made in the Week 7 game between the Packers and Lions, including two questionable roughing the passer calls on the Packers and a phantom illegal hit on a defenseless player by Lions linebacker Derrick Barnes.

The optics-driven reason is simple. Moving Moore's crew out of prime time, and relegating them to a secondary 4:25 p.m. ET broadcast on CBS, avoids Mike Tirico and/or Cris Collinsworth mentioning the rough day the crew had in Detroit.

Typically, the various crews are assigned without regard to the broadcast footprint of a given game. In this specific case, it shows that the league would rather avoid further talk about the multiple mistakes that Moore's crew made.

It’s an All-Day Pool Day at Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

An all-inclusive vacation with kids should make family time easier, not leave parents juggling restaurant reservations, activity schedules, and snack requests every few hours. At Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort, Spa & Casino in the Dominican Republic, families can explore a sprawling resort, splash in pools, and enjoy activities for all ages. 

The resort is part of the Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort, Spa & Casino complex in Punta Cana, a destination known for its white-sand beaches and turquoise Caribbean waters. With family accommodations, supervised kids’ activities, and options for teens who prefer to spend time away from their parents, Lopesan Caoba Lagoon offers a setup that makes multigenerational family trips or vacations with school-age kids easier to plan.

Whether your family wants to spend most of the day in the water or mix pool time with games, dining, and evening entertainment, there are plenty of ways to make the vacation your own.

Room for the Whole Family

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Coming from a family of five, one of the biggest challenges of a resort vacation is finding a room where everyone can sleep comfortably without feeling like you’re all sharing one oversized hotel room. Lopesan Caoba Lagoon has connecting-room options that give families more space and a little privacy.

The largest family accommodation configuration combines a Resort Junior Suite Connecting room with a Family Junior Suite Connecting room. Together, the rooms offer 1,179 square feet of space for up to seven guests, with one king bed, two queen beds, a sofa bed, and two bathrooms.

That layout is especially useful for families traveling with several children, grandparents, or another family member. Two bathrooms make getting ready for dinner much less chaotic, while connecting rooms let parents stay close to younger children without everyone sharing the same sleeping space.

Before booking, confirm the exact room configuration and occupancy with the resort. Connecting rooms can be a practical alternative to squeezing everyone into a single suite, especially on a multi-night trip.

Kids’ Club and Teen Activities Give Parents a Break

Even on a family vacation, it’s nice to have a little time apart. Lopesan Caoba Lagoon offers a Mini Club for children ages 4 to 12 and a Teen Club for guests ages 13 to 17, giving kids time with peers while parents enjoy downtime.

The clubs operate from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., with meal breaks to give families flexibility to plan around breakfast, lunch, pool time, or dinner. Parents can enjoy a quiet break, book a spa treatment, or simply spend a little time together while the kids have activities of their own.

For families with younger children who aren’t quite ready for a group kids’ club, babysitting is available 24 hours a day with advance arrangements at $20 per child per hour. 

Plenty of Water Time 

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

One of the highlights of Lopesan Caoba Resort is its water features. Families can ride the waves in the wave pool, let little ones splash around in the kiddie waterpark area, splash down on the waterslides, or find a spot in the shade to enjoy one of the lagoon pools. 

My favorite was the cenote swimming pool, something I haven’t seen at other all-inclusive resorts. This tranquil area never got crowded when I visited, offering a respite from the lively swim-up bars and water aerobics sessions. 

For those looking for beach time, the property is adding a beach club, and in the meantime, guests can visit the Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort beach club. Keep in mind that seaweed can be an issue in the Dominican Republic, depending on when you visit. 

Dining Without the Daily Planning

One of the main advantages of an all-inclusive resort is not having to figure out every meal. You can plan meals around swimming and activities instead of interrupting the day to find an off-property restaurant. The breakfast buffet was massive, offering everything from fresh fruit smoothies to pastries and waffles made to your liking. They even had a whole section for kid-friendly options and Dominican favorites. 

With eight traditional restaurants to choose from, you’ll find something for every taste, from deeply flavorful Indian dishes at Indra to upscale pasta, pizza, and more at Sotto Terra. If you decide to get a babysitter for the night, definitely book a meal at the adults-only steakhouse Brassa Nova. 

Evening Entertainment for the Whole Family

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Evenings can be a highlight of an all-inclusive vacation, especially when there’s entertainment close to your room. Instead of arranging transportation or trying to keep everyone awake for a late dinner in town, families can enjoy the resort’s evening offerings and return to their rooms when the kids are ready to wind down.

The Boulevard, which connects Lopesan Caoba to Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort, offers a bowling alley, theater, ice cream shop, café, and casino for adults. We enjoyed live music performances and karaoke at the bars during our stay. 

Getting There

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Families can fly into Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ), about 20–30 minutes from Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort, depending on traffic. Private airport transfers and transportation through the resort or a third-party provider make getting to the property easy, so you won’t need to rent a car. If it’s busy when you are departing, consider using the airport’s VIP Fast Track service.

13 'Practical Magic' Baby Names Sure To Charm You

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →
Warner Bros.

Pop culture baby names don’t always age well (we’re looking at you, Daenerys), but you certainly boost your odds when you pluck a name from a beloved cult classic. And if a timely sequel just happens to arrive right before your own production does, well, even better! That’s why we’re here for Practical Magic baby names.

Alice Hoffman’s bestselling 1995 book spawned a movie by the same name, and its long-awaited sequel, Practical Magic 2, hit the big screen just ahead of the spooky season. True fans know that the saga of the love-cursed Owens women actually spans four books — and we’ve combed through them to find the best Practical Magic names for your own wee witchy woman. Of course, like any good coven, there are 13.

Sally

Sandra Bullock’s Sally is fiercely protective and somewhat magic-averse… until she isn’t. The name, originally a diminutive for Sarah, means “princess,” so it’s especially fitting for a little girl. Even better, it’s just starting to climb back up the Social Security Administration’s list of popular baby names, meaning you can jump on this name before the spell of uniqueness is broken.

Gillian

Practical Magic’s other protagonist is the free-spirited Gillian. Beautiful and impulsive, she’s Sally’s more-fun little sister whose main fault is having terrible taste in men. But let’s focus on the positive, shall we? Gillian — or Jillian — means “youthful,” making it another apropos choice for a baby. And just like sister Sally, it hits that classic-but-not-common sweet spot. Call it baby name magic!

Bridget

One of the two feisty aunts who raise the young Owens sisters, Bridget is the proper name of Jet and is one of the main protagonists in The Rules of Magic, the second book in the series. Also the name of another literary darling (that’d be Bridget Jones, of course), the Irish name conjures up pluckiness and means “power.”

Jet

Hear me out: Aunt Bridget’s nickname is a standout choice for a baby girl all on its own! I mean, if Bridget means power, what more powerful image is there than… a jet? And as played by absolute screen legend Dianne Wiest, Aunt Jet is nothing if not powerful — in both love and magic.

Frances

Frances, along with the nickname Franny, is another one of those granny-chic names that is so old-fashioned it feels modern again. (Indeed, it reached number 318 on the SSA’s most recent list of baby names — and Stockard Channing’s return to the role might help it keep soaring.) Plus, it’s wise, eccentric Aunt Frances who has one of the book's most enduring lines: “My darling girl, when are you going to realize that being normal is not necessarily a virtue? It rather denotes a lack of courage.”

Antonia

Practical and dutiful, Sally’s oldest daughter (played by Maisie Williams in the sequel) grows up to be a med student. But as a name, Antonia has a flowy, delicate feel that’s, dare we say, ethereal in nature. Sitting outside of the top 1,000 baby names, it’s a vintage name that’s more than ready for a comeback. We think it’s spellbinding.

Kylie

And you thought Kylie Jenner made this name famous! In the Practical Magic series, Kylie Owens is Sally’s youngest daughter (except, oddly, in the sequel, where she’s the elder sister), and she shares her Aunt Gillian’s flaming red hair and rebellious streak. While Kylie as a name is on a bit of a downward trajectory, it’s still a very popular choice, coming in at number 204 on the SSA list.

Maria

The original witch and matriarch of the Owens family, Maria mysteriously arrives in America in 1680 (just in time for the Salem witch trials!) and was feared because of her magical gifts. She cast the spell that ultimately cursed any man who fell in love with an Owens woman. What a legacy! As a name, Maria has enduring popularity and multiple meanings, including “beloved.”

Faith

Faith Owens is Maria’s daughter and a character in Magic Lessons, the first book chronologically in the series but the third written. Separated from her mother, Faith becomes headstrong and practices blood magic. (Can you blame her?) As a name, Faith has long been a popular choice, although it’s not usually associated with witchcraft!

Hannah

In Magic Lessons, Hannah Owens is the one who finds baby Maria, raises her, and teaches her the “nameless arts.” She even gives her the “Grimoire”: a leather-bound book of spells. Hannah means “grace” or “favor.”

Regina

In Practical Magic, Regina is Sally and Gillian’s deceased mother. While her storyline may not be a happy one, the name itself conjures up regal elegance and means “queen” or “lady.” Dare we say, it’s a magical choice.

Susanna

Aunt Frances and Aunt Jet’s mother, Susanna stars in the prequel book The Rules of Magic. You won’t find Susanna anywhere on the SSA’s list of the top 1000 baby names, so it’s a clever pick for anyone who wants a name that’s classic but not common.

April

April Owens is Regina’s mother and Sally and Gillian’s grandmother; she appears in The Rules of Magic. April comes from Latin and means “to open.” It hits that popular-but-not-too-popular sweet spot, ranking at 542 on the most recent SSA baby names list.

If there’s one takeaway from this list, it’s that the baby girl names in the Practical Magic universe are less stereotypically witchy or pop culture-trendy than they are enduringly classic and granny-chic. So tell us: Which name has cast a spell on you?

Groups ask Supreme Court to leave lower court order in place halting border wall construction in southwestern Texas

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The plaintiffs challenging the Trump administration’s efforts to build a border wall in the Big Bend region of southwestern Texas urged the Supreme Court on Thursday to leave in place an order by a federal trial judge in El Paso temporarily halting the construction. Eric Glitzenstein, a lawyer representing the plaintiffs, told the justices in a 40-page filing that granting the federal government’s request to lift that order would allow the Trump administration to “immediately resume blasting, bulldozing, road construction, and other border barrier-related construction across one of the most remote and ecologically and culturally significant landscapes in the United States.”

The dispute centers on three waivers of various environmental and conservation laws that then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and her successor, Markwayne Mullin, issued earlier this year to authorize construction of the border wall in the Big Bend region, including areas in Big Bend National Park. Noem and Mullin relied on a provision of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act of 1996 that gives the Secretary of Homeland Security the power to “waive all legal requirements” necessary “to ensure expeditious construction” of border walls and roads. With the waivers, Noem and Mullin determined that additional border barriers are required in the Big Bend area because it “is an area of high illegal entry.”

The plaintiffs in this case – an environmental group, a local landowner, and a group formed to preserve a church – went to federal court to challenge the waivers’ legality. On Oct. 2, U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone temporarily barred the Department of Homeland Security from continuing construction on the border wall in the Big Bend region “until and unless” the government complies with environmental and other applicable laws.

In a written opinion issued on Oct. 14 to explain her reasoning, Cardone concluded that the challengers were likely to prevail on their claim that under the major questions doctrine – the idea that if Congress intends to give a federal agency the power to make decisions of vast economic or political significance, it must say so explicitly – the DHS secretary did not have the authority to issue the waivers.

As an initial matter, Cardone rejected the government’s contention that she did not have the power to consider the challengers’ claims because they arose from a statute, while IIRIRA only allows federal courts to hear challenges to waiver decisions if they involve the Constitution. The major questions doctrine, she said, is a “constitutional … rule” based on the idea that “courts should expect Congress to speak clearly when it delegates major lawmaking authority to the executive.”

Turning to the question of whether the major questions doctrine barred the waivers, she observed that both the government and the challengers “appear to agree that the waiver of all legal requirements to construct a border wall in the Big Bend Sector is of ‘major economic and political significance.’” The key issue, she continued, is thus whether Congress clearly gave the DHS secretary the power to issue the Big Bend waivers. It did not, she wrote. She emphasized that another provision of IIRIRA authorizes the DHS secretary to “take such actions as may be necessary to install additional physical barriers and roads … in the vicinity of the United States border to deter illegal crossings in areas of high illegal entry into the United States.” When that provision is read together with the waiver provision, she said, it means that the DHS secretary can only waive legal requirements for “construction ‘to deter illegal crossings in areas of high illegal entry.’”

But the Big Bend region is not, she contended, such an area: although it “comprises over 25% of the U.S.-Mexico border,” it accounts for only a tiny percentage (“between 0.3% and 2.2%”) of all of the people apprehended crossing the border. This means, she said, that “if the Big Bend Sector is an area of high illegal entry, so is every other sector, and the waiver authority would apply to the entire U.S.-Mexico border. If that were the case, Congress could have simply authorized construction ‘to deter illegal crossings.’” The major questions doctrine instructs courts to avoid “interpretations that would render limiting language meaningless,” Cardone concluded.

The Trump administration came to the Supreme Court on Oct. 21, asking the justices to pause Cardone’s order and to grant review and summarily reverse it. First, U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued, the district court did not have the power to consider the challengers’ claim based on the major questions doctrine because their complaint specifically alleged that the DHS secretary “lacked ‘clear congressional authorization’ … to issue the waivers” under the IIRIRA – a statutory claim that “Congress denied district courts” the authority “to entertain.” But in any event, Sauer continued, “[e]ven assuming that the Secretary’s waiver authority implicates a question of ‘vast economic and political significance,’ Congress did clearly authorize the Secretary both to build border barriers and to waive legal requirements in order to facilitate their expeditious construction.”

In their brief on Thursday, the challengers insisted that the major questions doctrine applied to their claim, stressing that “the decision to waive myriad federal, state, local, and tribal laws across vast geography for the purpose of building border infrastructure, even through national and state parks where border crossings are miniscule, is a truly ‘extraordinary’ assertion of power.” “Whatever authority Congress delegated, it did not clearly authorize the sweeping exercise of power asserted here, and” the government’s “actions therefore amount to a constitutional violation of the major questions doctrine.”

The challengers also argued that DHS will not suffer any permanent harm if the construction is temporarily blocked – one of the factors that courts consider in deciding whether to award temporary relief. To the contrary, they noted, before Cardone issued her order halting construction, “in response to intense local opposition,” DHS “voluntarily paused construction for weeks in vast portions of the project area.”
