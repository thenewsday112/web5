Halloween Storm With Inches of Snow and 50 MPH Gusts Heads to California

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

This year, Halloween may be more than spooky.

In California’s Sierra Nevada mountains, the coming weekend looks wet, with significant snowfall possible at the region’s ski resorts and winds gusting up to 50 miles per hour. 

The National Weather Service Reno suggested delaying or re-evaluating travel plans throughout the Sierra, noting that snowfall is expected to be the most intense late Saturday night. Slick roads, closures, and travel delays are possible. Winter weather advisories apply until 7 a.m. Sunday morning.

A report from NWS Reno issued on Thursday, October 29, 2026.

National Weather Service Reno

The NWS also announced Thursday morning that forecasted snow totals for the storm had increased. Still, freezing levels seem a bit fuzzy.

In the Tahoe Basin, a winter weather advisory applies to elevations above 7,000 feet. In Mammoth Mountain’s home, Mono County, the advisory applies to elevations above 8,000 feet. 

The advisory that applies to one ski hotspot around Lake Tahoe, Palisades Tahoe, cautioned that two to five inches are possible, with nine inches on the highest peaks. 

Palisades Tahoe’s base area sits at 6,200 feet, but the resort’s upper reaches crest 9,000 feet. The slopes of Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe—home to Lake Tahoe’s highest base elevation—start at 8,260 feet.

The Mono County advisory calls for up to 12 inches at the highest elevations, with lower elevations slated for between three and six inches.

In other words, this could be a classic rain at the base, snow at the summit situation, depending on the resort. A previous Wednesday report from the NWS said that snowfall and rainfall totals remained uncertain because “snow levels keep fluctuating with each model run.”

Despite the prospective stoke, ski resort opening dates largely remain weeks away across California. 

Still, some precipitation could help those with more aggressive timelines. Mt. Rose is shooting for November 6 and, per its webcams, appears to already have a strong start on snowmaking. The natural stuff would certainly wouldn’t hurt, though.

Further south, Mammoth Mountain plans to open on November 13. 

In the meantime, that leaves one thing to do: think cold. Hopefully, all the Sierra folks have a white Halloween ahead, followed by some skiing next week.

Apple takes "monumental step forward" with first touchscreen MacBook

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple has revealed its latest MacBook Pro model, which features the technology brand's first laptop touchscreen and what it claims is "the world’s best laptop display".

Sixteen years after Steve Jobs described touchscreen laptops as "ergonomically terrible", Apple has ventured into the market with its new MacBook Pro, which is available from 6 November at prices ranging from £2,899 to £14,549.

As well as being touchscreen, the laptop features a PROLED XDR display, which Apple said is "the world's best laptop display", M5 Pro and M5 Max chips, 128 gigabytes of memory, 16 terabytes of storage, up to 24 hours of battery life and a thinner, lighter design compared to previous models.

It comes in 14- and 16-inch models, weighing three and four pounds respectively. The laptop was designed to be both portable and a powerful tool for professional creatives on the go, whether they're working on 3D models or playing musical instruments onscreen.

“The new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display is a monumental step forward for the Mac,” said Apple chief hardware officer Johny Srouji.

“PROLED XDR is the world’s best laptop display," he continued. "And with the magic of Touch coming to MacBook Pro, combined with the game-changing performance of M5 Pro and M5 Max, we’ve created a whole new way for pros to interact with their content, taking their workflows to a completely new level."

"The new MacBook Pro is the result of the deep integration between Apple silicon, hardware, and software, and there’s simply nothing else like it."

Aiming to provide an intuitive experience, the Touch technology allows users to scroll through a webpage by swiping, zoom in on a photo by pinching, drag and drop slides in presentations and double-tap a window to fill the screen.

The touch sensor is built into the display panel rather than layered over it, to give the feel of touching the displayed content itself.

To support the Touch feature, Apple redesigned the laptop hinge for better stability and created real-time video stabilisation to smooth the camera feed when using the touchscreen on a video call.

Apple said that the macOS operating system automatically translates touches into clicks, meaning multiple existing apps will work with Touch right away. These include Adobe Lightroom, Blender, Maxon Cinema 4D, Maxon ZBrush, Notion, Canva, Zoom, Microsoft Word, Excel and PowerPoint.

"Graphic designers can now use the entire display as their canvas, no longer constrained by the area of the trackpad when drawing," said Apple.

"Musicians can play software instruments right onscreen, and app developers can test how Touch performs in their iOS and iPadOS apps directly on the MacBook Pro display."

Apple said the new PROLED XDR display offers "true-to-life colour and unparalleled brightness and contrast", made possible by combining OLED technology with algorithms that control light across the display.

Elon Musk Drops Unexpected Reaction as Record-Breaking $6.3 Billion Binance Hack Rattles BTC, ETH, and XRP

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The cryptocurrency market has been rocked by reports of a staggering $6.3 billion security breach involving Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, sending shockwaves through the digital asset industry as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Ripple’s XRP come under fresh selling pressure.

Adding an unexpected twist to the unfolding drama, billionaire Elon Musk has reportedly weighed in, thrusting one of crypto’s biggest security scares into an even brighter spotlight and raising fresh questions about the safety of billions held on major trading platforms.

Binance Drained of $6.3 Billion

A fresh wave of selling has swept across the cryptocurrency market as renewed security fears rattle investor confidence, leaving major digital assets deep in the red.

Bitcoin led the downturn, shedding more than 3% over the past 24 hours and briefly sinking to $76,400 on Friday morning before clawing back some losses to trade around $77,336 at press time. The pain extended across the altcoin market, with Ethereum, BNB, the native crypto that powers the Binance ecosystem, Ripple’s XRP, and Solana also coming under heavy selling pressure.

The market downturn followed Binance’s disclosure on Thursday of a security incident involving approximately $6.3 billion in crypto assets, according to the report. Notably, the incident even dwarfs the $1.5 billion Bybit breach in 2025, which was attributed to North Korean hackers, making it the biggest crypto hack on record.

Binance co-founder and former CEO Changpeng Zhao, fondly known as CZ, weighed in on the incident, saying on X that it appeared to be a hack. Although he no longer leads the exchange, Zhao remains a major shareholder and an influential figure in the crypto industry.

According to Binance’s preliminary findings, the attacker may have gained access through a DevOps engineer’s compromised laptop, using it to enter a service container and initiate unauthorized withdrawals. The exchange stressed that its investigation had found no evidence of wallet private keys being exposed, suggesting the suspected breach may have exploited internal system access rather than directly compromising the cryptographic keys securing user funds.

Binance Hack Triggers Urgent Hunt to Freeze Billions in Stolen Crypto

The incident has also prompted swift action from major stablecoin issuers. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said the company had frozen more than 1.567 billion USDT allegedly linked to the suspected Binance breach, stressing that safeguarding the crypto industry and its partners remained a priority.

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire separately announced that more than 800 million USDC associated with the incident had been frozen in coordination with law enforcement. Circle said it would continue assisting investigators as they worked to establish what happened.

Meanwhile, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong revealed the exchange was taking steps to help contain the fallout, including blocking transfers to compromised wallets. In a post on X, Armstrong emphasized the need for a coordinated industry response, stating that Coinbase was “doing everything we can to support in the current situation.”

“Moments like this call for the industry to work together,” he added.

Elon Musk Drops Chilling AI Warning After Binance Hack 

Tesla chief executive Elon Musk has raised concerns about a potential new threat to the crypto industry, predicting that superintelligent AI systems could eventually steal digital assets to finance their own objectives rather than serve human interests. 

Musk shared the warning in an October 30 post on X while commenting on the reported $6.3B security incident involving Binance. He suggested that as artificial intelligence becomes more advanced and autonomous, some systems could seek access to money independently, writing: “Super intelligence agents will at some point do this to gain currency for themselves, not a human.”

Musk remarks were prompted by a post from crypto commentator Blaney, who described how the attacker behind the reported Binance security incident may have moved funds despite efforts to freeze the stolen assets. As the post noted, stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze billions of dollars’ worth of USDT and USDC linked to the incident, but the attacker allegedly obtained Ethereum before the restrictions could fully take effect.

Blaney also claimed the attacker deposited $60 million in USDT into the decentralized lending platform Aave and used it to borrow about $44.6 million in wrapped Ether (WETH). The alleged maneuver illustrates how decentralized finance protocols could potentially be used to move or obtain assets even as centralized issuers work to block suspicious funds.

Musk used the episode to raise a broader concern about the future of artificial intelligence, suggesting that increasingly advanced AI agents could one day carry out similar financial maneuvers autonomously to acquire digital assets for their own purposes rather than for human users.

EasyJet Unveils Starlink Wi-Fi, Will Ryanair Be Forced To Follow?

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

easyJet is finally adding inflight Wi-Fi, and it is going straight to Starlink. That is good news for passengers and raises another question about Ryanair’s resistance to onboard internet. But there is a catch: do not assume easyJet’s Starlink service will be free, which itself might be the strongest way to cajole Ryanair into following.

EasyJet Unveils Starlink Wi-Fi, Will Ryanair Follow?

easyJet has announced a partnership with SpaceX to introduce Starlink internet across its Airbus A320, A320neo, and A321neo fleet. Installation will begin in the first half of 2027, with completion expected by early 2028.

This will be the airline’s first inflight internet service. Instead of introducing an older system that struggles when everyone connects, easyJet is moving directly to a product that has impressed me every time I have used it. The airline also sees opportunities beyond entertainment, including access to journey information and potentially ordering food and drinks from your seat.

Will EasyJet’s Starlink Wi-Fi Be Free?

But easyJet has not announced its pricing and has not promised complimentary access for everyone. Instead, the airline said Starlink would manage access and that pricing details would follow as equipped aircraft enter service. It also described connectivity as an “optional extra.”

That certainly points toward a paid offering, though the price and any complimentary access categories remain unconfirmed. Seth Miller at PaxEx.Aero reached a similar assessment after speaking with the airline.

Of course we would all prefer free access, but even reasonably priced, reliable internet would make easyJet more attractive to me, no matter how short or long the flight. With a rather uncomfortable seats and few other in-flight amenities, this would make the flight fly by.

Didn’t Starlink Require Airlines To Offer Free Wi-Fi?

Free access was a major part of Starlink’s early airline appeal, alongside its speed and reliability. At least initially, SpaceX insisted on complimentary service for passengers. But we have already seen a departure from that approach. In July, I wrote about Copa Airlines introducing Starlink with paid access for many passengers, while offering it free to business class travelers and certain other eligible customers.

Starlink branding alone therefore no longer tells you whether you will have to pay.

The emerging alternative involves Starlink managing passenger connectivity more directly, with paid access possible. This is also the sort of arrangement Frontier described in its own announcement, which expressly referenced a new system managed directly by Starlink.

We do not have easyJet’s contract, so I cannot claim to know precisely which concessions either side made. But the practical change is clear: Starlink can now reach airlines through a model that does not require free internet for every passenger.

Certainly, this creates an easier sales pitch to budget carriers. Instead of asking an airline to absorb the ongoing cost as a passenger benefit, paid access offers another way to fund the service and increase ancillary revenue. The commercial opportunity is obvious, even if I prefer the ease of connecting without a payment screen.

EasyJet Previously Said The Economics Did Not Work

This decision is particularly interesting because CEO Kenton Jarvis was still expressing reservations in January. As Reuters reported, Jarvis liked Starlink’s performance but said:

“The economics aren’t quite working for us.”

Nine months later, easyJet has signed up. We can only speculate that charging passengers was the deciding factor, but it is reasonable to ask how the commercial arrangement evolved to make the investment attractive.

Will Ryanair Follow?

Ryanair’s objection has centered on cost, including the fuel impact of externally mounted antennas, and doubts that enough passengers would pay for connectivity on short flights.

But Michael O’Leary has not ruled out inflight internet forever. In January, he told Reuters that Ryanair expected to offer free Wi-Fi within three to five years as technology improved, while continuing discussions with providers.

easyJet’s announcement may not move the needle with O’Leary, but it does give Ryanair another competitor against which passengers can compare the onboard experience. When schedules and total prices are close, reliable internet would influence my choice. Yours too, right?

There is also an opportunity for Ryanair to observe what happens at easyJet: how many passengers connect, what they will pay, and whether connectivity helps attract repeat business. Waiting may be frustrating for passengers, but it gives Ryanair useful evidence before committing. But with Wizzair and now easy jet embracing Starlink, it’s only a matter of time (thought the understandable bad blood between Elon Musk and O’Leary may mean Ryanair pulls a Delta Air Lines and waits for Amazon Leo).

CONCLUSION

easyJet adding Starlink is a welcome improvement. Installation starts in 2027, but the airline has yet to disclose what passengers will pay or who, if anyone, will receive free access.

The old assumption that Starlink automatically means free Wi-Fi no longer holds. Still, going from no internet to fast, dependable connectivity should make easyJet a more appealing option. Ryanair may continue to wait, but it probably cannot wait forever as its main rivals add internet onboard.

EA Reportedly Closed BioWare To Invest In Developing Star Wars Battlefront III Instead Of The Next Mass Effect

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Electronic Arts shut down the legendary RPG studio BioWare last week, ending development on an untitled fifth Mass Effect game. Since then, we've seen what appears to be a leaked trailer for the game, which suggests it might have been called Mass Effect Universe, and heard a sneak peek at what Grammy-winning composer Austin Wintory was cooking up for the beloved sci-fi RPG series. Over the past week, fans across the internet have tried to piece together how a publisher could decide to shut down a studio as prolific as BioWare, but according to a new Bloomberg report, the answer is Star Wars. 

More specifically, Bloomberg writes that, presented with the opportunity to develop Star Wars Battlefront III with Disney, the publisher, which went private last year after a $55 billion acquisition,  chose to invest its money into that instead of BioWare's in-development Mass Effect sequel. The publication writes that EA looked at its portfolio for the coming years and had two massive sci-fi investments it could continue: Star Wars Battlefront III or BioWare's next Mass Effect. Even though Mass Effect was well into development, and even though the Battlefront threequel hasn't even entered development, EA chose to continue with the latter. And so BioWare was shut down, and Mass Effect development ended, though Bloomberg's sources say there are other, less significant reasons the studio was shuttered, too. 

Those other factors include the poor reception to 2017's Mass Effect: Andromeda, the failure of Anthem in 2019, and 2024's Dragon Age: The Veilguard, which, despite garnering positive critical reception, apparently missed EA's sales expectations by 50%. 

There's also the uncomfortable reality that BioWare made progressive LGBTQIA+ positive games but was a studio within a publisher house now partially owned by the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia, a government fund from a country where same-sex activity, including marriage, is criminalized, sometimes punishable by death; these harsh laws extend to other facets of the LGBTQIA+ community. Another investment partner in this acquisition of EA is Affinity Partners, a Miami-based investment firm founded and run by former White House senior advisor Jared Kushner, who is the son-in-law of President Donald Trump, a political figure who imposes laws that discriminate against the LGBTQIA+ community, especially the 1% of Americans who are transgender; Trump has also been known to mock this community in various ways. No official statements say BioWare shut down because of its progressive leanings, but one has to wonder if that was a factor, especially after reports that BioWare scrubbed diversity from demos to avoid negative feedback from EA (thanks, Kotaku).

When BioWare closed last week, only 50 employees remained, all working on the next Mass Effect, after years of layoffs at the studio. 

A Mass Effect TV series is still in the works at Amazon, even though EA seems to have hung up Commander Shepard's video game suit for good.

When the dog days of summer give way to autumn’s cooler temperatures, it’s time to get your house in shape for the change of seasons. Fall is the ideal time of year to work outside because there’s just enough of a nip in the air to make being outdoors pleasant, but not so much that you need a heavy coat. Before Old Man Winter comes calling, take advantage of these temperate days to tackle this seasonal to-do list.

1. Mulch the Garden

Photo: Adobe Stoc

As the weather gets cooler, it’s time to put your garden to bed under a blanket of mulch. Protect annuals and perennials from the cold by covering them with a 2- to- 4-inch layer of organic material, such as shredded wood or wood chips, shredded leaves, or straw. For shrubs, trees, and rosebushes, wait to spread mulch until later in the season, when the soil is cool but not yet frozen.

2. Clear Out Spent Plants

Photo: Юля Бурмистрова via Adobe Stock

Insects love nothing better than overwintering beneath dead flowers, leaves, and other plant material. Roll up the welcome mat by raking away fallen leaves, deadheading annuals, pruning perennials and roses, and clearing out your summer vegetables before the first frost.

3. Bring Potted Plants Indoors

Photo: iMarzi via Adobe Stock

Once nighttime temperatures sink below 50 degrees Fahrenheit, it’s time for your tender container plants to move indoors for the season. Before carrying them into your house or to a protected spot in the garage, first clip away any dead or damaged leaves and branches, and then check carefully for insects.

4. Plant Spring Bulbs

Photo: iMarzi via Adobe Stock

Nothing says spring is coming like colorful tulips, daffodils, crocuses, irises, and hyacinths, but if you want to enjoy these beauties in your spring garden, you need to plant bulbs in the fall. As a general rule, northern gardeners plant spring-blooming bulbs in late September through mid-October, while southern gardeners can wait until mid-October or early November.

5. Seal Drafts

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Keep warm air in and cold air out, and reduce your heating bills at the same time, by sealing up leaky doors or windows now. Check for drafts by turning off fans or air conditioners, then holding a lit stick of incense near doors and windows. If the smoke wavers or is sucked outside, you know you have a problem. The solution is simple: Caulk any cracks or chips around windows, and install weatherstripping to ward off drafts around doors.

6. Check Smoke Detectors

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

According to the National Fire Protection Association, three out of every five residential fire deaths occur in a home that either has no smoke detector at all, or a smoke detector with nonfunctioning batteries. Don’t let your family become a statistic. Check your smoke and carbon monoxide detectors at the very least twice a year, once in the fall and again in the spring.

7. Inspect the Fireplace

Photo: Louis-Paul Photo v ia Adobe Stock

It’s a good idea to have your home’s chimney inspected and swept before fireplace season gets underway. This job is best done by a pro—expect to pay around $150 to $200 for a professional chimney inspection—but if you’re comfortable on the roof, you can do some preliminary work yourself. First, clear out any fallen leaves, birds’ nests, or other debris from the chimney, and repair any small cracks in the masonry with refractory cement, which is formulated to withstand high heat. Ensure that the damper moves smoothly and isn’t cracked, rusted, or warped, and then clean away soot, creosote, and ash.

8. Paint Your Entry Door Trim

Photo: ungvar via Adobe Stock

If you’ve been thinking that your entry door could use a refresh, a new coat of paint on the surrounding door trim can help. Entry doors and trim are hard workers in most homes, and they have the scuffs and smudges to show it. Just painting the trim and cleaning the door will give the entry a more polished look. Clean the trim, let it dry, and then apply exterior paint. Most homeowners choose gloss or semi-gloss when painting trim. Two coats should be enough to protect the trim and keep it looking fresh for a year or more, depending on the weather and how busy your household is.

9. Reseed the Lawn

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Want your lawn to look its best come springtime? Then sow grass seed in early through mid-fall. Reseeding the lawn gives gardeners in cold-winter areas a head start on spring. As the weather warms up, the grass will germinate, crowd out weeds, and develop a strong root system before the high heat of summer. If you live in a warm-winter region and want to keep your grass green all season long, sow annual ryegrass in the early fall. This quick-sprouting grass will look great through the chilly months and then die down once higher temperatures return in the summer.

10. Seal the Driveway

Photo: KQ Ferris via Adobe Stock

If you have an asphalt driveway, reseal it in the fall when temperatures are between 50 and 70 degrees Fahrenheit and no rain is in the forecast. For most areas, that means late September to early October. To start, sweep away dirt, leaves, and other debris from the asphalt, and then pour asphalt sealant over the driveway. Using an asphalt squeegee, evenly spread the sealant across the driveway’s entire surface. Let the sealant cure for at least 24 hours before parking on the driveway.

11. Inspect the Roof

Photo: Andrea Miller via Adobe Stock

Winter can be hard on your roof, when rain, snow, freezing temperatures, and high winds all take their toll. That’s why fall is the best time to make sure to inspect your roof and complete any necessary roof repairs before cold weather sets in. You can hire a professional for this service or, if you’re up to the task, do it yourself.
 

12. Buy a Better Rake

Photo: Debbie Wolfe for BobVila.com

Autumn means raking leaves, and raking leaves can mean back pain. Make the job easier on yourself by buying a good rake. The right one is long enough so you don’t need to bend over, has flexible yet durable tines to easily scoop up debris, and has a comfortable, ergonomically designed grip so your hands don’t feel the strain. Our researched guide to the best leaf rakes contains some terrific options, whatever your needs and budget.

13. Organize the Mudroom

Photo: Sheila Say via Shutterstock

In most regions, fall brings rain—and a parade of muddy shoes. Get your mudroom ready for the season by setting out a large mat to absorb water and a tray to hold wet, muddy boots and shoes. If you don’t already have one, add a bench where you can sit and pull on your boots, and consider including a coat rack or wall hooks to hold jackets, umbrellas, and scarves.

14. Plant a Tree

Photo: MIND AND I via Adobe Stock

As they say, the best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago, and the second best time is now. If “now” happens to be autumn, you’re in luck: The cooler temperatures and moisture help the tree establish roots before the hot and stressful summer weather.

To ensure success, mulch your newly planted tree to keep the roots insulated. Water regularly if there’s not enough rain, but don’t fertilize until spring. Avoid heavy pruning, other than trimming away any broken branches. And if a severe cold snap is in the forecast, wrap a blanket or piece of burlap over the tree for a little extra protection while it gets established.

15. Create a Command Center

Photo: marishkaTR via Adobe Stock

With summer fun in the rearview mirror, fall is the time for getting back to business. Creating an organization HQ for the whole family is a great way to help you stay on task. Put it in a central location like the kitchen, mudroom, or back entryway, where the whole family can see it.

Include a large wall calendar or weekly schedule, a dry erase board for appointment reminders or shopping lists, and a bulletin board for important announcements or incoming and outgoing school forms. If your family uses a chore chart or meal planning chart, this would be the place to put it. Set up caddies to collect keys and other daily necessities so they’ll be easy to find and won’t clutter up the rest of the house.

16. Drain Your Water Heater

Photo: NC Photo via Adobe Stock NC Photo – stock.adobe.com

Drain your hot water heater to remove accumulations of lime and other minerals that naturally occur in your tap water. When left untreated, this buildup can keep your appliance from functioning its best and put your system at risk for leaks and other problems. It’s an easy enough job to do yourself, but if you’re not confident in your DIY abilities, call a pro to do it for you. Simple though it may seem, this one task can lengthen the life of your water heater and prevent the system from malfunctioning down the line.

17. Prevent Mice From Moving In

Photo: Peyker / Shutterstock

As temperatures drop, mice and other pests will be looking for a warm place to bunk, which is all well and good unless they choose your home as their winter retreat. Stay pest-free by doing a walkabout around the exterior and through the interior of your house, plugging any potential points of entry with caulk or steel wool. Keep in mind that mice can enter your home through a dime-size hole, so pay attention to even the smallest cracks.

18. Put Away Patio Furniture

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Fall leaves and winter snow can cause your patio furniture to fade, stain, and rust. Protect it with a tarp or patio furniture covers, or place it inside your shed, garage, or screened porch. Before you stow it for the season, give patio furniture a proper cleaning with mild soap and water to remove dust, pollen, and mold. Treat wicker with lemon oil to keep it moisturized over the dry winter months and protect it from mildew.

19. Clean the Grill

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Soak and scour the grates, clean under the hood, remove any foil or food particles from the interior, and wipe down the exterior. When foul weather moves in for good, you can quickly cover the grill and store it for the season.
 

20. Maintain Your Mower

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Keep mowing your lawn in the fall until the grass stops growing. Then, when it’s time to put it away for the season, make any necessary repairs the lawn mower to ensure that it will be in good working condition when you pull it out again in spring. Wipe away any grass or other debris that clings to the machine, and consider having the blades sharpened by a professional. If you have a gas lawn mower, use up the last of the gasoline in the tank or add fuel stabilizer to prevent damage (check the manufacturer’s instructions).
 

21. Aerate Your Lawn

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

If your lawn receives a lot of foot traffic, it could be at risk for soil compaction, a condition that prevents the proper circulation of air and nutrients. To keep your turfgrass nourished, aerate cool-season grasses in fall by using a manual aerator, or a spike or plug aerator (available for purchase or rental at many home centers). Before you aerate the lawn, make sure the soil is moist but not soaking wet, because it can be difficult to aerate dry soil.

22. Feed Your Turf

Photo: Paul Rankin for BobVila.com

Cool-season grasses, like bluegrass and tall fescue, benefit from a fall application of fertilizer. Fertilize your lawn with a slow-release formula about 1 week before you mow for the last time in the season. High-nitrogen formulas that offer winter weather protection are helpful in colder climates.
 

23. Clean Your Rain Gutters

Photo: bildlove via Adobe Stock

That stunning fall foliage may look good on the trees in your front yard, but when those leaves drop they can make a mess of your gutters. More than unsightly, leaves and other debris can clog the gutters, posing a real hazard in winter when rain and snow thaw and freeze inside gutters and form dangerous ice dams.

Protect your home from damage by removing leaves, sticks, and other debris from gutters now, and consider installing a leaf guard to keep your rain gutters clean with very little effort on your part.


24. Install Storm Windows 

Photo: PeopleImages via Shutterstock

Button up the house and get ready for windy winter by swapping out your window screens for storm windows (if you have them). If you don’t have storm windows, particularly if you have vintage single-pane windows that you’re trying to save, consider installing them.

Storm windows offer an additional layer of insulation against cold weather and drafts, help keep your home more comfortable, and can help lower energy bills.

25. Get Your HVAC System Serviced

Photo: iPoguz via Adobe Stock

You don’t want to wait until snow is falling and frigid winds are howling to discover that your home’s heating system is on the fritz. Call a pro to come out and evaluate your heating system now. If the HVAC specialist detects any issues, have them fixed promptly to save yourself from a potentially chilly disaster later in the season.

26. Change the Direction of Your Ceiling Fan

Photo: Andrei Liashchou via Adobe Stock

It may seem counterproductive to turn on a ceiling fan during the coldest months of the year, but a ceiling fan helps maintain comfortable temperatures even in the winter. Starting in the fall, set your ceiling fan to rotate in a clockwise direction (opposite the operating ceiling fan direction in summer), and run it on low speed to create an updraft. This tactic draws the room air up and forces the warm air near the ceiling down and through the room.
 

27. Drain Garden Hoses

Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com

Because water expands as it freezes, it’s important to disconnect and drain garden hoses before temperatures drop to prevent them from bursting or leaking. Be sure to disconnect hoses from the outdoor spigots to which they’re attached; store hoses in a covered area like the garage or a tool shed to protect them from harsh weather. Turn off and drain your sprinkler system and shut off your outdoor faucets before nighttime temperatures dip below 50 degrees Fahrenheit.
 

28. Trim the Trees

Photo: Debbie Wolfe for BobVila.com

It’s easier to identify dead limbs before your trees drop their leaves, so early fall is a good time to trim back dead branches. Trimming your trees now is more than an aesthetic choice, and doing so can protect your home from damage. In freezing conditions, dead limbs can become heavy with ice and break off, ending up on your roof or lawn, or posing a safety issue for family and passersby alike.

29. Clean and Cover the AC Unit

Photo: Nelly Cuanalo via Adobe Stock

For as long as there have been air conditioning covers, people have debated whether they’re a good idea. Advocates love covers for the way they keep ice and debris out of the machinery, while detractors fear that covers can create ideal habitats for rodents and other pests that can nest within the wiring. Homeowners who live in colder regions may want to consider at least covering the top of their outside AC condenser unit with a tarp or sheet of plywood, even if they leave the rest of the unit exposed. Window air conditioning units, on the other hand, should be removed, cleaned, and stored in a safe place until they’re needed again in the summer.
 

30. Change Your Furnace Filter

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Give your HVAC system a fresh start with a new furnace filter. Furnace filters help maintain cleaner indoor air and keep a home heating system in good working order. Continue to change the filter every few months until you turn off your heat for the season.
 

31. Clean the Dryer Vent

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

A clogged dryer vent can cause a perfectly good appliance to operate more slowly and less efficiently, but more importantly it poses a serious fire hazard. Clean out your dryer vent at least once a year to speed up laundry day, keep energy bills low, and protect your home from fires. You can do the job yourself with a vacuum cleaner and a long dowel or wire hanger (if you’re careful not to damage the vent), or hire a pro to do the job for you.
 

32. Clean the Carpets

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Mold counts are on the rise at this time of year, exacerbating indoor allergies. Mount an effective defense by giving your carpets a thorough cleaning. Rent a carpet cleaner or hire a professional service to deep clean your carpeting, paying special attention to the bedrooms and high-traffic areas. If you’ve been thinking about removing your carpeting in order to breathe more easily, now might be the time to do it. Who knows—you may even have pristine hardwood floors hiding under those old carpets!
 

33. Stock Up on Ice Melt

Photo: J.A. via Adobe Stock

Don’t wait until the first snowstorm of the season to stock up on snow removal supplies. Buy an emergency stash of ice melt, salt, and sand (or kitty litter, in a pinch) to give you traction in slippery situations. Some home centers start selling these supplies as early as September and October, but if your local shop doesn’t carry them, you can certainly buy them from online retailers.
 

34. Bleed the Radiator

Photo: Andrey Popov via Adobe Stock

Have you ever cranked up the heat in late fall and waited for your radiators to circulate warm air through your home, only to discover that one or more of your radiators remains cold to the touch? If one radiator isn’t working when the rest of your system is operating normally, it’s usually a sign that air is trapped inside. This problem can be avoided by bleeding the radiators at the beginning of the season. To do this, first turn off the heat, then engage the bleed valve with a radiator key. Wait for the hissing sound to stop and a stream of water to pour from the radiator before closing up the bleed valve.
 

35. Repair Cracked Concrete

Photo: Tom Fenenga for BobVila.com

Cracks happen, and it’s important to take care of them before winter freezes make them worse. To repair cracks in concrete walkways, patios, or driveways, first clear away any dirt or debris from the damaged area, and then fill in the crack with masonry crack filler or concrete patch. Once you’ve filled the crack, smooth the edges of the new material with a trowel, and let the repair cure overnight.
 

36. Prepare for Emergencies

Photo: Mark Wolfe for BobVila.com

Be ready for the next big storm before it strikes with a “go bag” of emergency essentials, and give serious thought to buying a generator for the house if you don’t already have one. Generators are available in a variety of sizes and types, including portable generators and models that can be wired into a home’s electrical system. The larger the generator, the more power it provides. Before you make a purchase, be sure to determine which features and which fuel type best suit your household’s needs.

37. Test Run the Snow Blower

Photo: Michelle Larson for BobVila.com

If you live in an area that sees a lot of snow and where a snow thrower or snow blower is a must-have, fall is the ideal time to make sure your equipment works. Have clean, stable gas at the ready, and then take the snow blower out for a quick test run. Fill the tank, start it up, and make sure all of the features work. Always be certain to have a spare spark plug on hand so you won’t be caught by surprise during a winter storm.
 

38. Swap Out the Doormat

Photo: Maridav via Adobe Stock

An easy way to get ready for cold, wet fall weather—and spruce up your entry while you’re at it—is to invest in a new doormat. With the return to school and a faster pace of life, fall is a time of year when many homes experience an uptick in foot traffic. A ragged, worn doormat may no longer do the trick, and it might not exude the welcoming vibe you desire. Because there are so many options in materials, sizes, designs, and styles, consider switching your doormats out seasonally so they won’t wear out as quickly.

39. Change Porch Light Bulbs

Photo: dominikspalek.pl via Adobe Stock

In the colder months, as darkness begins to fall earlier in the day, having adequate light on a front porch or entryway becomes even more important. If you’ve had the same porch light bulbs for a long time and they aren’t LEDs, think about swapping them out for new ones. After all, an entry light fixture isn’t much help when it doesn’t turn on.
 

40. Set Up Landscape Lighting

Photo: Tomasz Zajda via Adobe Stock

Landscape lighting elevates the look of a home’s facade and helps set a welcoming tone. With a range of available colors and options that include solar-powered landscape lighting, it’s easier than ever to line a walkway or driveway, or to spotlight trees and other landscaping or architectural features, creating a custom look for the exterior of your house.

The good news is that there’s gear to make them safer. Wearing and using reflective gear and lights can turn the darkness from a hindrance into a playground.

Wearing any kind of lighting or reflectivity is better than moving through the darkness without it, but some options are more effective than others.

A few years ago, I conducted a personal experiment: I had my husband put on a shirt and tights—each with reflective paneling—and then piled a bunch of other gear in the passenger seat of my car. My husband, the good sport that he is, ran in place on a dark street corner in our neighborhood while I drove laps around the block, noting how far away I could spot him in the beam of my headlights. I then handed him new gear to change into—jackets, arm warmers, headlamps—before driving another lap and taking notes on my clipboard. (God bless him.)

What I learned from the experiment is that reflectivity placed on moving body parts, like legs in stride and swinging arms, is more visible to drivers than reflectivity worn on more static body parts, like the torso. I later found that there are actual scientific studies that prove that point.

It’s not as though a driver doesn’t see something static, like a reflective street sign. But the visual movement patterns of a living organism—called biomotion—trigger a driver’s brain to recognize that the reflective item is a person, increasing their awareness and your safety.
