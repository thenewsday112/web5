If California undid its ‘original sin’ and matched Texas on taxing property, it would raise more revenue than the billionaire tax, economists say

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

California voters will decide on Proposition 40 soon, but looming over the controversial billionaire wealth tax proposal is another state ballot measure from nearly 50 years ago.

In 1978, voters approved Proposition 13, which capped property taxes at just 1% of assessed value, limited annual hikes to 2%, and allowed reassessments to market value only after sales or substantial renovations.

It came amid a wider tax revolt leading up to Ronald Reagan’s election as president and an era of conservative governance in America. After Prop 13’s passage, the state had to find other ways to raise more revenue and relied more on taxing incomes, including capital gains. 

California now has the most progressive income-tax structure in the country with a top marginal rate of 13.3%. When combined with federal income tax rates, that can send the total to more than 50%.

The top 1% of earners account for up to half of state revenue, though it also means state coffers are volatile and swing on stock market returns. At the same time, the ultra-wealthy often don’t receive much income and instead leverage assets to finance their lifestyles, avoiding some taxes.

Now enter the billionaire tax, which calls for residents worth more than $1 billion to pay a one-time tax equivalent to 5% of their assets. The union pushing Prop 40 has said the wealth tax could raise $100 billion in revenue and help offset federal cuts to health spending.

Supporters point to the AI boom and say California’s ultra-rich would still be among the world’s wealthiest. Opponents have warned about the impact the tax could have on economic growth and startups. They have also said it would force founders to sell big pieces of their companies and eventually cause the wealthy to flee to another state, resulting in a net loss of revenue over time.

If California is looking for a large source of undertaxed wealth, “a much better, bigger target lies right beneath their feet,” according to Princeton economist Owen Zidar and University of Chicago Booth School of Business economist Eric Zwick, authors of the book The Everywhere Millionaire.

“The original sin underlying California’s tax structure is Prop 13, the 1978 law that rewrote the rules of property ownership,” they wrote in a recent New York Times op-ed. “Getting rid of it would be a more efficient and durable way to make the rich pay their fair share.”

Prop 13 also contributed to rapidly increasing home values, which have risen faster than property taxes. As a result, California homeowners pay an average of just 0.8% tax on their property, Zidar and Zwick estimated, generating $100 billion in revenue in fiscal 2025.

By contrast, Texas homeowners pay 2% on average. If California matched Texas, overall residential property tax revenue would more than double, while also easing home prices by around 13%, they added.

To be sure, property taxes in Texas have stirred their own voter revolt during the midterm election season. While the state doesn’t levy income taxes, Texans complain that the overall tax burden remains onerous due to property taxes, which are levied by local governments as well.

And getting rid of Prop 13 in California wouldn’t go down well with voters either as many would suffer big increases on their property taxes. Still, California’s tax base is already narrow, and Prop 40 would make it even more top-heavy. Meanwhile, the state plans to spend $540 billion this fiscal year.

“It is simply not possible to fund the state’s needs by leaning on a few hundred people, as politically appealing as that may be,” Zidar and Zwick argued. “For California to tax wealth in a progressive fashion and meaningfully improve its fiscal position, it needs to broaden the aperture beyond billionaires to the hundreds of thousands of other very wealthy people who could easily afford to contribute more by paying a real estate tax that finally works.”

Laura Tyson, a former White House economic adviser, and Lenny Mendonca, a former chief economic adviser to California Governor Gavin Newsom, warned billionaires respond strongly to estate taxes when deciding where to live.

Already, six billionaires who would have owed about one-quarter of Prop 40’s expected revenues left the state before the proposed effective date on the wealth tax, they pointed out in a Project Syndicate column.

And since Prop 40 proposes a new, untested method of taxation based on all assets, it would fuel a “tidal wave of litigation, rendering the promised funding both speculative and unreliable,” they wrote.

“Instead of a tax on billionaires, the U.S. needs to raise taxes on the capital income that feeds wealth,” Tyson and Mendonca argued. “There are a variety of ways to do so: through higher corporate tax rates; through taxes on realized and unrealized capital gains; by closing the most egregious loopholes, like carried interest; and by increasing inheritance taxes.”

Marotta: ‘Milan vs. Inter an advertisement for Italian football’

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Inter President Beppe Marotta hopes the clash with Milan is ‘an advertisement for Italian football’ as they salute San Siro in one of its final Derby della Madonnina nights.

It kicks off at the iconic Stadio Giuseppe Meazza in San Siro at 19.45 GMT (20.45 CET).

You can follow all the build-up and action as it happens from today’s Serie A games on the Liveblog.

This match is also being aired live on BBC2 in the UK and on the iPlayer.

The design for the new Generali Stadium was unveiled just two days ago and construction is expected to begin in 2028, but the plan to knock down the Stadio Meazza isn’t until 2032.

Marotta expects entertaining Milan-Inter

MILAN, ITALY – JULY 29: AC Milan President Paolo Scaroni (L) poses with FC Internazionale CEO Giuseppe Marotta during the Serie A 2019/2020 fixture unveiling on July 29, 2019 in Milan, Italy. (Photo by Emilio Andreoli/Getty Images for Lega Serie A)

“We must look to the present and the past of San Siro, so these decades are to be respected,” Marotta told Sky Sport Italia.

“At the same time, we must also look to the future, made of different needs, for stadiums that are safe and hospitable. Without mentioning the superior revenue streams that they give to clubs.

“The effort made by Oaktree and RedBird is completely extraordinary for a structure that will be unique in the world. The tenacity of the two ownerships must be applauded and the other day we finally managed to present this stratospheric project.”

ITALY – OCTOBER 31: General view outside the stadium before the Serie A match between AC Milan and FC Internazionale at Stadio Giuseppe Meazza on October 31, 2026 in Italy. (Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images)

Inter had won six editions of the Derby della Madonnina in a row, but then that was followed by five defeats and two draws, failing to even find the net in the last three competitive meetings.

Their last victory over city rivals Milan was 2-1 in April 2024, albeit an extremely special one, as it mathematically guaranteed the Scudetto.

“We know the derby has a unique place in the calendar, it is an extraordinary moment. We hope this will be an advertisement for Italian football, as it is badly needed,” continued the Inter President.

“Today this match will be broadcast all over the world to hundreds of millions of spectators. I hope it is a spectacle for all concerned. We want to win, we are motivated, but we also have the utmost respect for our opponents.”

A view of the planned Generali Stadium, to be built by AC Milan and Inter in the San Siro area of the city (inter.it)

Although Inter are unbeaten in Serie A this season and joint top of the table with Lazio, they have been riding their luck recently, needing two goals in added time to beat Fiorentina, then relying on two own goals to get the better of rock bottom Venezia in the midweek round.

Captain Lautaro Martinez is still out of action along with Luis Henrique, Curtis Jones, John Stones and Benjamin Pavard, so there is a change of system with Petar Sucic and Andy Diouf in more advanced roles supporting lone striker Marcus Thuram.

Marotta was asked about the upcoming FIGC elections, seeing as some Serie A clubs, the Italian Players’ Association and Coaches’ Association all backed Adriano Galliani rather than Giovanni Malagò.

“I represent Inter today and wish to talk about the derby,” replied Marotta.

“I will just say that some things were written that are not accurate and I hope they will be clarified soon. Everyone wants what is best for Italian football, even if there is a strong difference of opinion on how to achieve that.

“Our football movement as a whole needs to restore its status and value, we must get back to shining on the European and global stage. But today we are concentrated on this marvellous evening.”

Tottenham have been a distinctly midtable Premier League side since appointing Roberto De Zerbi at the end of March, but three wins in a row suggest they might finally be turning a corner.

De Zerbi walked into a club in crisis, scrambling to avoid an unthinkable relegation thanks to the combined efforts of Thomas Frank, Igor Tudor and several culpable board members.

Spurs were narrowly above the relegation zone, but dropped points against Sunderland and Brighton in De Zerbi’s first two outings saw them sink below West Ham and into 18th place.

But the 2-2 draw with Brighton saw the first real signs of fight in months. Spurs won their next two matches against Wolves and Aston Villa to haul themselves back above the danger zone.

Four points from their final three games saw Tottenham hold off West Ham and ensure their Premier League status.

The fans expect a lot better this year, but De Zerbi’s appointment was one that paid off for the beleaguered Spurs board – at least in the short term.

The Italian has been backed with a massive transfer outlay in the summer, but it’s taken some time for things to click into place.

Spurs failed to win any of their first six league games of the season before beating Coventry 3-1, winning at Stamford Bridge and making it three-in-a-row with a 2-0 success over Crystal Palace.

That leaves De Zerbi with 16 Premier League games under his belt, with six wins, five draws and five defeats. Spurs sit eighth in the table for the period since his appointment, having scored 18 goals and conceded 17.

The overall picture therefore remains fairly iffy when you take a wider view of his tenure. Not relegation-bad catastrophic, but still some way off pushing for Europe.

Still, there are finally signs of genuine progress. De Zerbi has some much-needed goodwill, and Spurs will hope it’s the start of something rather than another false dawn.

BBC pundit Jobi McAnuff was left thoroughly impressed by the growing midfield partnership between Tottenham Hotspur duo Sandro Tonali and Mateus Fernandes following their performance against Crystal Palace.

Speaking live on BBC Radio 5 Live after Tottenham capitalized on Jean-Philippe Mateta’s early red card to secure victory, former Palace winger Jobi McAnuff highlighted the emerging chemistry between Spurs’ central pair as a defining feature of the team’s evolution under manager Roberto De Zerbi.

Sandro Tonali and Mateus Fernandes praised for Tottenham performance

McAnuff pointed out that despite facing a 10-man Palace side for over an hour at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, the tactical synchronization between Tonali and Fernandes allowed Spurs to dictate the tempo and dominate central areas effortlessly.

He said:

“Sandro Tonali has been good. That relationship with Mateus Fernandes seems to be growing. Of course, today in different circumstances against one less player, but it hasn’t all been easy. That is one sign of improvement under Roberto De Zerbi in recent weeks.”

£200m midfield duo beginning to justify marquee price tags

The midfield pairing arrived in North London for a combined transfer fee around £200 million during a blockbuster summer transfer window, with Tottenham breaking their club transfer record twice to secure their signatures.

While both players initially required a adjustment period to adapt to De Zerbi’s demanding, high-possession tactical system, their recent performances demonstrate exactly why the Spurs hierarchy made such an immense financial investment.

In particular, Italian international Sandro Tonali has been magnificent over the past few weeks, serving as the primary rhythm-setter in midfield alongside the energetic, line-breaking distribution of Portuguese talent Mateus Fernandes.

As Spurs begin to gain momentum, with three wins in a row, the partnership of the Tonali-Fernandes pivot could prove to be key as De Zerbi’s looks to turn season around after a slow start and continue to winning run.

Gasperini: ‘Numbers count, Roma are scoring a lot of goals, a good signal’

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Gian Piero Gasperini was delighted with his Roma players after another free-flowing attacking performance in Saturday’s 5-2 victory away against Udinese, agreeing that ‘the numbers count, we score a lot of goals with a lot of players and that’s a good signal’. 

Roma extended their unbeaten start to the 2026-27 Serie A season to 10 matches with a 5-2 defeat away against struggling Udinese on Saturday evening. Five different goalscorers in Bryan Cristante, Rodrigo Mora, Donyell Malen, Evan Ndicka and Daniele Ghilardi were on the scoresheet, and over their last four matches, Roma have been averaging four goals scored per game. 

You can see how the action from Saturday’s game unfolded on the Football Italia Liveblog.

Gasperini speaks after Udinese 2-5 Roma: ‘A good signal’

ROME, ITALY – OCTOBER 20: Gian Piero Gasperini, Manager of AS Roma, looks on during the UEFA Champions League 2026/27 League Phase MD3 match between AS Roma and SK Slovan Bratislava at Stadio Olimpico on October 20, 2026 in Rome, Italy. (Photo by Paolo Bruno/Getty Images)

Speaking to DAZN after full-time on Saturday, Gasperini could not deny the fact that Roma are putting up encouraging numbers at the start of the season, particularly in front of goal. 

“The numbers count and they’re definitely telling us something. We score a lot of goals and with a lot of different players. Now we believe we can be decisive in terms of creativity and finishing. We’re scoring goals with lots of players and that’s a good signal,” said the Giallorossi head coach. 

Roma were 4-0 up at half-time against Udinese, but did drop off slightly in the second half. Whilst have scored more than any other team in the division so far this season, they have also conceded seven across their last three league matches. 

NAPLES, ITALY – OCTOBER 24: Gian Piero Gasperini AS Roma head coach during the Serie A match between SSC Napoli and AS Roma at Stadio Diego Armando Maradona on October 24, 2026 in Naples, Italy. (Photo by Francesco Pecoraro/Getty Images)

“Even the teams that compete for first place will run into problems,” Gasperini suggested. 

“We came out 4-0 up and I’d asked for us to not concede any goals, but we have to play in Manchester in three days time. This was our eighth game in 20 days and it’s normal to feel a bit tired. The second goal hurts more because we were static. But as soon as we put our foot back on the gas, we put them in difficulty.”

Once again, Roma were able to rely on a number of emerging youngsters against Udinese: Rodrigo Mora was on the scoresheet again. Emanuele Lulli scoffed midweek and Niccolo Pisilli has been playing well, too. 

“I’m lucky enough to have lots of players, young and old, who give me their availability,” said Gasperini. 

ROME, ITALY – OCTOBER 27: Rodrigo Mora of AS Roma celebrates scoring his team’s third goal during the Serie A match between AS Roma and Cagliari Calcio at Stadio Olimpico on October 27, 2026 in Rome, Italy. (Photo by Paolo Bruno/Getty Images)

“Generally, the young ones want to stand out more and improve. We’ve got a good mix. We have other flaws, but we’re doing everything well. Ghilardi was annoyed about his disallowed goal in the Champions League, a great goal, so today he’s feeling satisfied.”

It has been an encouraging run of results for Roma since the last international break, in a period that Gasperini says is unprecedented in terms of the fixture pile-up. 

“I’m struggling to remember having such a long period with three games a week. It happened to me at Atalanta when we won the Europa League, but that was in March/April. I can’t remember another period like this around this time of year, and with games at such a high level. 

“I think that we will have a few fewer commitments after the break, and then we will also be able to divide minutes a bit better when we recover Svilar, Kone, Dybala and Pellegrini.”
