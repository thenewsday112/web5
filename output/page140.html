The median monthly mortgage payment is still sitting at its second-highest level on record, spooking would-be buyers. 

What to know about the U.S. housing market for the four weeks ending October 25: 

  • The median U.S. home-sale price has fallen for the first time in more than three years. The median home-sale price was $389,601, down 0.1% year over year. That marks the second straight week in which prices ticked down–it follows a 0.2% decline last week–after three straight years of increases.
  • Homebuying demand fell to a new low. Pending home sales declined 1.8% from a week earlier to the lowest level in records dating back to 2021. For the third week in a row, pending sales declined in all 50 of the most populous U.S. metro areas. 
  • The typical homebuyer’s monthly payment hit its second-highest level on record. The median mortgage payment was $2,786, up 10.7% year over year to the highest level in our record except the prior week. The weekly average mortgage rate is 7.32%, down from 7.4% a week earlier but still near a three-year high. 
  • Would-be sellers pulled back. New listings declined 0.6% from a week earlier to their lowest level since the start of the year as homeowners react to slow homebuying demand. 
  • Total supply is creeping up. The total number of homes for sale ticked up 0.3% from a week earlier. Even though new listings are falling, pending sales are falling more, keeping the pool of inventory partly filled. There are hundreds of thousands more home sellers than buyers nationwide, giving buyers more negotiating power than they’ve had in over a decade.

Weini Kelati Has Taken a One-Mile at a Time Approach for Taking On the NYC Marathon

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

There was a time when eight miles felt like an eternity to Weini Kelati. Now, toward the tail end of preparing for her first full marathon, she can find herself 10 miles into a 20-mile workout and realize the distance that once signaled the end of a hard day has now become the halfway point.

This progression says a lot about what marathon preparation requires. The obvious difference between a half marathon and 26.2 miles is distance, but Kelati discovered that building a marathon body involved more than adding more miles to her workload. The body must be conditioned to tolerate additional volume, know how to fuel, how to recover, and eventually trust that the work was enough when fatigue arrives.

Kelati, the American half-marathon record holder, Olympian, and five-time national champion will make her marathon debut at the TCS New York City Marathon on Nov. 1. Speaking with Muscle & Fitness through New York Road Runners, she explained that she had always seen the half marathon as a gateway toward eventually racing 26.2, even giving herself a loose requirement of completing four or five before considering making the move.

“I think first I was nervous,” Kelati said. “I was like, ‘Oh my goodness. This is going to be hard because I used to be at low mileage.’”

What surprised her most isn’t how difficult the transition was, but how quickly the human body can begin adjusting to something once intimidating as simply another day in training.

Redefining Normal for the Body

During Kelati’s half-marathon preparation, she said her highest weekly mileage reached around 75 miles. For this build, she knew that number would have to climb into the 90s. The increase initially made her nervous. Once she started, however, something unexpected happened: She began enjoying it.

The biggest adjustment was redefining what constitutes a long effort. In the lead-up to a 10K or half-marathon, a six- or eight-mile tempo run could represent one of the bigger sustained sessions on her schedule. Marathon preparation introduced two different types of long runs, including easier road efforts and workouts, which she can cover approximately 20 miles at a specific pace.

“I’m not the type of person who rushes into something,” Kelati said. “I always take a lot baby steps to get used to something.”

That patience allowed her body to establish a new baseline without making every unfamiliar distance feel like a test. Her weekly structure wasn’t radically different from half-marathon training, either. The biggest change is that familiar paces and workouts extended further, asking her to sustain what she already knew for considerably longer.

The result was a changed internal relationship with distance.

“When you get to eight miles, you start saying, ‘Oh, I’m already halfway,’ instead of saying, ‘Oh, I used to stop here,” Kelati says. “The mind and the body change the way I have to handle it.”

New York Road Runners

Marathon Training Won’t Let Kelati Run on Empty

The added mileage exposed an area Kelati admits hasn’t always been a strength for her: nutrition.

At shorter distances, she could get away with habits that weren’t conducive with marathon preparation. For years, she could wake up and run without eating anything because consuming food before a morning session wasn’t something she enjoyed.

The marathon build forced her to adjust that part of her routine. When there is a large increase in workload, personal preferences eventually run into physical necessity.

“You have to help the body as much as it’s working,” Kelati said. “During the marathon training, you have to fuel your body. You have to get ready for the next hard run.”

She learned that fueling for her bigger sessions didn’t mean forcing down a huge meal. If appetite was an issue, she would have smaller meals spaced throughout the day. This same logic changed her behavior after a run.

Meals were the common recovery method for Kelati,  but the increased workload led her to incorporate recovery drinks and pay closer attention to getting nutrition back into her body sooner.

Kelati’s favorite recovery method didn’t require scheduling or involve any expensive equipment.

“I love taking naps after every single run,” she said. “It doesn’t matter how hard or easy the run I do, I just have to take a little nap every day and, of course, more than eight hours of sleep during the night.”

Kelati fully believes giving her body adequate downtime has been an important reason she was able to absorb demanding training and be able to show up the following morning and ask her body to perform again.

Training the Mind for a Foreign Distance

There is one part of marathon preparation Kelati couldn’t completely simulate.

She could run 20 miles in training, nap after training, and ask experienced marathoners what waits beyond the distances she already knows.

Until New York, however, she won’t know exactly how her own body and mind will respond to racing 26.2 miles. That uncertainty doesn’t seem to bother her as much as thinking about the entire distance at once.

“I don’t like counting the miles,” Kelati said. “It seems pretty long for me to think about it while you’re standing on the start line or you just started. You’re in the first mile, and you’re just like, ‘Oh, we have like 25 miles to go.’”

Instead, she intends on remaining completely in the moment.

“I just like to be in the present and focus one mile at a time,” she said.

Insights have helped. Kelati did the same thing before moving into half marathons, quizzing runners about which points of the race are the most challenging so she could have some idea of what awaited her. This time, the answer is a different number.

“They keep saying, ‘You’re going to start racing when you get to 30K,’” she says. “It can be different, but also it’ll give you what to expect or what’s coming when you get to that distance and how you’re going to handle it.”

Discomfort will eventually arrive, and Kelati’s strategy is a simple one. She reminds herself that the pain is temporary and that she’d rather find out what she’s capable of than finish wondering whether she would have given more.

It’s a mindset that comes from weeks of watching her body handle workloads she once was uncertain about. Kelati says one of the things she’s most proud of during this build has been how naturally she adapted to the training her coach put in front of her. Each completed run and training session has chipped away the stone of uncertainty that existed when 26.2 was still just a thought.

“When you try to do something new, you always have these thoughts that can prevent you from doing something,” Kelati said. “But then once you just start doing it, I feel like we can do anything when we believe.”

When Kelati reaches the start line in Staten Island, there will be lots of thoughts in her head. All the early morning runs, how well she’s done in races in New York, and all the months spent gradually extending what her body can tolerate.

She will let those thoughts flow. Her goal for that morning speaks to her readiness.

“I want to feel good just standing there and say, ‘Today is a great day,’” Kelati said. ‘“I feel good. Let’s do this.’”

“Being talked about…”: Everton plotting January move for “interesting” England international

Everton could look to make a major statement in the January transfer window as David Moyes considers ways to strengthen his side’s attacking options.

After concerns over the team’s lack of goals, the Toffees are reportedly exploring a move for a forward with proven Premier League experience.

Manchester United’s Marcus Rashford has emerged as a potential target, with reports suggesting Everton are discussing the possibility of bringing the England international to Merseyside.

A deal would give Moyes another versatile attacking option, although several key details would need to be resolved before a transfer could progress.

Toffees Discuss Marcus Rashford Move

According to Football Insider, former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness believes a move for Rashford is being discussed seriously ahead of the January window.

Speaking on Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness said: “Yes, this is definitely being talked about and quite strongly. And I think that could be an interesting solution.”

The former Everton chief executive also pointed to Rashford’s previous loan spells and suggested that a move closer to Manchester could benefit the player.

“Rashford did well when he was on loan at Villa and at Barcelona as well. I think it could work, and I think being close to where he is in Manchester, just along the M63, could also benefit him greatly as well,” Wyness explained.

The comments suggest that a January switch is a possibility worth monitoring, although neither club has confirmed an agreement. Separate reporting from FootballTransfers has also indicated that Everton and Manchester United have held early discussions over a potential winter move.

Wyness drew comparisons with Jack Grealish, who has made an impact at Everton when available, adding: “We’ve seen Jack (Grealish) moving across that way as well, and when he’s fit, Jack is certainly a big asset for Everton. So I think Rashford would be a very interesting signing, and I’m hearing there’s a lot of smoke here; it’s definitely being discussed.”

He then added: “I wouldn’t be surprised to see something happening in January.”

For Everton, Rashford’s versatility could be a major attraction. Although he has often operated from the left, he can also play centrally, potentially giving Moyes additional options across the frontline. His arrival could provide support for Thierno Barry and help address the team’s need for more goals.

However, the financial structure of any deal would be important. Everton would need to consider Rashford’s wages, Manchester United’s valuation and whether a permanent transfer or loan arrangement would be realistic. The forward’s willingness to join another Premier League club would also be a significant factor.

What This Means for Everton

Rashford could represent an ambitious addition for Everton if the club can find an agreement that works financially. His Premier League experience and ability to operate in several attacking positions would give Moyes greater flexibility, while a change of environment could offer the player a fresh start.

The comparison with Grealish is particularly relevant to Everton’s approach. The club could look to provide Rashford with an environment in which he can rebuild his confidence and contribute more consistently, although any potential success would depend on his fitness, form and role within the team.

Nevertheless, a transfer is far from guaranteed. The reported discussions remain at an early stage, and Everton must still resolve the financial details and establish whether Rashford would be open to the move.

For now, Wyness’s comments add weight to speculation surrounding Everton’s January plans. If negotiations progress, Rashford could become a name to watch as Moyes looks to strengthen his attacking options for the second half of the season.

For an account with 126,000 followers, that is the equivalent of tweeting your bank PIN with the caption “free yourselves.” Which, as it happens, is roughly what the post said.

Another Day Another Horrible Launch: What Happened With MIKA Crypto?

Two launches, two crashes, one afternoon. More or less this is the timeline:

  • 1:36 p.m.: a token called Free Mika (MIKA) goes live on pump.fun and runs to a $1.6 million market cap in about three minutes.
  • 1:40 p.m.: Mika posts “free yourselves knights / its time to eat. no cabal.” followed by an 87-character string. A contract address on Solana is 44 characters. Traders quickly pointed out it was the dev wallet’s private key.
  • Minutes later: Odaily traced the creator address sending about 500.44 million MIKA, half the initial supply, to another wallet, which sold about 500.83 million on PumpSwap seven seconds later. The next trades printed about 99% lower. By 1:53 p.m. the market cap was $85,500.
  • 2:08 p.m.: a second Free Mika token launches, and Mika posts its contract address a minute later, this time the right string. On GeckoTerminal, it peaks near a $1.5 million market cap within minutes and is below $300,000 within the hour.

The relaunch now sits at a market cap of about $240,000, with $41,700 of liquidity and $1.65 million traded in 24 hours. The internet, meanwhile, did what it does: DexScreener lists more than a dozen copycats with names like Free Mika King, Jail Mika and Mute Mika.

FREE MIKA Source: Dexscreener

Was the MIKA Crypto Key Post Really a Mistake?

Maybe. But it was a very specific kind of mistake. Copying a private key instead of a contract address means opening the wallet’s export screen, revealing a string twice as long as the one you meant to share, and pasting it under a caption that starts with “free yourselves.” Plenty of people in crypto have done dumber things. Few have done them with such good copywriting.

The story split in two within the hour:

  • The sympathy version: a widely shared post said Mika had been “drained for 40k” and was hosting an X Spaces “in tears.”
  • The skeptic version: Solana trader fabiano.sol, who has 88,000 followers, said the loss was staged. His screenshot shows the wallet as a System Program account holding 80 bytes of data, the size of a durable nonce account, a setup that can stop whoever holds a leaked key from moving the funds. He also flagged that, in the relaunch, the dev held about 50% of supply, and called the holder map “awful.”

Are Celebrity Tokens Back?

They never really left, they just keep ending the same way. The latest big one came seven weeks ago. Hunter Biden’s LAPTOP token launched on Base on September 9 and jumped to $190.81 two minutes in, on a pool holding about $48,000 of liquidity, then fell to $3.70 within the hour. Biden blamed a market maker and denied his team sold. Bubblemaps found about 80% of LAPTOP traders ended up underwater.

But that is just one in a long list of failed launches by these “celebrities”:

  • TRUMP (January 2025): launched three days before the inauguration and peaked at $73.43 on January 19. It trades at $1.67.
  • MELANIA (January 2025): peaked at $13.05 the same day and is now worth about 8 cents.
  • LIBRA (February 2025): Argentina’s President Javier Milei posted about it, the market cap shot past $4 billion, and it lost more than 90% within hours. He deleted the post.
  • YZY (August 2025): Kanye West’s token peaked at $2.95 and trades at $0.29.
  • HAWK (December 2024): “Hawk Tuah” influencer Hailey Welch’s token lost more than 90% within hours of launch.
  • MOTHER (May 2024): Iggy Azalea’s token is 99.9% below its peak.

Celebrity tokens, drop from all-time high. Source: CoinGecko

The pattern barely changes. A famous name brings the buyers, insiders or snipers own a large share of the supply, liquidity is thin, and the chart looks like a ski jump. The famous name moves on to the next interview. The people who bought the third green candle don’t.

Do We Really Need Celebrity Tokens?

Short answer: the buyers apparently think so, the charts disagree. A celebrity token has no product, no cash flow and, in most cases, no plan beyond launch day. What it sells is access to a name, and that lasts about as long as the first big holder’s patience.

The tools for spotting trouble keep getting better, too. A Bubblemaps holder map showed the relaunch’s 50% wallet within the hour, and on-chain sleuths had the nonce account screenshotted before the Spaces ended. The second token still traded $1.65 million in 24 hours.

MIKA crypto will probably be forgotten by next week, replaced by the next name with a ticker. If there is a lesson, it’s the oldest one in memecoins: when someone posts “its time to eat,” check who is setting the table.

Giorgi Mamardashvili has said that the information given to him about Chelsea’s penalty takers prior to Liverpool’s Carabao Cup shootout win over the Blues was erroneous.

With the teams level at 1-1 at the end of normal time, the tie would be settled on spot kicks, and the Reds prevailed 6-5 after sudden death to advance to a home quarter-final against Bournemouth.

At the end of the shootout, the home players flocked to the Georgian goalkeeper, whose save from Emmanuel Emegha proved the turning point as the Dutchman had the opportunity to win it for the visitors with his kick.

Mamardashvili: Chelsea penalty notes were wrong

It was the only one of Chelsea’s eight penalties that Mamardashvili actually saved (Valentin Barco struck the post and Wesley Fofana skied his into the Kop), and the 26-year-old joked afterwards that his notes on the Blues’ penalty takers weren’t of much use to him.

Speaking to ITV after the match, Liverpool’s number 25 said: “I had this paper as well, but everything was wrong!”, adding that the information provided to him by club staff wasn’t helpful during the shootout.

On the save from Emegha, the goalkeeper explained: “Before the shot I was looking at his [standing] leg, where he puts it down. I decided to go to my right side. I wanted to save more but sometimes one is enough.”

The Lat Training Guide For Building a Stronger and Wider Back

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Most lifters want lats for one reason:  the V-taper. Well-developed lats give your upper body that powerful look from the front, side, and back, and yes, they make T-shirts fit better.

Besides looking awesome, the lats keep your shoulders stable, support better upper-body posture, and connect your arms to your torso, improving form during heavy lifts. Strong lats improve pulling mechanics, create a stronger pressing setup, and help your upper back stay in a better position during squats and deadlifts.

Here we’ll go over all things lats, including:

  • Lat anatomy and movement
  • Why they matter
  • What the lats need for size, strength, and better movement
  • The best ways to train them
  • How to program sets, reps, frequency, and weekly volume
  • Common lat training mistakes
  • Lat training myths
  • Two sample lat workouts

By the end, you’ll know how to train your lats for a stronger back, better pulls, healthier shoulders, and an upper body that will fill out your T-shirts.

Lat Anatomy

The latissimus dorsi—or lats for short—are the body’s widest muscles and cover much of the back. They’re broad, flat, and fan-shaped, which is why well-developed lats help create that V-Taper look.

Because of their size, the lats attach to the

  • Spine
  • Pelvis
  • Shoulder blades
  • Humerus

Because they attach to the upper arm and spine, they influence both shoulder movement and posture.

Their main movements are:

  • Shoulder adduction: When you pull your arm down toward your side.
  • Shoulder extension: When you pull your arm behind your body.
  • Shoulder internal rotation: When the inside of your shoulder joint brings your forearm and hand closer to you.

They also help with scapular depression, which pulls the shoulders down away from your ears and is an important part of good pulling form. Train those actions well, and you’ll build a wider back, stronger pulls, and better control from your shoulders through your hips.

 

Why They Matter, Besides the Obvious

We all know that lats are the engine behind rows, pull-ups, and chin-ups, but some don’t realize the vital role they play beyond them.

During the deadlift, strong, engaged lats help keep the bar close to your body as you pull. When the bar drifts away, the lift gets harder, and your lower back gets unhappy.

Your lats matter during the bench press, too. When engaged, they help create a stable base to press from, improve your pressing path, and transfer force from your leg drive to your upper body.

That is why strong lats aren’t just a “back day” thing: they set the table for better pressing form.

Pulling your lats down and back creates upper-body tightness for barbell back squat. Doing so helps stabilize the bar and keeps you in a stronger, more upright position instead of folding forward into a good morning.

The lats also matter for shoulder health and posture because they influence shoulder position and upper-body alignment. When the lats are balanced in length, they let the shoulder blades rest flat, and the shoulders open back naturally. This supports better posture.

And yes, they matter outside the gym, too. Pulling open heavy doors, climbing, carrying objects close to your body, and lifting things from the floor all involve the lats. Bigger lats create the look, but stronger lats help you pull, brace, carry, and move well.

What the Lats Need

Because the lats are large and their muscle fibers run in different directions, train them through several pulling paths. You need vertical pulling, horizontal pulling, straight-arm work, loaded stretches, and good form to keep the tension where it belongs.

  • Start Vertical: Pull-ups and chin-ups remain the gold standard, while lat pulldowns let you use multiple grips and body positions to target the lats from different angles to increase volume for size and strength.
  • Rows That Train The Lats: The Underhand dumbbell row is a great example. Use an underhand grip and pull the dumbbell back in an arc, keeping your elbow close to your torso, starting below your shoulder and ending near the outside of your hip. This shifts the focus from the upper back to the lats.
  • Straight-Arm Work: Straight-arm pulldowns and their variations are as close as you’ll get to a lat isolation exercise. They’re useful because they reduce bicep involvement, making them great for lifters who struggle to feel their lats.
  • Unilateral Variations: Bilateral variations let you use more load but often hide muscle imbalances between sides. Strengthening these imbalances improves performance and strength on bilateral lifts and enhances lat development between sides.

Lat Consideration for Growth

You know the exercises, but that’s only part of it. For maximum growth and strength, keep these four things in mind.

  • Feel the Stretch: All muscles, including the lats, respond well to the loaded stretch because you’ll work through a full range of motion and the muscle stays under tension longer. Performing chin-ups and pull-ups from a dead hang will have your lats saying thank you.
  • Don’t Go Crazy:  Good form, full range of motion, and shoulder mobility all matter. Train the lats hard, but keep the reps clean. Big lats are great, but big lats that move well are better.
  • Full Range of Motion: Own both ends of the rep. Reach into the stretch under control, then finish by pulling the elbows down, back, or toward the hips, depending on the exercise. Cutting the ROM short changes your gains.

Progressive Overload: Add load, reps, sets, range of motion, or better control over time. But don’t sacrifice lat tension, shoulder position, or form to move more weight.

Sets, Reps, and Frequency

Here are general recommendations that work for most lifters most of the time.

Lat Strength

Use sets of three to six reps on heavier pulls. Good options include weighted pull-ups, heavy chin-ups, and lat pulldowns. Rest 2 to 3 minutes between sets, and keep the reps clean.

Lat Size

Use sets of eight to 15 reps on pulldowns, rows, dumbbell rows to the hip, seated machine rows, or cable rows. This is your bread-and-butter hypertrophy work. Use a full range of motion, control the stretch, and finish each rep by pulling the elbows toward the ribs or hips, depending on the exercise.

For a Better Connection

Use sets of 12 to 20 reps on straight-arm pulldowns, kneeling straight-arm pulldowns, cable pullovers, and band straight-arm pulldowns. These are great for lifters who struggle to feel their lats because these exercises isolate them.

For Deadlift and Big-Lift Carryover

Straight-arm pulldown variations are useful before deadlifting because engaging the lats helps maintain a neutral spine and keep the bar close. One or two sets of 10 to 15 reps work well as a primer.

Training Frequency

Most lifters can train the lats directly two to three times per week.

Think of 12 to 16 direct lat sets per week as your weekly target. How you divide those sets depends on your goal.

If strength is the priority, spend more sets in the 3 to 6 rep range with weighted pull-ups, heavy pulldowns, chin-ups, and rows. If size is the goal, focus more work in the 8 to 15 rep range with pulldowns, rows, single-arm work, and machine or cable variations. If you struggle to feel your lats, include 12 to 20 rep sets of straight-arm pulldowns, pullovers, or band work.

Here’s an example of how to organize your weekly volume. 12 weekly sets might include four heavier sets, six muscle-building sets, and two higher-rep straight-arm sets. That gives you a fantastic combination of strength, size, and lat connection.

Common Lat Training Mistakes

Using too much weight you cannot control might stroke the ego, but it does nothing for your gains. Here are a few more mistakes to watch out for to get the most out of your lat training.

Arm Heavy

Your hands hold the bar, but your elbows should lead the movement. When lifters focus only on pulling with their hands, chin-ups, pulldowns, and rows often turn into biceps-heavy exercises with a side order of forearm fatigue.

Fix: Drive your elbows down during pulldowns, pull your elbows toward your hips during rows, and tuck your shoulder blades into your back pocket for chin-ups.

Missing The Stretch

Many lifters cut the top of pulldowns, pull-ups, and straight-arm exercises short. That leaves a lot of potential lat tension on the table, because the lats love a loaded stretch.

Fix: Reach overhead or forward under control, keep the ribs down, and feel the stretch without dumping on the shoulder joint. The goal is to control a stretch, not just hang through the connective tissue.

Rib Flare

When the ribs flare, the lower back often arches, turning a lat exercise into a lower-back exercise. This can happen during pulldowns, pullovers, and straight-arm work when someone tries to create range they don’t own.

Fix: Keep the ribs stacked over the pelvis by breathing out and bracing the core on top of that. This gives the lats a better chance to lengthen and shorten without the lower back taking over.

Too Much Upper Traps

If your shoulders shrug toward your ears during rows or pulldowns, your upper traps dominate. Doing so takes the focus off the lats and can lead to upper trap and neck pain.

Fix: Remember to keep your shoulders away from your ears and your chest up. You do this by keeping your neck long.

4 Common Lat Training Mistakes That Ruin Back Gains

Lat training has its share of gym myths because everyone wants a wider back. Some advice sounds good until you realize it ignores range of motion, elbow path, body position, and the fact that your biceps will love to steal the show if you let them.

Wide Grip Builds Wide Lats

Wide-grip pulldowns and pull-ups are great, but they aren’t magic—and they don’t build wide lats. A wide grip doesn’t automatically increase lat tension. In fact, it often shortens your range of motion, limits the load, and shifts stress to the shoulders and upper back for many lifters. Shoulder-width, medium, and neutral grips offer a longer ROM, and you can lift more weight.

All You Need Is Pull-Ups

Pull-ups and chin-ups are gold-standard lat builders—but the lats need more. The problem is that pull-ups lock you into a single line of pull. Because the lats are large and wide, with muscle fibers running in different directions, dumbbell and machine variations let you adjust your body position and grips to change the line of pull and target muscle fibers that chin-ups miss.

Rows Are Only For The Upper Back

Rows hammer the upper back, but they can also hit the lats with the right setup, grip, and elbow path. Rowing high with flared elbows shifts more attention to the traps, rear delts, and upper back. Rowing back toward the hip with your elbow close gives the lats more work.

Lats Are Only For Back Day

They assist with deadlifts, squats, bench press setup, posture, and shoulder control. So yes, train them for width. But don’t forget they also help you lift better when no one is admiring your V-taper.

Two Sample Lat-Focused Workouts

The lats don’t need a separate “back day or bust” approach. You can train them hard during an upper-body session or weave them into a full-body workout.

Here are two examples.

 

WORKOUT 1: UPPER-BODY LAT-FOCUSED WORKOUT

Goal: Build lat size and strength while still training the chest, shoulders, upper back, and arms.

  • 1A. Chin-up, Pull-Up, or Lat Pulldown: 4 sets, 6-12 reps
  • 1B. Dumbbell Bench Press: 3 sets, 6-12 reps
  • 2A. Underhand Dumbbell Row: 3 sets, 12-15 reps per side
  • 2B. Dumbbell Overhead Press: 3 sets, 6-12 reps
  • 3A. Chest-Supported Row: 3 sets, 10-15 reps
  • 3B. Biceps Curl Variation: 3 sets, 10-15 reps

WORKOUT 2: FULL-BODY LAT WORKOUT

Goal: Train the lats as part of a full-body workout.

  • 1A. Band-Resisted RDL: 3 sets, 6-8 reps
  • 1B. Dumbbell Bench Press: 3 sets, 6-12 reps
  • 2A. Half-Kneeling Single-Arm Lat Pulldowns: 3 sets, 12-15 reps per side
  • 2B. Goblet Split Squat: 3 sets, 6-12 reps per side
  • 3A. Unilateral Cable Row: 2-3 sets, 15 reps per side
  • 3B. Back Extension: 2-3 sets, 12-15 reps
  • 3C. Dumbbell Pullover: 2-3 sets, 10-15 reps

The Final Set

The winning formula is this: Train the lats with vertical pulls, rows, straight-arm work, single-arm variations, and exercises that challenge the stretch position. Keep the ribs down, control the shoulders, pull toward the hips to engage more lats, and stop letting your biceps, traps, and lower back take over.

Remember to lock the lats in during your squat, bench, and deadlift because they are the difference between a sloppy rep and a clean one. Use enough volume to grow and enough weight to build muscle and strength, but never shortchange the range of motion.

Series B Funding Is Up, But Fewer Deals Are Getting Done

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

More money, fewer rounds. That, in short, is the state of U.S. Series B startup funding in 2026, as venture capital investment continues to concentrate heavily around a handful of AI hotshots.

Crunchbase data shows that so far this year, investors have poured around $48 billion into financings around the Series B stage 1, the highest tally in years. Yet round counts are below year-ago levels and are on track for a multiyear low.

Smaller Series B rounds grow scarcer

One trend driving capital concentration is a sharp decline in small and mid-sized Series B rounds.

So far this year, less than a third of deals in our sample set 2 were for under $30 million. By contrast, between 2021 and 2025, rounds in this range accounted for about half of deals, per Crunchbase data.

The smallest Series B deals have gotten particularly scarce. This year there were just 26 rounds under $10 million in our sample set.

Big rounds still a big thing

On the flip side, really large Series B rounds are quite popular.

At least 110 companies have raised rounds of $100 million or more around this stage so far this year. That’s roughly a quarter of all deals.

Of these, the largest by far was a $12 billion round 3 for Prometheus, Jeff Bezos’ physical AI startup that launched last year.

Model developer World Labs, led by AI pioneer Fei-Fei Li, was tied for next-biggest fundraiser, closing on $1 billion in February. Nuclear energy startup Valar Atomics also secured $1 billion in Series B funding across tranches this year, with Sequoia Capital as lead investor.

For  a wider snapshot, below we put together a sample list of more of this year’s biggest U.S. Series B recipients.

Mostly an AI story

Meanwhile, one of the least surprising findings from our analysis of Series B recipients is that it was a heavily AI-focused group.

So far this year, about two-thirds of Series B funding has gone to AI-focused startups, per Crunchbase data. In 2025, by comparison, about half of investment went to companies in AI categories.

AI companies were also heavily represented by round count. So far in 2026, more than half of all reported U.S. Series B deals have been for AI companies.

Bullish climate for Series B

Overall, the numbers point to a bullish climate for Series B investment, particularly for AI startups with demonstrated traction. The environment looks particularly favorable for companies that have already raised considerable sums at Series A and seed.

Even so, these don’t look like the best of times for startups seeking smaller Series B rounds. Seems like in the current AI-driven cycle, investors are increasingly focused on big checks for high-valuation companies.

Jumbo Series A Rounds Hiding The Real Squeeze On Seed Startups

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Global startups have closed at least 114 Series A rounds of $100 million or more so far this year, Crunchbase reported in late September, and more than 70% of them went to AI companies. For most seed founders, that headline is a warning.

U.S. startups closed 272 Series A rounds in the third quarter, according to Crunchbase data, about 28% fewer than the 377 a year earlier. Seed held up better, at 984 rounds vs. 1,059. The megarounds hide how few A rounds are getting done.

Seed checks keep growing. The median U.S. seed round was about $3 million last year, 3x its 2018 level, according to Crunchbase. Yet only 24% of companies that raised a $1 million-plus seed in 2023 have moved past seed, and just 16% of the 2024 cohort, down from 55% or higher through 2020.

Size your seed against the Series A

Uncork Capital‘s Andy McLoughlin told Crunchbase News that Series A investors now expect $2 million to $3 million in ARR, sometimes $4 million, and the time from seed to A has stretched past two years. If a seed won’t carry you to that bar with six months to spare, a smaller round is the better deal.

A bigger seed isn’t required. Take Atomic, a New York-based supply chain planning software company founded in 2023 (and not an MGV portfolio company). It raised a $3 million seed, right at the median, in April 2025, led by Madrona and DVx Ventures, then closed a $12.5 million Series A led by Madrona and Klass Capital about 17 months later.

Map your Series A leads now

Funds of $100 million to $500 million typically lead the Series A. Figure out which ones lead at your stage and when their current fund closes, then send the right partners updates for a year before you ask for anything.

Sell your way to the bar

The Series A bar is now a revenue bar, which makes it a go-to-market problem first. Most seed companies I meet have a stronger product than sales motion.

At MGV, our work starts with a first session we call Sales Jumpstart: we interview the founders and their team. We then score the company on our proprietary matrix: where it is today, where it should be, and what it needs to become a scalable go-to-market machine.

From there, founders work through workshops on outbound reach, qualification, profiling, the funnel, objection handling, hiring assessment, compensation plans, fundraising, deck building, storytelling and investor intros, with mentoring alongside.

A company at $2 million in ARR with a sales process a new hire can run is a better Series A candidate than one at $3 million built on founder-led deals nobody else can repeat. The founders who make it are already planning for the round after this one.

Arsenal Max Dowman Red Card Appeal Faces Rejection, Says Dermot Gallagher

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former Premier League official Dermot Gallagher has said he thinks Arsenal won’t be successful in their appeal against Max Dowman’s red card in their Carabao Cup loss to Fleetwood Town.

The 16-year-old Arsenal winger was shown a red card for violent conduct after lashing out at Harrison Neal after a strong challenge from the Fleetwood defender. The Gunners then lost the fourth-round tie on penalties.

Arsenal are appealing the sending off of Max Dowman. They need to have their paperwork in by 1pm today (Thursday). They will be hoping to get a decision before their Premier League game against Liverpool on Sunday.

But Gallagher does not think they will be successful in their appeal as he believes there was clear intent from Dowman.

“I think it was a red card for the actions of the teenager,” Gallagher said.

He also thinks the referee should have given Arsenal a free kick as well as booking Neal for his challenge on Dowman.

Dermot Gallagher Explains Why Arsenal’s Appeal Could Fail

Gallagher told Sky Sports News that the attitude of Dowman could be an issue for Arsenal in their red card appeal.

“I think he went out of his way to kick his leg up. It makes it difficult to overturn the decision,” Gallagher said.

“He’s got more of an aggressive reaction than what Cole Palmer had recently with Mateus Fernandes.”

But Gallagher was critical of the challenge made by Neal on the Arsenal youngster. He said that while Fleetwood’s defender did win the ball back, he didn’t think the challenge was made with enough consideration for Dowman’s safety.

“I think he should have got a free-kick and a yellow card,” he added.

The debate leaves Arsenal facing a difficult decision ahead of their Premier League fixture against Liverpool. If the appeal fails, Dowman’s dismissal will stand.

Paul Merson Questions Max Dowman’s Red Card

Sky Sports pundit Paul Merson said he disagreed with Gallagher’s comments, saying it was a harsh red card.

“It was a classic old fashioned tackle. He (Neal) wins the ball and then follows through. I think the reaction from the players might have had something to do with the referee giving Dowman a red card.”

Merson also warned the young Gunner that he will always be a target for opposition players.

Dowman has had disciplinary problems in the past, being sent off whilst playing for England against the USA. Merson feels the youngster needs to learn to control his emotions and not get caught up in the taunts of opposition players.

The incident has also raised more questions over how Arsenal are developing one of their most promising young talents.

Mikel Arteta Faces Questions Over Max Dowman Reaction

Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta also attracted criticism for his reaction to Dowman after the Carabao Cup defeat to Fleetwood Town.

As the teenager walked towards the tunnel, Arteta leaned towards him and spoke to him. Merson questioned the approach, arguing that the Arsenal manager should have offered reassurance to the 16-year-old instead.

Arteta declined to reveal exactly what he told Dowman, explaining only that they had discussed the matter before the match.

The Arsenal boss also said the team had prepared for the emotional challenges they expected to face during the tie.

Jobi McAnuff similarly questioned Arteta’s response, arguing that Dowman remains young and will inevitably make mistakes as he develops.

Arsenal must now wait for the outcome of their red card appeal. With Liverpool next on the fixture list, the decision could have important implications for Arteta’s selection plans.
