Olaver unveils multi-purpose Laneway Studio with "lantern-like" glass-block facade

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

In the garden of a Melbourne family home, a facade of glass blocks set within recycled white brick and a concrete band beam tops this two-storey studio by Australian architecture practice Olaver.

Olaver designed Laneway Studio to appear as a "beautiful object" reflecting light into the garden in the day and glowing like a lantern at night. The multi-purpose building was designed in response to the growing needs of the family of five, offering a practical alternative to renovating the existing home.

It houses a study-cum-guest-room with an en-suite and terrace on the first floor and an open-plan living area below, fitted with concealed laundry facilities and a bar.

The new studio block was constructed behind the main house and its newly configured garden, designed by landscape architect Penny Starr to include a pool and areas of planted foliage.

Large glass-panelled sliding doors leading into the garden from both the house and studio create a seamless flow between the interior and exterior living spaces.

Another sliding door on the opposing wall of the studio, made from oak with a steel-clad exterior, connects the studio's living space with the titular laneway behind the property.

"With large sliding doors on opposing walls, the ground floor can open right through, so it works as an extension of the garden and pool terrace to the laneway rather than a closed room", Olaver director Emlyn Olaver told Dezeen.

"Open, the space feels far bigger than its footprint, and the family can spread across the house, the pool and the studio as one connected space when entertaining", she continued.

Inside, the ground floor was designed to be minimal and open-plan to ensure its multi-functionality. It features neutral terrazzo flooring throughout. Oak cabinetry conceals storage, laundry facilities and a TV nook and frames a central bar area with a stainless-steel sink and surround.

"We wanted the studio to be calm, with joinery doing the heavy lifting so each space can change roles without feeling cluttered", Olaver explained.

"Every space had to do more than one job; a laundry, a living room or a bar depending on the day. That flexibility also gives the building a longer life, because the family's needs will keep changing as the kids grow up, move out and come back."

On the first floor's east-facing wall, overlooking the garden, a facade of glass blocks was installed above a concrete band beam to bypass planning restrictions on the use of conventional windows.

The glass-block facade brings in soft morning light while offering privacy to the first-floor interior and, viewed from the garden and house, transforms the studio into a "glowing lantern" at night, Olaver said.

The first-floor interiors were also designed to be multi-functional, comprising a combined study and guest bedroom with an en-suite and small roof terrace. Red wraparound tiling in the bathroom is echoed by red panelling in the nook of the foldable bed, itself concealed behind more oak joinery when not in use.

"The new studio's interiors echo the existing home's rich but calm textures," Olaver said. "Natural materials and tactile elements exist in both spaces, but the studio is slightly more playful in its approach."

The project tasked Olaver with extending the home of a family rapidly outgrowing their existing space.

"In Melbourne, the usual answer is yet another extension that eats into the backyard," she said. "Laneway Studio goes the other way, keeping the garden at the centre of the site and showing how a well-designed studio can add flexibility and density to an existing lot."

More studio developments recently featured on Dezeen include a Camber Sands studio made using waste materials and a green-tiled studio in a London garden.

World Anti-Doping Agency’s Latest Banned List Sends a Warning to Athletes Looking for ‘Loopholes’

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There is a certain kind of athlete who treats the World Anti-Doping Agency’s prohibited list like a treasure map. He reads it, finds something that isn’t printed there, and figures he’s discovered a loophole. The molecule has a name that sounds like a Wi-Fi password, the vial says “research use only,” and some guy with a podcast swears it repairs tendons, melts fat, or turns you into a gazelle. Perfect. What could possibly go wrong?

Well, WADA just made that little game harder. Its 2027 Prohibited List, which takes effect Jan. 1, now explicitly mentions peptides in the category for non-approved substances. The U.S. Anti-Doping Agency put out an advisory explaining the change and warning athletes not to assume an experimental peptide is legal simply because its particular alphabet soup isn’t spelled out on the list. That’s a useful distinction, because the peptide business has become a sprawling mess of legitimate medicine, promising research, questionable claims, and little bottles of who-the-hell-knows-what.

What Changes Are Included in WADA’s 2027 Prohibited List?

Before anybody starts screaming that WADA banned every peptide on earth, it didn’t. Peptides are chains of amino acids, and some are established medicines. Insulin is a peptide. The relevant category, called S0, covers pharmacological substances without approval for human therapeutic use by a government health authority anywhere in the world, provided they aren’t already covered by another prohibited class. Some peptides fall into other categories. The word “peptides” appearing in S0 is a clarification, not a declaration that every peptide drug is forbidden.

The important point is that experimental substances weren’t necessarily fair game yesterday. BPC-157, for example, was already prohibited under the rules for non-approved substances. Others, including certain growth-hormone-related compounds, are covered elsewhere. What changed is the language, making it even harder to pretend that an unlisted research chemical gets a free pass. And if you compete in a tested sport, “I couldn’t find it on Google” ranks right up there with road rage. No one is gonna buy it.

There are some other interesting additions as well. WADA names two experimental metabolic modulators, SLU-PP-332 and SLU-PP-915, as examples in its hormone and metabolic modulators category. The list also adds examples of diuretics and masking agents, including eplerenone and finerenone, drugs that have perfectly legitimate medical uses. That’s another reality athletes forget: the prohibited list isn’t just a catalog of things somebody invented to cheat. It can include ordinary prescription medications, depending on the drug and circumstances.

Meanwhile, semaglutide and tirzepatide, the active ingredients in two widely used families of weight-loss and diabetes treatments, remain on WADA’s monitoring program for 2027. Monitoring is not banning. WADA is collecting information about their use in sport; it has not put them on the prohibited list. The same distinction matters with caffeine, which is monitored in competition but not prohibited. Which makes me feel good that the people making the rules recognize a difference between watching something and convicting it.

While they were at it, they also tuned up the cannabis language. WADA clarified that psychoactive THC-related cannabinoids, including certain synthetic and semi-synthetic versions, are prohibited in competition. CBD itself isn’t prohibited, but a CBD product containing undeclared THC can still create a problem. Meaning, you run the risk of failing a drug test.

It doesn’t matter if you’re an Olympian, a fighter, a weightlifter, or some local competitor. Under anti-doping rules, athletes are responsible for what’s in their bodies. What’s missing off a label is not an alibi. Neither is a coach or a doctor. You’re on your own. So play the game wisely.

I am 100% for researching new performance drugs. That’s how some useful things eventually get discovered. But research is not approval, and a promising animal study is not proof in humans. None of that really matters to many athletes because they usually rely on anecdotal evidence. They will do it anyway—if they think it works and can find a way of not getting caught. Which, for the most part, in my view of things, they do. But if your competitive career matters to you, you need to be very careful, as peptides create a very broad gray area. Make sure you check the actual status of the substance before you put it in your body—and remember, just because WADA has banned it doesn’t mean that there’s a test for it. January isn’t far away, and WADA is making one thing abundantly clear: They’re always a step behind. I guess it never occurs to them to switch hats and discover the new stuff before we do.

Rock Spire Collapses at Popular Turkish Tourist Site, Killing Three

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Part of a rock spire known as a fairy chimney collapsed yesterday near the entrance to one of Turkey's most popular tourist destinations, killing three people and injuring five others.

The incident occurred around 3:15 pm local time in the town of Goreme, in central Turkey’s Cappadocia region yesterday. Rocks broke away and fell onto people near the parking area. The site is part of a UNESCO World Heritage landscape, famous for its cave dwellings and cone-shaped rock formations. Nevsehir Governor Huseyin Kok said that heavy rainfall triggered a landslide that caused the rocks to detach from the formation, according to Reuters.

Rescue teams initially recovered two bodies from the rubble before discovering a third victim overnight, bringing the death toll to three. The deceased include two Turkish citizens and one foreign national. Five others were injured in the collapse. Some of them were later discharged from hospital, according to AFP.

Sudden collapse

Eyewitnesses described a loud noise followed by a large dust cloud. The area was closed to the public, and search operations concluded overnight under floodlights.

Turkish Justice Minister Akin Gurlek said an investigation by the chief prosecutor’s office, along with expert assessments, would begin. The collapse occurred on Turkey’s Republic Day, a public holiday when visitor numbers were high.

Cappadocia, in central Anatolia, is renowned for its distinctive fairy chimneys. These tall, thin spires of soft volcanic rock topped by harder caps were shaped over millions of years by wind and water erosion. The Goreme Open-Air Museum and surrounding sites were put on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985 for their blend of natural landforms and human history, including Byzantine rock-cut churches and underground settlements. The region’s soft rock is geologically fragile and prone to weathering and occasional rockfalls, particularly after heavy rain.

Turkish authorities have previously carried out restoration and monitoring work to address erosion and climate-related risks, and smaller non-fatal collapses were recorded in the area in 2024 and 2025. The site remains one of Turkey’s most visited destinations, drawing tourists for hot-air balloon rides, hiking, and exploration of its unique geology and heritage.

JASON AVELAR AND GARRETT BEAL SHARE THE STORY OF PRESCOTT JUNIOR HIGH’S LEGACY OF PHYSICAL EDUCATION EXCELLENCE

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump announced the re-establishment and restoration of the Presidential Fitness Test on July 31, 2025, when he signed an Executive Order revitalizing the test along with the President’s Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition. Since then, the Trump administration also announced the Presidential Fitness School Innovation Challenge, a competition which invites “schools, districts, and youth-serving organizations to design and execute creative, repeatable models that inspire student participation, teamwork, and enthusiasm for the Presidential Fitness Test (PFT).”

One school that could serve as an example for others to follow and may be a top contender should they enter that challenge would be Prescott Junior High School in Modesto, California. The school overall has a great reputation, but it is best known for their P.E. program. Current teachers Jason Avelar and Garrett Beal are two of the teachers for this program, but its legacy as a fitness-focused juggernaut goes back nearly a half-century. 

Beal, who also served as a police officer for a period of his life, played sports as a kid but credited a high school wrestling coach for introducing him to the weight room.

“It was the first sport where I saw that being strong helped,” he said. “I fell in love even more with fitness than wrestling.”

Avelar was bitten by the iron bug while playing football in his high school days but also found he had natural leadership skills as a linebacker. His coaches confirmed this and could have played a role in him becoming a gym teacher.

 “I had a few coaches and teachers pull me aside and said, ‘Hey, I think you’d be a great teacher.’”

Prescott Junior High

THE ROOTS OF PRESCOTT RUN DEEP IN THE COMMUNITY

 Both Beal and Alevar are reminded often about the history they are a part of. Modeled closely after the La Sierra High School Physical Education Program (the famous 1960s President’s Council on Physical Fitness model) Prescott’s program has challenged junior high students for decades using a high-standard and military-inspired structure.

In 1973, then Prescott P.E. teacher Mark Wilson saw a demonstration of a high-intensity, physical fitness-based curriculum. Wilson was inspired by the concept and used his own version on his students, challenging them to make fitness a priority at an early age.

There is a color and points based system to help gauge the students’ progress and motivate them to work harder. All students start with zero points and white trunks, and there are 20 different fitness tests. Each test offers one, two, or four points for their performances. Once a student achieves 60 points, they earn red trunks. However, the scoring goes back to zero because they are at a new level with harder challenges. Once they work back up to 60 points, they then earn blue trunks.

“The tests at that point get very challenging,” Avelar said. There are also gold and purple trunks for those students that do very well in the program. Prescott even has a P.E. Hall of Fame, where students can be recognized. Avelar acknowledged one student who even cleared the purple trunk scoring early, meaning they had to create a new level just for her.

“We decided on custom trunks. Once you pass purple, you get to pick your own.”

The bars may be seen as super high for some, but Avelar and Beal share that either students rise to the occasion and reach it, or they get closer than they may have had there not been that high bar in the first place. 

The results speak for themselves. One student ran two miles in 12 minutes, 27 seconds. Another performed over 6,000 continuous sit-ups, and multiple students have reportedly performed over 1,000 unbroken pushups. The Prescott program has had a generational impact in Modesto.

Beal said, “I wear my Prescott shirt anywhere around town, and people stop to talk to me about when they went there.” He even met one man at a book club, and he recognized the last name on their Hall of Fame wall.

“He told me, ‘Yeah, my grandma.”

Prescott Junior High

THE PASSION FOR FITNESS STAYS WITH THEM

Other teachers at Prescott were also students there, and both Beal and Avelar confirmed that they strive to be the best examples possible by making their wellness a priority. They also said that even know the high school P.E. programs may not have as intense of programs, the students who went to Prescott continue their fitness journeys after they move on. Others may take the school for granted but realize what they were a part of later on.

Avelar said, “As they leave, they realize ‘Oh crap, this is a super special place.’”

Both teachers take great pride in not only what they are a part of but having added their own chapters to the Prescott story. They are reminded of how important their work is every day, and they definitely don’t take the positions they are in for granted.

Avelar stated. “The fulfillment I get from showing up to school every day and seeing kids reach their goals, push themselves, do things they’ve never done before, and seeing the excitement on their faces is why I do it.”

Beal added, “This is such a special place to be,”

There is no official word on how many schools will enter the Presidential Fitness School Innovation Challenge, but both Avelar and Beal were pleased that the Presidential Fitness Test has been reinstated, and they hope that it can serve as a catalyst for other P.E. programs to do more to help their students embrace fitness, pushing themselves, and taking on great challenges. As they have seen first hand, the impact goes beyond the school walls and can last for many years to come.

To learn more about the PFT or the School Innovation Challenge, go to fitness.gov. 

You can follow the Prescott program on Instagram @prescott_standard and Beal and Alevar on LinkedIn.
