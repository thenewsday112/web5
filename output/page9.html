Shopify’s Base USDC Checkout Puts Stablecoins Into Mainstream E-Commerce

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The case study referenced more than $42 million of net settled payments and over 448,000 payments on the underlying Commerce Payments Protocol dashboard as of October 2. Availability, merchant eligibility, foreign exchange terms and processing fees remain important.

Base published a Shopify case study on October 29 describing how Shopify and Coinbase built a Commerce Payments Protocol that lets eligible merchants accept USDC through Shopify Payments. Under the design, customers pay with a dollar stablecoin while merchants may receive payouts in their local currency.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Shopify and Coinbase. It offers a specific basis for assessing Shopify’s Base-based USDC checkout model without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The case study referenced more than $42 million of net settled payments and over 448,000 payments on the underlying Commerce Payments Protocol dashboard as of October 2. Those figures cover activity tracked across the protocol and must not be misrepresented as Shopify-only sales or Shopify order counts.

The development arrives as digital-asset markets remain sensitive to funding conditions, infrastructure reliability and the gap between promotional claims and operational results. It warrants attention on its own facts, without assuming a particular cryptocurrency must rise or fall in response.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Shopify’s Base-based USDC checkout model establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

The important shift is reducing the visibility of the blockchain itself. Stablecoin acceptance is more likely to spread if stores can activate it without managing smart-contract tooling, operating their own crypto wallet or taking unnecessary exposure to currency fluctuations.

Successful payment systems hide most of their technical complexity. Customers generally care that money arrives, merchants can account for it and transaction costs are predictable. Network choice matters to builders, but it is not usually the feature buyers want to navigate.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

The consumer sees a checkout process; under the hood, a stablecoin transaction reaches the merchant through a protocol that can integrate with ordinary payment and accounting operations. A merchant need not treat holding USDC as its commercial objective if it prefers normal fiat settlement.

Availability, merchant eligibility, foreign exchange terms and processing fees remain important. The report’s zero customer gas-fee figure should not be confused with an absence of merchant fees or other costs.

Automated or stablecoin-based payments are also exposed to ordinary commercial risks: incorrect recipients, operational downtime, liquidity shortages and inconsistent compliance coverage. New rails need dependable safeguards, not just faster transaction confirmation.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Retail adoption should be judged by participating merchants, transaction completion rates, actual purchase demand and the payout experience. Protocol-level figures offer one measure of activity, not a complete view of Shopify consumer acceptance.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Cowboys vs Cardinals: Writer predictions for Week 8

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Photo by Sam Hodde/Getty Images | Getty Images

Dallas returns home with some momentum after a convincing 38-21 win over Philadelphia. The Cowboys are now 3-4 and face a 2-5 Arizona team that has struggled for consistency on both sides of the ball. The challenge for Dallas is making sure Monday night’s performance becomes the start of something rather than another one-off result.

When Arizona has the ball

Arizona will continue with Jacoby Brissett at quarterback, and the biggest threat in this offense is tight end Trey McBride. McBride produced 111 receiving yards against Denver and leads Arizona with 59 catches for 472 yards. Expect the Cardinals to use him heavily over the middle, especially against Dallas linebackers and safeties.

Dallas will try to keep everything in front of them and force Arizona to put together long drives. The opportunity for the Cowboys pass rush is also there. Brissett was pressured on more than half of his dropbacks against Denver, while Dallas generated two sacks and an interception against Philadelphia. The Cowboys only have 10 sacks for the season, so this is another matchup where Quinnen Williams and the defensive front need to turn pressure into actual negative plays.

When Dallas has the ball

Dallas should start exactly where it finished in Philadelphia with Javonte Williams. The Cowboys rushed for 204 yards against the Eagles, with Williams producing 155 yards and a touchdown on 25 carries. That balance completely changed the offense. Dak Prescott only needed 27 pass attempts, was not sacked and threw three touchdowns without an interception. That is the formula Dallas has been searching for all season.

Arizona gives them every reason to continue with it. The Cardinals allowed 377 total yards and 30 points to Denver last week, including a 62-yard quarterback run and several explosive plays. Arizona entered that game at the bottom of the NFL in points allowed, so Dallas should make this defense handle Williams before spreading the field with CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens. Lamb scored twice against Philadelphia, while Pickens added another touchdown, giving Prescott several different ways to attack once Arizona starts committing defenders toward the run.

Now onto the predictions from your BTB writers…

Tom Ryle (4-3)

I wasn’t really surprised to see the win last week, or to see the early game issues, but the finish was better than I expected. Frankly, they need something very similar against the Cardinals for the same reasons. This is not a good Arizona team and it has been getting kicked around by the good teams it plays. If the Cowboys really expect to be taken seriously, they need another strong win. I don’t know if this is more a prediction or a hope, but I’ll say DAL 34 ARI 19.

Brandon Loree (5-2)

Since 2021, the Dallas Cowboys haven’t won a game where they’ve scored 18 points or less. Conversely, Dallas has the fifth-most wins when they score 21 or more points. 

The Arizona Cardinals seem to have the Cowboys’ number, winning the last four matchups. Arizona isn’t great defensively, but their offense can be feisty headlined by Trey McBride and Jeremiyah Love potentially coming back from injury.

I think this week’s game comes down to a Brandon Aubrey field goal, and the Cowboys are on the positive side of the stat above, winning 24-21.

Dyllan Rivers (4-2)

I feel less comfortable this week against a struggling Cardinals teams that still has some healthy playmakers on offense. Assuming he plays, the Cowboys have to eliminate running back Jeremiyah Love as well as tight end Trey McBride. I think they do just enough defensively to get the ball back to the offense to stack some scores at some point against a struggling Cardinals run defense. I’ll take Dallas 34-24

Matt Holleran (3-4)

The Cowboys showed last week they are capable of beating an inferior opponent. The Arizona Cardinals fit that billing again this week, but it’s hard to trust the Cowboys when any sort of consistency is expected. I see this being a sloppy game, but when it matters most, Dallas’ offense makes the plays they need to. The Cowboys get a win and move back to .500 on the year. Give me Dallas in a weird one, 26-24. 

Mike Poland (3-4)

The Cowboys have momentum and home field advantage here. Arizona are struggling to finish drives. They rank 22nd in the NFL in total offensive yards but rank 26th in points per game, and that’s down to the fact they rank dead last in redzone efficiency. They can move the ball to some degree through the air, but once in the redzone they fail to convert. 

On the opposite side, the Cardinals defense is allowing the most points per game, have the 11th-worst redzone efficiency, and rank 15th in third-conversion allowed. Dallas takes the win here 28-17. 

Position grades from Michigan Football’s narrow win at Rutgers

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
PISCATAWAY, NEW JERSEY - OCTOBER 31: Salesi Moa #12 of the Michigan Wolverines celebrates a catch against the Rutgers Scarlet Knights during the first half at SHI Stadium on October 31, 2026 in Piscataway, New Jersey. (Photo by Ed Mulholland/Getty Images) | Getty Images

True to form, Michigan made things interesting, but still got the win 28-21 over Rutgers on Saturday. It definitely wasn’t perfect, but here’s a look at how each position group graded out in the victory.

Quarterback – A-

After a less-than-stellar debut as the starting quarterback last week, Tommy Carr looked really good against Rutgers. Granted, it was against a defense that isn’t scaring anyone (even on Halloween), but Carr went 22-of-27 for 266 yards and one touchdown. He also added 54 yards on the ground (82 excluding sacks) with two scores.

The ground game was where Carr made his biggest impact, with his fourth-quarter touchdown deciding the game. He also extended several plays that he wasn’t well-protected and ended up being able to make something out of nothing through the air. He’ll get to make his first start in a rivalry game next week against Michigan State.

Running Back – C

Once again, the run game wasn’t impressive, with fault falling on both the offensive line and the running backs. Jordan Marshall wasn’t featured past the start of the game and had five carries for zero yards, despite scoring a two-yard touchdown. Savion Hiter was the featured back and had a decent performance with 20 carries for 76 yards. Bryson Kuzdzal was pretty efficient with 37 yards on just six carries.

Had the run game been better, this one probably wouldn’t have been nearly as close.

Wide Receiver – B

With Carr looking more comfortable, the pass game got going a bit more than we’ve seen recently. Andrew Marsh was Michigan’s leading receiver with seven receptions for 51 yards while also getting interfered with on what would’ve been a deep touchdown pass in the fourth quarter.

JJ Buchanan had another solid performance with five receptions for 63 yards, including an impressive touchdown grab on which he was interfered with and still made the play. Salesi Moa also continues to get involved and had a 44-yard catch-and-run on the opening drive of the game.

Blocking on the perimeter was rough, but the receivers did their primary job of getting open a lot more.

Tight End – B-

Carr was able to spread the ball around a bit, leading to the tight ends being much more involved. Hogan Hansen reeled in three passes for 42 yards, continuing to be a nice safety valve in the short passing game. Mason Bonner also made his first reception of the season with a 27-yard grab in the first quarter. Meanwhile, Jalen Hoffman had two receptions for 17 yards. Continuing to get this group involved can be really helpful in making the offense more productive down the stretch,

Offensive Line – D+

Michigan got right tackle Blake Frazier back in the starting lineup and rolled with Brady Norton and Nathan Efobi as the starting guards. Going up against one of the worst defensive fronts in the conference, Michigan’s offensive line still wasn’t able to put anything promising together, struggling in both the run and pass games.

Of note, Norton did go down with an injury in the third quarter, while center Jake Guarnera, who’s been Michigan’s best offensive lineman all season, also came up hobbled in the fourth quarter. Both their statuses will be worth monitoring heading into next week.

Defensive Line – C+

Michigan’s defense struggled on Saturday, giving up its highest point total of the season. The issues started up front with the defensive line, which failed to take advantage of a bad Rutgers offensive line. The pass rush was pretty quiet all day, but the run game was a bit more stout, holding Rutgers to 126 yards on the ground with just 54 of those coming on one run by Antwan Raymond.

On the bright side, sophomore Benny Patterson broke out with the best day of any Wolverine defensive lineman, leading the team in tackles with seven.

Linebacker – B-

It’s not saying much, but the linebackers were the best defensive position group against Rutgers. They did a nice job keeping Rutgers’ run game in check (save for the Raymond run) and generating some modest pressure on quarterback A.J. Surace. The lone major blemish came in coverage when Nathaniel Staehling got burned in coverage by Raymond for a 32-yard gain in the second quarter. Overall though, there wasn’t nearly as much havoc as we’ve seen from the linebackers in previous games.

Secondary – D

Michigan’s secondary had a rough afternoon, making a handful of mistakes that played a role in making this a close game. Jyaire Hill, Smith Snowden and Zeke Berry were all tagged for penalties to help Rutgers get downfield, even with its backup quarterback throwing the ball up for grabs much of the time. There was also a blown coverage in the second quarter on which Snowden fell down, allowing Jourdin Houston to score on an 80-yard play. There were also dropped interceptions by Snowden and Mason Curtis.

The secondary has been in the good graces of fans for much of the year, so hopefully this performance doesn’t become a trend.

Special Teams – D+

The special teams continued to be pretty bad. Kicker Trey Butkowski once again missed two field goals, coming from 38 and 45 yards out. Additionally, the Wolverines were flagged on a confusing kick-catch interference that negated a muffed punt, which would’ve set Michigan’s offense up with an instant red zone opportunity. The blunders didn’t cost the Wolverines in this game, but things have been trending downward in the third phase of the game for a few weeks now.

Coaching – D

Michigan accomplished probably its two biggest goals of the week in getting the win and making its offense more functional. But the fact this game was close at all is still an indictment of those at the top of the program. Michigan pretty much sleepwalked through this game and showed to be pretty undisciplined once again with seven penalties for 90 yards, but avoided what would’ve been its worst loss to date.

Next week, Michigan will get to take on a Michigan State team that will surely be ready to play. Let’s see how Kyle Whittingham can prepare his team for a rivalry game that will draw plenty of attention despite down years for both teams.

Cadillac Made a Blacked-Out Escalade America Isn’t Getting

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

One of the Few in Japan

American cars aren’t a big deal in Japan, although some models are sold there; the appeal is mostly in niche products like Jeeps or sports cars like the Corvette. Another niche segment that one American car maker competes in is luxury, and you already know that’s Cadillac.

For those who don’t know, America’s definition of premium SUV mobility, the Cadillac Escalade, is actually sold in Japan. More recently, Cadillac Japan announced a special edition that will be sold only in its local market.

Cadillac

Noir Edition

Just launched by General Motors Japan, with its President and CEO, Itaru Wakamatsu, is the special edition, Cadillac Escalade Noir Edition. As we know, Noir means black, and that’s the overall theme of this Escalade, turning it into a stealthy, menacing SUV. Its main exterior detail is the matte black XPEL Stealth PPF, which the brand claims gives it an elegant sheen. Since it’s PPF, the whole thing is scratch-resistant and easy to maintain. Cadillac also added XPEL’s patented PPF on the hood, body sides, and rear tailgate.

Other exterior panels, like the front grille, were finished in gloss black to contrast the flatter shine of the matte panels. The exterior also features a new 22-inch 18-spoke rim design finished in midnight aluminum. Cadillac says the overall exterior finish exudes strength and beauty. Inside, the Escalade continues the blacked-out motif but adds a contrasting wood-grain panel. The interior includes exclusive Noir Edition trims and features, like an exclusive “ornament” on the passenger side. It also has Noir Edition floor mats, with a limited-edition nameplate and silver-colored piping.

Cadillac

Familiar Performance

The Cadillac Escalade Noir Edition is now available in Japanese dealerships for 21,000,000 Japanese Yen ($133,000). It will also be limited to just 20 units, making it truly special.

The Noir Edition remains basically the same in performance and features. The Escalade in Japan still rides on the brand’s Air Ride Adaptive Suspension, Magnetic Ride Control, and GM’s much-loved 6.2-liter V8 engine. The Escalade still has the massive 55-inch curved LED display, AKG in-car surround system, and the tablet-sized command center.
