Step Inside ‘The Susman House’: A Flawlessly Preserved Modernist Retreat on California’s Coast

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A three-bedroom seaside sanctuary perched high above a sandy cove at Faria Beach in Ventura, CA, has hit the market for $5.1 million, marking its first listing since it was built in 1984.

Known as The Susman House, the 2,640-square-foot residence was commissioned by former actress Tracy "Babs" Susman and designed by modernist architect Carl Maston, who also designed Dakota Johnson's home.

"The house was built by the current owner, who purchased the property in 1981 and spent a lot of time on this coast growing up," said Shannon Menzel, who co-lists the property with Chuck Menzel of The Menzel Group. "It was a lifelong dream to purchase it, and she has maintained it impeccably. It’s completely original and super modern."

The 2,640-square-foot residence was commissioned by former actress Tracy "Babs" Susman. (Sterling Reed Photography) It features an open floor plan—and plenty of texture. (Sterling Reed Photography) The home boasts stunning views of the Pacific. (Sterling Reed Photography)

Inside the oceanfront retreat, where the Pacific is "always in view," clean lines and expansive windows frame the coastal scenery.

Brick details, exposed beams, vaulted ceilings, built-ins, cedar-clad walls, and retractable glass doors further define the home’s timeless modernist aesthetic.

A double-sided fireplace anchors the cozy living area, complete with built-in window seating.

The sun-drenched kitchen features custom cabinetry, an original wood-slat ceiling, modern appliances, and oversized windows that capture the spectacular ocean views.

The home boasts a timeless interior. (Sterling Reed Photography) One of the bedrooms in the home. (Sterling Reed Photography) The Pacific Coast is "always in view". (Sterling Reed Photography)

In the adjoining dining area, retractable glass walls open onto an expansive 42-foot ocean-facing deck, creating an ideal setting for entertaining and alfresco dining.

"The seller would care for everything in this home like it’s a piece of art," Shannon noted.

Upstairs, an airy primary suite boasts vaulted ceilings, floor-to-ceiling glass, a fireplace, a soaking tub, and access to a private deck overlooking the Pacific Ocean.

"I think the condition of the home has been maintained so well over the years," she added. "It’s just so beautiful."

The open floor plan is complemented by a floating staircase, custom brick flooring, and exposed wood beams, reinforcing the home’s distinctive architectural character.

"I think it has enough character that the next buyer will want to keep it much the same and not make too many changes," Chuck said. "I hope the next buyer falls in love with its architectural heritage, design, and the timelessness of it."

The home also includes a secondary suite with its own fireplace and a guest suite featuring a "clever" privacy divider.

Set on Mondo's Cove about an hour northwest of Los Angeles, the property offers views of the Channel Islands, access to private beach entry points, and use of a community tennis court.

"Along this coast, about 30 to 40 percent of the owners are permanent, full-time residents, so the majority are second homes," Chuck said.

"It’s a very sweeping cove that looks like it could be in Greece. It’s very private and quiet, with surf breaks everywhere. This cove always has sand in the summertime. It’s like Waikiki. It would be perfect for someone who wants to surf or have that surfing experience."

Why the Ilhan Omar saga isn't going away

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump has his favorite riffs during campaign rallies.

He’s recently complimented the physique of Sen. Jon Husted, R-Ohio. He still calls out former President Joe Biden and Vice President Kamala Harris.

But in recent weeks, the president spun Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., into heavy rotation on his playlist. The Department of Homeland Security and Justice Department are now probing Omar for alleged immigration fraud and financial disclosure reports.

TRUMP SAYS ILHAN OMAR COULD GO TO JAIL OR BE DEPORTED, CALLS HER ‘A DISGRACE TO OUR COUNTRY’

"What she did was use a fake name and other things that are fake," the president groused about Omar. "She used the fake name to be sworn in. I assume she must immediately resign from Congress."

Trump argued that Democrats "just conceded her seat."

They didn’t.

And the president declared that "she will not be legally allowed to be a member of that august body of politicians."

Not accurate.

The president doesn’t decide who gets to sit in Congress. Article I, Section 5 of the Constitution grants that power to the House and Senate. It charges both bodies with authority to "be the Judge of the Elections, Returns and Qualifications of its own Members." The Constitution goes on to say that the House and Senate may "punish its Members for Disorderly Behavior; and, with the Concurrence of two thirds, expel a Member."

We bring up those responsibilities because it’s possible that the House may need to tangle with the "qualifications" of Omar. Especially since the New York Times reported that the Minnesota Democrat faced scrutiny by the Obama-era Justice Department in 2016. Moreover, that inquiry says the feds found evidence that she may have entered into a fraudulent marriage.

If that’s the case, there could be questions about Omar’s citizenship. And thus, whether she’s even permitted to serve in Congress. Especially if it’s found she was naturalized as a citizen via bogus means.

Republicans have been after Omar for years. Across the aisle, Democrats are concerned this could be a distracting headache. Especially if they seize control of the House after the midterms next week.

THE HITCHHIKER'S GUIDE TO WHY REMOVING ILHAN OMAR FROM CONGRESS COULD BE HARDER THAN TRUMP EXPECTS

The inquiry into Omar began in 2016 when she ran for the state legislature. Former President Barack Obama was in office. The investigation continued into the Biden administration. The feds terminated the inquiry in 2021.

After the Trump administration raided Omar’s home in October — seizing electronics and documents — Obama defended the congresswoman.

"It is a basic principle of American democracy that the government can’t selectively target individuals simply because of their political beliefs or what they say," the former president said. "But that’s exactly what the Trump administration is doing to Congresswoman Ilhan Omar."

Obama said the current inquiry flipped her life "upside down" and characterized it as an effort to "intimidate someone."

Controversial influencer and livestreamer Hasan Piker asked Omar about how her week was going amid the investigation.

"It tracks for me," replied Omar. "That’s kind of what I’m used to."

A spokeswoman for Omar said that she "has repeatedly and unequivocally denied this baseless, disgusting conspiracy theory."

But indications that Omar faced scrutiny ten years ago make it harder to characterize this as wild, right-wing conjecture emanating from the blogosphere.

"Who ordered [the investigation] closed?" asked Fox contributor Paul Mauro. "Reportedly, both administrations had opened cases into this. We now know how much smoke there is."

Omar campaigned for the state legislature in 2016. Her campaign released a letter from the U.S. attorney saying it "wasn’t investigating" nor requesting an inquiry into Omar.

So was she cleared? Or does word of the old probe pour petrol on the flames?

"Omar will talk about and say, ‘They've looked into this. Nothing came about it,’" said University of Minnesota political science professor Kathryn Pearson. "But I think it's also a talking point that Republicans will use. So I think sort of both sides will try to use that to their advantage."

John Nagel is Omar’s Republican opponent on the ballot next week. Nagel views the new investigation as a political wedge.

"With the raid on her home, people should keep this in mind. It's a very serious accusation," Nagel said. "As far as I'm concerned, Somalia comes first in her life. And we are nothing more than a piggy bank for her."

TRUMP PUSH TO OUST OMAR FACES HISTORIC SUPREME COURT TEST, BUT LEGAL EXPERTS SEE ‘UNCHARTED’ TWIST

At least one Republican is ready to bounce Omar from the House.

"If the evidence shows that she is not an American, she has to be removed immediately and probably put in prison," said Rep. Randy Fine, R-Fla. "I think the idea that a foreigner might be actually serving in Congress should be terrifying to Americans."

But another House Republican urges caution.

"We just need all the facts," said Rep. Chris Smith, R-N.J. "I hope that the investigation is thorough, fair, and absolutely aboveboard."

And then there is the question of due process.

"The Ethics Committee should commence an investigation. It takes two-thirds of the House to expel a sitting Member. I would highly doubt the Democrats would agree. And certainly not without findings," Rep. Mike Lawler, R-N.Y., told Fox. "I think at a minimum, the Ethics Committee should move forward with an investigation immediately upon our return."

This could develop into a real hornet’s nest for the House when lawmakers descend on Washington after the midterms. That’s to say nothing of questions about seating Omar when the new Congress starts in January. If Democrats win the House, would House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y. — perhaps as speaker — be forced to grapple with the question of seating Omar if Republicans move to exclude her?

In fact, such a motion to exclude comes right before the new speaker swears in the entire House on Jan. 3. The speaker would swear in 434 members. Then takes votes related to the potential member in question. If Democrats win the House, new, moderate House Democrats might take their first formal floor vote on whether to seat Omar.

Voting against Omar might be good for some of these new members, considering the political nature of their battleground districts. But would the Democratic brass pressure new members to vote with the caucus on such a nettlesome issue?

Democrats who vote to seat Omar give Republicans an immediate opportunity to build a case against newly-christened members representing swing districts. A vote to exclude or expel Omar would go down as one of the most important roll calls of the entire two-year Congress.

We haven’t even explored how progressives and Democratic Socialists — new to the House — might rage at moderate Democrats for not supporting their colleague. That could produce a serious schism among Democrats.

This isn’t going away. Omar is a lightning rod. The GOP believes it can continue to milk this. President Trump has his favorite riffs about Omar. And it’s possible that House Republicans may as well.

United is bringing its nicest plane (with the Polaris Studios) to a city that never sees it

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

United Airlines is bringing its most premium plane to a city where it's rarely spotted.

In January, the Chicago-based carrier will operate a few round trips between Las Vegas and Tokyo, and TPG has learned United plans to use one of its brand-new, luxe Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners on the route.

This is the posh plane United debuted earlier this year, featuring the "Elevated" interior, complete with its next-generation Polaris Suites and the extra-spacious Polaris Studios.

To score a seat on one of those aircraft, you typically have to be flying out of one of United's major hubs like San Francisco or Houston. So far, the carrier has prioritized these planes — which have a whopping 99 premium seats — on routes to destinations where high-end seats are in high demand, like London, Singapore and Seoul.

But it will make a limited appearance on a route United doesn't typically fly: Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) in Las Vegas to Narita International Airport (NRT) in Tokyo.

United Airlines' new Boeing 787-9 "Elevated" Dreamliner. SEAN CUDAHY/THE POINTS GUY

A special route for CES 2027

The occasion for the special route with the luxe plane is the 2027 Consumer Electronics Show, which is set to run in Vegas from Jan. 6 to Jan. 9.

It's one of the biggest conventions in the city each year, which draws visitors from all over the world.

Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) in Las Vegas. SEAN CUDAHY/THE POINTS GUY

Airlines often add special temporary routes around major events — sometimes with planes fit for the occasion, such as when American Airlines sent its popular Airbus "A321T" to Sin City for the Super Bowl in 2024.

We also noted last month how carriers are leaning more heavily into major sporting events, with new and unusual routes focused on college and professional football games.

Caviar, lie-flat beds and a cool snack bar: 17 hours in United's swanky new Polaris suites

Delta also planning special CES routes

United's 787 will certainly be a unique sight at LAS, but top rival Delta Air Lines is the U.S. airline that has historically made the biggest splash at CES.

Nearly four years ago, at that convention, Delta laid out its plans to make on-board Wi-Fi free for all SkyMiles members — a move that nearly every major U.S. carrier matched.

And in January 2025, Delta took over Vegas' iconic Sphere for a keynote presentation by its CEO, Ed Bastian.

Delta Air Lines' January 2025 keynote at the Sphere in Las Vegas. SEAN CUDAHY/THE POINTS GUY

Delta, for its part, is also adding special routes centered around CES.

It'll operate one-off services from Las Vegas to Hong Kong and Taipei, along with extra daily flights during the event to its hubs and its focus city, Austin.

Route Details: United's CES 2027 flights

As for United, its "Elevated" 787 service from LAS to NRT will run on three days toward the tail-end of CES: Jan. 9, 10 and 11.

SEAN CUDAHY/THE POINTS GUY

United's premium-configured 787-9 features:

  • Eight Polaris Studios
  • 56 standard Polaris business-class suites
  • 35 Premium Plus recliners
  • 123 seats in the main cabin, including 33 extra legroom Economy Plus seats

Flights from Tokyo to Las Vegas (Jan. 3, 4 and 5) will be on an older Boeing 777-200ER.
