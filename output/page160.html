The bottleneck in every classroom: Working memory, cognitive load, and the conditions for learning

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Throughout my career, I have held various roles (teacher, principal, director of curriculum and instruction, author, speaker, and solutions provider) but I fundamentally see all of them as forms of teaching. The core principle remains unchanged: once a teacher, always a teacher. My actions are consistently guided by one fundamental question: how can we best support students’ learning processes based on cognitive science principles to achieve optimal understanding and retrieval?

That question eventually led me to the science of learning, and specifically to cognitive load theory. It’s a simple idea with enormous implications for how we design classrooms, and I think every educator and every EdTech company that builds classroom products needs to understand it.

The bottleneck we all share

For learning to happen, information has to pass through two memory systems. Long-term memory is where lasting knowledge lives, and it’s effectively unlimited. But nothing reaches it directly; everything has to move first through working memory, the small mental workspace where we actively hold and make sense of new information. That’s the bottleneck every lesson has to move through. Working memory can hold only three to four items at once (Cowan, 2010). If those items make the transfer into long-term memory, they stay with us. That’s how students build durable learning. If they don’t make it all the way through, we forget them.

Everyone deals with this. It’s why we break phone numbers into short chunks instead of holding all ten digits at once: working memory can only juggle a handful of items in any given moment, and chunking is how we work around that limit. Miller’s classic estimate (1956) put the number higher, seven, give or take two, but more controlled research since has revised it down to about four (Cowan, 2010). Either way, the lesson holds: it’s also why we forget someone’s name two minutes after meeting them at a gathering. This isn’t a student limitation or a young-brain limitation. It’s a human-brain limitation.

Two kinds of load — and only one of them helps

Cognitive load theory splits the demand on that bottleneck into two categories (Sweller, 1988; Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas, 1998). Intrinsic load is the necessary difficulty of the content itself, the load required for students actually to learn the material. Extraneous load is the load imposed by the surroundings: distraction, clutter, anything competing for the same mental space as the learning. Only extraneous load is pure waste. Our brains are built to focus on one important thing at a time, so anything extraneous simply crowds out the material we actually want students to keep.

That distinction reframed how I think about technology in the classroom. My instinct isn’t to keep equipping classrooms with more technology. It’s a better question to ask whether a given tool is reducing cognitive load, removing barriers, and protecting the relationship between the teacher, the student, and the task they’re working on.

Why the explicit, guided approach wins

This is where the research is unambiguous. Novices learn far better with clear, guided, step-by-step teaching than they do being left to discover material on their own, and it comes down to protecting that same narrow channel. Rosenshine’s Principles of Instruction (2012) and Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark’s foundational 2006 paper on minimally guided instruction both point in the same direction: every step protects working memory:

●      Small steps that introduce one element at a time

●      Modeled examples and discussion before students go it alone

●      Guided practice with support before release

●      And review spaced out until recall becomes automatic.

In practice, this means chunking and pacing instruction: seven to eight minutes of instruction, then retrieval, so students can encode what they just heard before you move on. Retrieval isn’t a quick quiz or exit ticket tacked onto the end of a lesson. It’s the mechanism of the learning itself. When you think about the classroom, think about what conditions are most conducive to durable learning — the barriers we can remove, and what the research says deep learning actually requires.

A real classroom example: The acoustic environment

Extraneous load isn’t abstract, and it isn’t rare. Recent classroom measurements found that ambient noise during active instruction averages around 50 decibels, well above the range where researchers consistently see cognitive performance decline (Visentin & Prodi, 2026). I’ve seen this play out in real classrooms over the years. Consider a classroom that doesn’t have equal voice distribution. A student in the further from the teacher doesn’t hear the same lesson as one who happens to be closer to the teacher. Sound diminishes sharply with distance, so that student is already at a disadvantage before a single distraction even hits. Add HVAC noise, hallway sound, and the classroom next door, and the brain now has to reconstruct words it didn’t fully hear.

That reconstruction isn’t free. Recent research on listening effort confirms as much: even after a word is correctly understood, the brain keeps spending working memory tracking down what it thinks was said, because comprehension and reconstruction draw on the same limited resource (Flaherty & Klug, 2026). When noise creates a gap, that reconstruction effort comes straight out of the capacity a student needs for the actual content.

I’ve seen classrooms move from no voice distribution at all to a teacher wearing a microphone paired with classroom audio, so every seat receives that same voice at the same strength, regardless of where a student sits. That’s not a nice-to-have. It’s a direct reduction in extraneous cognitive load, and it benefits every student in the room, not just the ones who already have a formal accommodation in place, with the clearest gains for younger students, whose auditory processing is still maturing, and multilingual learners who are decoding an unfamiliar language on top of new content.

Next steps for the classroom

If you’re an educator or a leader deciding what to invest in next, the question isn’t, “What new tool should we add?” A better question is, “What’s currently competing with learning for a student’s limited working memory, and how do we remove it?”

  1. Think about the classroom learning conditions and what research says is most conducive for deep learning. What barriers can we remove?
  2. Audit the environment itself: noise, visual clutter, and redundant text and narration, with the same rigor you’d apply to a lesson plan.
  3. Explicit, direct instruction is the most well-supported, evidence-based instructional strategy, and that doesn’t mean lecture alone. It includes guided independent practice, worked examples, modeling, and retrieval: all of it requiring direct communication between teacher and student.
  4. For teachers, chunk instruction for seven to eight minutes. Then practice retrieval, so students can encode. Then provide another seven to eight minutes of instruction. This helps students move information from working memory to long-term memory.
  5. The pedagogy matters, but so does whether every student can actually hear it. Fixing the environment is not separate from good teaching. It’s what makes good teaching reach every student in the room.

Early Puberty More Common in Girls with ADHD

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Puberty is not a single event, but a years-long process that is both universal and highly individual. It shapes brain and emotional development, identity, and lifelong physical and mental health. If it begins before a child is developmentally ready, it can increase the risk for physical and mental health problems.

Research suggests that girls with ADHD are more likely to enter puberty early and to experience the transition more intensely, placing them at heightened risk for anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and other challenges. Parental guidance, emotional regulation skills, and professional support can buffer these risks by bolstering self-confidence in girls as they navigate the pubertal transition.

The Early Onset of Puberty

Researchers are examining why girls with ADHD have about twice the risk for early puberty, and why autistic girls are up to four times more likely to experience early puberty compared to other children.1 In addition to higher rates of obesity and social stress, which could accelerate the onset of puberty, neurodivergent girls may experience differences in dopamine activity, stress response, and immune and neuroendocrine systems — all of which may affect sexual development.

What Is Precocious Puberty?

Early relative puberty — development that begins earlier than average but remains within the typical age range — is different from precocious puberty. In girls, precocious puberty refers to physical and hormonal changes before age 8, including breast and pubic hair development, rapid growth, and menstruation. It requires medical evaluation and treatment. Left unmanaged, it can contribute to shorter adult height, psychosocial distress, and other complications. Two forms exist:

  • Central precocious puberty is the most common. In most girls, no underlying medical cause has been discovered. Treatment often involves puberty blockers.
  • Peripheral precocious puberty may result from tumors, genetic conditions, or exposure to hormone creams.

Early Puberty and ADHD

When estrogen and progesterone rise in girls, ADHD symptoms fluctuate. Here’s how:

The Emotional Impact

Rising hormone levels affect the physiological and neural systems involved in emotional processing and regulation.

  • More rumination: Girls who experience early puberty are more likely to dwell on negative thoughts and worries, a pattern associated with anxiety and depression.
  • Less equipped to regulate: Hormonal changes also affect the autonomic nervous system, which helps the body regulate emotions. Resting respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) — a variation in heart rate linked to breathing — is an indicator of physiological readiness for emotion regulation that tends to be lower in girls with ADHD.

Acute ADHD Symptom Fluctuation

Girls who menstruate may also experience pronounced changes in ADHD symptoms across the menstrual cycle.

  • The same girl may appear highly symptomatic at one point in the month and minimally symptomatic at another.
  • Low estrogen phases, including the beginning of the menstrual cycle, may intensify inattention and hyperactivity.

Psychological and Social Impacts

Girls who mature early may feel distressed when their physical development outpaces their cognitive, emotional, and social development.

  • A mature appearance may expose them to attention and expectations they are not prepared to manage.
  • Visible physical differences from peers may increase self-consciousness and preoccupation with weight. Some girls attempt to control their bodies through purging or other disordered eating behaviors.

How to Protect and Empower Young Girls

  • Treat their ADHD. Accurate ADHD assessment and treatment is critical for protecting girls’ mental health. To ensure this, clinicians must understand the distinct and often subtle ways symptoms can appear during adolescence. Hyperactivity, for example, may present as excessive talking or heightened sociability.
  • Impart knowledge. Girls who are equipped with an understanding about puberty feel much more confident about managing changes. When needed, enlist an older family member or another trusted adult with whom your child feels comfortable talking.
  • Seek support. Remind your child that puberty looks different for everyone. If they need help with emotional regulation, find a mental health clinician who is well-versed in the Unified Protocol, a treatment that teaches emotional regulation skills across diagnoses.

Plus500 now expects its 2026 revenue and EBITDA to beat what analysts had penciled in, after a third quarter in which its US futures and prediction markets arm kept growing. The London-listed trading group said so in its Q3 2026 trading update on Monday morning. Just over three weeks earlier, in a brief statement on October 2, the company had only said it was trading in line with expectations.

Revenue for the three months to September 30 came in at $203.2 million, an 11% rise on the $182.7 million booked a year ago. EBITDA grew 7% to $88.4 million, a 44% margin. Across the first nine months, revenue reached $666.1 million and EBITDA $275.9 million. Plus500 did not put a new number on its full-year outlook.

Prediction markets move into the core US offer

The part of the update most relevant to the event contract debate is the US business. Plus500 groups futures, options on futures and its prediction market products under what it calls non-OTC, as opposed to its traditional CFD business. That segment produced roughly 16% of group revenue and about 31% of new customers so far this year.

During the quarter, the company added CFTC-regulated prediction market contracts covering both sports and non-sports events, as well as single stock futures. It also signed two international B2B partners, Wealthsimple and Nelogica, which plug into its execution and clearing infrastructure. Funds held for customers in segregated accounts passed $1 billion at the end of September.

The timing matters. Federally regulated exchanges have become a crowded field this year, with sportsbook operators such as DraftKings now running their own platforms. DraftKings CEO Jason Robins recently claimed DKeX had become the third-largest US prediction exchange. Plus500 is taking a different route, acting as broker and clearing firm rather than building a consumer sportsbook brand.

A US super-app planned for early 2027

Plus500 also confirmed plans for a single US app in the first quarter of 2027. The company says the app will bring together futures, options on futures, prediction markets, stocks, options and ETFs. The stock and ETF side depends on Hedge, a US broker-dealer Plus500 has agreed to buy, subject to conditions. Hedge would give the group access to SEC-regulated securities for the first time. A second deal, for Guru in Brazil, would give Plus500 its first local operation in the country and a base for growth elsewhere in the region.

“In the US, Hedge will unlock access to SEC-regulated markets, supporting the planned launch of a new, multi-asset ‘super-app’ in Q1 2027,” chief executive David Zruia said in the statement.

Customer numbers and cash

New customers in the quarter rose 27% to 28,747, while the average cost of acquiring each one fell 14% to $1,150. Active customers totaled 119,528 for Q3. Customer deposits hit a quarterly record of $1.9 billion.

The group finished September with more than $775 million in cash and no debt, after paying out $87.4 million in dividends and spending around $63.5 million on buybacks during the quarter. Both acquisitions will be paid for from existing cash.

For anyone tracking how event contracts are spreading beyond Kalshi and Polymarket, the numbers show a traditional CFD broker now leaning on US prediction markets as a growth line. Douglas Luiz has finally scored for Juventus. The Brazilian midfielder found the net in Sunday’s 2-0 win at Lecce, more than two years after his €50m move from Aston Villa, and he also set up the opening goal for Weston McKennie.

It was a night that changed the conversation around a player who has spent much of his time in Turin under scrutiny. Luciano Spalletti was quick to single him out afterwards, and the club’s own numbers show just how rare an evening like this has been for him.

A first in more than 260 top-flight games

According to the standout stats published by Juventus on Monday, this was the first time Douglas Luiz had both scored and assisted in the same match in Europe’s top five leagues. Before Sunday he had made 261 appearances in those competitions without managing it.

The club also points out that he now has two goal involvements from eight Serie A appearances this season, one goal and one assist. In the 2024/25 campaign he played 19 league games for Juventus and failed to register either.

Both contributions came before the break. His free kick was dinked over the Lecce defence for McKennie to break the offside line and slide in on 29 minutes. Twelve minutes later, Federico Gatti laid the ball off and Douglas Luiz hit it first time from outside the box into the far corner.

What Douglas Luiz said

The midfielder did not dwell on the personal milestone. In comments shared by the club’s press room after the game, he put the result first.

“The most important thing today was to win. I’m really happy with the result because we’ve worked so hard, and I’m delighted for the team. This victory also gives us the confidence we need,” he said.

He was also honest about the second half, when Lecce pushed Juventus back. “We played well in the first half, but I think we could definitely have done better in the second. We need to improve and perform well for the full 90 minutes.”

Speaking to DAZN, he added that the set piece routine involving Teun Koopmeiners is something the squad practises a lot, and that the players were pleased for their coach after a difficult week, as Tuttosport reported.

Spalletti’s verdict

Spalletti had already explained after the 3-0 defeat by Lazio that he wanted changes, as covered in our piece on the changes Spalletti identified. Since then Juventus have moved to a three-man midfield, and Douglas Luiz has been one of the beneficiaries.

Speaking to Sky Sport Italia, the coach was full of praise for the Brazilian. “He had a great game today, he fought and ran, he battled on all sides and he helped clean up certain situations when we needed him to,” Spalletti said, in quotes reported by Football Italia.

He came off after 82 minutes, replaced by Sarr, having done the bulk of the work in a side that had only 17 outfield players available. With Manuel Locatelli and Khéphren Thuram among the absentees listed in our latest injury update, Spalletti needs exactly this kind of reliability from his remaining midfielders.

A chance to build

One good night does not settle a debate that has followed Douglas Luiz since his arrival. What it does give him is a platform. If he can keep producing this level of energy and quality in a system that suits him, the midfielder who struggled to make an impact in his first season may yet become an important figure for Spalletti this term.

15 Things Nobody Tells You About Pawpaws

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

As soon as late summer edges toward fall in New York, I begin to lick my pawpaw-loving lips. As this native North American fruit gains mainstream recognition it has become easier to source both the fresh fruit as well as pawpaw saplings. Despite the rising tide of awareness, many people are still unfamiliar with them and have yet to taste a fresh pawpaw. And if you have lusted after the perfumed fruit but have not met one, there are few things about these green-mango lookalikes you may not know.

Here are 15 of them.

Photography by Marie Viljoen.

1. “Pawpaw” actually refers to two completely different and unrelated fruit.

Above: These are pawpaws (Asimina triloba), ripe from late summer through fall.

The pawpaw under discussion is the native North American fruit belonging to the genus Asimina. The cold-hardy common pawpaw is A. triloba, but there are over a dozen other species, with regional distributions that cleave to the eastern and Mid-Western parts of the United States, from southern Canada down to Florida.

Above: Is this a pawpaw? Yes, in some countries. But in others, it is a papaya (Carica papaya).

In regions and countries with Commonwealth and British colonial histories (think Australia, England, India, South Africa, the West Indies, and more) the common name “pawpaw” refers to what anyone from the Americas calls papaya. Soft-skinned and subtropical, small or football-sized, that pawpaw, Carica papaya, has yellow to deep rose flesh and is filled in the center with little black, peppery seeds. It is available in many grocery stores, year-round.

2. For the Shawnee, September is the month of the pawpaw moon, ha’siminikiisfwa.

Above: A pawpaw fruit suspended beneath its plush leaves.

The botanical genus name Asimina is derived from the Shawnee word for pawpaw: ha’simini. For the Shawnee, September is the month of the pawpaw moon, ha’siminikiisfwa, according to Joel Barnes, Archives Director for the Shawnee Tribe (via a West Virginia Public Radio interview in a 2020).

Until the 19th century, before they were forcibly removed by the US government, the Shawnee’s ancestral lands were in pawpaw-rich Appalachia, including current southeast Ohio.

Above: A country road in southeast Ohio. Can you spot the pawpaw tree?

3. The fruit has inspired an annual three-day festival.

Above: A best-tasting pawpaw competition at the Ohio Pawpaw Festival.

The three-day Ohio Pawpaw Festival in southeast Ohio in September is a celebration of everything pawpaw. There are pawpaw tastings, and dozens of pawpaw dishes to choose from. Chris Chmiel is one of the founders of the festival and the co-owner (with Michelle Gorman, his wife) of Integration Acres, which has sold the fresh fruit and frozen purée, and other native wild things to the public since 1996. (The first pawpaws I tasted were shipped from Ohio’s woodlands to my Brooklyn front door in 2016.)

4. The crew of the trans-continental Lewis and Clarke expedition were sustained by “poppaws.”

Above: A handful of wild pawpaws.

On 18 September, 1806, William Clark wrote, in the detailed journal that Thomas Jefferson dictated the expedition keep, that “…our party entirely out of provisions Subsisting on poppaws…the party appear perfectly contented and tell us that they can live very well on the pappaws [sic].”

There are many similar entries (reading the journal is equal parts recoil and wonder; but it is a compelling chronicle of plants encountered).

5. Pawpaws taste like a medley of ripe tropical and subtropical fruits. Or creme brûlée.

Above: Southeast Ohio pawpaws from Integration Acres.

The aroma of pawpaws is strong—they will perfume a room. Their texture when ripe is creamy. Different cultivars vary in flavor: Some pawpaws taste like a kaleidoscope of passionfruit and pineapple, lychee, and mango. Many tasters mention banana; personally, I detect none, but perhaps it is the pawpaw’s soft consistency that inspires the comparison. Others taste like caramel, lightly scorched. Think crème brûlée, with tropical overtones. A fruit may taste intense, or mild. The little slippery sac around each large seed is slightly bitter, as is the flesh closest to the skin.

6. Pawpaws come in different shades.

Above: The ripe flesh of pawpaws varies from a rich apricot, to bright yellow, to a creamy shade of butter.

7. Bruising isn’t bad.

Above: Petite windfalls. These pawpaws are ripe (and taste like tropical caramel).

Unless they are very close to ripe on the branch, pawpaws do not ripen well once picked (they are not like avocados). Visiting your local tree/s after a windstorm or heavy rain is a great way to find windfalls. Their skin may bruise or discolor, but this seldom means that the fruit itself is damaged. Rather, it’s a sign of ripeness.

8. Pawpaws contain many large, glossy seeds.

Above: A custard apple, closely related to pawpaw.
Above: A soursop and a pawpaw.

Pawpaws are  close cousins of custard apple (cherimoya) and soursops. All these large-seeded, soft-fleshed, deeply aromatic fruit belong to the family Annonaceae. The pawpaw is the cold-hardiest among them.

9. Pawpaw seeds are a laxative.

Above: Scoop and slurp, just don’t eat the seeds.

There’s no reason why you would, but don’t chew up and swallow pawpaw seeds. Eating the skin has the same effect.

10. Some people may be allergic to pawpaws.

Above: Pace yourself when eating pawpaws.

While the evidence is anecdotal, there is enough chatter in online forums to suggest that some people react badly to eating pawpaws. The result is dramatic diarrhea and, sometimes, nausea. Eat pawpaws in moderation (I cut myself off at three in one sitting. Is that moderate?). Also, some pawpaw purveyors, like Integration Acres, recommend against drying the pulp for use in fruit leather, since it can cause gastrointestinal distress. The reasons for this are not understood (yet).

11. Pawpaws contain toxins.

Above: Pawpaws very close to ripeness, on a Brooklyn tree.

Some researchers posit that eating pawpaws regularly could be detrimental to the eater. The seeds and pulp of pawpaws contain acetogenins, including annonacin, which is toxic to cortical neurons. Could regular pawpaw ingestion cause neurotoxins to build up over time? A connection has been drawn between pawpaws and their cousins, custard apples, which are popular in the Caribbean, where clusters of Parkinson’s in Guadalupe have been documented, possibly linked to a diet where the custard apples feature regularly. (Annonacin was also thought to play a role in cancer-cell death. That published paper has since been retracted.)

12. The common pawpaw is cold-hardy.

Above: Splendid pawpaws in Lola Horwitz’s south-facing Brooklyn garden (USDA zone 7b).

The common pawpaw is hardy from USDA growing zones 5 through 8 (according to Cornell).  It’s the tropical-tasting fruit many cool-climate gardeners can grow. Bradley Farms in New Paltz, New York (zone 5), brings pawpaws to greenmarkets in October. While pawpaws in the wild tend to grow in clonal colonies in the woods and on woodland edges, pawpaw trees will thrive in full sun as long as they have ample moisture in summer. They like hot, humid summers and cold winters.

13. For propagation, do not allow pawpaw seeds to dry out.

Above: Pawpaw seeds, ready for planting.

Sowing pawpaw seed is most successful when the seeds are planted before they dry out. Even three days of drying can reduce germination rates to 20%, according to the University of Kentucky. (If you can’t plant them at once, keep them in a baggy with potting soil until you can). Sowing them in fall (right after you eat them) allows them to cold-stratify naturally, outdoors.

14. Pawpaws transplant badly.

Above: Pawpaw saplings in a colony on Staten Island.

If you’re mosying around a pawpaw patch and are tempted to remove a sapling, think twice:  Pawpaws have long taproots and respond poorly to being yanked out.  Unless you have emerald-green thumbs, either grow them from seed or find a nursery that sells pawpaw trees. More and more do.

15. Pawpaw pulp freezes beautifully, with no loss of flavor.

Above: Pawpaw purée after removing the seeds and whizzing the pulp in a food processor.

It’s some trouble to scoop out all the seeds and purée the pulp in a food processor (make sure you have no seeds or skin), but once done it is easy to freeze pawpaw pulp in batches. It it is delicious in ice cream, but also cooked in cakes, cookies, breads, and savory dishes. Contrary to some online opinion, the flavor of pawpaw holds up very well to heat.

In the Stillness: A 1640s Georgian in an Affecting Palette by Cassandra Ellis

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A while back the New Zealand-born, London-based designer Cassandra Ellis sent a note from across the pond: She’d completed a project in East Sussex, not far from her redone flint barn but “a completely different color palette,” she wrote. And because this is a designer who often describes physical spaces as poetry (see Expert Advice: Developing Style with Designer Cassandra Ellis), I was, as always, intrigued.

The house is Well House, a historic Georgian building on a cobblestone street in the market town of Lewes. The structure dates to around 1640, with timber framing and a stone foundation taken from the ruins of Lewes Monastery, but it was dilapidated when Cassandra found it. “The previous owner had been here for 50 years, and the house hadn’t been touched for about 43 of those,” she told Inigo, the UK real estate site. “It looked like a pub from the 1970s.”

Cassandra and her partner, Ed, embarked on a gentle but thorough and exacting renovation, removing and replacing the entire roof, re-plumbing, re-wiring, re-plastering, insulating, swapping in new-old windows, hand-scrubbing the floors with wire brushes, and updating for weather and modern times.

My favorite thing about the project just might be the palette Cassandra mentioned, the interiors painted in rich, nuanced, dark tones. “It was such a specific moment in time for me,” says Cassandra of choosing them. “I didn’t realize (until almost too late) that I had pneumonia. I just thought I was overwhelmed from juggling too many things. For me, when life is too much, I always retreat to art and books and words (and the Financial Times), and so the palette came from this.” Many of the hues are from Cassandra’s own Atelier Ellis paint collection, This Stilling That I Love, a name that gives a hint to the house’s effect.

“When people visit the cottage, there is usually genuine emotion by the time they get to the top,” says Cassandra. “It’s not the fanciest house, but if it makes people feel, then I’ve done what I think is right.”
